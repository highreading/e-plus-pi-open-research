# Arbitrary algebraic-integer multipliers make the trace construction universal

Checked: 2026-08-26 UTC

## Verdict

Let



$$
K=\mathbb Q(\zeta _{20})^+,\qquad s=e+\pi,
 \tag{1}
$$



and let the minimally rationally cleared $n=5,c=f=0$ edge be



$$
q_d\Theta_d=u_ds+v_d,\qquad
             u_d,v_d\in\mathcal O_K,\qquad \Theta_d=-i\Lambda_{5,d}.
 \tag{2}
$$



Allow an arbitrary multiplier $\theta\in\mathcal O_K$, rather than a
unit, and define



$$
\Phi_d(\theta)=
 \left(\operatorname {Tr}_{K/\mathbb Q}(\theta u_d),
       \operatorname {Tr}_{K/\mathbb Q}(\theta v_d)\right)
 \in\mathbb Z^2.
 \tag{3}
$$



The outcome is decisive but does not prove anything about $e+\pi$:

* $\Phi_1$ has rank one because the coefficient on $s$ vanishes;
* $\Phi_d$ has rank two for every $d\geq2$;
* consequently $\Phi_d(\mathcal O_K)$ is a finite-index sublattice of
  $\mathbb Z^2$, and after ordinary integer gcd division it realizes
  **every primitive pair** $(P,Q)\in\mathbb Z^2$.

Universality already occurs at $d=2$.  In the integral basis used below,



$$
[\Phi_2]=
 \begin{pmatrix}
   10&-10&0&0\\
   -20&20&50&-50
 \end{pmatrix}.
 \tag{4}
$$



For arbitrary integers $P,Q$, the algebraic integer with coordinate
vector



$$
(5P,\,0,\,Q+2P,\,0)
 \tag{5}
$$



maps exactly to $(50P,50Q)$.  Primitivization therefore gives
$Ps+Q$.

This is not a Padé-based construction of special rational approximations;
it is a parametrization of all rational linear forms.  If $s$ were known
irrational, generic continued fractions would supply decreasing nonzero
forms.  If $s$ were rational, exact zero forms would occur.  Thus using
arbitrary algebraic-integer multipliers moves the unresolved
nonvanishing/irrationality question into the choice of $(P,Q)$; it cannot
establish either algebraicity or transcendence of $e+\pi$.

The exact Smith computation through $d=200$ confirms rank two throughout.
The image is usually far from the full lattice before primitivization, but
that index has no projective effect.

## 1. Trace matrix and Smith invariants

Use the integral basis



$$
\begin{aligned}
 b_0&=1,\\
 b_1&=\zeta _5+\zeta _5^{-1},\\
 b_2&=i(\zeta _5-\zeta _5^{-1}),\\
 b_3&=i(\zeta _5^2-\zeta _5^{-2})
\end{aligned}
 \tag{6}
$$



of $\mathcal O_K$.  Its trace Gram matrix is



$$
G=\left(\operatorname {Tr}(b_jb_k)\right)_{j,k}
 =\begin{pmatrix}
 4&-2&0&0\\
 -2&6&0&0\\
 0&0&10&0\\
 0&0&0&10
 \end{pmatrix},
 \qquad \det G=2000.
 \tag{7}
$$



If ${\bf u}_d,{\bf v}_d\in\mathbb Z^4$ are the coordinate columns of
$u_d,v_d$, then



$$
M_d=
 \begin{pmatrix}
  (G{\bf u}_d)^t\\
  (G{\bf v}_d)^t
 \end{pmatrix}
 \tag{8}
$$



is the matrix of (3).  Suppose its Smith invariants are
$\alpha_d\mid\beta_d$ when its rank is two.  Then



$$
[\mathbb Z^2:\Phi_d(\mathcal O_K)]=\alpha_d\beta_d,
 \tag{9}
$$



where



$$
\begin{aligned}
 \alpha_d&=\gcd\{\text{all entries of }M_d\},\\
 \alpha_d\beta_d&=\gcd\{\text{all \(2\) by \(2\) minors of }M_d\}.
\end{aligned}
 \tag{10}
$$



Smith normal form also gives the general universality statement directly.
Every rank-two image lattice with invariants
$\alpha_d\mid\beta_d$ contains



$$
\beta_d\mathbb Z^2.
 \tag{11}
$$



Hence for each primitive $(P,Q)$ there is a $\theta\in\mathcal O_K$
with



$$
\Phi_d(\theta)=\beta_d(P,Q).
 \tag{12}
$$



After division by the common integer factor $\beta_d$, the form is
exactly $Ps+Q$.  Formula (5) is an explicit instance that avoids any
abstract Smith argument.

There is a useful closed determinantal formula in this family.  The
coefficient $u_d$ lies in $F=\mathbb Q(\sqrt5)$, so write



$$
\begin{aligned}
 G{\bf u}_d&=(a_0,a_1,0,0)^t,\\
 G{\bf v}_d&=(b_0,b_1,c_2,c_3)^t.
\end{aligned}
 \tag{13}
$$



Then



$$
\begin{aligned}
 \alpha_d={}&\gcd(a_0,a_1,b_0,b_1,c_2,c_3),\\
 \alpha_d\beta_d={}&
 \gcd\!\left(a_0b_1-a_1b_0,\,
       \gcd(a_0,a_1)\gcd(c_2,c_3)\right).
\end{aligned}
 \tag{14}
$$



This is an exact all-degree reduction of the Smith computation to six
integers.

## 2. Relation to the coordinate-content ideal

Let



$$
\mathfrak c_d=(u_d,v_d).
 \tag{15}
$$



The first invariant has a clean ideal interpretation:



$$
\alpha_d\mathbb Z
                    =\operatorname {Tr}_{K/\mathbb Q}(\mathfrak c_d).
 \tag{16}
$$



Indeed, the right side is generated by
$\operatorname {Tr}(b_ju_d)$ and
$\operatorname {Tr}(b_jv_d)$, precisely the entries of $M_d$.

The second invariant is not determined by $\mathfrak c_d$ alone.  It is
the divisibility of the alternating trace form



$$
(\theta_1,\theta_2)\longmapsto
 \det\begin{pmatrix}
  \operatorname {Tr}(\theta_1u_d)&
  \operatorname {Tr}(\theta_2u_d)\\
  \operatorname {Tr}(\theta_1v_d)&
  \operatorname {Tr}(\theta_2v_d)
 \end{pmatrix}.
 \tag{17}
$$



For example, the exact content ideal is the unit ideal at both $d=5$
and $d=6$, while



$$
\begin{aligned}
 \beta_5&=9\,377\,500,\\
 \beta_6&=1\,264\,050.
\end{aligned}
 \tag{18}
$$



Thus content controls the common trace divisor $\alpha_d$, but not the
image index.

The finite computation gives



$$
\alpha_d=
 \begin{cases}
 10,&d\equiv2\pmod5,\\
 2,&d\not\equiv2\pmod5
 \end{cases}
 \qquad(2\leq d\leq200).
 \tag{19}
$$



This is an exact finite pattern, not an all-degree assertion.  The
ubiquitous factor $2$ is already visible from
$\operatorname {Tr}(\mathcal O_K)=2\mathbb Z$.  The factor $5$ in the
first line reflects the ramified $5$-part of $\mathfrak c_d$.  The
nonrational ideal factor above $19$, when present, does not force every
rational trace to be divisible by $19$.

## 3. Rank two for every $d\geq2$

It remains to justify that the finite rank pattern is an all-degree fact.
Let



$$
x=\eta=(1+\zeta _5)^{-1},\qquad y=\bar\eta=1-x,\qquad
 \rho=|x|=|y|=\varphi^{-1}<\frac58.
 \tag{20}
$$



On the edge,



$$
A_d(z)=(-1)^d\sum_{j=0}^d\frac{(-z)^j}{j!}.
 \tag{21}
$$



For $z=x,y$, the exponential-tail estimate, $e^\rho<2$, gives for
every $d\geq2$



$$
\left|A_d(z)-(-1)^de^{-z}\right|
 \leq e^\rho\frac{\rho^{d+1}}{(d+1)!}<\frac1{10}.
 \tag{22}
$$



Here $e^\rho<2$ follows without decimal estimates from
$\rho<5/8<2/3<\log 2$, where
$\log 2=2\sum_{m\geq0}((2m+1)3^{2m+1})^{-1}>2/3$.  The same inequality
also implies $e^{-1/2}>1/2$.  Thus, since
$\operatorname {Re}x=\operatorname {Re}y=1/2$,



$$
|A_d(x)|,|A_d(y)|>\frac25.
 \tag{23}
$$



Also $A_d(1)=(-1)^d {!d}/d!\ne0$ for $d\geq2$.  Hence the coefficient
$u_d$, which is a nonzero rational multiple of
$A_d(1)A_d(x)A_d(y)$, is a nonzero element of $F$.

The anti-fixed component of $v_d$ is a nonzero rational multiple of



$$
J_d=A_d(x)B_d(y)-A_d(y)B_d(x).
 \tag{24}
$$



To prove $J_d\ne0$, put
$R_d(z)=A_d(z)\operatorname {Log}(1-z)-B_d(z)$.  Since its Taylor
coefficients through degree $d$ vanish, for $|z|=\rho$



$$
|R_d(z)|
 \leq 3\sum_{m\geq1}\frac{\rho^{d+m}}m
 <6\left(\frac58\right)^d.
 \tag{25}
$$



Here we used $e<3$ and
$-\log(1-\rho)<2$.  Direct substitution gives



$$
\begin{aligned}
 J_d={}&A_d(x)A_d(y)
        \{\operatorname {Log}x-\operatorname {Log}y\}\\
 &+A_d(y)R_d(x)-A_d(x)R_d(y).
\end{aligned}
 \tag{26}
$$



The principal-log difference has modulus $2\pi/5$.  By (23) and
$\pi>3$, the first line of (26) has modulus greater than



$$
\frac{24}{125}.
 \tag{27}
$$



The elementary upper bound $|A_d(x)|,|A_d(y)|<3$ and (25) bound the
second line by



$$
36\left(\frac58\right)^d.
 \tag{28}
$$



For $d\geq20$, (28) is smaller than (27); equivalently, it is enough to
check the exact integer inequality



$$
375\cdot5^{20}<2\cdot8^{20}.
 \tag{29}
$$



Thus $J_d\ne0$ for $d\geq20$.  The archived exact field computation
checks $J_d\ne0$ for $2\leq d\leq19$.  Therefore $v_d$ has a
nonzero anti-fixed component for every $d\geq2$.

We have shown that $u_d\in F\setminus\{0\}$, whereas
$v_d\notin F$.  They are linearly independent over $\mathbb Q$.
The trace pairing on $K$ is nondegenerate, so the two trace functionals
in (3) are independent.  This proves



$$
\operatorname {rank}\Phi_d=2
                              \qquad(d\geq2).
 \tag{30}
$$



## 4. Exact computation and logical limitation

The exact script and complete degree-$200$ output are
<scripts/arbitrary_integer_trace_lattice.py> and
<results/arbitrary_integer_trace_lattice_d200.json>.  They use the already
audited minimal-clearing arithmetic and content records.  The output
contains every Smith pair, selected trace/Hermite matrices, the exact
low-degree anti-fixed checks, and the literal $d=2$ witness (5).

Arbitrary $\mathcal O_K$ multipliers therefore do generate genuinely new
primitive rational forms—indeed, all of them.  This is a viable
surjectivity statement but a no-go for extracting special arithmetic
information from the Padé origin alone.  Any decreasing sequence selected
from this universal family must import an independent rational
approximation and an independent nonvanishing argument for $e+\pi$.
