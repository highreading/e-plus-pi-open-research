# Arithmetic normalization of the raw-arctangent endpoint family

Date: 2026-08-26

This note isolates what the all-degree $2$-adic determinant argument in
`raw_arctan_bordered_rank_proof.md` does, and does not, say about the
integer linear form at $z=1$.  There are three main conclusions.

1. The determinant which represents the unnormalized coefficient $B(1)$
   has an exact $2$-adic valuation in every degree.
2. Primitive endpoint normalization introduces a second determinant.  An
   exact cofactor identity shows that almost all of the large divisibility
   of the first determinant may disappear.  Thus the rank proof alone gives
   no lower bound for the primitive endpoint denominator.
3. The first unconstrained Taylor coefficient is represented by a third,
   independent augmented determinant.  Its nonvanishing was checked exactly
   through degree $30$, but it is not a consequence of the accepted rank
   theorem and is not proved here in all degrees.

The finite calculation is archived in
`results/raw_arctan_endpoint_arithmetic_n30.json`, generated by
`scripts/raw_arctan_endpoint_arithmetic.py`.

## 1. The integral high-jet matrix

Fix $n\geq0$, put $m=n+1$, and write

$$
B(z)=\sum_{a=0}^n b_az^a,\qquad
C(z)=\sum_{a=0}^n c_az^a.                         \tag{1}
$$

For $r\in\mathbb Z$, set

$$
\tau_r=\begin{cases}
(-1)^{(r-1)/2}(r-1)!,&r>0\text{ odd},\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}                                       \tag{2}
$$

Let $J_n$ be the $(2n+1)\times(2n+2)$ integer matrix whose first $2n$
rows, indexed by $k=n+1,\ldots,3n$, are

$$
\bigl((k)_0,\ldots,(k)_n\mid
      (k)_0\tau_k,\ldots,(k)_n\tau_{k-n}\bigr),  \tag{3}
$$

and whose last row is

$$
(-4,\ldots,-4\mid1,\ldots,1).                    \tag{4}
$$

The row (3) is $k!$ times the coefficient functional
$[z^k](Be^z+C\arctan z)$.  Thus $J_n(b,c)^T=0$ is precisely the high-order
part of

$$
A+Be^z+C\arctan z=O(z^{3n+1}),\qquad C(1)=4B(1). \tag{5}
$$

The all-degree rank theorem proves

$$
\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb Q}J_n=2n+1.          \tag{6}
$$

Let

$$
u_B=(1,\ldots,1\mid0,\ldots,0),\qquad
\Delta_{B,n}=\det\begin{pmatrix}J_n\\u_B\end{pmatrix}. \tag{7}
$$

Equation (6) and Proposition 2.1 of `raw_arctan_endpoint_remainder.md`
give $\Delta_{B,n}\ne0$.

## 2. Exact all-degree valuation of the unnormalized cofactor

Write $\nu=\nu_2$ and

$$
\phi(k)=\nu(k!),\qquad
S(q)=\sum_{j=0}^{q-1}\phi(j),                     \tag{8}
$$

$$
G(q)=\frac{q(q-1)}2+S(q),                         \tag{9}
$$

$$
H(q)=\frac{(q-1)(q-2)}2+S(q-1)+(q-1)
      +\mathbf1_{2\mid q}.                       \tag{10}
$$

Here $H$ is the bordered-Cauchy valuation denoted $\beta$ in equation (12)
of the rank note; a new letter is used to avoid confusion with the primitive
endpoint coefficient below.

**Theorem 2.1.**  For $n=0$, $\nu(\Delta_{B,0})=0$.  For $n\geq1$, define
$D_n$ as follows.  If $n=2r$, then

$$
D_n=\sum_{k=n+1}^{2n}\phi(k)+S(n)+3G(r)+H(r+1).  \tag{11}
$$

If $n=2r+1$, then

$$
D_n=\sum_{k=n+1}^{2n}\phi(k)+S(n)
    +2G(r+1)+G(r)+H(r+1).                         \tag{12}
$$

Then

$$
\boxed{\nu(\Delta_{B,n})=D_n.}                   \tag{13}
$$

In particular,

$$
D_n=3n^2+O(n\log n).                              \tag{14}
$$

**Proof.**  Divide each jet row (3) by $k!$.  Make the unimodular change of
coordinates

$$
B(z)=x+(z-1)\widetilde B(z),\qquad
C(z)=y+(z-1)\widetilde C(z),                      \tag{15}
$$

where $x=B(1)$, $y=C(1)$, and
$\deg\widetilde B,\deg\widetilde C<n$.  The final two rows (4) and (7)
become $y-4x$ and $x$.  Eliminating the $x,y$ columns leaves exactly the
$2n\times2n$ matrix $T_n$ of the bordered-rank proof.  All coordinate and
final-row determinants are units.  Consequently

$$
\nu(\Delta_{B,n})=
\sum_{k=n+1}^{3n}\phi(k)+\nu(\det T_n).           \tag{16}
$$

For $n=2r$, the unique least-valuation term found in Sections 2--4 of that
proof gives

$$
\nu(\det T_n)=S(n)-\sum_{k=2n+1}^{3n}\phi(k)
                    +3G(r)+H(r+1).                \tag{17}
$$

For $n=2r+1$, it gives

$$
\nu(\det T_n)=S(n)-\sum_{k=2n+1}^{3n}\phi(k)
                    +2G(r+1)+G(r)+H(r+1).         \tag{18}
$$

(The extra Vandermonde term written as $\nu(n)$ in that proof is zero here
because this case has odd $n$.)  Substitution in (16) cancels the range
$2n+1,\ldots,3n$ and proves (11)--(13).  Finally
$\phi(k)=k+O(\log k)$ in the summed ranges, which gives (14). $\square$

Theorem 2.1 is a genuine arithmetic consequence of the rank proof.  It is
an exact statement about a cofactor, not yet about a primitive linear form.

## 3. The second determinant introduced by endpoint normalization

The low Taylor equations uniquely determine $A$.  Its endpoint value is the
rational functional

$$
A(1)=-\sum_{k=0}^n[z^k](Be^z+C\arctan z).         \tag{19}
$$

Multiplication by $n!$ makes this an integral row $u_A$.  Explicitly,

$$
(u_A)_{b_j}=-n!\sum_{r=0}^{n-j}\frac1{r!},        \tag{20}
$$

$$
(u_A)_{c_j}=-n!\sum_{\substack{1\leq r\leq n-j\\r\text{ odd}}}
             \frac{(-1)^{(r-1)/2}}r.              \tag{21}
$$

The integrality of (21) uses only $r\mid n!$ for $r\leq n$.  Put

$$
\Delta_{A,n}=\det\begin{pmatrix}J_n\\u_A\end{pmatrix}. \tag{22}
$$

The elementary cofactor lemma for a full-row-rank $(N-1)\times N$ matrix
says that, for any appended row $u$, its determinant is the value of $u$ on
a fixed cofactor vector spanning the kernel.  Applying it to $u_A$ and
$u_B$ gives the exact, scale-independent identity

$$
\boxed{\frac{A(1)}{B(1)}=
       \frac{\Delta_{A,n}}{n!\,\Delta_{B,n}}.}    \tag{23}
$$

Let $(\alpha_n,\beta_n)$ be the primitive integral endpoint pair, so that

$$
\alpha_n+\beta_n(e+\pi)                           \tag{24}
$$

is the endpoint-primitive form and
$\gcd(|\alpha_n|,|\beta_n|)=1$.  Equation (23) gives, up to a common sign,

$$
h_n=\gcd\bigl(|\Delta_{A,n}|,n!|\Delta_{B,n}|\bigr), \tag{25}
$$

$$
\boxed{
\alpha_n=\frac{\Delta_{A,n}}{h_n},\qquad
\beta_n=\frac{n!\Delta_{B,n}}{h_n}.}              \tag{26}
$$

Therefore, for every prime $p$ (with $\nu_p(0)=+\infty$),

$$
\boxed{
\nu_p(\beta_n)=
\max\{0,\nu_p(n!\Delta_{B,n})-\nu_p(\Delta_{A,n})\}.} \tag{27}
$$

This is the missing normalization formula.  Theorem 2.1 determines the
$\Delta_B$ term when $p=2$, but the bordered-rank proof contains no estimate
for $\Delta_A$.  In particular, the large value $D_n\asymp3n^2$ cannot be
quoted as divisibility of $\beta_n$.

There is nevertheless an unconditional, if coarse, height bound.  Let
$\mathcal H_n=\sum_{r=1}^n1/r$ and $m=n+1$.  In a high jet row with
$n+1\leq k\leq3n$, the $B$ entries are at most $k^n$ in absolute value and
the nonzero $C$ entries satisfy

$$
(k)_a|\tau_{k-a}|=\frac{k!}{k-a}\leq k!.         \tag{28}
$$

Also $\|u_A\|_2\leq\sqrt{2m}\,n!(3+\mathcal H_n)$.  Hadamard's inequality
applied to (7) and (22), followed by (26), yields

$$
\boxed{
\max(|\alpha_n|,|\beta_n|)
\leq \sqrt{34}\,m\,n!(3+\mathcal H_n)(2m)^n
\prod_{k=n+1}^{3n}\max\{k^n,k!\}.}               \tag{29}
$$

Thus this branch does supply an explicit all-degree primitive endpoint
height bound of size $\exp(O(n^2\log n))$.  It is much too large to overcome
the merely algebraic raw arctangent-tail bound in
`raw_arctan_endpoint_remainder.md`.

## 4. The first unconstrained coefficient is a third determinant

Put

$$
M=3n+1
$$

and let $u_M$ be the integer jet row (3) with $k=M$.  Define

$$
\Delta_{M,n}=\det\begin{pmatrix}J_n\\u_M\end{pmatrix}. \tag{30}
$$

For any nonzero solution of (5), let

$$
q_{M,n}=[z^M](Be^z+C\arctan z).                  \tag{31}
$$

The same cofactor lemma gives

$$
\boxed{
\frac{q_{M,n}}{B(1)}=
\frac{\Delta_{M,n}}{M!\,\Delta_{B,n}}.}          \tag{32}
$$

Consequently

$$
q_{M,n}\ne0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\Delta_{M,n}\ne0.                                \tag{33}
$$

This equivalence is useful because it identifies the exact missing theorem.
But full row rank of $J_n$, or equivalently $\Delta_{B,n}\ne0$, does not
imply (33): an unrelated appended row can lie in the row span of $J_n$.
Thus the accepted bordered-rank proof cannot be used to certify the exact
vanishing order $M$ without an additional determinant argument.

## 5. Exact finite audit and the inference it rules out

The archived script performed the following exact checks for every
$0\leq n\leq30$:

* construction of the one-dimensional rational solution line;
* primitive integral polynomial and endpoint normalization;
* nonvanishing of $q_{M,n}$;
* the finite pattern

  $$
  \nu_2(\beta_n)=n+2\left\lfloor\frac{n+2}{4}\right\rfloor. \tag{34}
  $$

The all-degree formula (13) was evaluated on the same range.  Direct
determinant computations independently checked (13), (23), and (32) for
$0\leq n\leq10$.  Selected exact valuations are:

| $n$ | $\nu_2(\Delta_B)$ | $\nu_2(n!\Delta_B)$ | $\nu_2(\Delta_A)$ | $\nu_2(\beta_n)$ | $q_{M,n}\ne0$ |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|:---:|
| 1  | 1    | 1    | 0    | 1  | yes |
| 2  | 6    | 7    | 3    | 4  | yes |
| 6  | 72   | 76   | 66   | 10 | yes |
| 10 | 226  | 234  | 218  | 16 | yes |
| 18 | 806  | 822  | 794  | 28 | yes |
| 24 | 1484 | 1506 | 1470 | 36 | yes |
| 30 | 2372 | 2398 | 2352 | 46 | yes |

The values of $\nu_2(\Delta_A)$ beyond the direct-determinant range follow
exactly from (23) and the independently computed rational endpoint pair.
For example, in degree $18$, primitive normalization removes $794$ of the
$822$ powers of $2$ in $18!\Delta_B$, leaving only
$\nu_2(\beta_{18})=28$.

Equation (34) and the nonvanishing in (33) are **finite observations through
degree $30$ only**.  No recurrence or all-degree valuation argument for
$\Delta_A$ or $\Delta_M$ has been proved, so neither observation is
extrapolated.  This distinction is not cosmetic: (27) rigorously shows why
the exact all-degree valuation (13) is insufficient after endpoint gcd
reduction.

## 6. Status of this branch

The arithmetic normalization is now exact at the determinant level:

$$
\Delta_B\quad\text{(proved all-degree valuation)},\qquad
\Delta_A\quad\text{(primitive reduction)},\qquad
\Delta_M\quad\text{(first free coefficient)}.    \tag{35}
$$

What remains is an all-degree theorem for $\Delta_A$ and/or $\Delta_M$, or
an Archimedean estimate strong enough to combine the height bound (29) with
the endpoint remainder.  The present results prove neither irrationality
nor transcendence of $e+\pi$, and they do not support treating an
unnormalized determinant as a small or highly divisible primitive linear
form.
