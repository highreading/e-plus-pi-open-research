# Item 287 — recurrence-universal annihilators are tautological

Checked: 2026-08-31 (Beijing time)

## 1. Scope and verdict

Retain



$$
q_0=q_1=1,\qquad q_{n+2}=(4n+6)q_{n+1}+q_n,            \tag{1.1}
$$



and the Item-285 boundary unit



$$
u_n=-q_{n+1}^{\,2}\pmod Q,                             \tag{1.2}
$$



where $Q$ is either the full target $q_n$ or a declared proper
target satisfying



$$
Q\mid\overline q_{m,n}
:={q_n\over\gcd(q_n,D_m)}.                             \tag{1.3}
$$



This item tests whether an $O(n)$-height lift or fixed-degree monic
annihilator can be generated by the beta recurrence itself.

There are two logically different notions.

1. A **recurrence-universal relation** is a formal identity valid for
   every order-two state satisfying the update, before the special seed
   $q_0=q_1=1$ is imposed.
2. An **orbit-specific relation** is an arithmetic congruence that may
   hold only on that one integral beta orbit.

The first class is closed exactly.

> **PROVED — full recurrence-state kernel.**
> Let $A$ be $\mathbb Q(n)$, a polynomial coefficient ring in
> $n$, or a localization obtained by inverting declared
> denominator units.  For algebraically free state variables $X,Y$,
> define
> 

$$
> \Phi:A[X,Y,U]\longrightarrow A[Y],
> \qquad
> \Phi(X)=0,\quad\Phi(Y)=Y,\quad\Phi(U)=-Y^2.           \tag{1.4}
>
$$


> Then
> 

$$
> \boxed{\ker\Phi=(X,U+Y^2).}                          \tag{1.5}
>
$$



Consequently:

* no positive-degree monic
  

$$
M_n(U)\in A[U]                                      \tag{1.6}
$$


  is a recurrence-universal annihilator of $-Y^2$ modulo $X$;
* every state-dependent universal annihilator has the exact form
  

$$
\boxed{
  M(U)=(U+Y^2)G(X,Y,U)+XH(X,Y,U);}                     \tag{1.7}
$$


* a recurrence-universal lift $V$ satisfies
  

$$
V\equiv-Y^2\pmod X
$$


  only if
  

$$
\boxed{V=-Y^2+XH(X,Y),}                              \tag{1.8}
$$


  which explicitly retains $Y=q_{n+1}$ or $X=q_n$.

Thus polynomial or rational coefficients in $n$, of fixed degree and
even of arbitrary height, cannot produce a formal annihilator from the
recurrence alone.  Allowing a fixed recurrence window adds no new
universal state variable: every $q_{n+j}$ is an $A$-linear form in
$(X,Y)$.

For a proper integer target $Q\ge2$, the parallel exact statement is



$$
\boxed{
\ker\!\left(
\mathbb Z[Y,U]\longrightarrow(\mathbb Z/Q\mathbb Z)[Y],
\ U\longmapsto-Y^2
\right)
=(Q,U+Y^2).}                                           \tag{1.9}
$$



Therefore every identity valid formally for arbitrary $Y\bmod Q$ is
a combination of the target $Q$ and the canonical boundary factor.
Inserting $Q$, $q_n$, or $q_{n+1}$ into the coefficients merely
repackages the target or boundary state.

The theorem does **not** exclude a new congruence special to the orbit
$q_0=q_1=1$.  That is exactly the remaining arithmetic problem.  In
degree one it is identical to the small-lift problem from Item 285.
No such orbit-specific $O(n)$-height object is proved here, and
booking remains zero.

## 2. Why the generic beta state has two free coordinates

At time $n$, write



$$
X=q_n,\qquad Y=q_{n+1}.                                \tag{2.1}
$$



The recurrence gives



$$
q_{n+2}=(4n+6)Y+X,\qquad
q_{n-1}=Y-(4n+2)X.                                    \tag{2.2}
$$



Iterating forward or backward expresses every fixed shift as



$$
\boxed{
q_{n+j}=A_j(n)X+B_j(n)Y}                               \tag{2.3}
$$



with $A_j,B_j\in\mathbb Z[n]$ after the relevant finite range is
fixed.  Each one-step transfer matrix has determinant $-1$.  Hence
the update is invertible and imposes no polynomial relation between
the two formal state coordinates $X,Y$.

This is the correct meaning of “generated by the recurrence” in the
universal class.  One may use polynomial expressions in a fixed
window, but after (2.3) they are simply elements of $A[X,Y]$.

Rational expressions are admitted only after a common denominator is
cleared and certified to be a unit modulo the declared target.  If the
denominator is not a target unit, the modular monic polynomial is not
defined at those primes; hiding such primes in a denominator is not an
annihilator theorem.

The actual initial condition selects one numerical orbit inside this
formal two-state space.  It may have arithmetic congruences not visible
as identities in $A[X,Y]$.  The kernel theorem deliberately does not
rule those out.

## 3. Proof of the full-state kernel

Consider the quotient



$$
A[X,Y,U]/(X,U+Y^2).                                    \tag{3.1}
$$



Setting $X=0$ and $U=-Y^2$ identifies this quotient canonically with
$A[Y]$.  The quotient map is exactly $\Phi$, so



$$
\ker\Phi=(X,U+Y^2),                                    \tag{3.2}
$$



proving (1.5).

Equivalently, monic division by $U+Y^2$ gives, for every
$M\in A[X,Y,U]$,



$$
M(U)=(U+Y^2)G(X,Y,U)+R(X,Y),                           \tag{3.3}
$$



where



$$
R(X,Y)=M(-Y^2).                                        \tag{3.4}
$$



The universal congruence $M(-Y^2)\equiv0\pmod X$ is precisely



$$
R(X,Y)\in(X).
$$



Writing $R=XH$ yields (1.7).

Now suppose



$$
M(U)=U^d+a_{d-1}(n)U^{d-1}+\cdots+a_0(n)\in A[U],
\qquad d\ge1.                                         \tag{3.5}
$$



Then



$$
\Phi(M)
=(-Y^2)^d+a_{d-1}(n)(-Y^2)^{d-1}+\cdots+a_0(n).        \tag{3.6}
$$



Its leading $Y$-term is



$$
(-1)^dY^{2d},                                         \tag{3.7}
$$



which cannot be cancelled by the lower powers.  Thus



$$
\Phi(M)\ne0.                                          \tag{3.8}
$$



No positive-degree monic polynomial with coefficients only in $A$
belongs to the kernel.  This proves the polynomial/rational
coefficient no-go for recurrence-universal identities.

For rational functions of $n$, let $E(n)$ clear all denominators.
At a target for which



$$
\gcd(E(n),Q)=1,                                       \tag{3.9}
$$



the original monic congruence is equivalent to the cleared one.
The leading term in (3.6) becomes



$$
E(n)(-1)^dY^{2d},                                     \tag{3.10}
$$



still nonzero.  If (3.9) fails, those target factors must be removed or
accounted for explicitly; they cannot be called annihilated by a monic
relation with unit leading coefficient.

## 4. Recurrence-generated coefficients and tautological forms

If coefficients may depend polynomially on a fixed recurrence window,
then by (2.3) they lie in $A[X,Y]$.  The exact classification is still
(1.7):



$$
M=(U+Y^2)G+XH.                                        \tag{4.1}
$$



After specialization to the beta orbit,



$$
X=q_n,\qquad Y=q_{n+1},                                \tag{4.2}
$$



the first generator is the canonical relation



$$
U+q_{n+1}^2,                                          \tag{4.3}
$$



and the second is the full modulus $q_n$.

Their heights are on the beta scale:



$$
\log q_n=n\log n+O(n),\qquad
\log q_{n+1}^2=2n\log n+O(n).                         \tag{4.4}
$$



Therefore the representation (4.1) is not an Item-285 $O(n)$-height
certificate.  It merely explains algebraically why the obvious
relations work.

No claim is made that every polynomial combination of window values
must retain the full height (4.4): exact cancellations can occur.  The
correct admission rule is to simplify the final coefficients and audit
their height.  If a non-tautological simplification produced
$O(n)$-height coefficients while preserving the congruence on the
special beta orbit, that would be precisely new orbit-specific
arithmetic.  It is not supplied by the recurrence-universal kernel.

State-dependent rational expressions obey the same rule.  After a
common denominator that is a target unit is cleared, their numerator
must lie in (4.1).  A nonunit denominator records an excluded target
factor, not a low-height relation.

## 5. Proper targets have a separate formal kernel

Fix an integer $Q\ge2$.  The quotient



$$
\mathbb Z[Y,U]/(Q,U+Y^2)                               \tag{5.1}
$$



is canonically



$$
(\mathbb Z/Q\mathbb Z)[Y].                             \tag{5.2}
$$



This proves (1.9).  Thus a polynomial identity in $Y$, valid for
every boundary state modulo $Q$, has the form



$$
\boxed{
M(U)=(U+Y^2)G(Y,U)+QH(Y,U).}                           \tag{5.3}
$$



For the full target $Q=q_n$, equation (5.3) is the numerical
specialization of the generic-state ideal (1.7).  For a proper target



$$
Q\mid {q_n\over\gcd(q_n,D_m)},                         \tag{5.4}
$$



the recurrence itself does not determine $Q$ as a formal state
variable.  Explicitly inserting $QH$ manufactures a congruence but
does not bound the target.

If



$$
\log Q=O(n),
$$



then $Q$ already has zero beta-saddle rate.  If instead
$\log Q\asymp n\log n$, carrying an explicit coefficient of size
$Q$ violates the desired $O(n)$ height budget.  A cancellation that
reduces the final coefficient to $O(n)$ height would need its own
uniform theorem; it is exactly the small-lift or orbit-specific
annihilator problem, not a consequence of (5.3).

The proper target may genuinely admit a relation that the full $q_n$
does not.  Nothing here transfers the full-target no-go for universal
identities into an impossibility theorem for such special proper
targets.

## 6. Degree one is exactly the lift problem

Let



$$
M(U)=U+a,\qquad a\in\mathbb Z.                         \tag{6.1}
$$



Then



$$
M(-Y^2)\equiv0\pmod Q
$$



if and only if



$$
a\equiv Y^2\pmod Q.                                   \tag{6.2}
$$



Equivalently, for some $k\in\mathbb Z$,



$$
\boxed{a=Y^2-kQ.}                                     \tag{6.3}
$$



With



$$
v=-a,                                                  \tag{6.4}
$$



equation (6.2) is



$$
\boxed{v\equiv-Y^2\pmod Q.}                            \tag{6.5}
$$



Thus a monic linear annihilator and an integer unit lift are the same
object, with exactly the same coefficient/evaluation height.

Choosing



$$
k=\left\lfloor {Y^2\over Q}\right\rceil               \tag{6.6}
$$



produces the symmetric residue, but the formula explicitly uses both
the $n\log n$-height state $Y$ and the target $Q$.  Without a
uniform proof that the resulting $a$ has logarithmic height $O(n)$,
this is computation of a residue, not a capacity theorem.

The deterministic checker records exact full-$q_n$ and proper-target
instances of (6.3)–(6.5).  They are algebraic replay rows only; their
observed sizes are not extrapolated.

## 7. Higher fixed degree and the Item-285 resultant

Let the actual unequal-degree residual polynomial be $F_n(U)$, with
original coefficient/product baseline $B_n$, as in Item 285.  If an
orbit-specific monic polynomial $M_n(U)$ satisfied



$$
M_n(-q_{n+1}^2)\equiv0\pmod Q,                         \tag{7.1}
$$



then Item 285 gives



$$
{\gcd(Q,\mathcal S_n)\over\gcd(Q,B_n)}
\mid
\operatorname{Res}_U(M_n,F_n/B_n).                    \tag{7.2}
$$



A nonzero resultant of logarithmic height $O(n)$ would therefore
bound the whole additive quotient by $O(n)$.  This remains a valid
open route.

The recurrence-universal choice



$$
M_0(U)=U+q_{n+1}^2                                    \tag{7.3}
$$



has



$$
\operatorname{Res}(M_0,F_n/B_n)
=(F_n/B_n)(-q_{n+1}^2),                                \tag{7.4}
$$



which is exactly Item 285's canonical evaluated residual.  For a
genuinely nonhomogeneous $F_n$, its conservative evaluation budget is
$\Omega(n\log n)$.  Hence (7.3) is not progress.

More generally, a formal relation (5.3) retains $U+Y^2$ or $Q$.
Calling its resultant a new invariant without simplifying and auditing
those coefficients would only rename the same target data.

The first genuinely admissible higher-degree object is therefore:

* fixed degree, or more generally Item-285 resultant budget $O(n)$;
* monic with all cleared denominators certified target units;
* coefficients specified without target factorization;
* valid on the actual beta seed;
* not a formal member of (1.7) or (5.3) merely by explicit insertion of
  $q_n,Q,q_{n+1}$; and
* nonzero resultant with $F_n/B_n$.

No such object is constructed here.

## 8. Product baseline and capacity admission

For every declared de-overlapped target (1.3), the relevant scalar
capture splits as



$$
\gcd(Q,\mathcal S_n)
=G_0X,\qquad
G_0=\gcd(Q,B_n).                                      \tag{8.1}
$$



The factor $G_0$ remains the Item-282 common product-return baseline.
An annihilator/resultant can control only



$$
X={\gcd(Q,\mathcal S_n)\over\gcd(Q,B_n)}.              \tag{8.2}
$$



Item 287 supplies no actual orbit-specific $M_n$, hence no new bound
even for $X$.  It also supplies no weighted-return theorem for
$G_0$.

The recurrence-universal no-go is structurally useful: it proves that
a successful low-height annihilator must contain new special-seed
arithmetic.  It is not itself a retained-capacity ceiling for the
actual family.

## 9. Strict labels

### PROVED

* The fixed-state recurrence-window representation (2.3).
* The full recurrence-state kernel
  $\ker\Phi=(X,U+Y^2)$.
* The exact classification (1.7) of all polynomial
  recurrence-universal annihilators.
* The no-go for positive-degree monic $M\in A[U]$, including
  denominator-cleared rational functions of $n$ at target-unit
  denominators.
* The recurrence-universal lift classification (1.8).
* The proper-target formal kernel $(Q,U+Y^2)$.
* The exact equivalence between degree-one annihilators and integer
  lifts.
* The Item-265 de-overlap and Item-282 product-baseline separation.

### PROVED SCOPED NO-GO

* Polynomial/rational coefficients in $n$ cannot give a
  recurrence-universal monic annihilator, at any fixed positive degree
  or coefficient height.
* Polynomial coefficients generated from a fixed recurrence window
  yield only the tautological ideal (1.7) unless they use arithmetic
  special to the selected orbit.
* A formal proper-$Q$ identity for arbitrary boundary state lies in
  $(Q,U+Y^2)$ and therefore repackages the target or canonical
  boundary relation.
* These are not impossibility theorems for orbit-specific congruences
  at the seed $q_0=q_1=1$.

### EXACT FINITE ONLY
