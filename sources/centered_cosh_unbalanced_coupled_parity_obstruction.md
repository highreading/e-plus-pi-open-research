# A coupled two-parity jet criterion and a terminal-band slope theorem

Checked: 2026-08-27 UTC.

## 1. Verdict

Let



$$
F(x)=\frac1{2\cosh\sqrt x}
\tag{1}
$$



and consider the two actual parity blocks in the centered-cosh lower
lift.  For $\epsilon\in\{0,1\}$, put



$$
d_\epsilon=\left\lfloor\frac{n-1-\epsilon}{2}\right\rfloor,
 \qquad
 B_\epsilon=\left\lfloor\frac{n+t-\epsilon}{2}\right\rfloor,
\tag{2}
$$





$$
V_\epsilon=
 \left\{A\in{\cal P}_{d_\epsilon}:
 [x^j](FA)=0\quad(d_\epsilon+1\leq j\leq B_\epsilon)\right\}.
\tag{3}
$$



The even endpoint-product image is



$$
{\cal E}_{n,t}=V_0^2+xV_1^2.      \tag{4}
$$



Cancellation between the two summands in (4) is a genuine issue.  The
separate one-block determinants do not settle it.  This note gives an
exact coupled criterion which neither assumes nor suppresses that
cancellation.

Let $D=n-1$.  Choose any spanning product columns for $V_0^2$, shift
the spanning product columns for $V_1^2$ by $x$, and form their full
coefficient matrix $C_{n,t}$ in degrees $0,\ldots,D$.  Let
$J_{n,t}$ be the submatrix in degrees $2,\ldots,D$.  Then



$$
\boxed{
 \dim\left({\cal E}_{n,t}\cap{\cal P}_1\right)
   =\operatorname {rank}C_{n,t}
      -\operatorname {rank}J_{n,t}.}                       \tag{5}
$$



Equivalently, after reversal about degree $D$, $J_{n,t}$ is the
coupled jet matrix in orders $0,\ldots,D-2$.  Formula (5) is an exact
intersection theorem and remains valid when the two product spaces have
a large intersection.

There is an all-$n$ infinite range in which the coupled obstruction is
now proved:



$$
\boxed{
 n\geq5,\quad n-3\leq t\leq n-1
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 {\cal E}_{n,t}\cap{\cal P}_1=\{0\}.}                      \tag{6}
$$



Thus the two parity blocks cannot produce a nonzero quadratic endpoint
in this terminal band.  The proof is not a finite rank extrapolation.
It uses exact secant Toeplitz rank, Padé normality, and, in the only
nontrivial edge, the automatic $r=\sigma=1$ jet determinant.

The general proportional-width range $t/n>1/3$ remains open.  In the
important equal-block parity, the coupled matrix reduces to one explicit
type-I Hermite--Padé jet.  If $\sigma=1$, it is generated by



$$
\{y^j,\ y^jK,\ y^jK^2:0\leq j\leq2r-1\},
 \qquad K=y\frac{\widehat Q}{\widehat G},                  \tag{7}
$$



and has $6r$ columns.  If $\sigma=0$, it is generated by



$$
\begin{split}
 &1,y,\ldots,y^{2r+1},\\
 &H,yH,\ldots,y^{2r}H,\\
 &H^2,yH^2,\ldots,y^{2r-1}H^2,
 \qquad H=y^2\frac{\widehat G}{\widehat Q},
 \end{split}                                               \tag{8}
$$



and has $6r+3$ columns.  These are coupled analogues of the separate
jets, with one additional multiplier degree contributed by
$U+xU$.  Their nonvanishing is not proved here.

An exact finite replay finds



$$
\operatorname {rank}C_{n,t}=\operatorname {rank}J_{n,t}
\tag{9}
$$



on the displayed bounded grid with $3t>n$, so it finds no exceptional
rational survivor of $x$-degree at most one.  Equation (9) outside the
terminal band is explicitly finite evidence only.

The Schur clearing from the one-block theorem gives coupled matrix entry
height $\exp(O(n^2))$; a generic coupled maximal minor still has
logarithmic height $O(n^3)$.  Coupling therefore supplies no hidden
low-height theorem by itself.  No conclusion about the arithmetic nature
of $e+\pi$ follows.

## 2. Exact product columns

Write



$$
B_\epsilon=2M_\epsilon+\sigma_\epsilon,\qquad
 r_\epsilon=d_\epsilon-M_\epsilon,
 \qquad \sigma_\epsilon\in\{0,1\}.                         \tag{10}
$$



In the positive-codimension regime, let $Q_\epsilon,G_\epsilon$
be the two neighboring normal Padé denominators



$$
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
 &\text{numerator degree}&\text{denominator degree}\\ \hline
 (P_\epsilon,Q_\epsilon)&M_\epsilon+\sigma_\epsilon&M_\epsilon\\
 (R_\epsilon,G_\epsilon)&M_\epsilon+1-\sigma_\epsilon&
                       M_\epsilon+2\sigma_\epsilon-1.
\end{array}                                                \tag{11}
$$



The one-block decomposition is



$$
V_\epsilon
 =Q_\epsilon{\cal P}_{r_\epsilon-\sigma_\epsilon}
  \mathbin\oplus
  G_\epsilon{\cal P}_{r_\epsilon-1}.                       \tag{12}
$$



When $r_\epsilon\geq1$ and
$M_\epsilon\geq2r_\epsilon-\sigma_\epsilon$, its product
space has the direct basis



$$
\begin{split}
 {\cal B}_\epsilon={}&
 \{x^jQ_\epsilon^2:0\leq j\leq2r_\epsilon-2\sigma_\epsilon\}\\
 &{}\cup
 \{x^jQ_\epsilon G_\epsilon:
              0\leq j\leq2r_\epsilon-\sigma_\epsilon-1\}\\
 &{}\cup
 \{x^jG_\epsilon^2:0\leq j\leq2r_\epsilon-2\}.
\end{split}                                                \tag{13}
$$



As usual, a set with negative upper endpoint is empty.  At the boundary
$r_\epsilon=0,\sigma_\epsilon=0$, the block is
$\mathbb Q Q_\epsilon$ and
${\cal B}_\epsilon=\{Q_\epsilon^2\}$.  At
$r_\epsilon=0,\sigma_\epsilon=1$, the block is zero.  If the number
of tail equations exceeds the number of coefficients, the strict
secant Toeplitz minors make the block zero as well.

Use the columns



$$
{\cal B}_0\ \cup\ x{\cal B}_1                             \tag{14}
$$



to define $C_{n,t}$.  They need not be independent as a combined
family.  This is why simply adding the two separate codimensions gives
no theorem.

## 3. The exact coupled intersection theorem

Let



$$
\pi_{\geq2}:{\cal P}_D\longrightarrow
 \operatorname {span}\{x^2,\ldots,x^D\}
\tag{15}
$$



discard the constant and linear coefficients.  Restricted to
${\cal E}_{n,t}$, its kernel is exactly
${\cal E}_{n,t}\cap{\cal P}_1$.  The dimension of
${\cal E}_{n,t}$ is $\operatorname {rank}C_{n,t}$, and the
dimension of its image under $\pi_{\geq2}$ is
$\operatorname {rank}J_{n,t}$.  Rank-nullity proves (5).

There is also a useful kernel quotient:



$$
\boxed{
 \ker J_{n,t}/\ker C_{n,t}
 \ \simeq\ {\cal E}_{n,t}\cap{\cal P}_1.}                 \tag{16}
$$



Thus a vector in the high-row nullspace is a genuine low survivor only
when it is not already a relation between the two product spaces.
This precisely separates high-tail cancellation from the usually large
intersection $V_0^2\cap xV_1^2$.

Define reversal about the common ambient degree $D$ by



$$
{\cal R}_D A(y)=y^DA(1/y).           \tag{17}
$$



The coefficient of $x^j$ becomes the coefficient of $y^{D-j}$.
Consequently $J_{n,t}$, with its row order reversed, is the coefficient
matrix through order $D-2$ of the reversed columns



$$
\{{\cal R}_D A:A\in{\cal B}_0\}
 \ \cup\
 \{{\cal R}_D(xA):A\in{\cal B}_1\}.                       \tag{18}
$$



Equations (16)--(18) are the requested exact coupled reversed-jet
criterion.

If one first chooses any independent column basis of
${\cal E}_{n,t}$, call the resulting high matrix $J^*_{n,t}$.
Then the square Gram determinant



$$
\Delta_{n,t}^{\rm coup}
   =\det\left((J^*_{n,t})^{T}J^*_{n,t}\right)              \tag{19}
$$



is nonzero exactly when no nonzero member of
${\cal E}_{n,t}$ has degree at most one.  Cauchy--Binet expresses
(19) as a sum of squares of all maximal coupled jet minors.  Formula
(19) is basis-dependent up to a nonzero rational square, while its
vanishing is intrinsic.

## 4. Equal parity blocks

Suppose $n$ is even and $t$ is odd.  Then



$$
d_0=d_1=:d,\qquad B_0=B_1=:B,
\qquad V_0=V_1=:V.                                       \tag{20}
$$



Write $B=2M+\sigma$ and $d=M+r$.  For $r\geq1$, the direct
product formula and
${\cal P}_a+x{\cal P}_a={\cal P}_{a+1}$ give



$$
\boxed{
\begin{split}
 V^2+xV^2={}&Q^2{\cal P}_{2r-2\sigma+1}\\
 &{}\mathbin\oplus QG{\cal P}_{2r-\sigma}\\
 &{}\mathbin\oplus G^2{\cal P}_{2r-1}.
\end{split}}                                               \tag{21}
$$



In the range $3t>n$, the degree inequalities needed for directness
hold automatically.  More precisely, when $\sigma=1$,



$$
t=2M-2r+1,\qquad 3t>n\Longrightarrow M\geq2r,             \tag{22}
$$



and when $\sigma=0$,



$$
t=2M-2r-1,\qquad 3t>n\Longrightarrow M\geq2r+2.           \tag{23}
$$



For $\sigma=1$, reverse (21) about
$D=2d+1$, factor the unit $\widehat G^2$, and put



$$
K=y\frac{\widehat Q}{\widehat G}. \tag{24}
$$



The reversed columns are exactly those in (7).  There are $6r$ of
them, and (22) ensures that their first $6r$ coefficient rows occur
among orders $0,\ldots,D-2$.

For $\sigma=0$, factor $\widehat Q^2$ and put



$$
H=y^2\frac{\widehat G}{\widehat Q}.\tag{25}
$$



The reversed columns are exactly (8).  There are $6r+3$ of them, and
(23) places their first $6r+3$ rows inside the coupled high jet.

Therefore the leading square determinants of (7) and (8) are explicit
sufficient nonvanishing criteria for the coupled theorem.  The full
Gram determinant (19) is the exact criterion if a leading determinant
vanishes.  Exact secant grids make both leading determinants nonzero in
the tested range, but no all-$M,r$ signed-Schur identity is asserted.

At $r=0,\sigma=0$, equation (21) is replaced by the elementary
identity



$$
V^2+xV^2=Q^2{\cal P}_1.           \tag{26}
$$



This edge is one ingredient in the terminal-band theorem.

## 5. Proof of the terminal-band theorem

We prove (6) by parity.  The tail coefficient matrices used below have
full possible rank.  Indeed, after alternating row and column signs,
every required square terminal minor is a Toeplitz minor of
$\sec\sqrt y$, hence a strictly positive rectangular Schur value.
This also covers the overdetermined zero blocks.

### 5.1 Even $n$

Let $n=2k$, so $d_0=d_1=k-1$.  For $t=n-1$, both blocks are
zero.  For $t=n-2$, one has
$V_0=0$ and $V_1=\mathbb Q Q$, with $\deg Q=k-1$.
Hence



$$
{\cal E}_{n,n-2}=\mathbb Q xQ^2,  \tag{27}
$$



which contains no nonzero polynomial of degree at most one.

For $t=n-3$, the two blocks coincide and



$$
B_0=B_1=2k-2,\qquad M=k-1,\quad\sigma=r=0.
\tag{28}
$$



They are both $\mathbb Q Q$, where normality gives
$\deg Q=k-1\geq2$.  Equation (26) yields



$$
{\cal E}_{n,n-3}=Q^2{\cal P}_1.   \tag{29}
$$



No nonzero element of (29) can have degree at most one.

### 5.2 Odd $n$

Let $n=2k+1$.  For each of
$t=n-1,n-2,n-3$, the odd factor block $V_1$ is zero: a square
submatrix of its tail equations is one of the strictly positive
Toeplitz minors just described.

For $t=n-1$ and $t=n-2$, the remaining block $V_0$ is
one-dimensional, spanned by a normal Padé denominator $Q$ of degree
$k$.  Its square cannot have degree at most one.

For $t=n-3$, one has



$$
d_0=k,\qquad B_0=2k-1,\qquad
 M=k-1,\quad r=\sigma=1.                                  \tag{30}
$$



Thus



$$
V_0=\mathbb QQ\oplus\mathbb QG.   \tag{31}
$$



Reverse $V_0^2$ about degree $2d_0$, factor
$\widehat G^2$, and use $K=y\widehat Q/\widehat G$.
The first three jet columns are $1,K,K^2$.  Since



$$
K=k_1y+O(y^2),\qquad
 k_1=\frac{\operatorname {lc}Q}{\operatorname {lc}G}\ne0, \tag{32}
$$



their coefficient determinant through order two is
$k_1^3\ne0$.  Thus every nonzero reversed combination has a nonzero
coefficient in one of orders $0,1,2$.  Reversing back about degree
$2d_0$, every nonzero member of $V_0^2$ therefore has degree at
least $2d_0-2=2M$, namely



$$
2M=n-3\geq2.                     \tag{33}
$$



This proves (6) in the last remaining case.

## 6. Finite coupled diagnostics

The companion replay constructs the Padé entries from exact rational
secant coefficients, forms the columns (13)--(14), and compares the two
ranks in (5).  Its bounded grid contains every integer pair in its
displayed $n$-range satisfying



$$
3t>n,\qquad 0\leq t\leq n-1.      \tag{34}
$$



It verifies (9) throughout that grid.  It separately verifies every
terminal-band parameter identity in (27)--(33), including the zero
blocks and the nonzero $k_1^3$ determinant.

The finite rank equality is evidence for a stronger all-slope theorem,
not its proof.  In particular:

* no coupled survivor of $x$-degree at most one occurs on the grid;
* therefore no exceptional small coefficient height exists there;
* no lower bound for a hypothetical survivor outside the grid follows;
* nonvanishing of the equal-block leading jets (7)--(8) remains
  finite-only.

## 7. Height ledger and scope

For each block, the uniform Schur factor
$\mathfrak D_{M_\epsilon,\sigma_\epsilon}$ clears its Padé
cofactors with logarithmic coefficient height $O(M_\epsilon^2)$.
Multiplying a whole product block by its squared clearing therefore
makes (13) integral with logarithmic entry height $O(n^2)$.

If $s=O(n)$ independent coupled columns are selected, Hadamard's
inequality gives the generic bound



$$
\log|\text{coupled maximal minor}|=O(n^3).\tag{35}
$$



This is only an upper bound for an explicit determinant or cofactor.
It is not a lower bound for primitive content, and it does not improve
the $n^2$-versus-$n\log n$ analytic ledger in the underlying
unbalanced construction.  The terminal theorem (6) is a nonexistence
statement, so there is no survivor whose height must be estimated in
that band.
