# Independent audit of the Machin endpoint-asymptotics branch

Date: 2026-08-26

## Verdict

**ACCEPT, subject to the sharply limited scope stated in the source note.**
The signed-tail theorem, the arbitrary-degree endpoint identities and upper
bounds, the diagonal cofactor height bound, the closed solution on the ray
$(a,b,c)=(N-1,1,1)$, and its $239$-adic divergent subsequence all rederive
correctly.  In particular, Section 6 proves that the full odd-$N$ sequence
does not tend to zero because a specified subsequence tends to infinity; it
does **not** prove divergence, or even a lower bound, at every odd $N$.

Three substantive precision defects found during this audit were corrected
in the audited source before the final hash was taken:

1. The coefficient-tail formulas originally assumed only $M>a$, although
   $E_{M-j}$ and $U_{M-j}$ require $M>b,c$.  The source now imposes
   $M>\max\{a,b,c\}$ for (17)--(20) and Theorem 4.1, while correctly retaining
   only $M>a$ for the Peano and contour formulas.
2. Endpoint-gcd division originally omitted the possibility of the zero
   endpoint pair, and (54) used $v_p(\Delta_A)$ without treating
   $\Delta_A=0$.  The source now excludes $(A(1),B(1))=(0,0)$ where division
   is made and explicitly adopts $v_p(0)=+\infty$, with the correct
   interpretation of (54) in that case.
3. The summary scale in the former (83) omitted the factor $16(n+1)$, and a
   cross-reference pointed to the definition (74) rather than the height
   condition (84).  The final source gives the exact diagonal threshold and
   the correct reference.

No numerical pattern is needed for, or was accepted as evidence for, an
all-index theorem.

## 2. Signed Machin tails

Let $o=o(L)=2h+1$ be the least odd integer at least $L$.  Expanding the
geometric denominator and integrating term by term gives

$$
(-1)^h x^{-o}\int_0^1\frac{t^{o-1}}{1+t^2/x^2}\,dt
=\sum_{m\geq0}\frac{(-1)^{h+m}}{(o+2m)x^{o+2m}}
=T_L(x).
$$

Since

$$
\frac{x^2}{x^2+1}\frac1o
\leq I_o(x)\leq\frac1o,
$$

the signed bracket in

$$
U_L=(-1)^h\left(16\,5^{-o}I_o(5)
                 -4\,239^{-o}I_o(239)\right)
$$

is positive.  Its first term is at least
$16(25/26)5^{-o}/o=(200/13)5^{-o}/o$, while its second term is at most
$4\,239^{-o}/o$.  This proves both the sign and (11); discarding the
positive subtracted term proves the upper bound $16/(o5^o)$.

For $f_x(t)=(1+t^2/x^2)^{-1}$, integration by parts yields

$$
I_o(x)=\frac{f_x(1)}o-\frac1o\int_0^1t^of_x'(t)\,dt
=\frac{x^2}{x^2+1}\frac1o+O_x(o^{-2}).
$$

Thus the leading constant is exactly $16(25/26)=200/13$, the relative
error of the $x=5$ term is $O(1/o)$, and the $239$ contribution is relatively
$O((5/239)^o)$.  The use of $o(L)$ is essential when $L$ is even; all indices
in the source are consistent with this convention.

## 3. Arbitrary degrees, endpoint normalization, and height

Under the corrected hypothesis $M>\max\{a,b,c\}$, evaluation at $1$ after
the first $M$ Taylor coefficients have vanished gives, coefficient by
coefficient,

$$
R(1)=\sum_{j=0}^b b_j\sum_{r=M-j}^{\infty}\frac1{r!}
    +\sum_{j=0}^c c_j\sum_{r\geq M-j}g_r,
$$

which is exactly (17).  Taylor's integral remainder gives (19), and the
signed-tail integral just audited gives (20).  For $M=3n+1$, pairing the
terms with indices $j-1$ and $j$ gives the coefficient
$c_{j-1}+c_j$ in (22); the unpaired top coefficient is precisely
$(-1)^nc_nx^{-(2n+1)}t^{2n}$.  Hence the factor $t^{2n}$ in (23) and the
claimed adjacent-coefficient cancellation mechanism are correct.

The Peano identity does not need $M>b,c$: if
$F=Be^z+CG$, then $R=O(z^M)$ and $\deg A<M$ imply
$A=-\sum_{k<M}F^{(k)}(0)z^k/k!$, so Taylor's integral remainder for $F(1)$
is exactly (24).  The same observation and the residues at $0$ and $1$ give
(25).  On $|z|=\rho$, the arctangent coefficient majorant is

$$
16\operatorname{arctanh}(\rho/5)
+4\operatorname{arctanh}(\rho/239),
$$

and contour length $2\pi\rho$ supplies the factor
$\rho^{1-M}/(\rho-1)$ in (27).  The balanced-degree exponents in (40) are
$1-a-c$ and $1-a-b$, as stated.

After division by the nonzero endpoint gcd, monotonicity of $E_K$ and of
$16/(o(K)5^{o(K)})$, together with

$$
E_K\leq\frac1{K!}\sum_{m\geq0}(K+1)^{-m}
=\frac{K+1}{K K!},
$$

proves (33).  For $M=a+b+c+1$, the exact minimum indices are
$M-b=a+c+1$ and $M-c=a+b+1$.  In the diagonal case, (35) becomes

$$
\frac{16(n+1)H_C^*}{(2n+1)5^{2n+1}}\longrightarrow0,
$$

so $H_C^*=o(5^{2n})$ is indeed the clean asymptotic sufficient condition.
These are one-sided upper-bound criteria, not converses; the source says so
explicitly.

For the diagonal integer matrix, scaling row $k$ by $k!q^k$ gives the
exponential entry $(k)_jq^k$ and, with $r=k-j$ odd, the exact Machin entry

$$
4(-1)^{(r-1)/2}\frac{k!}{r}q^j(4\,239^r-5^r).
$$

It is integral because $r\mid k!$.  There are $2n$ Taylor rows and one
endpoint row.  Their Euclidean row bounds give
$(2m)^{n+1/2}$, while

$$
\sum_{k=n+1}^{3n}k=n(4n+1),
$$

which verifies every exponent in (48).  Multiplication by
$L_n=n!q^n$ clears the low exponential factorials and Machin denominators.
Since $|g_r|<4$, each low $A$ coefficient is at most $5mW_n$ before this
last scaling, proving (49).  This is only a coarse universal upper bound;
the note correctly does not turn it into a lower bound or a no-go theorem.

Finally, the cofactor identity gives

$$
\frac{A(1)}{B(1)}=\frac{\Delta_A}{L_n\Delta_B}.
$$

Reduction of this rational pair proves (53)--(54), including the now-stated
$\Delta_A=0$ convention.  The rank theorem supplies only
$\Delta_B\ne0$, exactly as the source claims.

## 4. The explicit ray $(N-1,1,1)$

For odd $N$, the two high rows, after multiplication by $N!$ and
$(N+1)!$, are

$$
b_0+Nb_1+Xc_0=0,
\qquad
b_0+(N+1)b_1+(N+1)Xc_1=0,
$$

where $X=N!g_N$.  Adding $c_0+c_1=b_0+b_1$ and substituting (57)--(59)
annuls all three equations and gives both endpoint sums as
$XD$, $D=N^2+N-1$.  The indicated $3\times3$ minor, using the endpoint row
$C(1)-B(1)=0$, is $1+NX$.  Were it zero, comparison of $239$-adic
valuations in $X=-1/N$ would force $v_{239}(N!)=N$, contradicting
Legendre's formula.  Thus the solution line and the nonzero endpoint
normalization are valid for every positive odd $N$.

The endpoint tail is

$$
R(1)=XD(E_{N+2}+U_{N+2})+\frac{b_1}{(N+1)!},
$$

because $U_{N+1}=U_{N+2}$ and
$E_{N+1}=1/(N+1)!+E_{N+2}$.  Dividing by $XD$ gives (63) exactly.  The
leading term is $U_{N+2}$; the term $-g_N/D$ has the same sign and is
smaller by $O(N^{-2})$, and both factorial terms are superexponentially
smaller.  Therefore the sign, index $N+2$, and constant $200/13$ in (64)
are all correct.

Subtracting $e+\pi$ from (63) gives (67), including both occurrences of
$g_N$.  On $N=1+2\cdot239^k$ their combined coefficient is the
$239$-adic unit $-N(N+1)/D$, and

$$
v_{239}(g_N)=-N.
$$

For every earlier odd $r<N$, writing $v=v_{239}(r)$ gives $v\leq k$ and
$N-r\equiv1\pmod{239^v}$.  Since $N-r$ is positive and even,
$N-r\geq239^v+1>v$, hence $r+v_{239}(r)<N$.  The factorial denominators
also have valuation less than $N$.  The final $g_N$ contribution is
therefore uniquely of least valuation, proving

$$
v_{239}(A(1)/B(1))=-N,
\qquad v_{239}(\beta_N)=N
$$

on this subsequence.  Combining this with (64) proves the lower bound in
(72) and subsequence divergence.  This is a proof about an infinite
subsequence, not an extrapolation from the computed instance $N=479$.

## 5. General odd $N$ residue criterion

In (67), the combined $g_N$ coefficient is $-N(N+1)/D$.  Since
$v_{239}(g_N)=-N-v_{239}(N)$, its denominator exponent is

$$
W_*(N)=N-v_{239}(N+1)+v_{239}(D).
$$

Each earlier $-g_r$ has denominator exponent
$w(r)=r+v_{239}(r)$.  Multiplication by $239^{W_N}$ and reduction modulo
$239$ gives, for an earlier term,

$$
239^{r+v_{239}(r)}(-g_r)
\equiv4(-1)^{(r-1)/2}\bar r^{-1}\pmod{239},
$$

and for the final combined term,

$$
4(-1)^{(N-1)/2}
\overline{\frac{N+1}{239^{a_N}}}
\overline{\frac D{239^{d_N}}}^{-1}.
$$

These are exactly the summands in $4\mathcal C_N$; in particular, the
$239$-adic unit part of $N$ cancels and is correctly absent from (75).

The remaining rational terms cannot enter this residue.  Legendre's bound
and $a_N=v_{239}(N+1)$ give

$$
v_{239}((N+1)!)<N-a_N.
$$

For the extra term with denominator $D(N+1)!$, the same inequality remains
strict after adding $d_N$ to both sides.  Thus
$\mathcal C_N\ne0$ proves the exact valuation $-W_N$, and reduction of the
endpoint ratio proves $v_{239}(\beta_N)=W_N$.  Since
$a_N\leq\lfloor\log_{239}(N+1)\rfloor$, (76)--(77) follow.

The displayed exceptional example was also checked directly modulo $239$:
$239^2\cdot161=9\,196\,481$, $N=9\,196\,483$, the two leading residues are
$144$ and $95=-144\pmod{239}$, and their sum vanishes.  The source properly
concludes only that the first residue cancels; it does not assign the next
valuation or claim a uniform all-odd-$N$ bound.

As an additional adversarial check, I independently constructed the exact
rational number (67) and the residue (75) for every odd $1\leq N\leq501$.
Whenever $\mathcal C_N\ne0$, the rational valuation equaled $-W_N$; there
were no mismatches, including cases with $239\mid D$ and with
$239\mid N+1$.  This finite check is not used to prove Proposition 6.1.

## 6. Executable audits and finite-scope claims

All three scripts compile.  Regeneration with their archived parameters
produces outputs byte-for-byte identical to the three archived JSON files.
The JSON files parse without nonstandard values.

The regenerated records verify exactly the bounded claims made in Section 7:

* the diagonal file has 18 records, all with effective $C$-height above
  $5^{2n}$, total certified tail bound above $1$, and certified absolute
  endpoint form above $1$; the $n=18$ exponents are respectively $2067$,
  $2067$, and $2040$;
* the non-diagonal file has $455=\binom{15}{3}$ records, every nullity is
  one, $(0,1,0)$ is the sole record with $B(1)=C(1)=0$, no recorded tail
  bound or certified form is below $1$, and every interior best triple for
  total budget $3$ through $12$ is $(T-2,1,1)$;
* the fixed-ray file verifies the closed vector and endpoint ratio for odd
  $N\leq13$, the normalized $N=1$ pair $(12388,-1195)$, exactly $479$
  factors of $239$ in the reduced endpoint denominator at $N=479$, and only
  the first-residue cancellation at the explicit exceptional degree.

The phrase “endpoint-degenerate” in the finite scan means that the common
$e+\pi$ coefficient $B(1)=C(1)$ is zero.  At $(0,1,0)$ the full endpoint
pair is actually $(A(1),B(1))=(1,0)$, so this convention does not contradict
the nonzero-pair hypothesis in Section 4.  This is a harmless terminology
caveat, not a mathematical defect.

## 7. Residual scope

The accepted results do not show any of the following:

* that the diagonal effective height satisfies the sufficient upper bound;
* that the diagonal endpoint forms decay or fail to decay;
* that the fixed-$(b,c)=(1,1)$ forms diverge for every odd $N$;
* that every odd $N$ has $\mathcal C_N\ne0$ (the note gives an exact
  counterexample);
* a no-go result for other non-diagonal slopes; or
* irrationality, algebraicity, or transcendence of $e+\pi$.

The source note states these limitations accurately.
