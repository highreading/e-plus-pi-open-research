# Primitive content on the quadratic cyclotomic-unit rays

Checked: 2026-08-26 UTC

## Verdict

Continue with



$$
K=\mathbb Q(\zeta_{20})^+,
 \qquad F=\mathbb Q(\sqrt5),
 \qquad \Theta_d=-i\Lambda_{5,d},
 \qquad s=e+\pi.
 \tag{1}
$$



The rational-ray tropical theorem showed that every rational upper-wall
resonance has slope



$$
(c,c,-c),\qquad c\in\mathbb Q.
 \tag{2}
$$



This note passes from the raw trace to the primitive rational form on all
of those rays.  The conclusion is an all-degree asymptotic no-go:

> If
> 

$$
> (m_3(d),m_7(d),m_9(d))=d(c,c,-c)+O(1),
> \tag{3}
>
$$


> then every eventually nonzero primitive trace is bounded away from zero.
> If the bounded offset lies outside $F$, it either diverges in modulus or
> has a nonzero limiting lower bound.  If the offset lies in $F$, it is
> the already classified derangement/factorial form and diverges.

The proof does **not** assume an unproved upper bound for the coordinate
gcd.  It compares the value of the trace with its coefficient on $s$;
the quotient is unchanged by denominator clearing and gcd division.

For completeness, the exact gcd is also described in trace-dual and Smith
terms.  On a quadratic ray it reduces to a single gcd between a primitive
Lucas-type trace coordinate and a Padé-dependent Smith quotient.  This is
a precise arithmetic reduction, but the available ideal data alone do not
give a useful all-degree upper bound for that last gcd.  Finite computations
through $d=200$ show small gcds on several representative rays; those
records are diagnostics only.

Nothing here decides whether $e+\pi$ is algebraic or transcendental.

## 1. Trace-dual meaning of the ordinary gcd

Let the least positive integer coefficient clearing be



$$
q_d\Theta_d=u_ds+v_d,
                   \qquad u_d,v_d\in\mathcal O_K,
 \tag{4}
$$



and let $\theta\in\mathcal O_K^\times$.  Write



$$
\begin{aligned}
 A_d(\theta)&=\operatorname {Tr}_{K/\mathbb Q}(\theta u_d),\\
 B_d(\theta)&=\operatorname {Tr}_{K/\mathbb Q}(\theta v_d),\\
 g_d(\theta)&=\gcd(A_d(\theta),B_d(\theta)).
\end{aligned}
\tag{5}
$$



Assume that the pair in (5) is nonzero.  Put



$$
M_d=\mathbb Zu_d+\mathbb Zv_d,
 \qquad
 M_d^\vee=\{x\in K:\operatorname {Tr}(xM_d)\subseteq\mathbb Z\}.
\tag{6}
$$



Then the gcd has the exact trace-dual-module description



$$
\boxed{
 g_d(\theta)=\max\{m\geq1:\theta/m\in M_d^\vee\}.}
 \tag{7}
$$



Let $\mathfrak c_d=(u_d,v_d)$ be the coefficient ideal and let
$\mathfrak D_K^{-1}$ be the codifferent.  Its ideal dual is



$$
\mathfrak c_d^\vee
   =\{x:\operatorname {Tr}(x\mathfrak c_d)\subseteq\mathbb Z\}
   =\mathfrak D_K^{-1}\mathfrak c_d^{-1}
   \subseteq M_d^\vee.
\tag{8}
$$



The inclusion is usually strict: $M_d$ has only two $\mathbb Z$-generators,
whereas $\mathfrak c_d$ contains all their $\mathcal O_K$-multiples.
In fact, $M_d^\vee$ contains the rational orthogonal complement of
$M_d$ and is not a fractional ideal or a discrete full-rank lattice.
This is why the norm of the coefficient ideal does not by itself upper-bound
the gcd of one selected trace.

There is also an exact full-field Smith formula.  In an integral basis let
$C_d$ be the $4$ by $2$ coordinate matrix with columns
$u_d,v_d$, and let $G$ be the trace Gram matrix.  Thus



$$
\Phi_d=C_d^tG\in M_{2,4}(\mathbb Z)
 \tag{9}
$$



maps the coordinate column of $\theta$ to the pair in (5).  If



$$
U\Phi_dV=
 \begin{pmatrix}\alpha_d&0&0&0\\0&\beta_d&0&0\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad \alpha_d\mid\beta_d,
 \tag{10}
$$



is a Smith decomposition and $\mathbf y=V^{-1}[\theta]$, then



$$
g_d(\theta)=
              \gcd(\alpha_dy_1,\beta_dy_2).
 \tag{11}
$$



Formula (11) separates the image-lattice invariants from the selected unit
coordinate, but it does not force $y_1,y_2$ to be coprime in four
dimensions.

## 2. A sharper two-dimensional Smith formula on (2)

After passing to a subsequence to fix the bounded offset, write



$$
\theta_d=\varphi^{2n_d}\xi,
                    \qquad n_d=cd+O(1),
 \tag{12}
$$



where $\xi$ is a fixed unit in the group generated by
$u_3,u_7,u_9$.  Define



$$
x_{d,\xi}=\operatorname {Tr}_{K/F}(\xi u_d),
 \qquad
 y_{d,\xi}=\operatorname {Tr}_{K/F}(\xi v_d).
 \tag{13}
$$



In the integral basis



$$
1,\qquad \omega=\varphi-1,
 \tag{14}
$$



let $C_{d,\xi}\in M_2(\mathbb Z)$ have the coordinate columns of
$x_{d,\xi},y_{d,\xi}$.  Put



$$
\mathbf r_n=
 \left(\operatorname {Tr}_{F/\mathbb Q}(\varphi^{2n}),
       \operatorname {Tr}_{F/\mathbb Q}(\varphi^{2n}\omega)\right).
 \tag{15}
$$



Then, exactly,



$$
(A_d(\theta_d),B_d(\theta_d))
                      =\mathbf r_{n_d}C_{d,\xi}.
 \tag{16}
$$



The trace rows are primitive.  Indeed



$$
\mathbf r_0=(2,-1),\qquad
 \mathbf r_{n+1}=\mathbf r_n
      \begin{pmatrix}2&1\\1&1\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad \det\begin{pmatrix}2&1\\1&1\end{pmatrix}=1.
 \tag{17}
$$



Suppose $C_{d,\xi}$ has rank two and choose a Smith decomposition



$$
P C_{d,\xi}Q=\operatorname {diag}(\alpha_{d,\xi},
                                          \beta_{d,\xi}),
       \qquad \alpha_{d,\xi}\mid\beta_{d,\xi}.
 \tag{18}
$$



The row



$$
\mathbf z_{d,\xi,n}=\mathbf r_nP^{-1}
 \tag{19}
$$



is still primitive.  Right multiplication of the output pair by the
unimodular matrix $Q$ preserves its ordinary gcd, so (16)--(19) give



$$
\boxed{
 g_d(\varphi^{2n}\xi)
   =\alpha_{d,\xi}
      \gcd\!\left(z_1,\frac{\beta_{d,\xi}}{\alpha_{d,\xi}}\right).}
 \tag{20}
$$



The first determinantal divisor $\alpha_{d,\xi}$ is the gcd of all four
entries of $C_{d,\xi}$, and



$$
\alpha_{d,\xi}\beta_{d,\xi}=|\det C_{d,\xi}|.
 \tag{21}
$$



Thus every extra prime in the selected trace gcd must simultaneously divide
one primitive Lucas-type coordinate $z_1$ and the fixed Smith quotient
$\beta/\alpha$.  This is substantially sharper than the determinant
bound alone.  It is not, however, a proved subexponential bound as $d$
varies: both the Smith transformation $P$ and the Padé-dependent quotient
in (20) vary with $d$.  Standard gcd theorems for two fixed linear
recurrences do not directly apply.

If $\xi\in F$, the matrix in (18) has rank one: the anti-fixed
coefficient disappears and the two columns are rationally proportional.
That is exactly the derangement collapse treated in Section 5 below.

## 3. A gcd-free primitive comparison

Let



$$
T_d(\theta)=\operatorname {Tr}_{K/\mathbb Q}(\theta\Theta_d)
             =a_d(\theta)s+b_d(\theta)
 \tag{22}
$$



be the **uncleared** rational trace.  From (4)--(5),



$$
A_d=q_da_d,
                 \qquad B_d=q_db_d.
 \tag{23}
$$



The primitive form is



$$
L_d=\frac{A_d}{g_d}s+\frac{B_d}{g_d}.
 \tag{24}
$$



Whenever $A_d\ne0$, clearing and gcd division cancel from the quotient:



$$
\frac{L_d}{A_d/g_d}=\frac{T_d}{a_d}.
 \tag{25}
$$



Since $A_d/g_d$ is a nonzero integer,



$$
|L_d|\geq\left|\frac{T_d}{a_d}\right|.
 \tag{26}
$$



If $A_d=0$ but the pair is nonzero, the primitive form is a nonzero
integer constant and has modulus one.  Thus a lower bound for (25) bypasses
the coordinate-content problem completely.

## 4. Off-subfield bounded offsets

Assume $\xi\notin F$ in (12).  The rational-ray endpoint theorem gives



$$
|T_d|\asymp
 \begin{cases}
  d^{-1}\varphi^{(1-2c)d},&c<1/4,\\
  \varphi^{2cd},&c\geq1/4.
 \end{cases}
\tag{27}
$$



The coefficient on $s$ in $\Theta_d$ is



$$
U_d=2A_d(1)A_d(\eta)A_d(\bar\eta)\in F,
 \tag{28}
$$



and at both embeddings of $F$,



$$
U_d=2(-1)^de^{-2}+o(1).
 \tag{29}
$$



Consequently



$$
|a_d(\theta_d)|
                              \leq C_\xi\varphi^{2|c|d}.
 \tag{30}
$$



For $c<1/4$, (27) and (30) show, whenever $a_d\ne0$,



$$
\left|\frac{T_d}{a_d}\right|\gg_\xi
 \begin{cases}
  d^{-1}\varphi^d,&c<0,\\
  d^{-1}\varphi^{(1-4c)d},&0\leq c<1/4.
 \end{cases}
\tag{31}
$$



Both lines tend to infinity.

It remains to treat $c\geq1/4$, where the value and its $s$-coefficient
have the same exponential rate.  Let $\sigma_1,\sigma_9$ be the two
extensions of the distinguished embedding of $F$.  The $9$-summand
dominates the value, while (29) gives the full relative-trace contribution
to the coefficient.  Hence



$$
\frac{T_d}{a_d}\longrightarrow
       \frac{2\pi\,\sigma_9(\xi)}
            {\sigma_1(\xi)+\sigma_9(\xi)}.
 \tag{32}
$$



The denominator cannot vanish.  If
$\sigma_1(\xi)+\sigma_9(\xi)=0$, then
$\tau\xi=-\xi$.  The exact signed unit action shows that equality of
the exponent vectors forces $\xi=(u_3u_7/u_9)^b\in F$, on which the
sign of $\tau$ is positive.  Thus no unit monomial is anti-fixed.
The limit in (32) is finite and nonzero.

Combining (26), (31), and (32), every nonzero primitive form with
$\xi\notin F$ either diverges or has a fixed positive limiting lower
bound.  No estimate for $g_d$ was used.

## 5. Subfield offsets

If $\xi\in F$, then all of $\theta_d$ lies in $F$.  The anti-fixed
logarithmic component vanishes identically, and after rational
primitivization every nonzero trace is, up to sign,



$$
\frac{{!d}}{h_d}s-\frac{d!}{h_d},
              \qquad h_d=\gcd({!d},d!).
 \tag{33}
$$



The reduced ratio ${!d}/d!$ tends to $e^{-1}$; its reduced numerator
tends to infinity, and division of (33) by that numerator tends to
$\pi$.  Therefore the modulus of (33) tends to infinity.  This covers
all subfield offsets and completes the primitive no-go on (2).

Finally, a bounded integer offset and the residue of $d$ modulo the
denominator of $c$ take only finitely many values.  Applying the preceding
argument on each resulting subsequence makes the lower bounds uniform for
the original sequence in (3).

## 6. Finite diagnostics and remaining scope

The exact script
<scripts/cyclotomic_unit_quadratic_ray_primitive.py> checks (16)--(21) and
the literal trace pairs for four non-subfield offsets, six rational slopes,
and every admissible degree through $200$.  Its compact output is
<results/cyclotomic_unit_quadratic_ray_primitive_d200.json>.  The observed
ordinary gcds are small, but no finite pattern is used in Sections 3--5.

The result closes the primitive-content issue on every rational tropical
upper-wall ray.  It does not yet close arbitrary rational slopes away from
the upper wall: there the raw trace has a unique dominant embedding, but
its $s$-coefficient can have a larger unit-height rate, so (26) need not
be useful.  Nor does (20), by itself, give a uniform bound when its Smith
data vary with $d$.  Those off-wall primitive rays remain a separate
arithmetic problem.
