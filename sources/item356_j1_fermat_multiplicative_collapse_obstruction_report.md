# Item 356 — complete multiplicative relation lattice and the Fermat-collapse obstruction for fixed $j=1$

Date: 2026-09-01

## 1. Outcome and capacity first

Item 353 wrote the chosen-prime first quotient digit as



$$
{\mathcal T-\bar a\over p}
\equiv C+\sum_{\nu,x}\delta_p(y_{\nu,x})\pmod p,
\qquad
\delta_p(y)=yq_p(y),                                     \tag{1.1}
$$



where



$$
q_p(y)={y^{p-1}-1\over p}\pmod p                        \tag{1.2}
$$



for the canonical nonzero residue representative, $C$ is the
ordinary carry, and



$$
\bar a\equiv a_{r,n}\pmod p.                             \tag{1.3}
$$



The logical gate remains



$$
\boxed{
\text{ordinary fixed-}j=1\text{ collision}
\Longrightarrow \bar a=0.}                              \tag{1.4}
$$



No converse is used.

This item determines every multiplicative relation among the four
pointwise Kummer residue functions.  On the positive-rate bulk
$n>2$, their multiplicative rank is exactly three.  The complete
relation lattice is generated by



$$
\boxed{R_0R_{2b}=R_1^2.}                                 \tag{1.5}
$$



After inserting the four scalar weights from Item 353,



$$
\boxed{
y_0y_{2b}=\kappa\,y_1^2,\qquad
\kappa=
{(n-1)(n-2)(E-1)\over(2n-3)^2E},\quad E=p-r.}            \tag{1.6}
$$



There is no multiplicative relation involving $y_{2a}$, and every
integer relation among the four functions is a power of (1.6).

Fermat-quotient multiplicativity therefore reduces the four nonzero
pointwise quotient phases to three, but no further.  More importantly,
it does not collapse the first digit:

1. canonical reduction of (1.6) introduces a varying carry at each
   $x$;
2. the correction is $\sum yq_p(y)$, not an unweighted
   $\sum q_p(y)$;
3. after eliminating $q_p(y_{2b})$, three nonzero varying coefficient
   functions remain; and
4. the pointwise relation (1.6) is destroyed by the additive Kummer
   summation.

There is a formal one-Fermat-quotient identity.  Define the canonical
“entropy product”



$$
\mathscr P_{p,r,n}
=\prod_{\substack{\nu,x\\y_{\nu,x}\ne0}}
\langle y_{\nu,x}\rangle^{\langle y_{\nu,x}\rangle}.     \tag{1.7}
$$



Then



$$
\boxed{
\sum_{\nu,x}y_{\nu,x}q_p(y_{\nu,x})
\equiv q_p(\mathscr P_{p,r,n})\pmod p.}                  \tag{1.8}
$$



But this is not an admissible bounded-height compression:



$$
\log\mathscr P_{p,r,n}
\leq4(p-1)^2\log(p-1)=O(p^2\log p).                     \tag{1.9}
$$



Its exponents are the residue values themselves, so it is not a
bounded-degree rational-function norm or resultant.  The identity
(1.8) repackages the full moving cancellation rather than controlling
it.

The exceptional edge $n=2$ has $b_0=0$; at fixed $M$ it is one
row and has logarithmic mass $O(\log M)=o(M)$.  Outside that edge,
the exact theorem reaches every actual row and hence has enough raw
support to matter.  It nevertheless supplies no new condition forced
by (1.4), no weighted nonconcentration theorem, and no factor
localization.  Therefore



$$
\boxed{
\text{new forced condition}=0,\quad
\text{new linear log rate}=0,\quad
\text{new fixed-}j=1\text{ capacity reduction}=0.}       \tag{1.10}
$$



Booking is $0$, the $1/36$ ceiling per $6M$ is retained, and
Route 1 remains ACTIVE.

## 2. The four pointwise functions

Retain



$$
n=2h,\qquad r=2s+1,\qquad p=2n+3r,\qquad E=p-r.          \tag{2.1}
$$



For $x\in\mathbb F_p^\times$, put



$$
X=x^{-n},\qquad
A=G(x),\qquad B=(\vartheta G)(x),\qquad
D=(\vartheta^2G)(x).                                     \tag{2.2}
$$



The unweighted functions are



$$
\begin{array}{c|c}
\nu&R_\nu\\ \hline
0&XA^E\\
1&XBA^{E-1}\\
2a&XDA^{E-1}\\
2b&XB^2A^{E-2}.
\end{array}                                               \tag{2.3}
$$



Equation (1.5) is immediate from (2.3).  The issue is whether another
independent relation exists.

The relevant factorizations are



$$
\begin{aligned}
G(x)&=1-4x+6x^2-4x^3,\\
\vartheta G(x)&=-4x(1-3x+3x^2),\\
\vartheta^2G(x)&=-4x(1-3x)^2.                            \tag{2.4}
\end{aligned}
$$



Their discriminants and pairwise resultants are



$$
\operatorname{disc}(G)=-16,\qquad
\operatorname{disc}(1-3x+3x^2)=-3,                      \tag{2.5}
$$



and



$$
\operatorname{Res}(G,1-3x+3x^2)=1,\quad
\operatorname{Res}(G,1-3x)=5,\quad
\operatorname{Res}(1-3x+3x^2,1-3x)=3.                  \tag{2.6}
$$



Every actual $p\geq13$, so all divisor supports used below remain
disjoint.

## 3. Complete divisor-lattice proof

Suppose



$$
R_0^{m_0}R_1^{m_1}R_{2a}^{m_2}R_{2b}^{m_3}
\in\overline{\mathbb F}_p^\times.                        \tag{3.1}
$$



Valuations at four types of divisor give



$$
\begin{array}{c|rrrr}
&R_0&R_1&R_{2a}&R_{2b}\\ \hline
\text{root of }G&E&E-1&E-1&E-2\\
\text{root of }1-3x+3x^2&0&1&0&2\\
x=1/3&0&0&2&0\\
x=0&-n&1-n&1-n&2-n.
\end{array}                                               \tag{3.2}
$$



The third row gives $m_2=0$.  The second gives



$$
m_1=-2m_3.                                               \tag{3.3}
$$



Substitution in the first gives



$$
E(m_0-m_3)=0.                                            \tag{3.4}
$$



Because $0<E<p$, $m_0=m_3$.  The last row is then
automatic.  Thus



$$
(m_0,m_1,m_2,m_3)=t(1,-2,0,1),\qquad t\in\mathbb Z.     \tag{3.5}
$$



This proves that (1.5) generates the complete relation lattice.

The weights from Item 353 are



$$
\begin{aligned}
b_0&=-{(n-1)(n-2)\over2},&
b_1&=-{(3-2n)E\over2},\\
b_{2a}&=-{E\over2},&
b_{2b}&=-{E(E-1)\over2}.                                \tag{3.6}
\end{aligned}
$$



For $n>2$, all four are units.  Constants do not change divisor
relations, and (3.5) becomes (1.6).

For $n=2$, $b_0=0$.  This is the already zero-rate short edge, not
a positive-rate multiplicative collapse.

## 4. Canonical Fermat multiplicativity has a carry

For ordinary integers $U,V$ prime to $p$,



$$
q_p(UV)\equiv q_p(U)+q_p(V)\pmod p.                      \tag{4.1}
$$



However, (1.6) is an equality in $\mathbb F_p$, whereas Item 353 uses
canonical representatives in $\{0,\ldots,p-1\}$.  At a point where
$y_0,y_1,y_{2b}\ne0$, define



$$
\mathscr D_x=
{\langle y_0\rangle\langle y_{2b}\rangle
-\langle\kappa\rangle\langle y_1\rangle^2\over p}
\in\mathbb Z                                             \tag{4.2}
$$



and



$$
Z_x=y_0y_{2b}=\kappa y_1^2\in\mathbb F_p^\times.         \tag{4.3}
$$



If two integers $U,V$ satisfy $U=V+p\mathscr D$, then



$$
q_p(U)-q_p(V)\equiv-{\mathscr D\over V}\pmod p.           \tag{4.4}
$$



Applying (4.1)--(4.4) to (1.6) gives



$$
\boxed{
q_p(y_0)+q_p(y_{2b})-q_p(\kappa)-2q_p(y_1)
\equiv-{\mathscr D_x\over Z_x}\pmod p.}                  \tag{4.5}
$$



The right side is a canonical reduction carry and varies with $x$.
At zeros of the functions, $\delta_p(0)=0$; those finitely many
points are treated separately and create no missing quotient.

Thus even the sole multiplicative relation does not give a carry-free
relation among the canonical first-digit terms.

## 5. Why the coefficients prevent quotient collapse

Suppress the explicit carry in (4.5) momentarily.  Eliminating
$q_p(y_{2b})$ from the pointwise correction gives



$$
\begin{aligned}
\sum_\nu y_\nu q_p(y_\nu)
={}&(y_0-y_{2b})q_p(y_0)
+(y_1+2y_{2b})q_p(y_1)\\
&+y_{2a}q_p(y_{2a})
+y_{2b}q_p(\kappa)
-y_{2b}{\mathscr D_x\over Z_x}.                         \tag{5.1}
\end{aligned}
$$



All three quotient-phase coefficients in the first two lines are
nonzero rational functions on the bulk:

- $y_{2a}\not\equiv0$;
- $y_0-y_{2b}\not\equiv0$, since that would make
  $b_0G^2=b_{2b}(\vartheta G)^2$, impossible from their
  constant terms; and
- $y_1+2y_{2b}\not\equiv0$, since it contains the factor
  $\vartheta G$ times
  $b_1G+2b_{2b}\vartheta G$, whose constant term is
  $b_1\ne0$.

Hence multiplicativity reduces the formal quotient-phase rank from
four to exactly three and stops there.

This is also why an ordinary product or resultant does not appear.
Multiplicativity converts an *unweighted* sum
$\sum q_p(F(x))$ into the quotient of a product.  Item 353 instead
contains the residue-dependent coefficient $F(x)$:



$$
\delta_p(F(x))=F(x)q_p(F(x)).                            \tag{5.2}
$$



Those varying coefficients are not removed by the unique relation
(1.6).

## 6. The entropy product: exact but too large

The coefficient obstruction can be absorbed tautologically by using
the residue itself as an exponent.  For every $p$-unit $y$,



$$
q_p(y^{\langle y\rangle})
\equiv\langle y\rangle q_p(y)\pmod p.                   \tag{6.1}
$$



Multiplying over all nonzero values proves (1.8).

There are at most $4(p-1)$ factors; every base and exponent is at
most $p-1$.  Therefore



$$
\begin{aligned}
\log\mathscr P
&=\sum_{\nu,x:y_{\nu,x}\ne0}
 \langle y_{\nu,x}\rangle
 \log\langle y_{\nu,x}\rangle\\
&\leq4(p-1)^2\log(p-1).                                 \tag{6.2}
\end{aligned}
$$



This is $O(M^2\log M)$ on the tied family, not a subexponential
fixed-$M$ target of height $e^{o(M)}$.  Worse, the product changes
with the candidate prime and its exponents depend on reductions modulo
that same prime.  It is not one common fixed-$M$ integer whose prime
divisors could be localized.

Thus the “one Fermat quotient” description is exact but ledger-inert.
It does not supply a Kurepa/Wieferich-type fixed target with a useful
height bound.

## 7. Pointwise products do not survive Kummer summation

Let



$$
t_\nu=\sum_x y_{\nu,x}\in\mathbb F_p.                   \tag{7.1}
$$



Equation (1.6) is pointwise; in general



$$
t_0t_{2b}\ne\kappa t_1^2.                               \tag{7.2}
$$



The four bulk controls inherited from Item 353 give:

| $(h,s,p)$ | $\kappa$ | $(t_0,t_1,t_{2a},t_{2b})$ | $t_0t_{2b}-\kappa t_1^2$ |
|---|---:|---|---:|
| $(2,1,17)$ | $1$ | $(9,12,2,2)$ | $10$ |
| $(8,2,47)$ | $6$ | $(41,31,13,9)$ | $8$ |
| $(4,4,43)$ | $37$ | $(19,24,31,14)$ | $24$ |
| $(2,6,47)$ | $26$ | $(10,37,30,6)$ | $45$ |

Every displayed defect is nonzero.  These are exact counterexamples to
promoting (1.6) through additive summation.  They are not density
evidence and none is asserted to be a full original collision.

The fifth predeclared row, $(1,1,13)$, is the $n=2$ zero-rate
edge.  The certificate treats it separately.

## 9. Capacity admission and scoped no-go

The divisor theorem and pointwise relation cover all actual bulk rows,
so their raw support is not thin.  But a candidate mechanism must also
produce a condition forced by the ordinary collision and capable of
positive linear mass.

This mechanism fails both tests:

1. the original gate forces only $\bar a=0$;
2. neither the three surviving quotient phases nor the entropy quotient
   is forced to vanish;
3. the entropy product has $O(p^2\log p)$ logarithmic height and is
   candidate-prime dependent;
4. the only degenerate coefficient row $n=2$ has zero-rate mass; and
5. no weighted nonconcentration theorem is obtained.

The closed method class is precise:

> Starting only from Fermat-quotient multiplicativity and the forced
> pointwise product relations among the four Item 353 functions, one
> cannot collapse the first correction to a bounded-height,
> bounded-degree fixed target or a second collision-forced condition.

This does not rule out a genuinely additive identity among the Kummer
sums, a special evaluation of the entropy product, or new information
from the full original collision.
