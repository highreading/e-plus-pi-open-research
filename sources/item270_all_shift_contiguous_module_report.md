## 2. Constant-term kernel and twisted differential

$$

$$
\theta\log\Phi={P(x)\over(1-x)Q(x)},\qquad

## 3. Rank-three basis and the infinity audit

Consider a putative exact relation among the three state observations,



$$
\alpha(s)wQ+\beta(s)w+\gamma(s)w\Phi Q=\mathcal D_sR. \tag{3.1}
$$



The local pole audit is exhaustive:

1. At $x=0$, the right certificate needs pole order at most five.
2. At $x=1$, differentiation raises the pole order by one, so the
   certificate again needs order at most five.
3. At a zero of $Q$, the target is regular while
   $\theta\log\Phi$ has a simple pole.  Since $s$ is indeterminate,
   cancellation forces $R$ to be divisible by $Q$.
4. There are no other finite poles.
5. At infinity, $R$ may tend to a constant but may not have a positive
   polynomial part.

Consequently every certificate in (3.1) has exactly the form



$$
R={NQ\over x^5(1-x)^5},\qquad \deg_xN\le7. \tag{3.2}
$$



After clearing the common denominator, coefficient comparison gives a
linear system over $\mathbb Q[s]$ in
$(\alpha,\beta,\gamma,n_0,\ldots,n_7)$.  The exact $11\times11$
minor obtained by omitting the $x^{11}$ row is (1.5).  It is nonzero in
$\mathbb Q(s)$, so the three state classes are independent.

The upper bound uses the same audit one shift farther.  For three layers
the common denominator has $m=8$, and the certificate is



$$
R={NQ\over x^8(1-x)^8},\qquad \deg_xN\le13. \tag{3.3}
$$



The last two degrees are essential: $NQ/[x^8(1-x)^8]$ may tend to a
constant at infinity.  The exact reduction matrix has shape $72\times66$,
rank $64$, and nullity two.  Deliberately truncating to
$\deg_xN\le11$ gives shape $72\times60$, rank $60$, and nullity
zero.  Thus the earlier apparent rank-four obstruction is proved to be a
truncation error, not a new cohomology class.
