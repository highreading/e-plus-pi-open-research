# Successive Hardy minima and exterior no-amortization

## Exact lattice determinants, saturated contractions, resultants, and parity

Checked: 2026-08-27 UTC

## 1. Scope and verdict

Fix $d\geq1$, put $N=d+1$, and write



$$
A_p(X)=\sum_{j=0}^d p_jX^j,
 \qquad p=(p_0,\ldots,p_d)\in\mathbb Z^N.                    \tag{1}
$$



This note tests whether several linearly independent endpoint polynomials,
or several successive minima of a collapsing Hardy quotient, can evade the
one-vector Dirichlet boundary by a determinant, resultant, or exterior
power.

The answer for the canonical constructions is an exact no-amortization
reduction.

1. The endpoint lattice in evaluation/transverse coordinates has covolume
   one.  For its successive minima, Minkowski's second theorem gives the
   exact product window

   

$$
{1\over N!EH^d}\leq\prod_{j=1}^N\lambda_j(H,E)
        \leq {1\over EH^d}.                                  \tag{2}
$$



   Thus the Dirichlet scale $E\asymp H^{-d}$ only controls the product of
   the minima.  It does not force several independent short endpoints.

2. If $N$ independent integer endpoints all have transverse height at
   most $H$ and evaluation at most $E$, their exact coefficient
   determinant gives

   

$$
1\leq |\det P|\leq N!EH^d.                 \tag{3}
$$



   Full independence can therefore only meet, not beat, the exponent-$d$
   boundary.

3. For $1\leq k\leq d$, let $g$ be the content of the $k$-row
   Pluecker vector.  Contracting its primitive exterior vector with the
   evaluation covector produces explicit integer polynomials $R_J$ of
   degree at most $d$.  Exactly,

   

$$
\max_J H(R_J)=W,
       \qquad
       |R_J(\pi)|\leq {k!EH^{k-1}\over g},                    \tag{4}
$$



   where $W$ is the primitive Pluecker height.  At least one $R_J$ of
   height $W$ is nonzero.  Hence every useful exterior determinant is
   already an ordinary one-polynomial approximation to $\pi$.

4. Temporarily assume that $s=e+\pi$ is algebraic of degree $r$, and put

   

$$
\kappa_r(d)=r^2d+r-1.                \tag{5}
$$



   Combining (4) with the conditional $e$-measure proves an exact upper
   limit on any exterior-content discount.  If
   $E=H^{-d+o(1)}$, then

   

$$
\limsup {\log g\over\log H}
       \leq k-{d+1\over \kappa_r(d)+1}.                       \tag{6}
$$



   In the rational case $r=1$, this is $k-1$.  Under the usual
   noncollapse scale, the strict reverse inequality is exactly the condition
   for the exterior polynomial $R_J$ to beat the conditional measure.
   Thus a determinant discount cannot be amortized: proving enough discount
   would itself prove exceptional polynomial approximation to $\pi$.

5. A pairwise Sylvester resultant also contains only one small endpoint
   factor.  After translation to the target, the resultant lies in the ideal
   generated by the two constant terms.  Its elementary determinant bound
   costs $H^{m+n-1}$, which is no better than the separate degree
   thresholds.  A symmetric product multiplies the small values, but it also
   adds degrees and logarithmic heights.

The exact results below do not assume that several Hardy minima exist at the
Dirichlet scale.  They say what follows if they do.  They do not prove an
exceptional approximation, a Hardy collapse rate, or a classification of
$e+\pi$.

## 2. Endpoint lattice and successive minima

Put



$$
\lambda=(\pi,\pi^2,\ldots,\pi^d),\qquad
 u=A_p(\pi)=p_0+\lambda^tv,\qquad
 v=(p_1,\ldots,p_d).                                         \tag{7}
$$



The image



$$
\Lambda_\pi=\{(A_p(\pi),p_1,\ldots,p_d):p\in\mathbb Z^N\}
      \subset\mathbb R^N                                    \tag{8}
$$



is a lattice of covolume one.  Indeed the map
$p\mapsto(u,v)$ is triangular with determinant one.  Let



$$
K(H,E)=[-E,E]\times[-H,H]^d.                                \tag{9}
$$



Its volume is



$$
\operatorname {vol}K(H,E)=2^NEH^d.  \tag{10}
$$



Let
$0<\lambda_1(H,E)\leq\cdots\leq\lambda_N(H,E)$ be the
successive minima of $K(H,E)$ with respect to $\Lambda_\pi$.  Minkowski's
second theorem, with the constants retained, is



$$
{2^N\over N!}\leq
 \operatorname {vol}K(H,E)\prod_{j=1}^N\lambda_j(H,E)
 \leq2^N.                                                     \tag{11}
$$



Equations (10)--(11) give (2).  At $EH^d\asymp1$, only the product is
bounded above and below.  In particular, a very small first minimum may be
compensated by later minima.  Volume alone neither proves
$\lambda_k(H,E)\leq1$ for $k\geq2$ nor produces $k$ independent
polynomial approximations.

Primitivity does not change a successive direction.  If a nonzero integer
vector in a centrally symmetric convex body has coefficient gcd $c>1$,
division by $c$ stays in the body and preserves its rational line.  Notice,
however, that individual primitive rows do not make their exterior vector
primitive; Section 4 tracks the resulting index $g$.

## 3. Successive minima of a Hardy quotient

For an integer $H\geq1$, let



$$
\mathcal S_H=\{p\in\mathbb Z^N:\gcd(p)=1,
                         1\leq\|v\|_\infty\leq H\}.           \tag{12}
$$



For a nonnegative function $f$ on $\mathcal S_H$, define its $k$-th
rank threshold by



$$
\rho_k(f;H)=\min\{T:\dim_{\mathbb R}\operatorname {span}
       \{p\in\mathcal S_H:f(p)\leq T\}\geq k\}.             \tag{13}
$$



The minimum exists for the functions used below: every bounded sublevel has
bounded $p_0$, and $\mathcal S_H$ contains $N$ independent vectors.

Suppose the positive quotient form $Q_n(p)=p^tC_np$ has the item-72
normalization



$$
\left|\sqrt{Q_n(p)/a_n}-|A_p(\pi)|\right|
       \leq\varepsilon_n\|v\|_\infty.                        \tag{14}
$$



Set



$$
\Delta_k(H)=\rho_k(|A_p(\pi)|;H),\qquad
 \mathcal M_{n,k}(H)=\rho_k(\sqrt{Q_n(p)/a_n};H).             \tag{15}
$$



The pointwise bound (14) gives, for every $1\leq k\leq N$,



$$
\boxed{
  |\mathcal M_{n,k}(H)-\Delta_k(H)|\leq\varepsilon_nH.}       \tag{16}
$$



Indeed the $T$-sublevel of either function is contained in the
$(T+\varepsilon_nH)$-sublevel of the other, so their rank thresholds differ
by at most $\varepsilon_nH$.  This is the exact several-minimum extension
of the one-vector comparison.  As before, transferring an exponent along
$H_n\to\infty$ requires a quantitative bound on
$\varepsilon_nH_n$; qualitative quotient collapse has no exponent content.

For thresholds below one, $\Delta_k(H)\leq E$ is equivalent to the presence
of $k$ independent lattice points in $K(H,E)$: a nonzero constant integer
endpoint has value at least one, and primitive division causes no loss.

## 4. Full determinant and saturated lower exterior powers

Let $1\leq k\leq N$, and let



$$
p^{(1)},\ldots,p^{(k)}\in\mathbb Z^N                         \tag{17}
$$



be linearly independent primitive rows satisfying



$$
\max_{i,j\geq1}|p_j^{(i)}|\leq H,
 \qquad
 \max_i|A_{p^{(i)}}(\pi)|\leq E.                             \tag{18}
$$



Let $P$ be their $k$-by-$N$ coefficient matrix and put



$$
\Omega=p^{(1)}\wedge\cdots\wedge p^{(k)},\qquad
 g=\gcd\{\det P_{*,I}:|I|=k\},\qquad
 \omega=\Omega/g.                                            \tag{19}
$$



Thus $g$ is the index of the row lattice in its saturation, and
$\omega\in\bigwedge^k\mathbb Z^N$ is primitive.  Write



$$
W=\|\omega\|_\infty                 \tag{20}
$$



for its Pluecker height.  Individual row primitivity does not control $g$:
for example $e_0+e_1$ and $e_0+(M+1)e_1$ are primitive but their exterior
content is $M$.

First take $k=N$.  Replacing the first coefficient column of $P$ by the
evaluation column $u_i=A_{p^{(i)}}(\pi)$ is a determinant-one column
operation over $\mathbb R$.  Hence



$$
\det P=
 \det\begin{pmatrix}
 u_1&p_1^{(1)}&\cdots&p_d^{(1)}\\
 \vdots&\vdots&&\vdots\\
 u_N&p_1^{(N)}&\cdots&p_d^{(N)}
 \end{pmatrix}.                                               \tag{21}
$$



Expansion down the first column gives (3).  This identity is stronger than
the volume statement for a full set of independent endpoints.  At
$E=o(H^{-d})$, such a full set is impossible unconditionally.

Now take $1\leq k\leq d$.  For every
$J\subset\{1,\ldots,d\}$ with $|J|=k-1$, define



$$
R_J(X)={1\over g}\det
 \begin{pmatrix}
 A_{p^{(1)}}(X)&(p_j^{(1)})_{j\in J}\\
 \vdots&\vdots\\
 A_{p^{(k)}}(X)&(p_j^{(k)})_{j\in J}
 \end{pmatrix}.                                               \tag{22}
$$



Expanding the first column shows that



$$
R_J\in\mathbb Z[X],\qquad
                         \deg R_J\leq d.                      \tag{23}
$$



Its coefficients are signed primitive Pluecker coordinates.  Every
coordinate of $\omega$ occurs as a coefficient of at least one $R_J$,
and no two coordinates collide in the same coefficient.  Consequently,



$$
\boxed{\max_J H(R_J)=W.}             \tag{24}
$$



Equation (24) directly supplies a $J$ with $H(R_J)=W>0$, so $R_J$ is
nonzero.  Transcendence of $\pi$ then shows that every nonzero $R_J$ has
nonzero value at $\pi$.  Equivalently, contraction of the primitive
exterior vector by the nonzero evaluation covector cannot vanish on every
coordinate.

Expanding (22) at $X=\pi$ down its first column gives the uniform bound



$$
\boxed{|R_J(\pi)|\leq {k!EH^{k-1}\over g}.}      \tag{25}
$$



Let



$$
L_d=\pi+\pi^2+\cdots+\pi^d.          \tag{26}
$$



Equation (18) implies $|p_0^{(i)}|\leq E+L_dH$.  Applying the determinant
expansion to every primitive Pluecker coordinate yields the exact elementary
height bound



$$
W\leq {k!H^{k-1}\max\{H,E+L_dH\}\over g}.                  \tag{27}
$$



In particular, when $E\leq H$,



$$
W\leq {C_{d,k}H^k\over g},
 \qquad C_{d,k}=k!(1+L_d).                                   \tag{28}
$$



Equations (22)--(25) are the exact exterior-contraction theorem.  The
alternating construction has one evaluation column, so it receives one
factor $E$, not $E^k$.

## 5. Conditional measure and the exact content threshold

Assume temporarily that $s=e+\pi$ is algebraic of degree $r$.  The
fixed-degree conditional $e$-measure implies that, for every
$\delta>0$, there is $c_{r,d,\delta}>0$ such that every nonzero
$R\in\mathbb Z[X]$ of degree at most $d$ satisfies



$$
|R(\pi)|\geq c_{r,d,\delta}
        H(R)^{-(\kappa_r(d)+\delta)}.                          \tag{29}
$$



This uniform version follows by applying the primitive estimate at the
actual degree, weakening it to the ambient degree $d$, and absorbing the
finitely many small heights into the positive constant.  Nonprimitive
polynomials only make the inequality easier after division by their content.
If a particular $R_J$ has actual degree below $d$, its true conditional
threshold is smaller than the conservative ambient threshold used here.

Choose $J$ in (24) with $H(R_J)=W$.  It is nonzero, and (25), (28), and
(29) give



$$
c_{r,d,\delta}W^{-(\kappa_r(d)+\delta)}
       \leq {k!EH^{k-1}\over g},                              \tag{30}
$$



and, when $E\leq H$,



$$
\boxed{
 g^{\kappa_r(d)+\delta+1}
  \leq {k!\over c_{r,d,\delta}}
       C_{d,k}^{\kappa_r(d)+\delta}
       E H^{k-1+k(\kappa_r(d)+\delta)}.}                      \tag{31}
$$



Suppose $H\to\infty$, $k\leq d$ is fixed, and



$$
E=H^{-d+o(1)}.                       \tag{32}
$$



Take logarithms in (31), then let $\delta\downarrow0$.  This proves (6):



$$
\limsup {\log g\over\log H}
 \leq {k-1+k\kappa_r(d)-d\over\kappa_r(d)+1}
 =k-{d+1\over\kappa_r(d)+1}.                                \tag{33}
$$



For $r=1$, $\kappa_1(d)=d$, and (33) becomes



$$
\limsup {\log g\over\log H}\leq k-1. \tag{34}
$$



This is the precise rational-boundary obstruction.

There is a useful scale interpretation, but its hypotheses must be stated.
Assume in addition that the primitive exterior height does not collapse
beyond its content discount:



$$
g=H^{\gamma+o(1)},\qquad W=H^{k-\gamma+o(1)},
 \qquad 0\leq\gamma<k.                                      \tag{35}
$$



Then (25) has the exterior exponent



$$
\mu_{\rm ext}={d-k+1+\gamma\over k-\gamma}                 \tag{36}
$$



relative to height $W$.  Direct algebra gives



$$
\mu_{\rm ext}>\kappa_r(d)
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \gamma>k-{d+1\over\kappa_r(d)+1}.                          \tag{37}
$$



Thus the exact threshold required to beat the conditional measure is the
strict reverse of (33).  For rational $s$, equality
$\gamma=k-1$ only reproduces exponent $d$; a contradiction requires
$\gamma>k-1$.

If (35) fails because $W$ is exceptionally small, there is no loophole in
the reduction: the explicitly constructed polynomial $R_J$ in (22) then
has that exceptionally small height.  If (30) is violated, $R_J$ itself is
the exceptional polynomial approximation.  Hence exterior height collapse
and exterior content are legitimate arithmetic targets, but neither
amortizes the measure cost independently of one-polynomial approximation.

## 6. Products and pairwise resultants

Multiplying the $k$ scalar endpoint values is symmetric rather than
exterior.  If $H(P_i)\leq H$, $\deg P_i\leq d$, and
$|P_i(\pi)|\leq E$, then



$$
F=\prod_{i=1}^kP_i,\qquad
 \deg F\leq kd,\qquad
 H(F)\leq(d+1)^kH^k,\qquad
 |F(\pi)|\leq E^k.                                           \tag{38}
$$



At $E=H^{-d+o(1)}$, and absent exceptional product content or height
collapse, the value has exponent only $d$ relative to $H(F)$, while in
the rational case the conditional measure pays the actual degree, up to
$kd$.  If primitive normalization changes this ledger enough to help,
the primitive product $F/\operatorname {cont}(F)$ is itself an exceptional
integer-polynomial approximation.

The same one-small-column phenomenon occurs for a resultant.  Let
$P,Q\in\mathbb Z[X]$ have degrees $1\leq m,n\leq d$, heights at most
$H$, and



$$
|P(\pi)|,|Q(\pi)|\leq E.             \tag{39}
$$



Define



$$
B_{\pi,d}=\max_{0\leq j\leq d}
     \sum_{a=j}^d\binom aj\pi^{a-j}.                          \tag{40}
$$



The nonconstant coefficients of $P(\pi+Y)$ and $Q(\pi+Y)$ are bounded
by $B_{\pi,d}H$; their constant coefficients are $P(\pi)$ and
$Q(\pi)$.  Translation invariance gives



$$
\operatorname {Res}_X(P,Q)
   =\operatorname {Res}_Y(P(\pi+Y),Q(\pi+Y)).                 \tag{41}
$$



When both translated constant terms are set to zero, the two polynomials
share the factor $Y$, so the resultant vanishes identically.  Therefore
the Sylvester determinant lies in the ideal generated by those two constant
terms.  Expanding the determinant and discarding the identically cancelling
terms gives, whenever $E\leq B_{\pi,d}H$,



$$
|\operatorname {Res}(P,Q)|
 \leq(m+n)!E(B_{\pi,d}H)^{m+n-1}.                            \tag{42}
$$



If the resultant is nonzero, it is a nonzero integer and hence



$$
1\leq(m+n)!E(B_{\pi,d}H)^{m+n-1}.                           \tag{43}
$$



At the separate rational-case degree boundary
$E=H^{-\max(m,n)+o(1)}$, the power of $H$ on the right of (43) is
$\min(m,n)-1+o(1)$, never a negative constant for $m,n\geq1$.  If a
pair has unequal degrees, its lower-degree member is already beyond its own
degree threshold at this common scale.  Thus the only nonexceptional case to
test is the equal-degree case, where the right side of (43) has size
$H^{m-1+o(1)}$, not $o(1)$.  If the resultant is zero, the two
polynomials have a common nonconstant factor and the nonzero-integer route
disappears; analysis returns to the values of the individual factors.

Finally, an alternating determinant cannot place the same scalar evaluation
covector in two columns:



$$
\ell_\pi\wedge\ell_\pi=0.            \tag{44}
$$



A nonzero determinant must introduce coefficient, derivative, conjugate, or
other independent columns.  Their height bounds are exactly the cofactors
paid in (25) or (42).  Repeating the scalar endpoint alone supplies no new
small direction.

## 7. Phase alignment and parity

Gaussian phase alignment preserves the full theorem.  For



$$
C_p(z)=A_p(-iz)\in\mathbb Z[i][z],
 \qquad C_p(i\pi)=A_p(\pi),                                  \tag{45}
$$



the real coefficient vector $p$ retains its height, content, degree, and
all $N$ lattice directions.  Equations (2)--(37) apply without change to
that phase-aligned endpoint module.

For an ordinary real-coefficient polynomial at $i\pi$, write



$$
P_p(i\pi)=E_p+iO_p,
 \qquad |P_p(i\pi)|^2=E_p^2+O_p^2.                            \tag{46}
$$



The even and odd coefficient blocks have dimensions



$$
q_{\rm e}=\left\lfloor{d\over2}\right\rfloor+1,
 \qquad q_{\rm o}=\left\lceil{d\over2}\right\rceil.       \tag{47}
$$



There are now two evaluation covectors, one per parity block, not $k$
copies of a scalar covector.  The full-rank coordinate map



$$
p\longmapsto(E_p,O_p,p_2,\ldots,p_d)                         \tag{48}
$$



has determinant $\pi$.  Hence its image lattice has covolume $\pi$, and
the box with $|E_p|,|O_p|\leq E$ and
$|p_2|,\ldots,|p_d|\leq H$ has volume



$$
2^NE^2H^{d-1}.                       \tag{49}
$$



This gives the exact rank-two Minkowski product after replacing the
covolume one in (11) by $\pi$.  It does not supersede the lower-dimensional
pure-parity sublattices.  Their exact minimum is the better of the two block
minima, with dimension-only exponent



$$
\left\lfloor{d\over2}\right\rfloor. \tag{50}
$$



Within either parity block, Sections 2--6 apply with one evaluation
covector and the corresponding block dimension.  An even block of actual
degree $2j$ has Dirichlet exponent $j$, but its conditional measure
threshold remains



$$
2r^2j+r-1.                            \tag{51}
$$



An odd block is $\pi$ times an even block and has the same approximation
dimension.  Thus parity reduces the available dimension but does not
proportionally reduce the degree cost after $\pi=s-e$.  Several endpoints
can be distributed between the two blocks, but each block still contributes
only one small evaluation column.

## 8. Logical status and replay

The unconditional exact theorems are:

1. the lattice covolume, volume, and successive-minima identity
   (8)--(11);
2. the all-$k$ Hardy rank-threshold comparison (16);
3. the full determinant identity and bound (21), (3);
4. the saturated exterior contraction (22)--(28);
5. the product ledger (38);
6. the translated resultant identity and bound (41)--(43); and
7. the phase and parity decompositions (45)--(50).

Under the explicitly stated hypothesis that $e+\pi$ is algebraic, the
conditional-measure consequences are (29)--(37) and (51).  The scale formula
(36) additionally assumes the noncollapse relation (35); the exact
inequalities (30)--(31) do not.

The theorem proves that canonical successive-minimum, determinant,
resultant, and exterior constructions do not beat the rational-case
Dirichlet equality without producing an exceptional integer polynomial at
$\pi$.  It does not prove that exceptional approximation is impossible
unconditionally.  A construction which proves the required exterior
content or height collapse would still be meaningful precisely because it
would prove that exceptional approximation rather than obtain it for free
from dimension counting.

The certificate checks the determinant-one coordinate change, primitive

identities with exact arithmetic.  No finite numerical extrapolation is
used.  This note does not classify $e+\pi$.
