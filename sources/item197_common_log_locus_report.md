# Item 197 — the exceptional common-log locus is a simultaneous square-divisor problem

Checked: 2026-08-30 (Beijing time)

## 1. Scope and verdict

Stay in the Item 194 prime-number-theorem-side rank-zero cell



$$
4m+1=(2j+1)p-2s,
 \qquad 1\le s\le (p-3)/6,
 \qquad 3j+1<p.                                      \tag{1.1}
$$



Write $\ell_{\nu,0}$ for the two leading logarithmic coordinates of
Item 194, $\nu=0,1$.

> **PROVED — exact integer-residue bridge.**  Define
> 

$$
> C_\nu(m)=[z^{4m+\nu}]
> \frac{(1-z)^{6m}(1+z)^{1+3\nu}}
>      {(1+z^2)^{4m+1+\nu}}\in\mathbb Z.             \tag{1.2}
>
$$


> On every row (1.1), $p\mid C_0(m),C_1(m)$, and
> 

$$
> \boxed{\ell_{\nu,0}\equiv
> -2^{-2m-2\nu}\frac{C_\nu(m)}p\pmod p.}             \tag{1.3}
>
$$


> Consequently
> 

$$
> \boxed{\ell_{0,0}=\ell_{1,0}=0
> \iff p^2\mid C_0(m)\ \hbox{and}\ p^2\mid C_1(m).} \tag{1.4}
>
$$



> **PROVED — exact separated Frobenius-defect form.**  The two quotients
> $C_\nu/p\pmod p$ are dot products of a three-coordinate moving
> $j$-vector and two moving $s$-vectors.  Both sides are generated by
> fixed rational maps; see (4.8).  The only $p$-dependent kernels are two
> truncated logarithms of degrees $p-1$ and $2p-2$.

> **PROVED — radical and Smith interpretation.**  For fixed $m$, let
> $R_m$ be the product of the distinct primes occurring in (1.1) and
> satisfying the common-log condition.  Then
> 

$$
>                 \boxed{R_m^2\mid\gcd(C_0(m),C_1(m)).}             \tag{1.5}
>
$$


> This gcd is the first Smith divisor of the dyadically cleared one-row
> logarithmic coordinate matrix.  It feeds the already existing endpoint
> wedge/content; it is not a new independent matching or clearing reservoir.

> **PROVED SCOPED OBSTRUCTION — the height route is weaker than capacity.**
> The direct Cauchy majorant has exponential constant
> 

$$
> H=\min_{0<\rho<1}
> \{2\log(1+\rho)-4\log(1-\rho)-4\log\rho\}
> =6.327627545440858\ldots,                            \tag{1.6}
>
$$


> at $\rho=(\sqrt{33}-3)/6$.  Thus the nondegenerate coefficient-height
> route gives at best
> 

$$
> \frac{\log R_m}{6m}\le \frac H{12}+o(1)
> =0.5273022954534049\ldots.                           \tag{1.7}
>
$$


> This is weaker than the already frozen raw-cell mass
> $0.3370475079987658\ldots$ per $m$, equivalently the normalized
> ceiling $0.05617458466646097\ldots$ per $6m$.  If both coefficients in (1.2) were ever
> zero as integers, the height route would give no bound at that index and
> the raw-cell ceiling would still be the valid bound.  Hence no
> nonvanishing assumption is being hidden in the capacity conclusion.

> **EXPERIMENTAL EXACT FINITE.**  The default replay covers all 6,058 rows
> through $p\le151$.  It finds 44 separate $\ell_{0,0}$-zeros, 56
> separate $\ell_{1,0}$-zeros, 69 zeros of a necessary projective
> elimination determinant, and no common-log row.  This is not a density or
> nonoccurrence theorem.

> **OPEN.**  No uniform sublinear bound and no positive weighted family is
> proved for (1.4).  In particular, Item 197 does not improve the Route-1
> content exponent and proves nothing about the arithmetic nature of
> $e+\pi$.

## 2. The logarithmic coordinate is one residue

Retain Item 194's second-Cartier differential



$$
\Phi_\nu=\frac{u^{3j}H_\nu}{Q^{2j+2}}\,dx,
 \qquad u=x(1-x),\quad Q=(1+x)(1+x^2),\quad H_\nu(0)=0. \tag{2.1}
$$



Its only finite poles are $-1,i,-i$, and it is proper enough at infinity
to have zero residue there.  Therefore



$$
\operatorname {Res}_{-1}\Phi_\nu+
 \operatorname {Res}_{i}\Phi_\nu+
 \operatorname {Res}_{-i}\Phi_\nu=0.                \tag{2.2}
$$



The coefficients are real.  Item 194's definition of the logarithmic
coordinate and (2.2) give the exact simplification



$$
\ell_{\nu,0}=4\{\operatorname {Res}_{-1}\Phi_\nu+
                  \Re\operatorname {Res}_{i}\Phi_\nu\}
                =2\operatorname {Res}_{-1}\Phi_\nu.  \tag{2.3}
$$



This loses no exceptional zero case and involves no division by a
coordinate.

There is also a literal four-point form.  For $\eta^4=1$, put



$$
\Lambda_\eta(x)=\frac14\sum_{k=1}^4\eta^{-k}x^k,
 \qquad
 \rho_{j,\eta}=\operatorname {Res}_{x=-1}
 \frac{u^{3j}\Lambda_\eta(x)}{Q^{2j+2}}\,dx.          \tag{2.4}
$$



The $\Lambda_\eta$ are the evaluation basis of
$x\mathbb F_p[x]_{\le4}$.  Since Item 194 proves



$$
H_\nu(\zeta^p)=D_j(\zeta)T_\nu(\zeta),
 \quad D_j(1)=-4(3j+1),\quad
 D_j(-1)=D_j(i)=D_j(-i)=4(2j+1),                     \tag{2.5}
$$



equation (2.3) becomes



$$
\ell_{\nu,0}=2\sum_{\zeta^4=1}
 \rho_{j,\zeta^p}D_j(\zeta)T_\nu(\zeta).            \tag{2.6}
$$



Both the four-point coefficients and the primitives are fixed-rational-map
moments.  For example, with $t=x+1$,



$$
\rho_{j,\eta}=[t^{2j+1}]
 \frac{(-2+3t-t^2)^{3j}\Lambda_\eta(-1+t)}
      {(2-2t+t^2)^{2j+2}},                            \tag{2.7}
$$



while Item 194 gives



$$
T_\nu(\zeta)=\int_0^\zeta
 u^{(p-3)/2}\left(\frac{Q^2}{u^3}\right)^sQ^{-\nu}\,dx. \tag{2.8}
$$



Equations (2.6)--(2.8) are exact, but by themselves do not bound their
common zeros.

## 3. Proof of the integer-residue bridge

Let



$$
\omega_\nu=\frac{u^{6m}}{Q^{4m+1+\nu}}\,dx.
$$



Item 194's integration by parts is an identity over the rationals,



$$
\omega_\nu=d(F_j^pT_\nu)-p\Psi_\nu,
 \qquad \Phi_\nu=\mathcal C\Psi_\nu.                 \tag{3.1}
$$



Exact differentials have zero residues, and Cartier preserves the local
residue.  Hence



$$
\operatorname {Res}_{-1}\Phi_\nu\equiv
 -\frac1p\operatorname {Res}_{-1}\omega_\nu\pmod p. \tag{3.2}
$$



To evaluate the integer residue, put $x=-y$ and then



$$
z=\frac{1-y}{1+y},\qquad y=\frac{1-z}{1+z}.
$$



A direct substitution gives



$$
\omega_\nu=
 2^{-2m-1-2\nu}z^{-4m-1-\nu}
 \frac{(1-z)^{6m}(1+z)^{1+3\nu}}
      {(1+z^2)^{4m+1+\nu}}\,dz.                     \tag{3.3}
$$



Thus



$$
\operatorname {Res}_{-1}\omega_\nu
 =2^{-2m-1-2\nu}C_\nu(m).                            \tag{3.4}
$$



Equations (2.3), (3.2), and (3.4) prove (1.3).  The rank-zero construction
already says that the two residues are divisible by $p$; (1.3) also
reproves this integrally in the present cell.  Since $p$ is odd, the
dyadic factor is a unit, and (1.4) follows.

For fixed $(m,p)$, equation (1.1) has at most one admissible pair
$(j,s)$: changing $j$ by one changes the right side by $2p$, whereas
the permitted change in $2s$ is less than $p$.  Multiplying (1.4) over
the distinct exceptional primes proves (1.5).

## 4. Frobenius-defect and projective elimination

Put



$$
a=3j+1,\quad c=2j+1,\quad
 r=\frac{p-6s-3}{2},\quad q_\nu=2s-\nu.              \tag{4.1}
$$



Then $6m=ap+r$, $4m+1+\nu=cp-q_\nu$, and the coefficient in
(1.2) is



$$
[z^{cp-q_\nu-1}]G(z)^pP_\nu(z),                    \tag{4.2}
$$



where



$$
G(z)=\frac{(1-z)^a}{(1+z^2)^c},\qquad
 P_\nu(z)=(1-z)^r(1+z)^{1+3\nu}(1+z^2)^{q_\nu}.     \tag{4.3}
$$



The degree of $P_\nu$ is too small to meet the target exponent in
$G(z^p)P_\nu$.  Hence the coefficient of that term is exactly zero, and



$$
\frac{C_\nu(m)}p\equiv
 [z^{cp-q_\nu-1}]\frac{G(z)^p-G(z^p)}pP_\nu(z)\pmod p. \tag{4.4}
$$



Define the two truncated logarithms



$$
A_p(z)=-\sum_{k=1}^{p-1}\frac{z^k}{k},\qquad
 B_p(z)=\sum_{k=1}^{p-1}\frac{(-1)^{k-1}z^{2k}}k.    \tag{4.5}
$$



The binomial congruence $p^{-1}\binom pk\equiv(-1)^{k-1}/k\pmod p$
gives



$$
\frac{G^p-G(z^p)}p\equiv
 aA_p\frac{(1-z^p)^{a-1}}{(1+z^{2p})^c}
 -cB_p\frac{(1-z^p)^a}{(1+z^{2p})^{c+1}}.           \tag{4.6}
$$



Only one $z^p$-section of the $A_p$-term and two sections of the
$B_p$-term can reach (4.4).  Set



$$
\begin{aligned}
 X_\nu&=[z^{p-q_\nu-1}]A_pP_\nu,\\
 Y_\nu&=[z^{p-q_\nu-1}]B_pP_\nu,\\
 Y'_\nu&=[z^{2p-q_\nu-1}]B_pP_\nu,\\
 U_j&=[y^{2j}]\frac{(1-y)^{3j}}{(1+y^2)^{2j+1}},\\
 V_j&=[y^{2j}]\frac{(1-y)^{3j+1}}{(1+y^2)^{2j+2}},\\
 W_j&=[y^{2j-1}]\frac{(1-y)^{3j+1}}{(1+y^2)^{2j+2}}.
                                                               \tag{4.7}
\end{aligned}
$$



Coefficient separation in (4.6) proves



$$
\boxed{\frac{C_\nu(m)}p\equiv
 aU_jX_\nu-c(V_jY_\nu+W_jY'_\nu)\pmod p.}           \tag{4.8}
$$



This is a two-equation projective incidence problem.  The $s$-side is
generated by the fixed map



$$
\mathcal K(z)=\frac{(1+z^2)^2}{(1-z)^3},             \tag{4.9}
$$



because $P_\nu$ is a fixed prefactor times $\mathcal K^s$.  The
$j$-side is generated by



$$
\mathcal J(y)=\frac{(1-y)^3}{y^2(1+y^2)^2},          \tag{4.10}
$$



since $U,V,W$ are constant terms of fixed prefactors times
$\mathcal J^j$.

The necessary elimination determinant is



$$
V_j(X_0Y_1-X_1Y_0)+W_j(X_0Y'_1-X_1Y'_0)=0.          \tag{4.11}
$$



It is not sufficient: 69 rows in the finite replay satisfy (4.11), while
none satisfies both equations (4.8).

The rational maps (4.9)--(4.10) have bounded degree and fixed singular
sets.  The full family does not have bounded complexity, however: the exact
kernels $A_p,B_p$ have degrees $p-1,2p-2$, and



$$
A_p'(z)=-\frac{1-z^{p-1}}{1-z},\qquad
 B_p'(z)=\frac{2z(1-z^{2p-2})}{1+z^2}.               \tag{4.12}
$$



Thus the natural logarithmic defect has linearly growing degree and a
linearly growing zero set in its derivative.  This proves a scoped
obstruction to a direct bounded-degree Bezout or bounded-conductor count;
it does not prove that some different arithmetic representation is
impossible.

## 5. Smith, clearing, and height ledger

After the fixed dyadic clearing, $(C_0,C_1)$ is the one-row matrix of the
two $-1$-log residues.  Therefore $\gcd(C_0,C_1)$ is exactly its first
Smith divisor.  Every common divisor of this row divides the corresponding
cleared endpoint minors, so it is already a possible source of the frozen
primitive content.  Counting it again as a separate reservoir would be
double counting.

No identity with the deterministic clearing reservoir $K_m$ is proved.
There cannot be a universal divisibility in either direction for the full
gcd: at $m=2$,



$$
\gcd(C_0(2),C_1(2))=11,\qquad K_2=M_9/5=504.        \tag{5.1}
$$



For $0<\rho<1$, termwise absolute values in (1.2) give



$$
|C_\nu(m)|\le
 \rho^{-4m-\nu}(1+\rho)^{6m+1+3\nu}
 (1-\rho^2)^{-4m-1-\nu}.                             \tag{5.2}
$$



The coefficient of $m$ in its logarithm is the function in (1.6).
Differentiation gives $3\rho^2+3\rho-2=0$, hence the minimizer and
constant stated there.  Combining a nonzero coefficient bound with (1.5)
gives (1.7).  Since (1.7) is larger than the unconditional raw support
ceiling, this height argument supplies no new mass theorem.  A fortiori,
the absence of a proved all-$m$ nonvanishing theorem for the pair cannot
improve the conclusion.
