## 2. Prefix and suffix coordinates

Set



$$
w_1=7,
\qquad
w_j=4j+2\quad(2\le j\le m),
\qquad
w_{m+1}=A.
\tag{2.1}
$$



Define the usual prefix continuants



$$
Q_{-1}=0,\quad Q_0=1,
\qquad
P_{-1}=1,\quad P_0=0,
\tag{2.2}
$$





$$
Q_j=w_jQ_{j-1}+Q_{j-2},
\qquad
P_j=w_jP_{j-1}+P_{j-2}.
\tag{2.3}
$$



Thus



$$
Q_m=a,
\qquad
Q_{m+1}=b.
\tag{2.4}
$$



Let $\delta_1,\ldots,\delta_m$ be any canonical word representing
$0<R<a$, and set $\delta_{m+1}=0$.  For $0\le j\le m+1$, put



$$
R_j=\sum_{i=0}^{j-1}\delta_{i+1}Q_i,
\qquad
Z_j=\sum_{i=0}^{j-1}\delta_{i+1}P_i,
\tag{2.5}
$$





$$
E_j=R_jP_j-Z_jQ_j.
\tag{2.6}
$$



The universal prefix recurrence is



$$
E_{j+1}=w_{j+1}E_j+E_{j-1}+(-1)^j\delta_{j+1}.
\tag{2.7}
$$



Assume $E_m\ne0$, and define



$$
\sigma=\operatorname{sgn}(E_m),
\qquad
\kappa=|E_m|,
\qquad
\epsilon=(-1)^n\sigma,
\qquad
k_j=\sigma E_j.
\tag{2.8}
$$



Since $m=n-2$, $(-1)^m=(-1)^n$.  Multiplying (2.7) by $\sigma$
and solving backward gives



$$
k_{j-1}=k_{j+1}-w_{j+1}k_j
-\sigma(-1)^j\delta_{j+1}.
\tag{2.9}
$$



For the suffix tower, retain Item 323's boundaries and recurrence



$$
H_m=1,
\qquad H_{m+1}=0,
\qquad
N_m=N_{m+1}=0,
\tag{2.10}
$$





$$
H_{j-1}=w_{j+1}H_j+H_{j+1},
\tag{2.11}
$$





$$
N_{j-1}=w_{j+1}N_j+N_{j+1}+\delta_{j+1}.
\tag{2.12}
$$



Then (1.4) is exact at every depth.  No target assumption appears in
(2.1)-(2.12).

## 3. The single exact defect

Item 320 gives the universal appended relation



$$
U=(-1)^nE_{m+1}.
\tag{3.1}
$$



Define



$$
\boxed{\Delta=k_{m+1}-a.}
\tag{3.2}
$$



Then, without assuming the target,



$$
\boxed{U-\epsilon a=\epsilon\Delta.}
\tag{3.3}
$$



Indeed, $E_{m+1}=\sigma k_{m+1}$ and
$(-1)^n\sigma=\epsilon$.

The Item-316 master identity is



$$
aU=(-1)^nbE_m+R=\epsilon b\kappa+R.
\tag{3.4}
$$



Combining (3.3)-(3.4) gives the exact square-defect identity



$$
\boxed{
a^2-b\kappa-\epsilon R=-a\Delta.}
\tag{3.5}
$$



Therefore the following are equivalent for every candidate word:



$$
\boxed{
\Delta=0
\iff U=\epsilon a
\iff a^2=b\kappa+\epsilon R.}
\tag{3.6}
$$



If the word also satisfies Item 316's window and small-error hypotheses,
these are precisely the original actual target conditions.  Thus
$\Delta$ is not an auxiliary obstruction: it is the exact old target
defect, with every sign and factor of $a$ fixed.

## 4. The companion suffix and global factorization

Define the companion homogeneous suffix by



$$
G_m=0,
\qquad
G_{m+1}=1,
\tag{4.1}
$$





$$
G_{j-1}=w_{j+1}G_j+G_{j+1}
\qquad(1\le j\le m).
\tag{4.2}
$$



Thus



$$
G_{m-1}=1,
\qquad
G_j=K(w_{j+2},w_{j+3},\ldots,w_m)
\quad(0\le j\le m-1),
\tag{4.3}
$$



with the empty word at $j=m-1$.  Every $G_j$ in (4.3) is positive.

Put



$$
J_j=\kappa H_j+\epsilon N_j
\tag{4.4}
$$



and



$$
T_j=aG_j+(-1)^{m+j}k_j.
\tag{4.5}
$$



The load recurrence gives



$$
J_{j-1}=w_{j+1}J_j+J_{j+1}
+\epsilon\delta_{j+1}.
\tag{4.6}
$$



Equation (2.9), with $(-1)^m\sigma=\epsilon$, gives exactly the same
inhomogeneous recurrence for $T_j$:



$$
T_{j-1}=w_{j+1}T_j+T_{j+1}
+\epsilon\delta_{j+1}.
\tag{4.7}
$$



The top two differences are



$$
J_m-T_m=\kappa-k_m=0,
\tag{4.8}
$$





$$
J_{m+1}-T_{m+1}
=0-(a-k_{m+1})=\Delta.
\tag{4.9}
$$



Subtracting (4.7) from (4.6) leaves the homogeneous suffix recurrence.
The boundary values (4.8)-(4.9) and uniqueness therefore give



$$
\boxed{J_j-T_j=\Delta G_j\qquad(0\le j\le m+1).}
\tag{4.10}
$$



This is (1.8).  It is an exact identity for every canonical candidate,
not an identity obtained after setting the target defect to zero.

At the bottom, $k_0=0$, so the target would give the exact first-lift
endpoint



$$
\boxed{\kappa H_0+\epsilon N_0=aG_0.}
\tag{4.11}
$$



Equation (4.10) shows that (4.11) is itself equivalent to $\Delta=0$,
because $G_0>0$.  It is not an independent companion equation.

## 5. Ideal, gcd, and codimension closure

Define the bridge residuals



$$
\mathcal R_j
=J_j-aG_j-(-1)^{m+j}k_j.
\tag{5.1}
$$



Equation (4.10) is



$$
\mathcal R_j=\Delta G_j.
\tag{5.2}
$$



Since $G_{m-1}=1$,



$$
\mathcal R_{m-1}=\Delta.
\tag{5.3}
$$



This proves the integral ideal equality (1.9), including signs and unit
content.

The conclusion does not depend on retaining the near-top row.  From
(4.2),



$$
\gcd(G_j,G_{j+1})
=\gcd(G_{j+1},G_{j+2})
=\cdots
=\gcd(G_{m-1},G_m)=1.
\tag{5.4}
$$



Hence, for every $0\le j\le m-2$,



$$
\boxed{\gcd(\mathcal R_j,\mathcal R_{j+1})=|\Delta|.}
\tag{5.5}
$$



Over a prime $p$, any two adjacent bridge rows vanish simultaneously
if and only if



$$
p\mid\Delta.
\tag{5.6}
$$



A single row can contain the fixed suffix pollution $G_j$, but an
adjacent pair removes it exactly.  Therefore the all-depth portfolio has
rank one in the target defect.  Treating its many rows as independent
local conditions would be double counting.

This proves the scoped no-go:



$$
\boxed{
\text{all-depth divided-quotient bridge portfolio}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\text{the one original defect }\Delta,
}
\tag{5.7}
$$



with no new codimension at any depth.

## 6. Exact termination of the same-state $b$-adic lift

Recall



$$
L_j=RH_j-bN_j,
\qquad
J_j=\kappa H_j+\epsilon N_j.
\tag{6.1}
$$



A direct calculation gives



$$
\begin{aligned}
\epsilon L_j-a^2H_j+bJ_j
&=(\epsilon R-a^2+b\kappa)H_j\\
&=a\Delta H_j,
\end{aligned}
\tag{6.2}
$$



where (3.5) is used in the last line.  This proves (1.11).

On the target, $\Delta=0$, and therefore



$$
\boxed{\epsilon L_j=a^2H_j-bJ_j}
\tag{6.3}
$$



as an exact integer identity.  The zeroth residue is the projective state
of Item 327; its sole first divided coefficient is $-J_j$.  There is no
$b^2$ coefficient to adjoin.

Equivalently, the associated same-state lift has:



$$
\begin{array}{c|c}
\text{level}&\text{new generator}\\ \hline
b^0&H_j\\
b^1&J_j\\
b^r,\ r\ge2&\text{none}.
\end{array}
\tag{6.4}
$$



For a non-target candidate, every failure of (6.3) factors as
$a\Delta H_j$.  Therefore a congruence modulo $b^r$, at any order
$r\ge2$, measures only the valuation of $\Delta$ together with the
fixed factors $aH_j$.  It does not produce a second moving target
coordinate.

This is a no-go for repeatedly lifting the **same transducer state**.
It is not a no-go for nonlinear polynomials in the already-visible first
quotient $J$, or for adjoining an external period.

## 7. The first quotient is exactly the old digit word

The quotient tower retains information, but it is not new state.  Equation
(2.12) gives the exact inverse



$$
\boxed{
\delta_{j+1}
=N_{j-1}-w_{j+1}N_j-N_{j+1}
\qquad(1\le j\le m).}
\tag{7.1}
$$



Thus $N_0,\ldots,N_{m-1}$, with the two zero boundary values, recover
$\delta_2,\ldots,\delta_{m+1}$ without division or a modular inverse.
Given $R$, the remaining digit is



$$
\delta_1
=R-\sum_{i=1}^{m-1}\delta_{i+1}Q_i.
\tag{7.2}
$$



Consequently



$$
\boxed{
(R,N_0,\ldots,N_{m-1})
\longleftrightarrow
(R,\delta_1,\ldots,\delta_m)}
\tag{7.3}
$$



is an exact triangular coordinate change on canonical words.

Merely adjoining the divided quotients to Item 316 is therefore a
definitional extension of its all-digit state.  A successful next theorem
must prove new arithmetic **about** these loads—such as a nonlinear
nonvanishing, a valuation bound, or a transverse coupling—not count the
loads themselves as extra target equations.

## 8. Actual-family, endpoint, and scope audit

For an actual centered-square target, Item 316 gives every hypothesis in
(1.3), so $E_m\ne0$, $\kappa=|E_m|$, and
$\epsilon$ is exactly the sign of the actual centered remainder.  Then
(3.6) gives $\Delta=0$, and all identities (4.10), (6.3) are forced.
No arbitrary digit state is promoted to an actual one.

Conversely, if a canonical word satisfies Item 316's window and small-
error conditions and any one of the following holds,



$$
\mathcal R_j=0\quad(0\le j\le m-1),
\tag{8.1}
$$





$$
\epsilon L_j=a^2H_j-bJ_j\quad(0\le j\le m),
\tag{8.2}
$$



or



$$
U=\epsilon a,
\tag{8.3}
$$



then $\Delta=0$ and the word is the original exact target.  These are
equivalent forms, not additional necessary conditions.

All continuants in Sections 4-8 are positive in the indices where they
are divided or used to infer vanishing.  The only zero boundary is the
declared $G_m=0$.  No negative-index continuant appears.  There are no
analytic functions or poles.

The theorem closes:

* accumulation of independent conditions from multiple divided-quotient
  bridge depths;
* repeated same-state lifting beyond the first $b$-adic quotient; and
* any capacity claim which counts the fixed $G_j$ factors or repeated
  copies of $\Delta$ as fresh content.

It does not close:

* nonlinear arithmetic of $N_j$ or $J_j$;
* an integer-size theorem using the exact unit strip;
* canonical complement redigitization;
* a super-logarithmic dyadic condition coupled to odd arithmetic; or
* a genuinely external period or Frobenius coordinate.

## 9. Proper targets and capacity

Let $Q\mid b$ be any declared proper de-overlapped target.  Reducing
(5.2) modulo $Q$ gives



$$
\mathcal R_j\equiv\Delta G_j\pmod Q.
\tag{9.1}
$$



Because $G_{m-1}=1$, simultaneous use of the portfolio is exactly



$$
Q\mid\mathcal R_0,\ldots,\mathcal R_{m-1}
\iff Q\mid\Delta.
\tag{9.2}
$$



No proper-target lower bound follows: centering the original square
residue modulo $Q$ remains a separate problem.  Equation (9.2) only
prevents repeated bridge rows from being credited as independent prime
conditions.

The full modulus $b$ passed the raw-capacity screen, but the exact ideal
calculation reduces the entire candidate mechanism to the old defect.
Its **incremental** capacity is therefore zero.  Item 282's common
coefficient/product baseline is neither divided out nor rebooked here.
