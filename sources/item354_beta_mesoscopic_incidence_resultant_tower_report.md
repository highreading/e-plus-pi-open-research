# Item 354 — the mesoscopic incidence-resultant tower and its low-multiplicity barrier

Checked: 2026-09-01 (Beijing time)

## 1. Strict verdict and capacity admission

Assume the actual Item-316 target and retain the exact-zero omission,
$t$-avoidance, and de-overlapped divisor $Q\mid b$ from Items 343 and 346.
For each depth, let $B_j$ be Item 346's squarefree row carrier and define its
mesoscopic part



$$
C_j=B_j^{>A}
\qquad(3\le j\le m).
\tag{1.1}
$$



Thus $C_j=1$ on every exact-zero or inactive row, while every active row has



$$
\boxed{
C_j=p,
\quad A<p<A^2,
\quad p\mid Q\mid b,
\quad p\nmid t.}
\tag{1.2}
$$



The actual canonical digits then satisfy



$$
w_j\delta_{j-1}-\delta_j=p h_j,
\qquad 1\le h_j<A.
\tag{1.3}
$$



The raw capacity remains



$$
0\le\Xi_Q\le\log\operatorname{rad}(Q)\le\log b.
\tag{1.4}
$$



Consequently a pairwise-gcd route is admissible only if it gives a strict
aggregate restriction across linearly many actual depths.  Item 354 settles
the complete specialized gcd/resultant hierarchy and obtains the following
decision.

1. The full $r$-wise common-prime resultant layer has an exact all-depth
   description in terms of the incidence multiplicities of the actual word.
2. Its height is at most $2N_A\log A/r$, where $N_A$ is the number of
   active mesoscopic rows.
3. At $r=2$ this is still $(1+o(1))\log b$, a full unresolved target copy.
   Pairwise resultants therefore give no capacity reduction.
4. A growing threshold makes the high-multiplicity tail negligible and
   isolates the exact missing statistic: the weighted low-incidence
   squarefree support of the actual canonical word.

The theorem is conditional on the actual Item-316 target in exactly the same
sense as Item 346: if such a word exists, its specialized carriers obey every
identity below.  No ambient word is promoted to an actual target, no census
is used, and booking remains zero.

## 2. Actual incidence multiplicities and first hits

Let



$$
\mathcal P_A
=\{p:A<p<A^2,\ p\mid Q,\ p\nmid t,\ p\mid\mathfrak z_j
\text{ for some nonzero row }j\}.
\tag{2.1}
$$



For $p\in\mathcal P_A$, put



$$
\nu_p=\#\{j:C_j=p\},
\qquad
N_A=\#\{j:C_j>1\}=\sum_{p\in\mathcal P_A}\nu_p.
\tag{2.2}
$$



Because each $C_j$ is either one or one prime, the incidence graph with two
depths joined when their row carriers have a nontrivial gcd is a disjoint
union of complete graphs, one clique of size $\nu_p$ for every $p$.

The mesoscopic first-hit factor is exactly



$$
D_j^{>A}
=\frac{C_j}{\gcd(C_j,\operatorname{lcm}_{3\le i<j}C_i)}.
\tag{2.3}
$$



Hence $D_j^{>A}=p$ at the first row in the $p$-clique and is one at all
later rows.  In particular,



$$
\boxed{
\prod_{j=3}^{m}D_j^{>A}
=\prod_{p\in\mathcal P_A}p.}
\tag{2.4}
$$



The factors $D_j$ are pairwise coprime by construction.  Their pairwise
gcds are therefore identically one and contain no further arithmetic.  To
study repeated support one must use the pre-first-hit row carriers $C_j$.

## 3. The complete specialized gcd/resultant tower

For $r\ge1$, define the aggregate common-divisor resultant



$$
G_r
=\prod_{3\le j_1<\cdots<j_r\le m}
\gcd(C_{j_1},\ldots,C_{j_r}),
\tag{3.1}
$$



with an empty product equal to one, and put



$$
\mathcal R_r=\operatorname{rad}(G_r).
\tag{3.2}
$$



After specialization, the constant ideal generated by any selected rows is
the ideal generated by their gcd.  Thus (3.1) records the complete common-
prime information available to an $r$-wise integer resultant.

For a fixed prime $p$, exactly $\binom{\nu_p}{r}$ subsets in (3.1) lie
inside its incidence clique.  Therefore



$$
\boxed{
G_r=\prod_{p\in\mathcal P_A}p^{\binom{\nu_p}{r}},
\qquad
\mathcal R_r=\prod_{\substack{p\in\mathcal P_A\\\nu_p\ge r}}p.}
\tag{3.3}
$$



This is an exact all-depth identity.  It gives the nested tower



$$
\mathcal R_1\supseteq\mathcal R_2\supseteq\cdots,
\qquad
\frac{\mathcal R_r}{\mathcal R_{r+1}}
=\prod_{\nu_p=r}p.
\tag{3.4}
$$



The row product has the layer-cake factorization



$$
\boxed{
\prod_{j=3}^{m}C_j
=\prod_{p}p^{\nu_p}
=\prod_{r\ge1}\mathcal R_r.}
\tag{3.5}
$$



Moreover,



$$
\mathcal R_1=\prod_jD_j^{>A}.
\tag{3.6}
$$



For $r=2$, choose the first two rows in every clique with $\nu_p\ge2$.
These pairs are vertex-disjoint, and



$$
\boxed{
\mathcal R_2
=\prod_{p:\nu_p\ge2}p
=\prod_{\text{chosen disjoint pairs }(j,k)}\gcd(C_j,C_k).}
\tag{3.7}
$$



Thus no loss from a union bound is hidden here: (3.7) is the strongest
aggregate pairwise shared-support carrier.

## 4. Exact capacity of every multiplicity layer

Since every active $C_j$ is a prime below $A^2$,



$$
\log\prod_jC_j<2N_A\log A.
\tag{4.1}
$$



Every prime in $\mathcal R_r$ occurs in at least $r$ rows.  Equations
(3.3)-(3.5) therefore give



$$
\boxed{
r\log\mathcal R_r
\le\log\prod_jC_j
<2N_A\log A,}
\tag{4.2}
$$



or



$$
\boxed{
\log\mathcal R_r<\frac{2N_A}{r}\log A.}
\tag{4.3}
$$



Here $N_A\le m-2<n$, while



$$
\log b=n\log n+O(n),
\qquad
\log A=\log n+\log4+O(1/n).
\tag{4.4}
$$



At the pairwise level,



$$
\boxed{
\log\mathcal R_2<N_A\log A
\le(1+o(1))\log b.}
\tag{4.5}
$$



This does not beat the raw one-copy ceiling (1.4).  A configuration of
doubleton cliques can use $N_A/2$ distinct primes near $A^2$ and retain
the full logarithmic scale.  This is a capacity statement, not an assertion
that such an actual target configuration exists.

For every fixed $r\ge3$, (4.3) gives a strict $2/r$ ceiling on the support
appearing at least $r$ times.  It still says nothing about primes appearing
fewer than $r$ times.  In particular, every singleton prime is invisible to
all pairwise common-divisor resultants.

## 5. Growing multiplicity and the exact missing statistic

For an integer $L\ge2$, define the low-incidence carrier



$$
\mathcal S_{<L}
=\prod_{\substack{p\in\mathcal P_A\\1\le\nu_p<L}}p.
\tag{5.1}
$$



By (3.3),



$$
\boxed{
\mathcal R_1=\mathcal S_{<L}\mathcal R_L,}
\tag{5.2}
$$



and hence



$$
\boxed{
\log\mathcal R_1
\le\log\mathcal S_{<L}
+\frac{2N_A}{L}\log A.}
\tag{5.3}
$$



Together with Item 346's small-prime theorem, this yields the exact
asymptotic decision:



$$
\boxed{
\Xi_Q=o(\log b)
\iff
\text{there exists }L(n)\to\infty\text{ such that }
\log\mathcal S_{<L(n)}=o(\log b).}
\tag{5.4}
$$



The forward implication is immediate because
$\mathcal S_{<L}\mid\mathcal R_1$.  Conversely, (5.3), $N_A<n$, and
$L(n)\to\infty$ make the high-multiplicity tail $o(\log b)$.

Equation (5.4) is not a zero-density theorem.  It is a necessary-and-
sufficient reformulation which isolates the statistic that such a theorem
must control.  Pairwise gcds supply $\mathcal R_2$; they do not control the
singleton factor



$$
\mathcal S_{<2}=\prod_{\nu_p=1}p.
\tag{5.5}
$$



Even after singletons are controlled, doubletons retain the full ceiling in
(4.5).  A successful Closer argument must therefore control the actual
low-incidence distribution up to a threshold tending to infinity, or use a
different arithmetic invariant which bounds $\mathcal R_1$ directly.

## 6. Exact separation from squarefull denominator content

Put



$$
E(Q)=\frac{Q}{\operatorname{rad}(Q)}.
\tag{6.1}
$$



This is Item 265's excess-valuation carrier.  Split the low-incidence
support by the valuation of $Q$ and define the genuinely fresh part



$$
\mathcal S_{<L}^{(1)}
=\prod_{\substack{p\in\mathcal P_A\\1\le\nu_p<L\\v_p(Q)=1}}p.
\tag{6.2}
$$



Also put



$$
\mathcal R_L^{(1)}
=\prod_{\substack{p\in\mathcal P_A\\\nu_p\ge L\\v_p(Q)=1}}p,
\qquad
\mathcal R_1^{(\ge2)}
=\prod_{\substack{p\in\mathcal P_A\\v_p(Q)\ge2}}p.
$$



Then the support partition is exact, and the two latter factors satisfy



$$
\boxed{
\mathcal R_1
=\mathcal S_{<L}^{(1)}\mathcal R_L^{(1)}
 \mathcal R_1^{(\ge2)},
\qquad
\mathcal R_L^{(1)}\mid\mathcal R_L,
\qquad
\mathcal R_1^{(\ge2)}\mid E(Q).}
\tag{6.3}
$$



Therefore (4.3) gives



$$
\boxed{
\log\mathcal R_1
\le
\log\mathcal S_{<L}^{(1)}
+\log E(Q)
+\frac{2N_A}{L}\log A.}
\tag{6.4}
$$



Since $Q\mid b$, one has $E(Q)\mid E(b)$.  The middle term is precisely the
old Item-265 squarefull/excess branch and cannot be booked again.  Incidence
multiplicity $\nu_p$ and denominator valuation $v_p(Q)$ are different:
repetition of one prime in several cofactors does not imply $p^2\mid Q$.
Thus (6.3) neither hides repeated row support inside the squarefull branch
nor double-counts a new gain.

A sufficient combined theorem would be



$$
\log E(Q)=o(\log b),
\qquad
\log\mathcal S_{<L(n)}^{(1)}=o(\log b)
\quad\text{for some }L(n)\to\infty.
\tag{6.5}
$$



Item 354 proves neither input.

## 7. Resultant decision and the next arithmetic theorem

The specialized pairwise/resultant route is now exhausted in its natural
scope.

* Pairwise gcds of the first-hit factors $D_j$ are all one.
* Pairwise gcds of the raw actual row carriers give exactly
  $\mathcal R_2$, with the full one-copy ceiling (4.5).
* Every fixed shared-support level $r\ge2$ gives exactly $\mathcal R_r$ and
  is blind to every lower multiplicity layer.
* Item 350 shows only compatibility of selected distinct hit congruences
  with the linear recurrence and exact formal target.  It does not impose
  earlier-row avoidance and therefore proves nothing about singleton
  incidence.

The scoped no-go here comes from the exact tower (3.3): fixed-order common-
prime resultants contain no datum from lower multiplicity layers.  Item 350
did not construct actual canonical beta digits or a formal first-occurrence
pattern, and Item 354 does not infer actual singleton density from its
ambient controls.

The first genuinely arithmetic statistic is now explicit:



$$
\boxed{
\Sigma_{<L}^{(1)}(n)
=\sum_{\substack{A<p<A^2,\ p\mid Q,\ p\nmid t,\ v_p(Q)=1\\
1\le\#\{j:w_j\delta_{j-1}-\delta_j=p h_j, 1\le h_j<A\}<L}}
\log p.}
\tag{7.1}
$$



One must prove $\Sigma_{<L(n)}^{(1)}(n)=o(n\log n)$ for some slowly growing
$L(n)$, together with the old excess theorem, or obtain a direct stronger
bound for $\mathcal R_1$.  This statistic uses the unique canonical word,
the small positive quotients $h_j$, and the common divisor $Q\mid b$.
It is not determined by recurrence resultants.
