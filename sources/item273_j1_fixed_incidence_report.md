Q_0(p,h,s)=Q_1(p,h,s)=0\pmod p.                            \tag{1.5}

## 2. The exact fixed incidence

Use Item 218's polynomial



$$
P_0(z)=(1-z)^{2h}(1+z)(1+z^2)^{2s},\qquad
 F(z)=P_0(z)\log(1+z^2),                                    \tag{2.1}
$$



and put



$$
f_n=[z^n]F(z),\qquad d=\deg P_0=2h+4s+1.                   \tag{2.2}
$$



The two endpoint indices are



$$
T=2h+6s+2,\qquad L=4h+4s+2,\qquad E=L+1.                  \tag{2.3}
$$



Define



$$
S_n=(f_{n+1},f_n,f_{n-1},f_{n-2})^{\mathsf T},\qquad
 Y=(S_T,S_E)^{\mathsf T}.                                  \tag{2.4}
$$



The first gate coordinate is



$$
Q_0=2f_T-f_L.                                               \tag{2.5}
$$



Item 218's all-index identity writes $4sH_1(N)=R(N)$, where



$$
\begin{aligned}
 R(N)={}&(N+1)f_{N+1}+(N+2h+4s-1)f_N\\
       &+(4h+8s+1-N)f_{N-1}+(2h+4s+3-N)f_{N-2}.
\end{aligned}                                                \tag{2.6}
$$



Since $4sQ_1=2R(T)-R(E)$, the simultaneous gate is exactly



$$
A(h,s)Y=0,                                                   \tag{2.7}
$$



with



$$
A(h,s)=
\begin{pmatrix}
0&2&0&0&0&0&-1&0\\
4h+12s+6&8h+20s+2&4h+4s-2&2-4s&
-4h-4s-4&-6h-8s-2&2-4s&2h
\end{pmatrix}.                                               \tag{2.8}
$$



This matrix has rank exactly two on every actual row.  Indeed, the first
entry of its second row is zero in the first row and



$$
4h+12s+6\equiv6s+3\pmod p,\qquad 0<6s+3<p.                 \tag{2.9}
$$



Thus ambient rank-one and rank-zero branches are excluded on the actual
family without division.  They are not silently discarded from the
general affine analysis in Section 4.

## 3. Exact rank-four tail module

The logarithmic derivative is



$$
{P_0'\over P_0}=-{2h\over1-z}+{1\over1+z}
                  +{4sz\over1+z^2}.                         \tag{3.1}
$$



Multiplication by $1-z^4$ gives the cubic



$$
a(z)=(1-z^4){P_0'\over P_0}
      =(1-2h)+(-2h-1+4s)z+(1-2h)z^2+(-2h-1-4s)z^3.          \tag{3.2}
$$



Consequently



$$
(1-z^4)F'(z)-a(z)F(z)=2z(1-z^2)P_0(z).                    \tag{3.3}
$$



If $r_n=[z^n]2z(1-z^2)P_0(z)$, coefficient extraction yields



$$
\begin{aligned}
(n+1)f_{n+1}={}&(1-2h)f_n+(-2h-1+4s)f_{n-1}+(1-2h)f_{n-2}\\
&+(n-2h-4s-4)f_{n-3}+r_n.                                  
\end{aligned}                                                \tag{3.4}
$$



Thus



$$
S_{n+1}=T_nS_n+{r_{n+1}\over n+2}e_1,                      \tag{3.5}
$$



where the first row of $T_n$ is



$$
{1\over n+2}
 (1-2h,-2h-1+4s,1-2h,n-2h-4s-3),                           \tag{3.6}
$$



and the other rows are the standard shift rows.  Its determinant is



$$
\det T_n={2h+4s+3-n\over n+2}={d+2-n\over n+2}.            \tag{3.7}
$$



The forcing polynomial in (3.3) has degree $d+3$.  Both actual endpoint
states lie strictly above its active range:



$$
T\ge d+3,\qquad E\ge d+4,                                  \tag{3.8}
$$



and every step between them uses $r_{n+1}=0$.  The corridor also avoids
the determinant zero $n=d+2$.  More explicitly, its denominator indices
satisfy



$$
0<n+2\le\max(T+1,L+2)<p,                                  \tag{3.9}
$$



while $n-d-2$ is a positive integer strictly below $p$.  Every forward
or inverse transfer pivot is therefore a $p$-unit.

Let $P_{h,s}$ be the ordered product of the $T_n$, inverted when
$E<T$.  Then the actual relation is homogeneous and exact:



$$
S_E=P_{h,s}S_T.                                             \tag{3.10}
$$



The affine forcing in (3.5) has not been erased; it is simply zero on the
actual transfer corridor.

## 4. Every rank and affine branch

Write $A=(A_T\mid A_E)$.  On the actual family, (3.10) reduces the gate
to



$$
G_{h,s}S_T=0,\qquad G_{h,s}=A_T+A_EP_{h,s}.                 \tag{4.1}
$$



Its rank $\rho$ may be $0,1,$ or $2$.  No all-prime theorem in this
item excludes either rank-drop branch:



$$
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
\rho&\text{actual homogeneous collision fiber}&\text{dimension}\\ \hline
2&\ker G_{h,s}&2\\
1&\ker G_{h,s}&3\\
0&\mathbb F_p^4&4.
\end{array}                                                  \tag{4.2}
$$



For completeness, a transfer crossing the forcing range would have
$S_E=PS_T+b$ and hence



$$
GS_T+c=0,\qquad G=A_T+A_EP,\qquad c=A_Eb.                  \tag{4.3}
$$



Then a rank-one system is either a three-dimensional affine fiber or
empty according as the augmented rank stays one or rises to two.  A
rank-zero system is all of state space when $c=0$, and empty when
$c\ne0$.  These are the retained affine and rank-zero branches.  On the
actual corridor, $b=c=0$.

Even the generic rank-two case leaves a two-dimensional kernel.  Longer
bounded transport only changes coordinates by invertible matrices; it
does not create a third independent collision equation.  Moreover, the
actual series supplies one arithmetic state in this four-dimensional
space.  A codimension count in the ambient state space is not a theorem
about how that section is distributed as the characteristic varies.

The checker finds rank two in all 6,258 actual rows with $p\le1000$.
This is **EXACT FINITE ONLY** and is not used to claim universal rank two.

## 5. Bounded rational parameter module

The kernel has exact contiguous identities



$$
F_{h+1,s}(z)=(1-z)^2F_{h,s}(z),\qquad
 F_{h,s+1}(z)=(1+z^2)^2F_{h,s}(z).                           \tag{5.1}
$$



They give rational maps on both four-coordinate endpoint states.  The
$T$-endpoint shifts by $2$ under $h\mapsto h+1$ and by $6$ under
$s\mapsto s+1$; the $E$-endpoint shifts by $4$ in either case.
Hence $Y=(S_T,S_E)$ is a rational difference realization of rank at
most eight.

Here is an exact definition which avoids suppressing any matrix entries.
At an endpoint $N$, let $v_k$ be the row vector expressing
$f_{N+k}$ in the basis $S_N$.  Start with



$$
v_1=e_1^{\mathsf T},\quad v_0=e_2^{\mathsf T},\quad
 v_{-1}=e_3^{\mathsf T},\quad v_{-2}=e_4^{\mathsf T},         \tag{5.2}
$$



and generate $v_k$ for $k\ge2$ from the homogeneous form of (3.4).
For a multiplier $q(z)=\sum_jq_jz^j$ and endpoint shift $\delta$, the
four rows of the shift matrix are



$$
U(N;\delta,q)_{k,*}=\sum_jq_jv_{k-j},\qquad
 k=\delta+1,\delta,\delta-1,\delta-2.                        \tag{5.3}
$$



Direct exact determinant reduction gives



$$
\det U(T;2,(1-z)^2)=
 {16(h+1)(2h+1)\over(h+3s+2)(2h+6s+5)},                     \tag{5.4}
$$





$$
\det U(E;4,(1-z)^2)=
 {8h(h+1)(2h+1)^2\over
 (h+s+2)(2h+2s+3)(4h+4s+5)(4h+4s+7)},                      \tag{5.5}
$$





$$
\det U(T;6,(1+z^2)^2)=
 {256s(s+1)^2(2s-1)(2s+1)^2\over
 (h+3s+2)(h+3s+3)(h+3s+4)
 (2h+6s+5)(2h+6s+7)(2h+6s+9)},                              \tag{5.6}
$$



and



$$
\det U(E;4,(1+z^2)^2)=
 {128(s+1)^2(2s+1)^2\over
 (h+s+2)(2h+2s+3)(4h+4s+5)(4h+4s+7)}.                      \tag{5.7}
$$



Thus the module has fixed finite singular support: only the displayed
linear factors occur.  This is a bounded-rank rational/difference
recognition.  It is not asserted to be a minimal rank-eight module, and
no Frobenius equidistribution theorem is inferred from it.

### Exact determinant-certificate bound

The portable checker verifies (5.4)--(5.7) on a $25\times25$ exact
rational grid.  This is a proof certificate, rather than a numerical fit,
for the following explicit reason.  In (5.2)--(5.3), write



$$
D_k=\prod_{j=2}^{k}(N+j).                                   \tag{5.8}
$$



Induction in (3.4) shows that $D_kv_k$ has total degree at most $k-1$
in $(h,s)$.  For an endpoint shift $\delta\ge3$, clearing one row at a
time gives determinant numerator and denominator degree at most



$$
\sum_{k=\delta-2}^{\delta+1}(k-1)=4\delta-6.                \tag{5.9}
$$



For $\delta=2$, the corresponding bound is $3$.  Cross-multiplying
the claimed determinant adds at most six degrees.  The largest case is
$\delta=6$, so every cleared difference has total degree, and hence
separate $h$- and $s$-degree, at most



$$
(4\cdot6-6)+6=24.                                           \tag{5.10}
$$



A polynomial of degree at most 24 in each variable that vanishes on a
$25\times25$ Cartesian grid is identically zero.  This degree argument
is essential to the proof status of the grid.

## 6. Singular-factor weight

Every numerator factor in (5.4)--(5.7) is a $p$-unit on $h,s\ge1$.
Among the denominator factors, reduction on the actual phase gives poles
only on



$$
h=1,2,3\qquad\text{or}\qquad s=1,2.                         \tag{6.1}
$$



Indeed,



$$
\begin{array}{c|c}
\text{factor divisible by }p&\text{exact actual edge}\\ \hline
2h+6s+5&h=1\\
2h+6s+7&h=2\\
2h+6s+9&h=3\\
4h+4s+5&s=1\\
4h+4s+7&s=2.
\end{array}                                                  \tag{6.2}
$$



All other displayed factors lie strictly between zero and $p$, or
differ from $p$ by a nonzero integer smaller than $p$.  The three
$h$-edges are already inside Item 218's all-prime $h\le8$ strip; the
two $s$-edges contribute at most two prime rows for each $M$.

More generally, a fixed shift window translates the finite list into
finitely many factors



$$
\ell(h,s)=\alpha h+\beta s+\gamma.                          \tag{6.3}
$$



On the fixed-$M$ parametrization



$$
h=3M-2p,\qquad s={3p-4M-1\over2},                           \tag{6.4}
$$



one has the exact congruence



$$
2\ell(h,s)\equiv
 (6\alpha-4\beta)M+(2\gamma-\beta)\pmod p.                 \tag{6.5}
$$



None of the singular-factor slopes is proportional to the phase form
$4h+6s+3$, and translation does not change a slope.  Hence the right
side of (6.5) is not identically zero.  For every fixed window radius
$L$, every exceptional prime divides one of $O_L(1)$ nonzero integers
of size $O_L(M)$.  Therefore



$$
\sum_{p\text{ exceptional in a fixed }L\text{-window}}\log p
 =O_L(\log M)=o(M).                                          \tag{6.6}
$$



This removes only zero normalized mass.

## 7. Why the full gate is not one exact divisor

Let $K$ be the generic coefficient field of the open chart on which
the endpoint transfer is defined and $G_{h,s}$ has rank two.  After a
linear change of state coordinates, its two rows become two independent
linear forms $\ell_0,\ell_1\in K[x_1,x_2,x_3,x_4]$.  The full collision
scheme has ideal



$$
I=(\ell_0,\ell_1),                                          \tag{7.1}
$$



of height two.  It is nonprincipal.  Over an algebraic closure, Krull's
principal ideal theorem also shows that no one nonconstant polynomial can
have exactly this codimension-two zero set: every nonempty polynomial
hypersurface has a codimension-one component.

The scope is important.

* If the reduced rank is one, the gate fiber is one hyperplane; if the
  rank is zero, it is the whole state space.  These rank-drop branches are
  retained and make the event less restrictive.
* Either coordinate, for example $Q_0=0$, is a necessary divisor, but
  it contains a larger one-coordinate locus.  No weighted theorem for
  that locus is presently available.
* The norm $Q_0^2+Q_1^2$ is equivalent to the simultaneous zero on
  $\mathbb F_p$-points only when $p\equiv3\pmod4$.  When
  $p\equiv1\pmod4$, it contains two additional split lines.  The exact
  actual row
  
  

$$
(p,h,s)=(61,10,3),\qquad(Q_0,Q_1)=(46,18),                 \tag{7.2}
$$


  
  has $Q_0^2+Q_1^2=0\pmod{61}$ but is not a collision;
  $Q_1/Q_0=11$ and $11^2=-1\pmod{61}$.
* A finite-field-specific anisotropic norm on the inert rows, a
  determinantal/sheaf description, or a new density theorem for a larger
  necessary divisor is not ruled out.

Accordingly, the positive bounded-module recognition supplies no
one-variable local-root theorem.  A genuine result would need a uniform
weighted incidence or equidistribution theorem for the actual arithmetic
section, with all three ranks and the split/inert branches included.

## 8. Admission and de-overlap

Item 264's raw support is $M/6+o(M)$, or $1/36$ per $6M$.  Item 149
already books the first post-Cartier valuation copy on every row in this
interval.  The present module only rewrites the divided collision gate; it
does not prove an independent higher valuation.

The $O_L(\log M)$ singular-factor set in (6.6) has zero normalized mass.
On its complement, rank-two incidence still leaves a two-dimensional
kernel, and no bound of the form



$$
\sum_{p:\,Q_0=Q_1=0}\log p\le cM+o(M),\qquad c<{1\over6},  \tag{8.1}
$$



is proved.  Hence the master admission test fails quantitatively and the
full raw ceiling (1.4) remains.
