# Item 302 — base-reaching beta composition and its affine elimination ideal

Checked: 2026-08-31 (Beijing time)

## 1. Capacity-first verdict

Let



$$
q_0=q_1=1,\qquad
q_n=(4n-2)q_{n-1}+q_{n-2}\quad(n\ge2).               \tag{1.1}
$$



For $n\ge2$, define



$$
T_n=q_nq_{n-2}-q_{n-1}^2,\qquad
x_n=\frac{T_n}{q_n}.                                  \tag{1.2}
$$



At the base,



$$
q_2=7,\qquad T_2=6,\qquad x_2=\frac67.                \tag{1.3}
$$



The full base-reaching affine composition is exact, but it reconstructs
only the original Turán numerator. Item 302 proves:

1. The complete composition gives the alternating Turán formula
   

$$
T_n=(-1)^{n-2}6
   +4\sum_{j=3}^{n}(-1)^{n-j}q_{j-1}q_{j-2}.
$$


2. The beta denominators are exact continuants, and the endpoint
   centered residue is the least representative of
   $q_{n-1}^2\bmod q_n$.
3. The integral ideal generated by the base equation and every affine
   update has endpoint elimination ideal
   

$$
\langle q_nX_n-T_n\rangle.
$$


   Hence every endpoint polynomial identity generated by those affine
   relations is a multiple of the original endpoint relation.
4. The actual numerator $T_n$ is a unit modulo every divisor
   $Q\mid q_n$, and the centered endpoint is a square unit modulo
   $Q$. This gives only the lower bound $1$.

The exact scoped conclusion is:

> **BASE-REACHING AFFINE-ELIMINATION NO-GO.**
> Full composition from the fixed base, with arbitrary polynomial
> elimination of the affine update equations, produces no independent
> endpoint identity beyond $q_nX_n-T_n=0$.

This is not a no-go for all linear-depth methods. Nearest-integer
digits, Ostrowski restrictions, modular-square distribution,
inequalities, and nonlinear arithmetic invariants are not contained in
the declared affine ideal.

No half-bound or proper-$Q$ upper construction is proved. A full
$q_n$ lower bound would not transfer to a proper de-overlapped target,
and Item 282's product baseline remains separate. Therefore



$$
\boxed{
\text{new Route-1 rate}=0,\qquad
\text{new beta capacity reduction}=0.}                \tag{1.4}
$$



## 2. Exact full composition

For $j\ge3$, put



$$
F_j(z)
=\frac{q_{j-1}}{q_j}\bigl(4q_{j-2}-z\bigr).           \tag{2.1}
$$



The Turán recurrence is



$$
\boxed{
T_j+T_{j-1}=4q_{j-1}q_{j-2}.}                        \tag{2.2}
$$



Dividing by $q_j$ gives



$$
\boxed{x_j=F_j(x_{j-1}).}                             \tag{2.3}
$$



The derivative magnitudes telescope:



$$
\prod_{j=3}^{n}\frac{q_{j-1}}{q_j}
=\frac{q_2}{q_n}
=\frac7{q_n}.                                         \tag{2.4}
$$



Therefore the exact base-reaching composition is



$$
\boxed{
x_n
=
\frac{
(-1)^{n-2}6
+4\displaystyle\sum_{j=3}^{n}
(-1)^{n-j}q_{j-1}q_{j-2}
}{q_n}.}                                              \tag{2.5}
$$



Equivalently,



$$
\boxed{
T_n=(-1)^{n-2}6
+4\sum_{j=3}^{n}(-1)^{n-j}q_{j-1}q_{j-2}.}           \tag{2.6}
$$



This follows either by composing (2.1), or by induction from (2.2).
Substituting (1.2) into (2.2) verifies that (2.6) is exactly



$$
T_n=q_nq_{n-2}-q_{n-1}^2,                             \tag{2.7}
$$



not a second numerator formula with independent arithmetic content.

## 3. Continuant and continued-fraction coordinates

Use the continuant convention



$$
K(\varnothing)=1,\qquad
K(z_1)=z_1,
$$



and



$$
K(z_1,\ldots,z_m)
=z_mK(z_1,\ldots,z_{m-1})
+K(z_1,\ldots,z_{m-2}).                               \tag{3.1}
$$



For $n\ge2$,



$$
\boxed{
q_n=K(7,10,14,\ldots,4n-2),}                         \tag{3.2}
$$



where the word is just $(7)$ at $n=2$. For $n\ge3$,



$$
\boxed{
\frac{q_n}{q_{n-1}}
=[\,4n-2;\,4n-6,\,4n-10,\ldots,10,7\,].}             \tag{3.3}
$$



Thus



$$
\frac{q_{n-1}}{q_n}
=[\,0;\,4n-2,\,4n-6,\ldots,10,7\,].                  \tag{3.4}
$$



Writing



$$
b=q_n,\qquad a=q_{n-1},\qquad c=q_{n-2},
$$



one has



$$
x_n=c-\frac{a^2}{b}.                                  \tag{3.5}
$$



Hence



$$
\|x_n\|_{\mathbb Z}
=\left\|\frac{a^2}{b}\right\|_{\mathbb Z}.            \tag{3.6}
$$



If



$$
t_n=\operatorname{nint}(x_n),\qquad
r_n=bt_n-T_n,
$$



then



$$
\boxed{
r_n\equiv a^2\pmod b.}                                \tag{3.7}
$$



Consequently the desired half-bound is exactly a lower bound for the
least absolute representative of the modular square $a^2\bmod b$.
The full continued-fraction word identifies the modulus and multiplier,
but standard real convergent identities do not determine that centered
square phase.

There is also an exact inverse coordinate. For $n\ge3$, let



$$
S_n=K(10,14,\ldots,4n-2).                             \tag{3.8}
$$



Using



$$
M(z)=
\begin{pmatrix}
z&1\\
1&0
\end{pmatrix},
$$



the product



$$
M(7)M(10)M(14)\cdots M(4n-2)
$$



has first row $(q_n,q_{n-1})$, lower-left entry $S_n$, and
determinant $(-1)^{n-1}$. Therefore



$$
\boxed{
q_{n-1}S_n\equiv(-1)^n\pmod{q_n}.}                   \tag{3.9}
$$



Combining (3.7) and (3.9),



$$
\boxed{
r_nS_n^2\equiv1\pmod{q_n}.}                           \tag{3.10}
$$



This square-inverse congruence is exact, but it does not bound the least
absolute representative $r_n$.

## 4. The integral affine ideal

Fix $n\ge2$, and work in



$$
\mathbb Z[X_2,X_3,\ldots,X_n].                        \tag{4.1}
$$



Define the base relation



$$
E_2=7X_2-6,                                           \tag{4.2}
$$



and, for $3\le j\le n$, the affine update relations



$$
R_j
=q_jX_j+q_{j-1}X_{j-1}
-4q_{j-1}q_{j-2}.                                    \tag{4.3}
$$



Let



$$
\mathcal I_n
=\langle E_2,R_3,\ldots,R_n\rangle.                  \tag{4.4}
$$



For every $j$, put



$$
E_j=q_jX_j-T_j.                                       \tag{4.5}
$$



The endpoint relation belongs to the affine ideal by an exact
induction. The base is (4.2), and (2.2) gives



$$
\begin{aligned}
R_j-E_{j-1}
&=q_jX_j
-4q_{j-1}q_{j-2}
+T_{j-1}\\
&=q_jX_j-T_j\\
&=E_j.
\end{aligned}                                         \tag{4.6}
$$



Thus



$$
\boxed{q_nX_n-T_n\in\mathcal I_n.}                   \tag{4.7}
$$



## 5. Exact endpoint elimination

Extend scalars to $\mathbb Q$. The triangular system



$$
E_2=R_3=\cdots=R_n=0                                  \tag{5.1}
$$



has the unique solution



$$
X_j=\frac{T_j}{q_j}\qquad(2\le j\le n).               \tag{5.2}
$$



Therefore its rational endpoint elimination ideal is



$$
\mathcal I_n\mathbb Q[X_2,\ldots,X_n]
\cap\mathbb Q[X_n]
=
\left\langle
X_n-\frac{T_n}{q_n}
\right\rangle.                                        \tag{5.3}
$$



The endpoint polynomial is primitive over $\mathbb Z$. Indeed,



$$
\gcd(T_n,q_n)
=\gcd(q_nq_{n-2}-q_{n-1}^2,q_n)
=\gcd(q_{n-1}^2,q_n)
=1.                                                   \tag{5.4}
$$



Now take



$$
P(X_n)\in\mathcal I_n\cap\mathbb Z[X_n].              \tag{5.5}
$$



Equation (5.3) shows that $q_nX_n-T_n$ divides $P$ in
$\mathbb Q[X_n]$. Since $q_nX_n-T_n$ is primitive, Gauss's lemma
shows that it divides $P$ in $\mathbb Z[X_n]$. The reverse
containment follows from (4.7). Hence



$$
\boxed{
\mathcal I_n\cap\mathbb Z[X_n]
=\langle q_nX_n-T_n\rangle.}                          \tag{5.6}
$$



This is the exact base-reaching affine-elimination theorem. Polynomial
combinations of every affine update and the exact base produce no
independent endpoint polynomial relation.

## 6. The unit and square congruences

Let $Q>1$ divide $q_n$. From (5.4),



$$
\boxed{\gcd(T_n,Q)=1.}                                \tag{6.1}
$$



The centered residue modulo $Q$ is



$$
r_{n,Q}=\operatorname{cent}_Q(-T_n).                  \tag{6.2}
$$



Equations (3.7) and (5.4) imply



$$
\boxed{
r_{n,Q}\equiv q_{n-1}^2\pmod Q,\qquad
\gcd(r_{n,Q},Q)=1.}                                   \tag{6.3}
$$



Thus



$$
\boxed{|r_{n,Q}|\ge1.}                                \tag{6.4}
$$



This is a genuine all-$n$, all-divisor nonvanishing statement, but
its logarithmic height is zero on the $n\log n$ scale. It supplies no
positive-linear capacity reduction and no proper-$Q$ upper
construction.

For $Q\mid q_n$, (3.10) also reduces to



$$
r_{n,Q}S_n^2\equiv1\pmod Q.                           \tag{6.5}
$$



Again, this fixes a unit square class, not the size of its centered
representative.

## 7. Exact scope and the missing datum

The theorem in (5.6) applies to the **base-reaching
affine-elimination class**:

* the exact base relation $7X_2-6$;
* all exact affine update relations $R_j$;
* arbitrary polynomial combinations and elimination over
  $\mathbb Z$ or $\mathbb Q$;
* no nearest-integer digit, inequality, modular-order hypothesis, or
  extra seed arithmetic beyond those equations.

Within this class, the full composition merely reconstructs $T_n$.

The smallest missing arithmetic datum is a restriction on the centered
representative of



$$
q_{n-1}^2\bmod q_n,                                   \tag{7.1}
$$



equivalently on the final Ostrowski/nearest-integer digit. Primitivity
and the square-inverse congruence do not supply the required magnitude.

The following remain outside the no-go and remain open:

* a modular-square lower bound specific to the arithmetic-progression
  continuant word;
* a nonlinear Ostrowski digit restriction;
* a monotone inequality using signs or order, not just affine
  elimination;
* another linear-depth method carrying extra arithmetic state;
* an actual proper-$Q$ upper construction.

No claim is made that all base-reaching or all linear-depth approaches
are impossible.

## 8. Proper targets and admission

For a proper de-overlapped $Q\mid q_n$, (6.4) is the only new size
consequence:



$$
1\le|r_{n,Q}|\le\frac{Q-1}{2}.                        \tag{8.1}
$$



It neither proves a factorial lower bound nor constructs an
$O(n)$-height upper lift. Item 282's common coefficient/product
baseline remains separate.

Thus Item 302 proves no retained-capacity ceiling and no positive
weighted mass. Booking is zero.
