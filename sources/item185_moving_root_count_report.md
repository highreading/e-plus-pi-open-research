# Item 185 — moving determinant roots: rational maps, a sign theorem, and the unresolved sublinear bound

Checked: 2026-08-29 (Beijing time)

## Status and outcome

For a prime $p\ge7$, let



$$
r_p=\#\left\{0\le s\le\frac{p-1}{3}:\Delta_{p,s}=0\right\},
$$



where $\Delta_{p,s}$ is the moving $\kappa=0$ determinant of Items 174
and 180. The central target was a proof that $r_p=o(p)$.

> **OPEN — central target.** No sublinear root-count theorem is proved. In
> particular, neither $O(1)$ nor $O(p^\theta)$, $\theta<1$, is claimed.

Two all-degree structural results were obtained.

> **PROVED — fixed rational-map reformulation.** Every moving coefficient in
> the determinant is a fixed-level coefficient of a power of one of two fixed
> rational maps $H$ and $K=x^3H$. The entire root problem is therefore a
> coefficient determinant of two rational-power families; see (2.5)--(2.7).

> **PROVED — complete real-sign theorem in the positive-lift sector.** Put
> $b=p-5s-2$. If $b\ge1$, the lifted integer determinant $E_{p,s}$ is
> strictly negative. At $b=0$, it is zero exactly when
> $s\equiv1\pmod4$, and is positive when $s\equiv3\pmod4$. Thus the only
> *integer* zero in this sector is the known structural boundary ray. This
> does not prevent nonzero negative integers from vanishing modulo $p$, as
> the four Item180 witnesses already demonstrate.

The normalizations suggested by the structural rays and the near-$p$
hypergeometric coefficients were tested exactly. They do not reveal a
bounded-degree dependence on $s$: after structural linear factors,
factorial/gamma scaling, and endpoint-pivot normalization, the finite
interpolation complexity still grows linearly with $p$.

> **PROVED COMPUTATION — extended finite census.** For all 300 primes
> $7\le p\le2000$, the exact recurrence finds 322 roots among 92,496 pairs.
> The maximum is five roots, attained at $p=1471$. This remains finite
> evidence and is not extrapolated.

Nothing in this item decides the arithmetic nature of $e+\pi$.

## 1. Starting determinant

Write



$$
A_s(x)=\frac{(1-x)^{5s+2}}{(1-x^4)^{2s+2}}
       =\sum_{n\ge0}a_n(s)x^n,
 \qquad d=p-3s.
\tag{1.1}
$$



Item180 proved



$$
\Delta_{p,s}=(-1)^s\left[
 (a_{d-2}+a_{d-3}+a_{d-4})a_{p-5}
 -(a_{p-4}+a_{p-3}+a_{p-2})a_{d-1}
 \right]\pmod p.
\tag{1.2}
$$



All coefficients needed in (1.2) lie below $p$ and are generated by



$$
(n+1)a_{n+1}=(n-5s-2)a_n+(n+8s+5)a_{n-3}
 -(n+3s+2)a_{n-4}.
\tag{1.3}
$$



## 2. Fixed rational-map formulation

Factor the dependence on $s$:



$$
A_s=A_0H^s,
 \qquad
 A_0=\frac{(1-x)^2}{(1-x^4)^2},
 \qquad
 H=\frac{(1-x)^5}{(1-x^4)^2},
 \qquad K=x^3H.
\tag{2.1}
$$



For fixed $p$, define



$$
U_r(s)=[x^{p-r}]A_0K^s,
 \qquad
 V_r(s)=[x^{p-r}]A_0H^s.
\tag{2.2}
$$



Since multiplication by $x^{3s}$ shifts the coefficient index,



$$
a_{p-3s-r}(s)=U_r(s),
 \qquad a_{p-r}(s)=V_r(s).
\tag{2.3}
$$



Consequently



$$
(-1)^s\Delta_{p,s}=
 (U_2+U_3+U_4)V_5-(V_4+V_3+V_2)U_1.
\tag{2.4}
$$



The ordinary generating functions in the moving parameter are exact
coefficient extractions of bivariate rational functions:



$$
\sum_{s\ge0}U_r(s)y^s
 =[x^{p-r}]\frac{A_0}{1-yK},
 \qquad
 \sum_{s\ge0}V_r(s)y^s
 =[x^{p-r}]\frac{A_0}{1-yH}.
\tag{2.5}
$$



Equations (2.4)--(2.5) are the promised fixed-map reformulation. They provide
a precise entry point for creative telescoping, Picard--Fuchs recurrences, or
finite-field trace methods. The obstruction is that the extracted coefficient
level $p-r$ moves with the characteristic. A recurrence whose order or
conductor grows with that level gives no sublinear zero bound.

## 3. Hypergeometric complement formula

Let $u=5s+2$, $c=2s+2$, and write $n=4q+r$,
$0\le r\le3$. Direct convolution gives



$$
a_{4q+r}(s)=(-1)^r
 \sum_{\substack{0\le h\le q\\r+4h\le u}}
 \binom{u}{r+4h}
 \binom{c+q-h-1}{c-1}.
\tag{3.1}
$$



This is an exact terminating hypergeometric/binomial description of every
near-$p$ and moving-index coefficient. In the admissible range,
$c-1=2s+1<p$, so the gamma denominator $(2s+1)!$ is a unit modulo $p$.
The most immediate gamma normalization of the determinant is therefore
multiplication by $((2s+1)!)^2$; division by the same nonzero factor is also
available.

Formula (3.1) does not terminate after a bounded number of summands when
$s\asymp p$. Exact tests below show that neither gamma normalization reduces
the interpolation degree. Thus the complement formula is useful structure,
but not by itself a root-count theorem.

## 4. Complete sign theorem for the positive lift

Assume $b=p-5s-2\ge0$, put $c=2s+2$, and write



$$
G(x)=\frac1{(1-x)^b(1-x^4)^c}=\sum_{n\ge0}g_nx^n.
\tag{4.1}
$$



The lifted determinant is



$$
E_{p,s}=(g_{d-2}+g_{d-3}+g_{d-4})g_{p-5}
 -(g_{p-4}+g_{p-3}+g_{p-2})g_{d-1}.
\tag{4.2}
$$



### Theorem 1

For every admissible prime pair in this sector:



$$
\begin{cases}
 E_{p,s}<0,&b\ge1,\\
 E_{p,s}=0,&b=0,\ s\equiv1\pmod4,\\
 E_{p,s}>0,&b=0,\ s\equiv3\pmod4.
 \end{cases}
\tag{4.3}
$$



#### Proof

If $b\ge1$,



$$
(1-x)G=(1-x)^{-(b-1)}(1-x^4)^{-c}
\tag{4.4}
$$



has nonnegative coefficients. Hence $g_n$ is nondecreasing. If $b\ge2$,
the coefficients in (4.4) are positive at every index, so $g_n$ is strictly
increasing. The first parenthesis of (4.2) is then strictly less than
$3g_{d-1}$, while the second is strictly greater than $3g_{p-5}$, proving
negativity.

If $b=1$, the successive difference $g_n-g_{n-1}$ is positive exactly
when $4\mid n$. Here $p=5s+3$, and primality forces $s$ even and
$s\ge2$. Since $d-1=2s+2\equiv2\pmod4$, the first three-term comparison
is strict, again giving (4.3).

Finally let $b=0$. Then $p=5s+2$, so $s$ is odd, and $g_n>0$ exactly
when $4\mid n$. Now



$$
d-1=2s+1,
 \qquad p-5=5s-3.
\tag{4.5}
$$



Checking these two residues modulo four in (4.2) gives zero for
$s\equiv1\pmod4$ and a positive first product for
$s\equiv3\pmod4$. $\square$

The theorem closes the real-sign question, but not the finite-field question:
$p\mid E_{p,s}$ may hold even when $E_{p,s}<0$. It therefore sharpens the
archimedean obstruction of Item180 rather than proving $r_p=o(p)$.

## 5. Exact normalization tests

For consecutive admissible nodes $s=0,\ldots,\lfloor(p-1)/3\rfloor$, let
$D_p(S)\in\mathbf F_p[S]$ be the unique interpolation polynomial for
$\Delta_{p,s}$. The following exact tests were made:

1. raw $D_p$;
2. multiplication and division by $((2s+1)!)^2$;
3. exact removal of every admissible structural factor arising from
   $p=3s+4$, $p=5s+2$, or $p=5s+1$, with the required congruence;
4. on nodes where the pivots are nonzero, the scale-free ratio
   

$$
\mathcal N_s=
   \frac{a_{d-2}+a_{d-3}+a_{d-4}}{a_{d-1}}
   -\frac{a_{p-4}+a_{p-3}+a_{p-2}}{a_{p-5}}.
   \tag{5.1}
$$



For (5.1), a rational representation $P(s)/Q(s)$ with
$\deg P,\deg Q\le D$ would give a null vector in an exact interpolation
matrix. Full column rank therefore certifies that no such representation
exists.

| 101 | 34  | 33  | 0 | 33  | 33  | 15  |
| 211 | 71  | 70  | 2 | 68  | 70  | 34  |
| 337 | 113 | 112 | 1 | 111 | 111 | 54  |

For every row, both factorial-scaled interpolation degrees also equal the raw
maximum, `nodes - 1`. The rational exclusions are essentially the largest
degrees that the number of available nodes can rule out. Thus these natural
normalizations exhibit maximal finite interpolation complexity, not bounded
degree. This is exact finite evidence, not an all-prime impossibility theorem.

## 6. Extended finite root census

The deterministic recurrence scan through $p\le2000$ gives



$$
\begin{array}{l|r}
 \text{primes}&300\\
 \text{admissible pairs}&92{,}496\\
 \text{zero pairs}&322\\
 \text{primes with a root}&221\\
 \text{off-known-ray roots}&96.
 \end{array}
\tag{6.1}
$$



The root-count histogram is



$$
\begin{array}{c|rrrrrr}
 r_p&0&1&2&3&4&5\\ \hline
 \#p&79&144&60&11&5&1.
 \end{array}
\tag{6.2}
$$



The unique five-root prime in this range is



$$
p=1471,\qquad s=61,246,294,306,489.
\tag{6.3}
$$



The last root is on $p=3s+4$, the middle structural root $s=294$ is on
$p=5s+1$, and the other three are off the known rays. The increase from the
Item180 maximum four to five is a useful warning against conjecturing a bound
from a short scan. Nevertheless, $5/1471$ remains tiny; the census is
consistent with sublinearity but cannot prove it.

## 7. What remains

**PROVED**

- the fixed rational-map and bivariate generating-function identities;
- the terminating hypergeometric coefficient formula;
- the complete positive-sector real-sign theorem.

**PROVED COMPUTATION / FINITE ONLY**

- maximal interpolation complexity for the displayed normalization tests;
- the complete $p\le2000$ census and five-root example.

**OPEN**

- any uniform bound $r_p=o(p)$;
- a bounded-order recurrence whose zero set can be controlled uniformly in
  the moving coefficient level;
- a bounded-conductor trace-function interpretation permitting square-root
  cancellation;
- a $p$-adic argument controlling how often the nonzero integers in (4.3)
  are divisible by their defining prime.
