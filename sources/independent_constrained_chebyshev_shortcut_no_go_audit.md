## 1. The five-dimensional space

For $n=1$, write

$$
A=a_0+a_1x,qquad
B=b+(x-1)\widetilde b,qquad
C=b+(x-1)\widetilde c.
$$

The latter two expressions are the unique degree-at-most-one
parametrization of $B(1)=C(1)=b$.  Therefore

$$
A+Be^x+CG
=a_0+a_1x+b(e^x+G)
+\widetilde b(x-1)e^x+\widetilde c(x-1)G,
$$

which gives exactly the five functions in (1).  The nonzero Wronskian at
$x=0$ computed below proves that these spanning functions are linearly
independent, so “basis” and dimension five are justified without any
additional functional-independence assumption.

## 2. Independent endpoint determinant calculation

Put $g_r=G^{(r)}(a)$ and $E=e^a$.  At an arbitrary endpoint $a$, the
derivative columns through order four can be generated by

$$
f_3^{(r)}(a)=E+g_r,qquad
f_4^{(r)}(a)=E(a-1+r),
$$

and

$$
f_5^{(r)}(a)=(a-1)g_r+r g_{r-1},
$$

where the final term is omitted for $r=0$.  The first two columns are the
jets of $1$ and $x$.  For $r\ge1$, the required derivatives of $G$ come
from

$$
G'(x)=\frac{80}{25+x^2}-\frac{956}{57121+x^2}.
$$

I formed the resulting $5\times5$ matrix over exact rationals, treating
$e$ and the undifferentiated endpoint value of $G$ as independent symbols.
At $x=0$, with $e^0=1$ and $G(0)=0$, its determinant is exactly

$$
W(0)=
-\frac{935059672726675656}{2912107693477515625}.
$$

At $x=1$, the symbolic undifferentiated $G(1)$ cancels from the determinant,
so no use of a numerical approximation to the Machin identity is hidden.
The exact result is

$$
W(1)=
\frac{72e}{542800770374370512771595361}
\left(
50807650153010910313192727e
-55199386641542198623868748
\right),
$$

matching (4) coefficient for coefficient.

The first determinant is negative.  For the second, let the two large
positive coefficients in the bracket be $A$ and $B$, respectively.  Exact
integer subtraction gives

$$
2A-B=46415913664479622002516706>0.
$$

Hence $B/A<2$, while the positive exponential series gives $e>2$.  Thus
$Ae-B>0$ and $W(1)>0$.  All five functions are analytic on a neighborhood
of $[0,1]$, so their Wronskian is continuous.  The intermediate value
theorem therefore supplies an interior point $\xi$ with $W(\xi)=0$.

## 3. Exact extended-Chebyshev implication

If another basis is obtained from $(f_1,\ldots,f_5)$ by a constant
invertible matrix $M$, its jet matrix is the old jet matrix multiplied on
the right by $M$.  Its full Wronskian is consequently

$$
W_{\mathrm{new}}(x)=W_{\mathrm{old}}(x)\det M.
$$

Thus the zero set of the full Wronskian, and the fact that the two endpoint
signs are opposite, are basis-independent.

At the interior zero $\xi$, singularity of the jet matrix gives a nonzero
coefficient vector whose linear combination $f$ satisfies

$$
f(\xi)=f'(\xi)=\cdots=f^{(4)}(\xi)=0.
$$

Because the five functions form a basis, this $f$ is not the zero
function.  It has a zero of multiplicity at least five.  A
five-dimensional extended Chebyshev space permits at most four zeros,
counted with multiplicity, for any nonzero element.  The constrained space
therefore cannot be an extended Chebyshev space.  This is a necessary
Wronskian obstruction only; no converse or stronger zero-counting claim is
being used.
