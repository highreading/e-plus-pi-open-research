# A mixed $E/G$ division obstruction for $e+\pi$

Date: 2026-08-27 UTC

This note records an exact obstruction to a tempting mixed-arithmetic-Gevrey
route to the arithmetic nature of $e+\pi$.  It does **not** classify
$e+\pi$.  All assertions below are unconditional except those explicitly
introduced by the hypothesis



$$
\alpha=e+\pi\in\overline{\mathbb Q}.
$$



Let $\mathcal E$ denote the class of Siegel $E$-functions and let
$\mathcal G$ denote the class of germs at the origin of $G$-functions.
Both are understood to have algebraic Taylor coefficients.  We use the
standard fact



$$
\mathcal E\cap\mathcal G=\overline{\mathbb Q}[z].             \tag{1}
$$



Indeed, an $E$-function is entire, whereas an entire $G$-function is a
polynomial.  This is a function-class statement, not the open value-ring
conjecture $\mathbf E\cap\mathbf G=\overline{\mathbb Q}$.

## 1. An explicit two-slope zero

For $\alpha\in\overline{\mathbb Q}$, put



$$
H_\alpha(z)=\alpha-e^z-4\arctan z,
 \qquad
 Q_\alpha(z)=\frac{H_\alpha(z)}{z-1}.                         \tag{2}
$$



Here $\arctan z$ means its germ at the origin, analytically continued to a
neighbourhood of $z=1$.  We have



$$
H_\alpha(1)=\alpha-e-\pi.                                    \tag{3}
$$



Consequently,



$$
Q_\alpha\text{ is holomorphic at }1
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \alpha=e+\pi.                                                 \tag{4}
$$



As a formal power series at the origin,



$$
Q_\alpha(z)=\sum_{n\geq0}q_n(\alpha)z^n,
$$



where



$$
q_n(\alpha)
 =-\alpha+\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac1{k!}
 +4\sum_{0\leq m\leq (n-1)/2}\frac{(-1)^m}{2m+1}.             \tag{5}
$$



Thus $Q_\alpha$ is a holonomic power series over
$\overline{\mathbb Q}$ for every algebraic $\alpha$.  Formula (5) also
shows directly that $q_n(\alpha)\to e+\pi-\alpha$.

## 2. The all-parameter division obstruction

### Theorem 1

For every $\alpha\in\overline{\mathbb Q}$,



$$
\boxed{Q_\alpha\notin\mathcal E+\mathcal G.}                 \tag{6}
$$



In particular, if $e+\pi$ were algebraic, then $H_{e+\pi}$ would be an
explicit sum of an $E$-function and a $G$-function, with a zero at the
algebraic point $1$, for which division by $z-1$ leaves the class
$\mathcal E+\mathcal G$.  Hence there is no general André--Beukers
zero-removal theorem for this two-slope class.

### Proof

Suppose that



$$
Q_\alpha=E+G,
 \qquad E\in\mathcal E,\quad G\in\mathcal G.                  \tag{7}
$$



Multiplying (7) by $z-1$ and comparing with the displayed decomposition



$$
H_\alpha=(\alpha-e^z)-4\arctan z
$$



gives



$$
R(z):=(\alpha-e^z)-(z-1)E(z)
       =(z-1)G(z)+4\arctan z.                                 \tag{8}
$$



The left side of (8) is an $E$-function, and the right side is a
$G$-function.  By (1), $R\in\overline{\mathbb Q}[z]$.  Evaluating the
left side at $z=1$ yields



$$
R(1)=\alpha-e.                                                \tag{9}
$$



But $R(1)$ is algebraic, whereas $\alpha-e$ is transcendental by
Hermite's theorem because $\alpha$ is algebraic and $e$ is
transcendental.  This contradiction proves (6). $\square$

This argument is deliberately about classes of **functions**.  It does not
prove the conjectural intersection statement for their **value rings**.

## 3. The differential operator cannot see the target zero

Set



$$
r(z)=\frac1{1+z^2},
$$



and define the rational functions



$$
A(z)=-\frac{(z-1)(z^2-3)}{(z+1)(z^2+1)},
 \qquad
 B(z)=-\frac{2(z^2+2z-1)}{(z+1)(z^2+1)}.                      \tag{10}
$$



The order-three operator



$$
L=D^3+A(z)D^2+B(z)D                                           \tag{11}
$$



annihilates each of $1,e^z,\arctan z$.  Indeed,



$$
1+A+B=0,
 \qquad
 r''+Ar'+Br=0.                                                 \tag{12}
$$



### Theorem 2

For every complex $\alpha$, the minimal order over $\mathbb C(z)$ of a
homogeneous differential operator annihilating $H_\alpha$ is three, and
the operator (11) is independent of $\alpha$.  The minimal operator of
$Q_\alpha$ is the gauge transform



$$
\widetilde L=L\circ M_{z-1},
 \qquad
 (\widetilde L y)(z)=L((z-1)y(z)),                             \tag{13}
$$



and is likewise independent of $\alpha$.

### Proof

The $\mathbb C(z)$-differential module generated by
$1,e^z,\arctan z$ has the indicated basis, since these three functions are
linearly independent over $\mathbb C(z)$.  More concretely, the
coefficient matrix of $H_\alpha,H_\alpha',H_\alpha''$ in that basis is



$$
\begin{pmatrix}
   \alpha&-1&-4\\
   -4r&-1&0\\
   -4r'&-1&0
 \end{pmatrix},
$$



whose determinant is



$$
-\frac{16(z+1)^2}{(z^2+1)^2}\ne0.                           \tag{14}
$$



Thus the cyclic differential span has dimension three for every $\alpha$,
so no operator of lower order can annihilate $H_\alpha$.  Equations
(11)--(12) provide an operator of order three.  Multiplication by the nonzero
rational function $z-1$ is a gauge equivalence, proving the statement for
$Q_\alpha$. $\square$

The leading coefficient in (13), when (11) is written monically, is
$z-1$.  Hence $z=1$ is present in the scalar operator for every
$\alpha$.  Whether the particular cyclic solution $Q_\alpha$ has a pole
there or is holomorphic there is exactly the numerical connection condition
(3), and none of the operator, its order, its slopes, or its differential
Galois module changes with $\alpha$.

This proves a precise no-go statement: differential-module invariants alone
cannot distinguish the desired value $\alpha=e+\pi$ inside this family.

## 4. An equivalent finite-limit formulation

Let



$$
\operatorname{Si}(z)=\int_0^z\frac{\sin t}{t}\,dt,
 \qquad
 F_\alpha(z)=\alpha(1-e^{-z})-2\operatorname{Si}(z).           \tag{15}
$$



The function $F_\alpha$ is an $E$-function for algebraic $\alpha$, and



$$
\lim_{x\to+\infty}F_\alpha(x)=\alpha-\pi.                    \tag{16}
$$



Therefore



$$
\alpha=e+\pi
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \lim_{x\to+\infty}F_\alpha(x)=e.                            \tag{17}
$$



Fischler--Rivoal prove that finite directional limits at infinity of
$E$-functions are precisely values of $G$-functions.  In the present
case (17) is just the explicit witness that the algebraicity hypothesis would
put the $E$-value $e$ into the $G$-value ring.  There is no theorem
separating these value rings: the conjecture



$$
\mathbf E\cap\mathbf G=\overline{\mathbb Q}                  \tag{18}
$$



is explicitly described by Fischler--Rivoal as currently out of reach.

## 5. Period-theoretic location of the same obstruction

If $\alpha=e+\pi$ were algebraic, then $e=\alpha-\pi$ would be an
ordinary period (indeed a $1$-period).  No unconditional theorem excludes
this: it is presently unknown whether $e$ is an ordinary period at all.
The ordinary Kontsevich--Zagier period conjecture does not remember that this
same complex number is also $\exp(1)$.

Fresán--Jossen's **exponential** period conjecture adds exactly that missing
comparison.  Their Proposition 12.1.4 states, conditional on the conjecture,
that $e^\beta$, for nonzero algebraic $\beta$, is transcendental over the
field generated by ordinary periods.  With $\beta=1$, this would contradict
$e=\alpha-\pi$, and hence would prove that $e+\pi$ is transcendental.
The proposition is conditional; the construction and comparison isomorphism
for exponential motives do not prove injectivity of the numerical period map.

The failure can be located in a rank-two object.  Let



$$
M=E(1)\oplus\mathbb Q(-1),                                  \tag{19}
$$



where $E(1)$ is the one-dimensional exponential motive with period $e$,
and $\mathbb Q(-1)$ is the Tate motive with period $2\pi i$.  Its motivic
Galois group is



$$
G_M\simeq\mathbb G_m^2.                                     \tag{20}
$$



One way to see that the two factors do not merge is to use the perverse
realisation: it detects the nontrivial character of $E(1)$ and is trivial on
usual motives; the remaining Tate character is nontrivial by its Hodge
realisation.  Thus no nonzero tensor power of $E(1)$ can equal a tensor
power of $\mathbb Q(-1)$.

Individually, the rank-one period structures of $E(1)$ and
$\mathbb Q(-1)$ are normal, by the known transcendence of $e$ and $\pi$.
To pass from this assertion to the direct sum, one must also compute the
Galois group $G_P$ of its **period structure**; comparison with the motivic
group $G_M$ alone would not prove failure of normality.

For two one-dimensional invertible period structures with periods $e$ and
$2\pi i$, the character lattice of $G_P$ is $\mathbb Z^2/\Lambda$, where



$$
\Lambda=\{(a,b)\in\mathbb Z^2:
              e^a(2\pi i)^b\in\overline{\mathbb Q}^{\times}\}.             \tag{21}
$$



Under the hypothesis $\pi=\alpha-e$, this relation lattice is zero.  Indeed,
suppose that



$$
e^a(2\pi i)^b=c,
 \qquad c\in\overline{\mathbb Q}^{\times}.                    \tag{22}
$$



If $a=0$ or $b=0$, the transcendence of $\pi$ or $e$, respectively,
already forces $a=b=0$.  In general, absorb the nonzero algebraic factor
$(2i)^b$ into $c$, put $x=e$, and use $\pi=\alpha-x$.  Equation (22)
becomes



$$
x^a(\alpha-x)^b=c',\qquad c'\in\overline{\mathbb Q}^{\times}.             \tag{23}
$$



Negative exponents cause no problem: with
$a_+=\max(a,0)$, $a_-=-\min(a,0)$, and similarly for $b$, clearing
denominators in (23) gives the polynomial equation



$$
x^{a_+}(\alpha-x)^{b_+}
 -c'x^{a_-}(\alpha-x)^{b_-}=0.                              \tag{24}
$$



For $(a,b)\ne(0,0)$, the polynomial in (24) is not identically zero.  If it
were, the rational function $x^a(\alpha-x)^b$ would be constant, so its
logarithmic derivative



$$
\frac{a}{x}-\frac{b}{\alpha-x}
 =\frac{a\alpha-(a+b)x}{x(\alpha-x)}                         \tag{25}
$$



would vanish identically.  Since $\alpha=e+\pi>0$, this would imply
$a+b=0$ and $a\alpha=0$, hence $a=b=0$, a contradiction.  Thus (24)
would make $e$ algebraic, contrary to Hermite's theorem.  We conclude that
$\Lambda=0$ and therefore



$$
G_P\simeq\mathbb G_m^2.                                    \tag{26}
$$



On the other hand, if $\alpha=e+\pi$ were algebraic, the period algebra of
the direct sum would have transcendence degree one:



$$
\operatorname{trdeg}_{\overline{\mathbb Q}}
   \overline{\mathbb Q}(e,2\pi i)
   =1<2=\dim G_P=\dim G_M.                                   \tag{27}
$$



Thus the hypothesis would make (19) an explicit failure of normality for the
**direct sum** of two normal rank-one period structures.  Fresán--Jossen point
out abstractly that normality need not be stable under direct sums.  Equation
(27) shows that proving normality for this particular direct sum would already
exclude the target algebraicity hypothesis.  The stronger conjunction of
normality with the formal equality $G_P=G_M$, as predicted by the full
exponential period conjecture, would give the algebraic independence of $e$
and $\pi$.

Commelin--Habegger--Huber prove that several definitions of exponential
periods agree and realize their real and imaginary parts as volumes of sets
definable in an o-minimal structure.  Those comparison and definability
theorems establish membership in a common ring, not separation from ordinary
periods, and therefore do not decide (3).

The 2026 construction of $E$-periods by Snodgrass similarly enlarges the
period framework so that values of $E$-functions and exponential periods
can be represented together.  It does not prove a period-conjecture
injectivity or a numerical nonvanishing theorem for their mixed relations.

## Conclusion

The mixed $E/G$ quotient (2) gives a corrected, exact formulation of the
tempting zero-removal route, but Theorems 1--2 show why existing purity and
differential-operator results do not settle it.  Under the target
algebraicity hypothesis, the required cancellation is a numerical connection
constant invisible to the common differential operator and lying precisely
at the conjectural $E$-value/$G$-value and ordinary-period/exponential-
period separation frontier.
