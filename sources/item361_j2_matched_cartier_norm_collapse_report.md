# Item 361 - matched-diagonal Cartier reduction and old-norm collapse

Checked: 2026-09-01 (Beijing time)

## 1. Scope, capacity, and verdict

Keep Item 359's fixed-$M$ selector



$$
r=6M-7p,
 \qquad
 s=\frac{5p-4M-1}{2},
 \qquad
 k=r-1,
\tag{1.1}
$$



and its common rational coefficient kernel



$$
\mathcal K_M(X)
 =18R_-(X)U_-(X)^M
 +11\,16^{M-1}R_+(X)U_+(X)^M.
\tag{1.2}
$$



The actual nondegenerate collision remains the joint condition



$$
\boxed{
 p\mid\operatorname{num}[X^k]\mathcal K_M(X),
 \qquad
 H_{s-1}=\Theta_{r,s}\pmod p.}
\tag{1.3}
$$



This item applies Frobenius directly to the matched coefficient in
(1.3).  The calculation is exact and exhausts every base-$p$ digit
of the coefficient pair.  Its result is a no-go for the natural
Cartier/Lucas elimination, not a zero-density theorem.

> **PROVED - exact one-digit Cartier reduction.**  Put $t=M-p$.
> Then $M=(t,1)_p$, while $k<p$.  If
> 

$$
> A_{M,k}^{\pm}=[X^k]R_\pm(X)U_\pm(X)^M,
>
$$


> then
> 

$$
> \boxed{A_{p+t,k}^{\pm}\equiv A_{t,k}^{\pm}\pmod p.}
>
$$


> The high digit contributes only the constant term of
> $U_\pm(X^p)$.  No state or boundary term has been discarded.

> **PROVED - the low Cartier digit is exactly the old two-branch
> gate.**  Since $r=6t-p$, at $k=r-1$,
> 

$$
> A_{t,r-1}^-
> \equiv-\frac r{12}a_r,
> \qquad
> A_{t,r-1}^+
> \equiv-\frac r{12}b_r\pmod p.
>
$$


> Hence
> 

$$
> [X^{r-1}]\mathcal K_{p+t}(X)
> \equiv-\frac r{12}\bigl(18a_r+11z_pb_r\bigr),
> \qquad z_p=16^t.
>
$$


> Thus the complete matched-diagonal Frobenius reduction reproduces
> Item 314's selected determinant component; it does not create a new
> companion condition.

> **PROVED - phase elimination gives exactly Item 315's old endpoint
> norm.**  The tied relation $p=6t-r$ forces
> 

$$
> z_p^3=2^{2r+2}\pmod p.
>
$$


> Eliminating $z_p$ from the reduced linear gate gives
> 

$$
> \boxed{
> N_r=18^3a_r^3+11^3\,2^{2r+2}b_r^3\equiv0\pmod p,}
>
$$


> precisely the already known Item 315 resultant.  Over the target-free
> polynomial ring, the elimination ideal is generated by this norm.
> Retaining the phase gives the original selected gate; eliminating it
> gives the old norm.  There is no third ordinary-mod-$p$ invariant in
> this two-branch Cartier algebra.

> **PROVED - the base-$p$ digit tower is exhausted.**  On the actual
> interval, $0<t,r<p$ and $0\le k,m<p$.  The only high digit is the leading $1$
> of $M=p+t$, and the preceding theorem removes it exactly.  A second
> ordinary Lucas step cannot reduce the remaining low-digit gate.

> **OPEN.**  Arithmetic of the *selected* cubic component may still
> give weighted nonconcentration, and the legitimate
> $H_{s-1}-\Theta_{r,s}$ condition may correlate with it.  Neither
> is controlled by the Cartier collapse proved here.

The raw fixed-$M$ prime mass is still



$$
\frac{2}{35}M+o(M),
\tag{1.4}
$$



so



$$
\boxed{\text{new booking}=0},
 \qquad

The nondegenerate and degenerate charts remain disjoint parts of this

## 3. Exact Frobenius reduction of the two summands

Use Item 359's definitions



$$
\begin{aligned}
 R_-(X)&=\frac{(1+X)(1+X^2)P_-(X)}{E_-(X)^4},
 &U_-(X)&=\frac{(1+X^2)^4}{(1-X)^6},\\
 R_+(X)&=\frac{(X+2)(X^2+2X+2)P_+(X)}{E_+(X)^4},
 &U_+(X)&=\frac{(1+X+X^2/2)^4}{(1+X)^6}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{3.1}
$$



All Taylor coefficients lie in $\mathbb Z_{(p)}$, and



$$
U_\pm(X)^p\equiv U_\pm(X^p)\pmod p
\tag{3.2}
$$



as formal rational series.  For



$$
A_{M,k}^\pm=[X^k]R_\pm(X)U_\pm(X)^M,
\tag{3.3}
$$



equations (2.4) and (3.2) give



$$
\begin{aligned}
 A_{p+t,k}^\pm
 &=[X^k]R_\pm(X)U_\pm(X)^tU_\pm(X)^p\\
 &\equiv[X^k]R_\pm(X)U_\pm(X)^tU_\pm(X^p)\pmod p.
\end{aligned}
\tag{3.4}
$$



Because $k<p$ and $U_\pm(0)=1$, only the constant term of
$U_\pm(X^p)$ can contribute.  Thus



$$
\boxed{A_{p+t,k}^\pm\equiv A_{t,k}^\pm\pmod p.}
\tag{3.5}
$$



This is a complete coefficient identity.  It is not a bounded-depth
approximation to the rational function.

The scalar in the second summand also reduces exactly:



$$
16^{M-1}=16^{p+t-1}\equiv16^t\pmod p.
\tag{3.6}
$$



Combining (1.2), (3.5), and (3.6),



$$
\boxed{
 [X^k]\mathcal K_M(X)
 \equiv18A_{t,k}^-+11\,16^tA_{t,k}^+\pmod p.}
\tag{3.7}
$$



## 4. Identification of the low digit with $a_r,b_r$

At the selected coefficient $k=r-1$, equation $r=6t-p$ gives

 \qquad

\tag{4.1}
$$



The truncated exponent lemma from Item 359 applies because $r-1<p$.
Comparing (3.1) with Item 314's exact Lagrange formulas yields



$$
\boxed{
 A_{t,r-1}^-\equiv-\frac r{12}a_r,
 \qquad
 A_{t,r-1}^+\equiv-\frac r{12}b_r\pmod p.}
\tag{4.2}
$$



Put



$$
\boxed{z_p=16^t.}
\tag{4.3}

Then (3.7) becomes



$$
\boxed{

 \equiv-\frac r{12}

\tag{4.4}

Since $12r$ is a $p$-unit, the matched coefficient vanishes if and
only if



$$
\boxed{18a_r+11z_pb_r=0\pmod p.}
\tag{4.5}
$$



Equation (4.5) is just Item 359's Fermat-collapsed form of Item 314's
old determinant gate.  Frobenius has exposed it transparently, but has
not added a second equation.

## 5. Exact phase elimination

The tied identity $p=6t-r$ gives



$$
12t=2p+2r.
\tag{5.1}
$$



Therefore Fermat implies



$$
\boxed{
 z_p^3=16^{3t}=2^{12t}\equiv2^{2r+2}\pmod p.}
\tag{5.2}
$$



Let



$$
A=18a_r,
 \qquad B=11b_r,
 \qquad c=2^{2r+2}.
\tag{5.3}
$$



The elementary sum-of-cubes identity gives



$$
A^3+B^3c
 =(A+Bz)(A^2-ABz+B^2z^2)+B^3(c-z^3).
\tag{5.4}
$$



Thus eliminating $z$ from



$$
A+Bz=0,
 \qquad z^3-c=0
\tag{5.5}
$$



gives



$$
\boxed{
 A^3+B^3c
 =18^3a_r^3+11^3\,2^{2r+2}b_r^3=N_r.}
\tag{5.6}
$$



Because $p>3$ and $c\ne0$, $z^3-c$ is squarefree.  Over an
algebraic closure its three roots give three distinct linear components,
and their product is (5.6).  Hence the target-free elimination ideal is
exactly



$$
\boxed{
 (A+Bz,z^3-c)\cap\overline{\mathbb F}_p[A,B]
 =(A^3+B^3c).}
\tag{5.7}
$$



Item 315 already proved that $N_r$ is the old endpoint resultant and
is nonzero in characteristic zero on both actual rays.  Therefore the
matched-diagonal Cartier calculation has the exact dichotomy



$$
\boxed{
 \begin{array}{ll}
 \text{retain }z_p&\Longrightarrow\text{the original selected gate},\\
 \text{eliminate }z_p&\Longrightarrow\text{the old resultant }N_r.
 \end{array}}
\tag{5.8}
$$



No trace, norm, determinant, or polynomial elimination inside this
two-state algebra can yield an independent ordinary-mod-$p$ divisor.

When $p\equiv5\pmod6$, cubing is bijective and the norm identifies the
unique cubic component.  When $p\equiv1\pmod6$, the norm contains all
three components and the actual phase selects one.  This is precisely
Item 315's selected-component distinction, now recovered from the
fixed-$M$ Cartier digits.

## 6. Why there is no second ordinary Cartier digit

The complete digit data were displayed in (2.5).  After (3.5), the
remaining exponent $t$, coefficient index $r-1$, and hypergeometric
cutoff $m$ are all strictly below $p$.  Thus their base-$p$
expansions have no high digit.

Equivalently, the only Frobenius factor in the first coordinate was
$U_\pm(X^p)$, and the selected coefficient saw only its constant term.
Repeating the same ordinary Cartier operation acts trivially on the
remaining low-digit object.  This proves the scoped statement



$$
\boxed{
 \text{ordinary base-}p\text{ digit reduction of the matched first
 coordinate terminates at }(a_r,b_r,z_p).}
\tag{6.1}
$$



This does not rule out a $p^2$-adic Dwork derivative, a deformation in
another parameter, or a theorem about the arithmetic distribution of the
selected component.  Such information is not forced by the ordinary
collision and is not booked here.

## 7. The moving second coordinate remains independent

The actual collision is not merely $N_r=0\pmod p$.  On the
nondegenerate chart it is



$$
\boxed{
 18a_r+11z_pb_r=0,
 \qquad
 H_m=\Theta_{r,s}\pmod p,}
\tag{7.1}
$$



with $(r,s,m)$ as in (2.2).  The first equation implies
$p\mid N_r$, but the converse has foreign cubic components on the
$p\equiv1\pmod6$ ray.  The second equation is the legitimate moving
affine target and is not a polynomial consequence of (5.5).

Nothing in Sections 3-6 constrains $H_m-\Theta_{r,s}$.  In particular,
the norm collapse must not be credited as a new joint divisor or as a
replacement for the target.  A successful continuation must use either
the selected cubic component itself or a correlation with (7.1)'s
second coordinate.

## 8. Capacity and the exact remaining statistic

The first-coordinate support now has the necessary localization



$$
p\mid N_{6M-7p},
\tag{8.1}
$$



but (8.1) is exactly the old moving-index resultant support.  It is not a
single fixed-$M$ integer.  Item 315's pointwise exponential-height
bound, summed over the $O(M/\log M)$ prime rows, is still much larger
than the raw $O(M)$ interval mass.  The Cartier reduction does not
alter that comparison.

The prime number theorem in the two reduced classes modulo $6$ splits
the raw mass equally:



$$
\sum_{\substack{p\text{ in the fixed-}M\text{ interval}\\
                   p\equiv1\ (6)}}\log p
 =\frac{M}{35}+o(M),
 \qquad
 \sum_{\substack{p\text{ in the fixed-}M\text{ interval}\\
                   p\equiv5\ (6)}}\log p
 =\frac{M}{35}+o(M).
\tag{8.2}
$$



Thus each cubic ray carries capacity $1/210$ per $6M$.  Closing
only the bijective-cube $p\equiv5\pmod6$ ray would produce a strict
$1/210$ reduction; closing both would remove the full $1/105$.
No such weighted theorem is proved here.

The required joint weighted estimate remains



$$
\boxed{
 \sum_{p\text{ in the fixed-}M\text{ interval}}(\log p)
 1_{18a_{6M-7p}+11z_pb_{6M-7p}=0}
 1_{H_m=\Theta_{6M-7p,s}}
 =o(M).}
\tag{8.3}
$$



Even the selected-component estimate obtained by deleting the second
indicator would suffice.  Neither (8.1) nor the complete Cartier digit
calculation proves it.

Accordingly, Item 361 closes the following method class only:



$$
\boxed{
 \begin{array}{c}
 \text{ordinary Lucas/Dwork reduction of Item 359's two rational}\\
 \text{summands, followed by target-free polynomial elimination.}
 \end{array}}
\tag{8.4}
$$



It does not close cubic reciprocity, average gcds of the actual selected
component, the moving-target correlation, or higher $p$-adic lifts.

The ledger therefore remains unchanged at booking $0$, reduction
$0$, and ceiling $1/105$.
