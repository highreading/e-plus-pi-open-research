Unreviewed boundary audit: varying algebraic targets.

Let n≥1, let α_n∈Z_2, and suppose a nonzero polynomial P_n(X)=Σ_{j=0}^{d_n}a_{j,n}X^j∈Z[X] satisfies P_n(α_n)=0 and P_n(n)≠0. Put H_n=max_j|a_{j,n}|. Polynomial subtraction gives
P_n(n)=(n−α_n)Q_n,
where Q_n=Σ_{j=1}^{d_n}a_{j,n}Σ_{k=0}^{j−1}n^{j−1−k}α_n^k belongs to Z_2. Consequently,
v_2(n−α_n)≤v_2(P_n(n))≤log_2|P_n(n)|≤d_n log_2 n+log_2((d_n+1)H_n).
The first inequality uses v_2(Q_n)≥0; the second uses the fact that a nonzero integer divisible by 2^t has absolute value at least 2^t.

Thus, if d_n log_2 n+log_2((d_n+1)H_n)=o(n), then v_2(n−α_n)≥cn for a fixed c>0 is impossible for every sufficiently large n satisfying the nonzero-evaluation hypothesis. This includes selection from any fixed finite collection of algebraic roots, outside their finite set of positive integer equalities.

The nonzero-evaluation hypothesis is essential for varying targets: α_n=n and P_n(X)=X−n give infinite depth at every n despite degree one and height n. Even when evaluations are nonzero, unrestricted height defeats a uniform obstruction: for α_n=n+2^{⌈cn⌉} and P_n(X)=X−α_n, the depth is exactly ⌈cn⌉ and the height grows exponentially. These exact examples identify why fixed algebraicity cannot be replaced by unrestricted algebraicity of a varying target.
