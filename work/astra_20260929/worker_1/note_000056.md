Unverified supplementary audit consequence: exact information obtainable from a coefficient congruence class.

Let N≥1 and f∈Z_2⟨T⟩ satisfy f(T)≡T+ε modulo 2, with ε∈{0,1}. Fix t∈Z_2. Consider all integral restricted series g satisfying g≡f coefficientwise modulo 2^N. Each such g has a unique root β_g∈Z_2 and satisfies v_2(g(t))=v_2(t−β_g), with v_2(0)=∞.

For completeness, write g(T)=T+ε+2h(T). An integral restricted series h is 1-Lipschitz on Z_2, so T↦−ε−2h(T) is a contraction and has a unique fixed point. Moreover g(x)−g(y)=(x−y)(1+2H(x,y)) for integral H(x,y), proving the asserted isometry.

If d=v_2(f(t))<N, then every permitted g has v_2(t−β_g)=d: adding a quantity divisible by 2^N cannot change the smaller valuation.

If v_2(f(t))≥N, every permitted g has v_2(t−β_g)≥N, and every depth D∈{N,N+1,…}∪{∞} occurs. For finite D, take g_D(T)=f(T)+2^D−f(t); its constant perturbation is divisible by 2^N and g_D(t)=2^D. For D=∞, take g_∞(T)=f(T)−f(t), whose root is exactly t. Thus finite coefficient data determine the depth exactly below N, but in the unresolved residue class determine only the lower bound N. In particular, those data alone cannot certify either a finite upper bound or the stronger lower bound N+1.

Under the coordinate change n=a+2^s t and θ_g=a+2^sβ_g, where s is a nonnegative integer, the corresponding depths are v_2(n−θ_g)=s+v_2(t−β_g).

Requiring an exact zero before division does not by itself remove this ambiguity: F_g(Y)=Yg(Y) has F_g(0)=0 exactly, and F_g−F_f is coefficientwise divisible by 2^N, while its quotient by Y realizes all the preceding depths. Additional project identities could restrict these perturbations; none have been checked here. This is a general precision limitation, not a claim that the actual project root can be varied.
