Unverified supplementary lemma: root ambiguity under an exact divisibility constraint.

Let R=Z_2⟨T⟩, let N≥1, and suppose f∈R satisfies f(T)≡T+ε coefficientwise modulo 2, with ε∈{0,1}. Write β for its unique root in Z_2. Fix A∈R and consider the class g_h=f+2^N A h, where h ranges over R.

Every g_h has the same affine reduction modulo 2. Consequently, it has a unique root β_h and is an isometry: v_2(g_h(x)−g_h(y))=v_2(x−y). Indeed, writing g_h=T+ε+2u gives existence and uniqueness by contraction; the divided difference is 1 plus an even integral quantity.

Evaluating at β gives the exact displacement identity

v_2(β_h−β)=v_2(g_h(β))=N+v_2(A(β)h(β)),

with v_2(0)=∞. If A(β)=0, every permitted series therefore has the identical root β.

Suppose A(β)≠0 and put a=v_2(A(β)). The set of permitted roots is exactly β+2^(N+a)Z_2. The displacement identity proves inclusion. Conversely, take γ in that ball. Integral restricted series are 1-Lipschitz, so

v_2(A(γ)−A(β))≥v_2(γ−β)≥N+a>a.

Hence v_2(A(γ))=a. The isometry of f gives v_2(f(γ))≥N+a. Thus c=−f(γ)/(2^N A(γ)) belongs to Z_2, and the permitted series f+2^N cA has root γ. Constant h already realizes the entire ball.

For m≥1, requiring g≡f coefficientwise modulo 2^N and requiring their first m Taylor coefficients to agree exactly is precisely the case A=T^m. If β≠0 and σ=v_2(β), the possible roots form β+2^(N+mσ)Z_2. If β=0, the root is fixed. In particular, for a unit root, preserving arbitrarily many but finitely many exact Taylor coefficients at zero gives no improvement over precision N.

For a fixed t, the ball description determines the remaining depth ambiguity: depths below N+a are fixed, whereas if t belongs to the ball, every depth N+a,N+a+1,… and ∞ occurs among permitted roots.
