For each odd integer n≥3, put U_n=2^(2n)+n² and P_n(T)=T²−U_n. There is a root α_n∈Z₂ satisfying α_n≡n (mod 4). Indeed, U_n≡1 (mod 8). Starting with r₃≡n (mod 4), suppose r_k²≡U_n (mod 2^k). For k≥3, replacing r_k by r_k+ε·2^(k−1) changes its square by ε·2^k r_k modulo 2^(k+1). Since r_k is odd, exactly one ε∈{0,1} achieves the next congruence. These compatible approximations converge to the required root.

The identity (α_n−n)(α_n+n)=2^(2n), together with α_n+n≡2n≡2 (mod 4), gives the exact depth

v₂(α_n−n)=2n−1.

Every target has degree exactly two over Q. To see this, n²<2^(n+1) for n≥3: it holds initially and persists because (n+1)²/n²≤16/9<2. Consequently,

(2^n)²<U_n<(2^n+1)².

Thus U_n is not an integer square, hence not a rational square, and P_n is irreducible. In particular, P_n(n)=−2^(2n) is always nonzero, and α_n is never a rational integer.

The primitive minimal polynomial has coefficient height H_n=U_n, so log₂ H_n=2n+o(1). Therefore linear depth occurs with fixed degree two and exponential height, independently of zero-evaluation exceptions. The divided difference is P_n(n)/(n−α_n)=n+α_n and has valuation exactly one. This also checks the direction and possible strictness of v₂(n−α_n)≤v₂(P_n(n)).
