UNREVIEWED DERIVATION — sharper sufficient tail cutoff.

Assume the previously audited coefficientwise estimate for the interpolation summand indexed by b,c on a disk a+pZ_p, with p odd:

v_p(term) ≥ f_p(k) := k−v_p(k!),  k=floor((b+2c)/p).

Here coefficientwise valuation means the minimum valuation of the polynomial coefficients after substituting X=a+pT. Legendre’s formula gives

f_p(k)=((p−2)k+s_p(k))/(p−1),

where s_p(k) is the base-p digit sum. For k≥1, s_p(k)≥1, and f_p(k) is an integer. Put

K_p(d)=max(1,ceil((p−1)(d−1)/(p−2)))  for integers d≥1.

If k≥K_p(d), then f_p(k)≥d−1+1/(p−1)>d−1, hence f_p(k)≥d. Consequently retaining b+2c<pK_p(d) suffices modulo p^d. The same lower bound tends to infinity with k, so convergence justifies summing the entire omitted tail coefficientwise; this is not merely a termwise statement about a finite truncation.

For p=3, K_3(1)=1 and K_3(d)=2d−2 for d≥2. Thus the sufficient cutoff is 3 at d=1 and 6d−6 at d≥2. This strengthens the previous sufficient cutoff 6d−3. Neither cutoff is asserted optimal: digit sums can permit additional improvements at individual precisions. The argument does not use monotonicity of f_p, which can fail at powers of p.

Scope: conditional only on the audited interpolation estimate, this proves a sufficient precision cutoff. It neither bounds primitive endpoint cancellation nor estimates reduced denominators. Independent review has not been performed for this refinement.
