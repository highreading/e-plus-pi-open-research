For an integer base b≥2, let s_b(m) denote the digit sum of m≥0. Write S=(b−1)k+r, where 0≤r<b−1. The least nonnegative integer with digit sum S is M_b(S)=(r+1)b^k−1. Indeed, whenever a higher digit is positive and a lower digit is below b−1, moving one digit unit downward preserves the digit sum and decreases the integer. At the minimum, the bottom k digits therefore equal b−1, followed by the digit r.

Weighted arithmetic–geometric mean gives r+1≥b^{r/(b−1)}. Consequently m+1≥M_b(s_b(m))+1≥b^{s_b(m)/(b−1)}. Equality holds exactly when m=b^k−1 for some k≥0. More precisely, if m=bj+a with 0≤a<b, then

b^{s_b(m)/(b−1)}≤b^{a/(b−1)}(j+1),

with equality exactly when j=b^k−1. These statements hold for composite bases too and require no endpoint identities.

For a conditional denominator application, let n≥6, n≡1 modulo 5, and let q be a positive integer satisfying v_3(q)≥2v_3(n!) and v_5(q)≥2v_5(n!). Legendre’s formula implies

q≥(3√5)^n/[3^{s_3(n)}5^{s_5(n)/2}].

The general digit bound gives 3^{s_3(n)}≤(n+1)^2. Writing n=5j+1, the fixed-last-digit bound gives 5^{s_5(n)/2}≤√5(j+1)^2=(n+4)^2/(5√5). Hence

q≥5√5(3√5)^n/[(n+1)^2(n+4)^2].

This is a standalone conditional bound; optimality of its combined constant is not asserted. For application to an approximant, q must be its actual positive reduced denominator. Polynomial coefficient normalization alone does not establish the hypotheses.

If additionally |α−p_n/q_n|=Cρ^(−2n)(1+o(1)), with C>0, the displayed bound implies eventually

|q_nα−p_n|≥(5√5 C/2)(3√5/ρ²)^n/[(n+1)^2(n+4)^2].
