Independent audit of the additional optimality assertion; preparatory only.

Let L_p(k)=k−v_p(k!) and K=K_p(d). The previously audited coefficientwise estimate gives v_G(S_{b,c,r})≥L_p(floor((b+2c)/p)). Every k≥K satisfies L_p(k)≥d, whereas L_p(0)=0<d. Therefore the bad-index set B={k≥0:L_p(k)<d} is nonempty and finite, contained in {0,…,K−1}. Write j=max B and K*=j+1.

Sufficiency: if b+2c≥pK*, then floor((b+2c)/p)≥K*>j, so the summand belongs to p^d Z_p[T]. The same valuation bound tends to infinity with b+2c. Since only finitely many pairs have bounded b+2c, the entire omitted series converges in the Gauss norm. The ideal p^d Z_p⟨T⟩ is closed, so its sum retains the divisibility. This uses the last bad index, without assuming monotonicity of L_p.

Necessity: take r=0, c=0, b=pj and any a∈{0,…,p−1}. The corresponding summand is
S(T)=(−1)^(pj) ((a+pT)_[pj])²/(pj)!.
Among the pj consecutive factors, exactly j have index congruent to a modulo p. Each such factor has Gauss valuation exactly 1 because its T coefficient is p; every other factor has valuation 0. Multiplicativity of the Gauss valuation gives v_G((a+pT)_[pj])=j. Legendre’s formula gives v_p((pj)!)=j+v_p(j!), hence v_G(S)=L_p(j)<d.

For every integer block cutoff pH with H<K*, one has H≤j, so this witness lies in the omitted range, including equality pj=pH. Thus no smaller block cutoff has the asserted uniform individual-summand property. If j=0, the witness is the constant summand 1; this covers d=1, for which K*=1.

The argument establishes exactly the candidate’s termwise optimality. Cancellation between distinct omitted summands could permit a smaller cutoff for their sum. A fixed positive r could also supply additional valuation. Neither possibility contradicts the candidate or was excluded by this audit.
