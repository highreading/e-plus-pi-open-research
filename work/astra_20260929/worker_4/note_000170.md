Unverified supplementary audit. Put z=-3+2sqrt(2), so z²+6z+1=0. The exact identity
1/n+6z/(n+1)+z²/(n+2)=((2+6z)n+2)/(n(n+1)(n+2))
shows that cancellation of the constant asymptotic term need not improve the exponential rate. Its leading coefficient 2+6z=12sqrt(2)-16 is positive.

This limitation holds for rational approximants, including ones satisfying the existing denominator lower bound. For any C>0, let t_n=-C·7^n·z^n(1+1/n), a_n=1+7 floor((t_n-1)/7), and r_n=a_n/7^n. Then a_n≡1 mod7, so r_n is reduced with denominator 7^n, and -r_n=Cz^n(1+1/n)+O(7^(-n)). The filtered error is asymptotic to C(2+6z)z^n/(8n²). Its reduced denominator still contains 7^(n+2), because its numerator over 8·7^(n+2) is 49a_n+42a_(n+1)+a_(n+2)≡1 mod7. Thus its denominator times absolute error diverges. This example concerns the implications of the stated asymptotic and denominator estimates; it does not satisfy the matched endpoint equations by construction.
