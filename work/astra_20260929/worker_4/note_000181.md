Unreviewed arithmetic implication for the three-term filter.

Write r_k=p_k/q_k in lowest terms, q_k>0, and let Q_n be the positive reduced denominator of t_n=(r_n+6r_{n+1}+r_{n+2})/8. Assume the following eventual local statements for this same sequence:
(A3) v_3(q_k)=2v_3(k!) on k≡0,2 mod3, and v_3(q_k)=2v_3(k!)+δ_3(k), δ_3(k)≥1, on k≡1 mod3.
(A5) v_5(q_k)=2v_5(k!) on k≡1,2 mod5; v_5(q_k)=2v_5(k!)+δ_5(k), δ_5(k)≥1, on k≡3 mod5; and v_5(q_k)≤2v_5(k!) on k≡4 mod5.
All relevant ratios must be defined. Their eventual nonvanishing threshold remains unspecified.

For n≡2 mod3, put r=v_3(n!) and s=v_3(n+1)≥1. The three summands before division by 8 have valuations
−2r, 1−2r−2s, −2r−2s−δ_3(n+2).
The third is uniquely smallest, so cancellation is impossible and
v_3(Q_n)=v_3(q_{n+2})=2r+2s+δ_3(n+2)≥2r+3.

For n≡1 mod5, the three factorial valuations coincide. The first two summands have valuation −2v_5(n!), while the last has strictly smaller valuation −2v_5(n!)−δ_5(n+2). Thus v_5(Q_n)=v_5(q_{n+2})≥2v_5(n!)+1.

For n≡2 mod5, the middle summand has valuation −2v_5(n!)−δ_5(n+1). The first has valuation −2v_5(n!), and the last has valuation at least −2v_5(n!), by the residue-four upper bound. Hence v_5(Q_n)=v_5(q_{n+1})≥2v_5(n!)+1. The factors 6 and 8 are five-adic units. A zero last summand causes no difficulty.

Combining these conclusions gives, eventually on n≡2 or 11 mod15,
Q_n≥3^(2v_3(n!)+3)·5^(2v_5(n!)+1).
Legendre’s factorial valuation formula therefore gives log Q_n≥n log(3√5)−O(log n) on these two progressions.
