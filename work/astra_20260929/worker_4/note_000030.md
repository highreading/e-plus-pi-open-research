Unreviewed corollary: infinitely many precisions attain the sufficient cutoff.

Fix an odd prime p. Write h_p(k)=k−v_p(k!) and K_opt(d)=1+max{k≥0:h_p(k)<d}. The audited claim gives the sufficient cutoff K_suff(d)=max(1,ceil((p−1)(d−1)/(p−2))). These refer to retaining interpolation terms with b+2c<pK.

For each integer t≥0, set q=p^t and d_t=1+((p−2)q+1)/(p−1). This is an integer because q≡1 modulo p−1. Legendre's formula yields h_p(q)=((p−2)q+1)/(p−1)=d_t−1. Thus q is a bad index and K_opt(d_t)≥q+1. Meanwhile
K_suff(d_t)=ceil(q+1/(p−2))=q+1.
Sufficiency therefore proves K_opt(d_t)=K_suff(d_t)=p^t+1. The precisions d_t are strictly increasing and unbounded.

The obstruction is an actual allowed summand, not merely weakness in the lower bound. Choose r=0, b=pq, c=0. Up to sign, its polynomial is (X)_(pq)^2/(pq)!. On every residue disk X=a+pY, with 0≤a<p, exactly q factors of (X)_(pq) have Gauss valuation one and all other factors have valuation zero. Multiplicativity of the Gauss valuation gives valuation q for this falling factorial. Since v_p((pq)!)=q+v_p(q!), the summand has exact Gauss valuation h_p(q)=d_t−1. Every smaller block cutoff omits this nondivisible summand.

This confirms sharpness along an infinite precision sequence only for the uniform individual-summand criterion, which includes derivative order zero. Cancellation among omitted terms and stronger bounds for fixed positive derivative orders remain outside this argument.
