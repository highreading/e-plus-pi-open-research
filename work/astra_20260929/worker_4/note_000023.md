Let p be an odd prime, a an integer, and r,b,c nonnegative integers. Put R=b+2c, s=b+c, k=floor(R/p), q=floor(s/p), and

S_{b,c,r}(Y)=(-1)^b (a+pY)_{R+r}(a+pY)_s/(2^c b!c!).

Here (X)_L is the falling factorial, and v_G denotes the minimum p-adic valuation of polynomial coefficients. Among L consecutive factors of (a+pY)_L, at least floor(L/p) belong to p Z_p[Y]. Since p is odd, 2^c is a unit. Also b!c! divides s!, and Legendre’s formula gives v_p(s!)=q+v_p(q!). Therefore

v_G(S_{b,c,r}) ≥ floor((R+r)/p)+q−v_p(s!)
                    ≥ k−v_p(q!) ≥ k−v_p(k!).

The last inequality uses q≤k and monotonicity of factorial valuations; it does not use monotonicity of their difference from k. This establishes the original estimate directly from its summands, uniformly in a and r.

Write f_p(k)=k−v_p(k!). Legendre’s digit-sum identity gives

f_p(k)=((p−2)k+s_p(k))/(p−1).

For k≥1, s_p(k)≥1. If k≥K_p(d)=max(1,ceil((p−1)(d−1)/(p−2))), then

f_p(k) ≥ d−1+1/(p−1) > d−1.

Because f_p(k) is an integer, f_p(k)≥d. Thus every term with R≥pK_p(d), including equality, has coefficientwise valuation at least d. The correct sufficient retention condition is R<pK_p(d); retaining equality as well would merely retain unnecessary terms.

At d=1 the maximum with 1 matters: k=0 gives f_p(0)=0, whereas every k≥1 gives f_p(k)≥1. In particular, b=c=r=0 contributes the constant polynomial 1, so omitting the k=0 region is not justified by the estimate. The stated formula keeps that region. For p=3 it gives K_3(1)=1 and K_3(d)=2d−2 for d≥2, hence thresholds 3 and 6d−6. In particular, R≥6 can be omitted modulo 9.

Each finite partial sum of the omitted family belongs to p^d Z_p⟨Y⟩, and this ideal is closed, so its entire limit belongs there.
