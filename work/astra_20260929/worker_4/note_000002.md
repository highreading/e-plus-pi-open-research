Independent interpolation and finite-certificate derivation.

Write R=b+2c and s=b+c. For a nonnegative integer n, the corresponding term of H_n^{(r)}(1) is
(-1)^b (n)_{R+r}(n)_s/(2^c b!c!).
If R+r>n or s>n it vanishes, as required. This proves the interpolation identity without relying on the archive's verification labels.

On X=a+pY, every block of p consecutive factors of (X)_L contains a factor belonging to pZ_p[Y]. Hence its coefficientwise valuation is at least floor(L/p). With k=floor(R/p), q=floor(s/p), the integrality of binomial(s,b) gives
v_p(b!c!)≤v_p(s!)=q+v_p(q!).
Consequently each summand has coefficientwise valuation at least
floor((R+r)/p)−v_p(q!)≥k−v_p(k!).
Because q≤k and p is odd, this lower bound tends to infinity. The double series converges in Z_p⟨Y⟩: only finitely many pairs have bounded R. If R≥pK with K=ceil(d(p−1)/(p−2)), the bound v_p(k!)≤k/(p−1) proves a tail in p^dZ_p⟨Y⟩. Multiplying and adding integral interpolations to form the matrix and minors loses no precision.

For p=3 and precision 1, the source cutoff is R<6. In fact every term with R≥3 is already zero modulo 3: k≥1 implies k−v_3(k!)≥1. For R<3 all denominators are 3-adic units, so reduction on X=a+3Y is independent of Y. Thus, for a=0,1,2 and r=0,1,2,3, the reductions are obtained from the ordinary polynomials
H_0(x)=1,
H_1(x)=x−1,
H_2(x)=x²−4x+6.
At x=1 their derivative tuples are respectively
(1,0,0,0), (0,1,0,0), (3,−2,2,0).

Use precisely the source definitions
J_k=kH_k+H'_k,
K_k=k(k−1)H_k+2kH'_k+H''_k,
M_k=k(k−1)(k−2)H_k+3k(k−1)H'_k+3kH''_k+H'''_k,
with derivatives evaluated at 1. For X=3Y these give the three rows, modulo 3,
(H_X,J_X)=(1,0),
(J_{X+1},K_{X+1})=(1,2),
(K_{X+2},M_{X+2})=(0,0).
The minors are therefore (2,0,0) coefficientwise in F_3[Y]. This single polynomial identity certifies all Y∈Z_3 simultaneously. It requires no sampling assumption, lifting, or assertion about index derivatives. The first minor remains a unit at X=−3/2, so that exceptional prefactor cannot obstruct the certificate on this disk.
