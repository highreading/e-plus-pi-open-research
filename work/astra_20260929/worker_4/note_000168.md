Unreviewed supplementary derivation. Let T=e+pi, rho=(1+sqrt(2))^2, and z=-rho^(-1)=-3+2sqrt(2). Assume the published matched-approximant asymptotic T-r_n=C z^n(1+o(1)), with fixed C>0. The following deduction uses only that asymptotic.

Fix J and integers c_0,...,c_J independent of n. Put P(x)=sum_{j=0}^J c_j x^j and S=P(1). Since the number of shifts is fixed,
S T-sum_j c_j r_{n+j}=C z^n(P(z)+o(1)).
Thus the leading term cancels exactly when P(z)=0. The minimal polynomial of z over Q is g(x)=x^2+6x+1: its discriminant is 32, which is not a rational square. Division by this monic integer polynomial shows that P(z)=0 is equivalent to P=gQ for some Q in Z[x]. Consequently S=P(1)=8Q(1). In particular, S=1 makes leading cancellation impossible. Every fixed integer affine combination preserving T therefore retains the same exponential error rate, with nonzero leading constant C P(z).

More generally, let rational weights w_j have a positive common denominator d, write w_j=c_j/d with integer c_j, and require sum_j w_j=1. Then P(1)=d. Leading cancellation forces 8 to divide d. This applies in particular to the least common denominator of the weights. The minimum d=8 is attained by
r'_n=(r_n+6r_{n+1}+r_{n+2})/8,
for which T-r'_n=o(rho^(-n)). These are also the unique weights supported on these three shifts that both preserve T and cancel the leading term, because any polynomial of degree at most two vanishing at z is a scalar multiple of g.

Arithmetic limitation: if q_n is the positive reduced denominator of r_n, the reduced denominator of r'_n divides 8*lcm(q_n,q_{n+1},q_{n+2}). Cancellation can change this denominator substantially. The existing individual denominator lower bounds provide no lower bound for this new reduced denominator, and the little-o error estimate supplies no quantitative gain sufficient to decide whether its integer forms shrink. No conclusion about the rationality of T follows.
