Let p be an odd prime, a∈{0,…,p−1}, and b,c,r≥0. Write m=b+2c, s=b+c, and
T_{b,c,r}(Y)=(-1)^b(a+pY)_{m+r}(a+pY)_s/(2^c b!c!),
where (Z)_L is the falling factorial. For L≥0 define t_L(a)=#{j:0≤j<L, j≡a mod p}. Let v_G denote the minimum p-adic valuation of the polynomial coefficients, with v_G(0)=∞.

There is an exact formula
v_G(T_{b,c,r})=t_{m+r}(a)+t_s(a)−v_p(b!)−v_p(c!).
Indeed, the factor a−j+pY has Gauss valuation 1 precisely when j≡a mod p, and otherwise has valuation 0. Gauss valuations add under multiplication: after removing the respective powers of p, the nonzero reductions multiply to a nonzero polynomial over F_p. The denominator contributes the displayed factorial valuations, since 2 is a unit.

Put k=floor(m/p), j=floor(s/p), and F_p(k)=k−v_p(k!). Since t_{m+r}(a)≥k, t_s(a)≥j, and
v_p(b!)+v_p(c!)≤v_p(s!)=j+v_p(j!),
we recover v_G(T_{b,c,r})≥k−v_p(j!)≥F_p(k).

For every k≥0, choose b=pk and c=r=0. Each falling factorial then contains exactly k factors in the residue class a, including when k=0. Consequently
v_G(T_{pk,0,0})=2k−v_p((pk)!)=F_p(k)
on every residue disk. This proves attainment of the bound with the required uniform quantifiers.

For integer d≥1, the least block index ensuring that every summand with m≥pK belongs coefficientwise to p^d is therefore
K_opt(p,d)=1+max{k≥0:F_p(k)<d}.
The maximum exists because F_p(k)→∞, and its set includes k=0. Any smaller K includes the witness associated with the last failing block. This argument does not require F_p to be monotone.

The previously audited sufficient index is
K_suff(p,d)=max(1,ceil((p−1)(d−1)/(p−2))).
For k≥1, Legendre's formula gives F_p(k)=((p−2)k+s_p(k))/(p−1), where s_p(k)≥1. Thus k≥K_suff implies the integer F_p(k)>d−1. This includes the first omitted block and treats d=1 through K_suff=1. The same bound tends to infinity, so the entire omitted tail converges in p^d Z_p⟨Y⟩.

Two bounded boundary checks distinguish sufficiency from optimality. For p=3,d=5, F_3(6)=4 and F_3(7)=5; sufficiency already covers every k≥8. Hence K_opt=7, whereas K_suff=8. For p=3,d=6, F_3(9)=5 and sufficiency covers every k≥10, so both indices equal 10.
