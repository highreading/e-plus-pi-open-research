Unreviewed supplement: topology and size of the exceptional root set.

Fix c>0, an odd positive integer M, and an integer b. Define
E={α∈Z_2 : v_2(n−α)≥cn for infinitely many positive integers n≡b (mod M)}.
Then E is a dense G_delta subset of Z_2, has normalized Haar measure zero, and has Hausdorff dimension zero for the metric |x−y|_2.

Proof. Put k_n=ceil(cn) and B_n=n+2^{k_n}Z_2. Since valuations are integral, the required inequality is equivalent to α∈B_n. Thus E=intersection over N≥1 of U_N, where U_N is the union of B_n over n≥N with n≡b (mod M). Each U_N is open. To prove density, take any residue ball a+2^rZ_2. Because M is odd, CRT supplies arbitrarily large positive n satisfying both n≡a (mod 2^r) and n≡b (mod M). Choose one with n≥N and k_n≥r. Then B_n lies in the given ball. Hence U_N is dense, and completeness of Z_2 gives density of E by the Baire category theorem.

For every N, the balls defining U_N cover E. Their Haar measures are 2^{−k_n}, so μ(E)≤sum over n≥N of 2^{−cn}, which tends to zero. For any s>0, their diameters are 2^{−k_n}, their maximum diameter tends to zero as N increases, and the sum of their s-th powers is at most sum over n≥N of 2^{−scn}, again tending to zero. Therefore the s-dimensional Hausdorff measure of E is zero for every s>0, proving dimension zero.
