1. Definitions and state primitivity.

Let H_n(x)=n![s^n]e^(xs)(1−s+s²/2)^n and (h,u,v)=(H_n(1),H'_n(1),H''_n(1)). Define J_k=(x^kH_k)'(1), K_k=(x^kH_k)''(1), M_k=(x^kH_k)'''(1), and let Ω_n be the nonnegative gcd of the maximal minors of the matrix with rows (H_n(1),J_n), (J_(n+1),K_(n+1)), (K_(n+2),M_(n+2)).

Coefficient extraction gives H'_(n+1)=(n+1)(H_n−H'_n+H''_n/2) and H_(n+1)=xH''_n/2+(n+1−x)(H'_n−H_n). Differentiating the latter and subtracting the former gives xH'''_n+(n+2−2x)H''_n+2(x−1)H'_n−2nH_n=0. Thus the endpoint state advances by

T_n=[[-n,n,1/2],[n+1,−n−1,(n+1)/2],[n(n+1),n+1,−(n+1)(n+2)/2]],

whose determinant is (n+1)^4/2. Starting from (1,0,0), this proves that the state is nonzero over Q and primitive over Z_p whenever p is odd and p>n. This is primitivity of the three-coordinate state; it does not assert that each coordinate is a unit.

2. Full-depth ideal equality.

Put D=2(n+1)h²−2hu−(n−1)u²−uv, C=u³+(n−2)hu²+4h²u−2(n+2)h³, and E=−2n(n+2)h²+4nhu+(n+2)hv+(n²−2n−1)u²+nuv. Independent expansion from T_n gives

I_01=(n+1)D, I_02=(n+2)(2n+3)E,
uE+(nu+(n+2)h)D=−(n+1)C,
I_12|_(h=u=0)=(n+1)(n+2)²(2n+3)v².

For p>2n+4 all displayed prefactors are units. If h vanishes modulo p and u is a unit, then D=0 forces v=−(n−1)u and hence E=−(n+1)u²≠0. If both h and u vanish, state primitivity and the last minor evaluation give I_12≠0. Therefore p|h implies v_p(Ω_n)=0.

If h is a unit, subtract (n+u/h) times column one from column two. Expansion using the resulting first row (h,0) shows that the ideal of all three minors equals (I_01,I_02)=(D,E) in Z_p. When u is a unit, the homogeneous identity gives (D,E)=(D,C). When u is not a unit, D≡2(n+1)h² and C≡−2(n+2)h³ are units, so the equality still holds. Consequently

v_p(Ω_n)=min(v_p(D),v_p(C)) if p∤h,

and positive content valuation forces both h and u to be units. This proves equality at every depth, not merely equality of prime supports.

Writing z=u/h gives C/h³=f_n(z)=z³+(n−2)z²+4z−2(n+2). Therefore, in the unit chart, p^d|Ω_n is equivalent to D≡f_n(z)≡0 modulo p^d. Both equations are necessary. Substitution t=n+z gives P_n(t)=t³−(2n+2)t²+(n+2)²t−2(n+1)(n+2).

3. Adjacent contents.

Assume p>2n+6 divides Ω_n and Ω_(n+1). The first gate makes h,u units. From D_n=0 obtain w=v/h=2(n+1)/z−2−(n−1)z. Applying T_n gives

h_1/h=(n+1)(z²−2z+2)/(2z),
u_1/h=−(n+1)²(z²−2)/(2z),
v_1/h=(n+1)²(nz²−2n+4z−4)/(2z).

These identities require D_n=0, with denominators 2z invertible. Direct substitution gives D_(n+1)/h²=(n+1)²F_n(z)/(2z²), where

F_n=((2n+3)z−n−2)f_n−g_n,
g_n=(n²+4n+2)z²−(6n+4)z−2n².

The first minor at n+1 forces D_(n+1)=0; thus f_n(z)=g_n(z)=0 modulo p. But the exact integral identity is

A_n f_n+B_n g_n=−8(n+1)²(n+2),
A_n=−(3n³+14n²+14n+4)z−4n³+8n+4,
B_n=(3n+2)z²+(3n²−2)z+4n²+12n+8.

Its right side is a p-unit, a contradiction. This proves min(v_p(Ω_n),v_p(Ω_(n+1)))=0. The independently checked resultant −32(n+1)^4(n+2)² agrees, but is not needed. The stated cutoff also ensures that the endpoint-gcd theorem applies at both indices. No small-prime conclusion is inferred.

4. Nonvanishing for every n≥2.

If every minor vanished over Q, the same cases as in section 2, using the nonzero rational state, would force h,u≠0. Hence z=u/h is a rational root of the monic integer polynomial f_n, so is integral. Rearrangement yields n=2−z+(8−6z)/(z²−2). Thus z²−2 divides 8−6z, and multiplication by 8+6z shows z²−2 divides 8. The only integer candidates are z=0,1,−1,2,−2, giving n=−2,−1,−11,−2,14 respectively.

Here is an independent proof excluding the last candidate, without relying on the old differentiated congruence. Put a(s)=1−s+s²/2. Then

H_n(x)=Σ_(k=0)^n (n)_k [s^k]a(s)^n x^(n−k).

All coefficients involved belong to Z_(p) for odd p. If p|n, reduction modulo p gives a(s)^n=a(s^p)^(n/p). Therefore coefficients with 1≤k<p vanish modulo p. For k≥p, the falling factorial (n)_k contains a multiple of p. Every nonleading summand consequently vanishes, proving H_n(x)≡x^n modulo p as a polynomial. Differentiating this polynomial congruence gives H'_n(x)≡n x^(n−1)≡0. At n=14,p=7, h≡1 and u≡0, incompatible with u=−2h. Thus Ω_n≠0 for every n≥2.
