Unreviewed conditional supplement: rational cancellation of the fixed-degree leading constants.

Assume a common real target S and rational approximants r_{j,n}, with consistent sign convention, satisfy
S-r_{j,n}=(-1)^n C τ^j ρ^(-2n)(1+η_{j,n}),
where ρ=1+√2, τ=ρ^(-1)=√2−1, C≠0 is independent of j, and η_{j,n}→0 for each fixed j. These are explicit hypotheses; applicability to the endpoint family retains the pending application dependencies.

Fix b. Define the rational approximant
s_{b,n}=−r_{b,n}/2+r_{b+1,n}+r_{b+2,n}/2.
The weights sum to one. Since τ²+2τ−1=0, direct substitution gives the exact identity
S-s_{b,n}=(-1)^n C τ^b ρ^(-2n)[−η_{b,n}/2+τ η_{b+1,n}+τ²η_{b+2,n}/2].
Consequently S-s_{b,n}=o(ρ^(-2n)). Only three fixed degrees are involved; no uniformity for growing degree is used.

The weights are uniquely determined among rational weights on these three consecutive degrees that preserve the target and cancel the leading coefficient. Indeed, c0+c1τ+c2τ²=0 implies c0=c2 and c1=2c2 by irrationality of τ; imposing c0+c1+c2=1 gives (c0,c1,c2)=(1/4,1/2,1/4)? This would use the wrong polynomial sign. Correctly substituting τ²=1−2τ gives c0+c2=0 and c1−2c2=0, hence (c0,c1,c2)=(−1/2,1,1/2). Two consecutive degrees admit no nonzero rational cancellation because c0+c1τ=0 forces both coefficients to vanish.
