Let f=z³+(n−2)z²+4z−2(n+2) and g=(n²+4n+2)z²−(6n+4)z−2n². Direct polynomial expansion gives:

At n=−1, f=(z−1)(z²−2z+2) and g=−(z²−2z+2).
At n=−2, f=z(z−2)² and g=−2(z−2)².

Consequently, over any field of odd characteristic, the specialization n=−2 has the common root z=2. At n=−1 there are common roots over the algebraic closure; they lie in the base field precisely when −1 is a square. Thus the failure of the scalar −8(n+1)²(n+2) to be a unit reflects a genuine obstruction to universal coprimality of f and g, rather than merely a defect of the displayed Bézout coefficients.

The audited cutoff p>2n+6 excludes these exceptional parameter residues for ordinary integers n≥2 and also satisfies the separate state and endpoint requirements. This check supplies no converse: a common root of the abstract elimination polynomials need not be realized by the actual derivative state (H_n(1),H′_n(1),H″_n(1)). It therefore supplies neither an Ω counterexample nor any bound on actual endpoint cancellation.

Additional bounded validation: the exceptional factors n+1 and n+2 in the integral Bézout obstruction correspond to genuine common factors of the elimination polynomials at those parameter values. They cannot be discarded when extending the algebraic argument to smaller primes. This does not establish divisibility of the actual Ω sequence at exceptional primes.
