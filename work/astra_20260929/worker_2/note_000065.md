Unreviewed supplementary check: convergence rates must remain separate.

For n≥1, define T_{j,n}(f)=Σ_{k≥0}[t^k]f(t)/(n+j+k)!, and let a_n=1+n^(-1/2). Take φ_{0,n}(t)=1 and φ_{1,n}(t)=1+a_n t. Both functions equal 1 at zero, are uniformly bounded on every fixed disk, and converge locally uniformly. Their degree-0/1 coefficient determinant is a_n.

The corresponding factorial-transform determinant is exactly

D_n=det([[1/n!,1/(n+1)!],[1/n!+a_n/(n+1)!,1/(n+1)!+a_n/(n+2)!]])
=a_n(1/[n!(n+2)!]−1/[(n+1)!]^2)
=−a_n/[(n+1)!(n+2)!].

Consequently −(n+1)!(n+2)!D_n=1+n^(-1/2). The factorial factorization has zero remainder here, but its normalized determinant converges at order n^(-1/2), not O(1/n). Replacing n^(-1/2) by any bounded positive sequence tending to zero allows arbitrarily slow convergence.

This exact calculation validates the dependency-map distinction: a uniform O(1/n) factorial-transform remainder and local uniform convergence of the input functions imply convergence of the normalized determinant, but do not alone imply an O(1/n) rate toward its limiting constant. It concerns these explicitly constructed polynomials, not the convergence rate of the project's endpoint functions. No numerical experiment or independent registry approval is claimed.
