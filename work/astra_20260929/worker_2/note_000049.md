Unverified scoped derivation: exact projection and complete remainder.

Let n≥b≥1, F(z)=4 arctan(z/(2−z)), and R(z)=B(z)e^z+C(z)F(z)−A(z), with degree caps (deg A,deg B,deg C)≤(n,b,n). Suppose R(z)=O(z^(2n+b+1)) and B(1)=C(1). The sign convention for R is to be compared with the original source.

Put a±=(1±i)/2 and define the complex bilinear moment functional
L(f)=(2/i)∫_[a−,a+] f(t)dt=∫_[-1,1] f((1+ix)/2)dx.
For |z|<√2, direct integration, or comparison of derivatives and values at zero, gives F(z)=z L((1−zt)^(−1)). The contour satisfies |t|≤1/√2, so its geometric series converges absolutely at z=1.

Let P_m be the usual Legendre polynomial, normalized by P_m(1)=1, and set
p_m(t)=i^m P_m((2t−1)/i)/binom(2m,m).
These are monic, pairwise orthogonal for the bilinear form L(fg), with nonzero norms
h_m=L(p_m²)=2(−1)^m/[(2m+1)binom(2m,m)^2].
Thus the kernel K_n(t,s)=Σ_(m=0)^n p_m(t)p_m(s)/h_m is defined for every n. Its reproducing identity is L_s(K_n(t,s)Q(s))=Q(t) whenever deg Q≤n. No positivity of the complex bilinear form is needed.

Write B(z)=Σ_(j=0)^b B_jz^j, C*(t)=t^n C(1/t), and ell_j(t^k)=1/(n+k+1−j)!, extended linearly. Put ell_B=Σ B_j ell_j. All factorial arguments here are positive because n≥b and k≥0. For k≥0,
[z^(n+k+1)](Be^z+CF)=ell_B(t^k)+L(C*(t)t^k).
Consequently the approximation equations above degree n are exactly
L(C*t^k)=−ell_B(t^k), 0≤k≤n+b−1.
The first n+1 equations determine C* uniquely, since all h_0,…,h_n are nonzero. Their solution is
C*(s)=−ell_(B,t)K_n(t,s).
After these equations hold, the remaining b−1 equations are equivalent, by a triangular change from monomials to monic orthogonal polynomials, to
ell_B(p_(n+l))=0, 1≤l≤b−1.
Indeed L(C*p_(n+l))=0 because deg C*≤n.

Set V(t)=K_n(t,1). Since C(1)=C*(1)=−ell_B(V), endpoint matching becomes
Σ_(j=0)^b B_j(1+ell_j(V))=0.
Conversely, any B satisfying these b reduced homogeneous equations determines C by the displayed kernel formula, and determines A uniquely as the Taylor polynomial through degree n of Be^z+CF. These reconstructed polynomials satisfy both the approximation order and endpoint matching. This proves equivalence of the original system and the reduced rows, without assuming that the reduced system has full rank.

For the complete evaluated remainder, define
H_n(t)=L_s(K_n(t,s)/(1−s)),
W(t)=1/(1−t)−H_n(t).
Here H_n denotes the projection polynomial, distinct from the arithmetic auxiliary sequence. Extend ell_B to W by its Taylor coefficients at zero. This extension is absolutely convergent: H_n is polynomial, while the Taylor coefficients of 1/(1−t) equal 1 and the functional weights are finite sums of reciprocal factorials.

Since A removes precisely the Taylor terms through degree n,
R(1)=Σ_(k≥0){ell_B(t^k)+L(C*t^k)}
=ell_B(1/(1−t))+L(C*/(1−t)).
The exponential sum is absolutely convergent by its factorial denominators. The other sum is absolutely convergent on the contour because |t|≤1/√2 and C* is polynomial. Substituting C*(s)=−ell_(B,t)K_n(t,s), and interchanging only a finite polynomial functional with the contour integral, gives
L(C*/(1−t))=−ell_B(H_n),
and therefore the exact identity R(1)=ell_B(W).

This is a complete-tail identity: every Taylor coefficient above degree n is included. It does not replace the remainder by its first nonzero coefficient.

The only denominators inverted in this algebraic reconstruction are the explicit nonzero h_m and positive integer factorials; 1−s is nonzero on the integration contour. Division by Y=B(1) has not occurred. To conclude anything about R(1)/Y, one must separately establish Y≠0. Projective uniqueness likewise requires rank b of the b-by-(b+1) reduced matrix. The fixed-b determinant argument is intended to provide these eventual assertions, but they do not follow from the projection identity alone.
