Unverified conditional consequence of the fixed-b error asymptotic.

Fix an integer b≥1 and put ρ=1+√2 and C_b=4π/ρ^(b+1). Suppose reduced rational approximants r_n=p_n/q_n, with q_n>0, satisfy |α−r_n|=C_b ρ^(−2n)(1+o(1)). For the assigned matched family, this hypothesis uses the audited Padé prefactor together with the transfer theorem in work/session_20260927/fixed_exponential_degree_error_theorem.md. The latter remains an explicitly cited dependency; its determinant proof was not independently re-audited here.

Assume α=a/d is rational, with a,d integers and d>0. Eventual nonvanishing of the error gives positive integers

m_n=|a q_n−d p_n|=d q_n |α−r_n|.

Writing x_n=q_n/ρ^(2n), we obtain m_n=d C_b x_n(1+o(1)). Since m_n≥1, this proves the conditional bound

liminf x_n ≥ 1/(d C_b)=ρ^(b+1)/(4πd).

More precisely, suppose x_n→L<∞ along any infinite subsequence. Then m_n→d C_b L along that subsequence. A convergent sequence of positive integers is eventually constant, so there is an integer k≥1 such that

L=k/(d C_b)=kρ^(b+1)/(4πd).

Thus rationality forces every finite accumulation point into this discrete set. Each displayed value is transcendental: its numerator divided by 4d is a nonzero algebraic number, and an algebraic value of L would imply that π is algebraic. Consequently, existence of any finite algebraic accumulation point of x_n, including zero, would contradict rationality of α.

The elementary integer argument checks how the prefactor enters the arithmetic obstruction. It does not establish boundedness, convergence, or any accumulation point for the actual reduced denominators. No recurrence or asymptotic formula for those denominators is available from this argument. The criterion applies separately for each fixed b and asserts no uniformity when b grows. Dependencies are the stated error asymptotic and the standard transcendence theorem for π; independent publication review is still required.

A further conditional consequence of the audited prefactor is available: under rationality of the target, every finite accumulation point of q_n/ρ^(2n) must belong to an explicit discrete set. In particular, a finite algebraic accumulation point would suffice to prove irrationality. This is an unreviewed arithmetic criterion, not an estimate for the actual denominator sequence.
