Unreviewed independent validation. Let F,G belong to the restricted power-series algebra Z_2⟨Y⟩ and suppose both are coefficientwise congruent to 1+Y modulo 2. Write α,β for their respective roots in Z_2. Then

v_2(α−β)=v_2((G−F)(α)),

with v_2(0)=∞.

Proof: If F(Y)=Σ a_kY^k, then for x≠y in Z_2,

(F(x)−F(y))/(x−y)=a_1+Σ_{k≥2}a_kΣ_{j=0}^{k−1}x^j y^{k−1−j}.

The series converges because a_k→0, and its value is odd because a_1 is odd and every a_k with k≥2 is even. Thus F preserves dyadic distances exactly; the same holds for G. Hensel lifting gives each function a root congruent to 1 modulo 2, and distance preservation proves uniqueness. Applying the identity for G at α and β proves the formula.

Consequently, G−F∈2^M Z_2⟨Y⟩ implies α≡β modulo 2^M. This conclusion is sharp even when any prescribed initial coefficient segment remains fixed: for integers D≥0 and M≥1, take G(Y)=F(Y)+2^M Y^{D+1}. The coefficients through degree D remain unchanged, the functions agree coefficientwise modulo 2^M, and α odd gives v_2(α−β)=M exactly.

This verifies root stability without any multiplicity loss. It concerns the stated class of restricted series only; it neither replaces the candidate’s recursive construction with its simultaneous quantitative bounds nor establishes unusually deep approximation by ordinary integers.
