Since |t(u)|≤1/√2, the identity F(z)=zℒ((1−zt)^(−1)) gives an absolutely and uniformly convergent coefficient expansion at z=1. The monic polynomial p_n(t)=i^nP_n(−i(2t−1))/binom(2n,n) satisfies p_n(t(u))=i^nP_n(u)/binom(2n,n). Real Legendre orthogonality therefore gives ℒ(p_jp_n)=0 for j≠n and ℒ(p_n²)=2(−1)^n/((2n+1)binom(2n,n)²). The functional is bilinear, and no positivity of this complex functional is used.

Define C*_{0,n}=p_n/p_n(1), C_{0,n}(z)=z^nC*_{0,n}(1/z), and f_n=[T_n(C_{0,n}F)](1). Put K_n=binom(2n,n)p_n(1)=i^nP_n(−i). The ordinary Legendre recurrence gives K_0=K_1=1 and (n+1)K_{n+1}=(2n+1)K_n+nK_{n−1}. Hence K_n>0 for every n, so all these normalizations are legitimate.

For every k≥n+1, direct coefficient multiplication gives [z^k](C_{0,n}F)=ℒ(t^(k−n−1)C*_{0,n}). Summing this tail is justified by the preceding uniform geometric bound. Since C_{0,n}(1)=1, it follows that π−f_n=ℒ(p_n/(1−t))/p_n(1). The polynomial (p_n(1)−p_n(t))/(1−t) has degree at most n−1, so orthogonality permits replacement of p_n(1) by p_n(t) in the numerator after multiplication by p_n. Consequently

π−f_n=ℒ(p_n²/(1−t))/p_n(1)²=2(−1)^n I_n/K_n²,
I_n=∫_{−1}^1 P_n(u)²/(1+u²)du.

For the coefficient asymptotic, write ρ=1+√2 and c_j=binom(2j,j)/4^j. The recurrence for K_n implies that its generating function G satisfies (1−2t−t²)G′=(1+t)G, G(0)=1. Thus

For fixed j, c_{n−j}/c_n→1. To justify taking the limit through the expanding sum, choose positive absolute constants c,C such that c/√(m+1)≤c_m≤C/√(m+1) for all m≥0; these follow from Stirling’s formula, with finitely many small indices absorbed into the constants. If j≤n/2, then c_{n−j}/c_n≤(C/c)√2. The absolute summands in this range are therefore bounded by a fixed summable sequence proportional to ρ^(−2j).
