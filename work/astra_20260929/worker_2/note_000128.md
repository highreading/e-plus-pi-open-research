Fix n≥9 with n≡4 modulo five. Put a=v_5(n!), m=n+1, s=v_5(m), f=2^n/(n!)², N=X/f and Z=N/m. The approved residue-four statement gives v_5(D)=s and Z∈5Z_5. Write D=mδ, where δ is a five-adic unit. For the positive reduced denominator q of X/D,

X/D=fZ/δ,
v_5(q)=max(0,2a−v_5(Z))=max(0,2a+s−v_5(N)).

Here v_5(0)=∞, so these formulas include X=0 and q=1. In particular, the approved divisibility implies v_5(q)≤2a−1; this bounds only one prime component, not the whole denominator.

If N is known modulo 5^K with K≥s, division by m determines Z only modulo 5^(K−s). Consequently, a computation certifying v_5(Z)≤T by a nonzero residue modulo 5^(T+1) needs N to precision 5^(s+T+1). Vanishing at that precision proves a lower bound, not an upper bound, on the cancellation depth.

For exact determination of v_5(q), knowing N modulo 5^(2a+s) suffices. A nonzero residue reveals its valuation below 2a+s; a zero residue implies v_5(q)=0. Equivalently, Z modulo 5^(2a) suffices.

This precision threshold is optimal if the only additional information used is N∈5^(s+1)Z_5. Indeed, for any integer 0≤K<2a+s, let L=max(K,s+1), N₁=5^(2a+s), and N₂=N₁+5^L. Since a≥1, L<2a+s. These two nonzero inputs satisfy the prescribed divisibility and agree modulo 5^K, but yield denominator exponents 0 and 2a+s−L>0. This is an obstruction to inference from insufficient residue data; it does not assert that these artificial inputs occur in the endpoint family or preclude stronger deductions from its defining identities.
