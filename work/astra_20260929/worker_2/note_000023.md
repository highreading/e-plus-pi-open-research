Unverified refinement of the sharpness construction.

Let α=u/v be reduced, v>0, β>1 and C>0. Choose integers a,b with ua−vb=1. For any sequence of positive integers m_n, put s_n=(−1)^n and Q_n=m_n β^n/(vC). Choose t_n≡1 modulo m_n nearest to (Q_n−s_nm_na)/v, and define

q_n=s_nm_na+vt_n,
p_n=s_nm_nb+ut_n.

Then |q_n−Q_n|≤vm_n/2. The inverse identities

u q_n−v p_n=s_nm_n,
−b q_n+a p_n=t_n

show that gcd(p_n,q_n)=gcd(m_n,t_n)=1. These identities cover all reduced rational targets, without requiring u>0.

Write δ_n=(q_n−Q_n)/Q_n. The rounding bound gives

|δ_n|≤(v²C/2)β^(−n),

independently of m_n. Hence q_n>0 eventually, and

α−p_n/q_n=s_n C β^(−n)(1+η_n),
η_n=−δ_n/(1+δ_n).

Once (v²C/2)β^(−n)≤1/2, one has |η_n|≤v²Cβ^(−n). Also

q_n/β^n=[m_n/(vC)](1+δ_n).

For constant m_n=m this recovers every permitted finite lattice limit m/(vC). Allowing arbitrary m_n demonstrates that the prescribed leading error, even with this quantitative relative remainder, permits widely different denominator behavior among unrestricted rational approximants. It does not assert that these approximants satisfy any Padé or endpoint equations.
