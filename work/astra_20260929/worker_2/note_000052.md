Unverified audit supplement: normalized quotient limits and their coefficient determinants.

Use the original source conventions R=A+Be^z+CF, V=K_n(t,1), W=Ψ_n, U=p_(n+1), b_n=p_(n+1)(1)/p_n(1), and α_n=v_(n+1)/v_n. The preceding independent audit established b_n≥1/2, −1/6<α_n<0, V(0)>0, W(0)≠0, and the exact reference identity
W(0)/V(0)=(-1)^(n+1) ε_n(1+α_n/b_n)/2.

1. Uniform quotient convergence.

Put q_n(t)=p_n(t)/p_(n+1)(t). The tridiagonal representation has diagonal 1/2 and off-diagonal entries i√β_k, where β_k=k²/[4(4k²−1)]. Its Hermitian part is (1/2)I. Therefore its resolvent, and hence its corner entry q_n, satisfies
sup_(|t|≤r)|q_n(t)|≤1/(1/2−r),  r<1/2.
This also establishes analyticity on that disk. The recurrence gives
q_n=1/(t−1/2+β_n q_(n−1)).

Take r=1/10, so all these sup norms are at most 5/2. If d_n=||q_n−q_(n−1)|| on this disk, subtraction gives, for n≥2,
d_n≤(25/48)d_(n−1)+(125/8)|β_n−β_(n−1)|.
The β_n decrease to 1/16, so the forcing terms are summable. Summing this inequality proves Σd_n<∞. Thus q_n converges uniformly to an analytic q. Its limiting recurrence and its negative value at zero identify
q(t)=−1/[λ_+ f(t)],
λ_+=(1+√2)/4,
f(t)=[1−2t+√((1−2t)²+1)]/(1+√2),
with the square-root branch positive at zero. In particular f(0)=1 and f′(0)=−√2.

For each fixed k≥0, finite products of consecutive quotients consequently give
F_(n,k)(t):=[p_(n+1+k)(t)/p_(n+1+k)(0)]/[U(t)/U(0)] → f(t)^k
uniformly on a sufficiently small fixed closed disk. The limiting q is nonzero there, which justifies taking these finite products of inverses. This assertion is for fixed k only.

2. Exact normalized endpoint quotients.

Christoffel–Darboux and the complete remainder formula give
G_(V,n)(t):=[V(t)/V(0)]/[U(t)/U(0)]
=(1−b_n q_n(t))/[2(1−t)],
G_(W,n)(t):=[W(t)/W(0)]/[U(t)/U(0)]
=(1−α_n q_n(t))/[(1+α_n/b_n)(1−t)].
At zero, q_n(0)=−1/b_n, so both normalized functions equal one. The factors 1−t are bounded away from zero on the chosen disk. Also 1+α_n/b_n>2/3. These identities therefore introduce no vanishing denominator.

The positive endpoint recurrence gives b_n→λ_+. The second-kind backward recurrence
|α_n|=β_(n+1)/(1/2+|α_(n+1)|)
has contraction factor at most 1/3 on its bounded positive range. Iteration and β_n→1/16 give α_n→λ_-=(1−√2)/4.

Write τ=√2−1, so λ_-/λ_+=−τ² and 1−τ²=2τ. Substitution yields initially
G_V=(f+1)/[2f(1−t)],
G_W=(f−τ²)/[(1−τ²)f(1−t)].
The quadratic equation for f gives the exact identity
f(1−t)=λ_+(f+1)(f−τ²).
Since λ_+(1−τ²)=1/2, the limits simplify to
G_V(t)=2τ/[f(t)−τ²],
G_W(t)=2/[f(t)+1].
Uniform convergence follows from the already established bounds and nonzero denominators. Cauchy's formula consequently gives convergence of every fixed Taylor coefficient.

3. The limiting coefficient determinants.

For fixed b≥1, let E_Q be the determinant whose rows are the Taylor coefficients through degree b−1 of
1,f,…,f^(b−2),G_Q,
with the polynomial rows absent when b=1. Set s=b(b−1)/2. Changing local coordinate from t to y=f(t)−1 multiplies a coefficient determinant of this size by f′(0)^s. In y coordinates the first b−1 rows are (1+y)^k, k=0,…,b−2; their coefficient block is triangular with diagonal one. Thus only the coefficient of y^(b−1) in the last row remains.

Since
G_V=1/(1+y/(2τ)),   G_W=1/(1+y/2),
one obtains
E_V=(-√2)^s (-1)^(b−1)/(2τ)^(b−1),
E_W=(-√2)^s (-1)^(b−1)/2^(b−1).
Both are nonzero and E_W/E_V=τ^(b−1). The corresponding finite-n coefficient determinants converge to these values. For b=2 this recovers G_V′(0)→1+1/√2 and G_W′(0)→1/√2, independently checking the source constants.

This proves the analytic coefficient-determinant component. To deduce the matched-family transfer, the factorial-transform determinant must still be shown to have this coefficient determinant as its leading factor, with a sufficiently small remainder. Only after that step may one infer rank, divide by the matched endpoint Y, and combine the ratio τ^(b−1) with W(0)/V(0). No assertion here is uniform for b growing with n.

The normalized V and W quotients in the original degree-two source are correct. Their denominators are nonzero on a fixed disk around zero, uniformly in n. The exact reference identity W(0)/V(0)=(-1)^(n+1) ε_n(1+α_n/b_n)/2 also agrees with the independently derived projection formula.
