Independent derivation for the leading-term audit; not a registry approval.

Write K_n(r_1,...,r_d)=det(T_(n,j)(t^(r_i)F_n)) with rows i=1,...,d and columns j=0,...,d-1. Expanding F_n separately in each row gives a sum over k_1,...,k_d. For x_i=r_i+k_i, extraction of 1/(n+x_i+1)! from row i leaves the monic polynomials (n+x_i+1)_j in ascending column degrees. Their determinant is V(x)=product_(i<h)(x_h-x_i), with positive orientation. Thus there is no row-dependent sign in the exact factorial kernel.

After multiplication by (n!)^d n^(d+sum r_i), each summand is bounded in absolute value by
product_i [C^(k_i)(1+k_i)^(d-1)/k_i!] product_i (1+r_i)^(d-1).
This bound is summable independently of n. Microscopic convergence supplies [t^k]F_n/n^k → (-c)^k/k!, while the normalized factorial ratios tend to one for fixed k_i,r_i. Consequently the fixed-shift limit is the sum of V(r+k) against the product weights (-c)^k/k!.

To evaluate that sum independently, define the linear operator L on polynomials by L(q)(r)=exp(c) sum_(k≥0)(-c)^k q(r+k)/k!. It preserves the degree and leading coefficient of every polynomial. Applying L separately to the rows of the Vandermonde matrix therefore preserves its determinant: the columns remain monic polynomials of degrees 0,...,d-1. The fixed-shift limit is exactly exp(-dc)V(r). At r=(0,1,...,d-1), V(r)=product_(j=0)^(d-1)j!.

For general analytic factors G_(i,n), expand each row in its Taylor shifts. Repeated shifts give identical kernel rows and vanish exactly. The minimum sum of d distinct nonnegative shifts is S=d(d-1)/2. Summing the permutations of 0,...,d-1 produces the coefficient determinant E_n times K_n(0,...,d-1), with no additional factorial or sign. If the Taylor coefficients are bounded by MR^(-r), all other tuples contribute at most a constant times
n^S sum_(m≥S+1) binom(m+d-1,d-1)(1+m)^(d(d-1))(nR)^(-m)=O(1/n).
This establishes the full-factorial normalization (n!)^d n^(d+S), and distinguishes its uniform Taylor-tail error from the unspecified convergence rates of the leading factors.

For the endpoint constants, put alpha=f'(0)=-sqrt(2), s=(1+sqrt(2))^(-2), A=1-s, and x=f-1. In the x coordinate, the high rows are (1+x)^j, j=0,...,b-2. Their initial coefficient block is triangular with determinant one, and their coefficients in the remaining columns vanish. The denominator coefficient determinant in this coordinate is therefore (-1)^(b-1)A^(-(b-1)). The numerator reduces to the final two rows A/(A+x) and 2/(2+x), whose coefficients in degrees b-1,b have determinant
(2-A)/(2^b A^b)=(1+s)/(2^b A^b).
Changing coordinates back to t multiplies a coefficient determinant of size d by alpha^(d(d-1)/2). Hence
E_V=alpha^(b(b-1)/2)(-1)^(b-1)/A^(b-1),
E_N=alpha^(b(b+1)/2)(1+s)/(2^b A^b).
Both are nonzero. Since (1+s)/A=sqrt(2), their quotient is E_N/E_V=-2^((1-b)/2). For b=1 the high rows are absent, and the formulas give E_V=1 and E_N=-1 directly.

The cofactor sign is also fixed by the displayed ordering. In D_V, the row of ones is row b in one-based indexing. Successive column differences, performed from right to left, turn that row into (1,0,...,0) without changing the determinant. Expansion along it contributes (-1)^(b+1), leaving the difference determinant with high rows followed by V.

Apply the full-factorial result to N with dimension b+1, and the published difference lemma to the remaining determinant in D_V with dimension b. The amplitude quotient is W(0); the factorial quotient is 1/n!; the power difference is (b+1)(b+2)/2-b(b-1)/2=2b+1. The microscopic constants contribute exp(-sqrt(2)) and the products of factorials contribute b!. Including the cofactor sign and E_N/E_V yields
n! n^(2b+1) N/[D_V W(0)] → (-1)^b b! 2^((1-b)/2) exp(-sqrt(2)).
The first three constants are respectively -exp(-sqrt(2)), sqrt(2)exp(-sqrt(2)), and -3exp(-sqrt(2)).
