Unverified working component for the fixed-b matched endpoint transfer.

Fix b≥1 and n≥b. Set F(z)=4 arctan(z/(2−z)), and consider R=A+Be^z+CF with degree caps (n,b,n), R=O(z^(2n+b+1)), and B(1)=C(1)=Y. Write B(z)=Σ_{j=0}^b B_jz^j and C*(t)=t^nC(1/t).

Let ℒ(P)=∫_{−1}^1P((1+iu)/2)du and p_k(t)=i^kP_k(−i(2t−1))/binom(2k,k), where P_k is the Legendre polynomial. These monic rational polynomials have bilinear norms h_k=2(−1)^k/((2k+1)binom(2k,k)^2)≠0. Define
ℓ_j(t^r)=1/(n+r+1−j)!, ℓ_B=Σ_j B_jℓ_j,
K_n(t,s)=Σ_{k=0}^n p_k(t)p_k(s)/h_k,
V(t)=K_n(t,1),
H_n(t)=Σ_{k=0}^n ℒ(p_k/(1−t))p_k(t)/h_k,
W(t)=1/(1−t)−H_n(t).
Here H_n is the projection polynomial, not an arithmetic auxiliary sequence. Factorial summation extends each ℓ_j to every germ analytic on a disk of positive radius, with absolute convergence.

Exact finite reduction. The high Taylor equations are ℒ(t^rC*)=−ℓ_B(t^r), r=0,…,n+b−1. Nonzero norms give
C*=−Σ_{k=0}^n ℓ_B(p_k)p_k/h_k.
The remaining equations are ℓ_B(p_{n+l})=0 for l=1,…,b−1 and ℓ_B(V)+B(1)=0. Taylor reconstruction gives A=−T_n(Be^z+CF). Thus these are exactly b homogeneous rational equations in b+1 coefficients, without a rank assumption.

Complete remainder, including both tails. At z=1, the exponential tail beyond degree n equals ℓ_B(1/(1−t)). The logarithmic tail equals ℒ(C*/(1−t)): this follows by summing t^r along the moment segment, where |t|≤1/√2. Substitution of the finite projection makes this second contribution −ℓ_B(H_n). Hence exactly R(1)=ℓ_B(W). This is a complete convergent-tail identity, not an estimate of one Taylor coefficient.

Cofactors and rank. Let u_l=(ℓ_j(p_{n+l}))_{j=0}^b, e=(1,…,1), v=(ℓ_j(V)), w=(ℓ_j(W)), and M=[u_1;…;u_{b−1};e+v]. Choose the signed maximal-cofactor vector B so that B·z=det[M;z]. Then
Y=−det[U;e;v],
R(1)=det[U;e;w]+det[U;v;w],
where U denotes the ordered high rows. If det[U;e;v]≠0, then det[M;e]≠0, so M has rank b and B is a nonzero generator of its kernel. Thus this same endpoint determinant proves both projective uniqueness and Y≠0. No separate perturbative rank argument is necessary for that implication.

Exact reference normalization. Put χ_k=ℒ(p_k/(1−t)), U_0(t)=p_{n+1}(t), b_n=p_{n+1}(1)/p_n(1), and α_n=χ_{n+1}/χ_n. The ordinary Padé identity gives χ_n=(−1)^np_n(1)ε_n with ε_n>0, so α_n is defined. Symmetry gives p_k(0)=(−1)^kp_k(1). Christoffel–Darboux gives
V(t)=p_n(1)[U_0(t)−b_np_n(t)]/[h_n(t−1)]
and, after applying ℒ, p_{n+1}(1)χ_n−p_n(1)χ_{n+1}=h_n.
Consequently
W(t)=[χ_nU_0(t)−χ_{n+1}p_n(t)]/[h_n(1−t)].
To verify the latter without assuming it: its numerator divided by h_n equals 1 at t=1, so subtracting this expression from 1/(1−t) gives a polynomial of degree at most n. Its moment against every polynomial of degree at most n vanishes, by subtracting the test polynomial’s value at 1 and using orthogonality. It is therefore exactly the projection residual W.

In particular,
V(0)=−2p_n(1)U_0(0)/h_n>0,
W(0)/V(0)=(−1)^(n+1)ε_n(1+α_n/b_n)/2.
Whenever W(0)≠0, the normalized factors in V=V(0)(U_0/U_0(0))G_{V,n} and W=W(0)(U_0/U_0(0))G_{W,n} are exactly
G_{V,n}=[1−b_np_n/U_0]/[2(1−t)],
G_{W,n}=[1−α_np_n/U_0]/[(1+α_n/b_n)(1−t)].
These formulas identify all normalization factors explicitly.

Analytic dependency retained for independent audit. For fixed d, let a degree-(n+1) polynomial U_0 have U_0(0)≠0, reciprocal roots bounded uniformly, and U_0(z/n)/U_0(0)→exp(−cz) locally uniformly. Suppose P_i=a_i(U_0/U_0(0))G_{i,n}, with a_i≠0 and G_{i,n} uniformly analytic and bounded on a common fixed disk, converging locally to G_i. For D_j=ℓ_{j+1}−ℓ_j and S=d(d−1)/2, the needed factorial determinant statement is
(n!)^d n^S det[D_j(P_i)]/∏_i a_i → exp(−dc)(∏_{j=0}^{d−1}j!)det[[t^j]G_i].
The cancellation-scale estimate and summable Taylor remainder are essential parts of this dependency. A nonzero limiting Taylor determinant alone cannot replace it.

Application hypotheses and nonzero conditions. Put ρ=1+√2, κ=ρ^(−1), s=κ², a=ρ/4, and
λ(t)=(t−1/2−sqrt((t−1/2)^2+1/4))/2,
h(t)=λ(t)/λ(0).
The branch has λ(0)=−a. The required reference limits are p_{n+1}/p_n→λ uniformly on a fixed disk, U_0(z/n)/U_0(0)→exp(−√2z), b_n→a, and α_n→−as. The root bound is directly visible from the Legendre zeros: roots of U_0 have modulus at least 1/2. Since α_n/b_n=−ε_{n+1}/ε_n, its claimed limit also follows from the published ordinary prefactor. In particular 1+α_n/b_n→1−s>0, so W(0)≠0 eventually.

Under the stated uniform reference limits, the normalized high rows tend to 1,h,…,h^(b−2), while
G_V=(1−s)/(h−s), G_W=2/(h+1), h(0)=1, h′(0)=−√2.
Changing local variable to h−1 shows that both b-row Taylor determinants are nonzero. Their ratio is ((1−s)/2)^(b−1)=κ^(b−1), including b=1 with an empty high-row list. The factorial determinant lemma then gives eventual nonzero det[U;e;v] and
 det[U;e;w]/det[U;e;v] ∼ (W(0)/V(0))κ^(b−1).

The remaining full determinant requires a factorial smallness estimate. Uniform analytic bounds give |ℓ_j(P_i)|≤C n^j|a_i|/n! for fixed j. Comparing the resulting determinant bound with the nonzero denominator asymptotic yields
 det[U;v;w]/det[U;e;v] = (W(0)/V(0))O(n^(b²)|V(0)|/n!).
The reference coefficient and norm bounds give |V(0)|=exp(O(n)), so this term is negligible. Conditional on the audited factorial lemma and uniform reference hypotheses, the exact identities therefore give
