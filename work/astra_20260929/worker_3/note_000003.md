Independent refinement of the ordinary Padé error.

Let C*_{0,n}=p_n/p_n(1), reverse it to obtain C_{0,n}, and set f_n=[T_n(C_{0,n}F)](1). Define K_n=binom(2n,n)p_n(1)=i^nP_n(−i)>0. Directly summing the Taylor tail gives

π−f_n=ℒ(p_n(t)/(1−t))/p_n(1).

The polynomial (p_n(1)−p_n(t))/(1−t) has degree at most n−1. Its product with p_n has zero moment, so the last expression equals ℒ(p_n(t)^2/(1−t))/p_n(1)^2. Substituting t=(1+iu)/2 and integrating the odd imaginary part to zero proves

π−f_n=2(−1)^n I_n/K_n²,   I_n=∫_{−1}^1 P_n(u)^2/(1+u²)du.

This also proves the error is nonzero for every n.

First evaluate K_n. Its generating function, obtained from the Legendre recurrence, is

Σ_{n≥0}K_nt^n=(1−2t−t²)^(−1/2)=(1−ρt)^(−1/2)(1+ρ^(−1)t)^(−1/2).

Put a_j=binom(2j,j)/4^j. Coefficient convolution gives

K_n/(ρ^na_n)=Σ_{j=0}^n(−1)^j a_jρ^(−2j)a_{n−j}/a_n.

For fixed j the last ratio tends to 1. The elementary bounds a_m comparable to (m+1)^(−1/2) show that the terms j≤n/2 are dominated by a summable geometric sequence. The absolute sum over j>n/2 is O(√n ρ^(−n)), hence tends to zero. Therefore the displayed sum tends to (1+ρ^(−2))^(−1/2). Stirling’s formula gives

K_n²~ρ^(2n)/(πn(1+ρ^(−2))).

Next evaluate I_n without using oscillatory asymptotic formulas. The normalized Legendre polynomials ℓ_n(u)=√((2n+1)/2)P_n(u) satisfy

uℓ_n=a_{n+1}^{L}ℓ_{n+1}+a_n^{L}ℓ_{n−1},   a_n^{L}=n/√((2n−1)(2n+1))→1/2.

For each fixed integer m, expansion of this recurrence m times expresses ∫u^mℓ_n(u)^2du as a sum over paths returning to index n. For n>m there is no boundary contribution. Odd moments are zero; for m=2k the limit is binom(2k,k)/4^k. These are exactly the moments of the probability measure du/(π√(1−u²)) on [−1,1]. Polynomial approximation, together with the fact that all these measures have mass one, extends convergence to the continuous function g(u)=1/(1+u²). Its limiting integral is

(1/π)∫_0^π dθ/(1+cos²θ)=1/√2.

Consequently I_n~1/(√2 n). Combining the two asymptotics and using 1+ρ^(−2)=2√2/ρ yields the rigorous refinement

ε_n:=|π−f_n|=2I_n/K_n²~(4π/ρ)ρ^(−2n).

Transfer to the matched family.

The previously audited fixed-b theorem supplies

(−1)^n R(1)/(Yε_n)→(√2−1)^b=ρ^(−b)>0.

Using that theorem as the fixed-b input, the new calculation gives

R(1)/Y=(−1)^n κ_bρ^(−2n)(1+o(1)),   κ_b=4π/ρ^(b+1)>0.

Thus Y and the endpoint error are eventually nonzero, and the normalized error has sign (−1)^n. Every unbounded subsequence inherits this estimate. Multiplication by q_n preserves the relative error even if q_n grows rapidly:

|L_n|=κ_b(q_n/ρ^(2n))(1+o(1)).

For b=2, κ_2=4π/ρ³. Hence shrinking along a prescribed subsequence is equivalent to q_n=o(ρ^(2n)) there. A sufficient condition is infinitely many n satisfying q_n≤exp((2logρ−δ)n), δ>0. If q_n/ρ^(2n)→d∈(0,∞), then |L_n|→κ_bd, so this sequence does not shrink. The condition (log q_n)/n→2logρ allows both this behavior and shrinking; its equality case cannot be decided without finer denominator information.
