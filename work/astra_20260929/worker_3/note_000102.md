UNVERIFIED AUTHOR DERIVATION: five-adic numerator unit on the residue-three progression.

Use exactly the definitions in the two assigned published originals. Write v=v_5, r=v(n!), f=2^n/(n!)², and assume n≥8 with n≡3 (mod 5). Let N=n+1, so N≡4 and k=N²≡1. The exact contractions are
D=kbC−2aS,
X/f=2(P*/f)S−k(U*/f)C−2ηW.
The rational identities apply at 5 without invoking the separate large-prime ideal theorem.

1. Auxiliary derivatives and contractions.

The original's falling-factorial interpolation proves coefficientwise that terms with u+2v≥5 vanish modulo 5 on every disk a+5Z_5. Indeed, putting h=floor((u+2v)/5) and ℓ=floor((u+v)/5), its valuation bound is h−v_5(ℓ!)≥h−v_5(h!), which is at least one for h≥1 and tends to infinity. Retained denominators are units, so their reductions depend only on the residue of the index.

Directly from the defining polynomial,
H_3(x)=x³−9x²+27x−24,
H_4(x)=x⁴−16x³+96x²−240x+204.
Thus (H_3(1),H'_3(1),H''_3(1))≡(0,2,3), and the corresponding triple at index four is (0,3,3). It follows on the entire progression that
(h_n,J_n,η,J_{n+1},K_{n+1})≡(0,2,0,3,2),
(S,C,W,k)≡(4,1,1,1).

The published Legendre endpoint digit identity A_{5m+d}≡c_d A_m, c=(1,2,3,2,1), shows that a and b are units and b≡3a. Consequently D≡b−8a≡0. This congruence alone does not prove D≠0.

2. Complete exponential contractions.

Put c_{m,t}=[z^t](1−z+z²/2)^m and e_j=j!E_j. The exact Rodrigues sums in the original become
T_0(P)/f=Σ_{t=0}^n (n)_t c_{n,t}e_{2n−t},
T_0(U)/f=(2/N²)Σ_{t=0}^N (N)_t(2N−t)c_{N,t}e_{2N−1−t}.
These are exact rational identities, including t=0 in the second sum. Their denominators are five-adic units. The recurrence e_j=je_{j−1}+1 gives residues (1,2,0,1,0) for j≡0,1,2,3,4 modulo 5.

For P, every t≥4 term contains the factor n−3 in (n)_t and vanishes modulo 5. The four retained t=0,1,2,3 terms have:
(n)_t ≡ (1,3,1,1),
c_{n,t} ≡ (1,2,2,1),
e_{2n−t} ≡ (2,1,0,1).
Their contributions are (2,1,0,1), so T_0(P)/f≡4.

For U, every t≥5 term contains N−4 in (N)_t. The five retained t=0,1,2,3,4 terms have:
(N)_t ≡ (1,4,2,4,4),
c_{N,t} ≡ (1,1,3,0,1),
2N−t ≡ (3,2,1,0,4),
e_{2N−1−t} ≡ (0,2,1,0,1).
Before multiplication by 2/N²≡2, their contributions are (0,1,1,0,1). Thus T_0(U)/f≡1. These lists retain every potentially surviving term; no truncation is inferred from numerical samples.

3. Normalized moment estimate.

The quotient polynomials defining w_P,w_U have integral coefficients and degrees at most n. The exact monomial moment formula gives v(μ_j)≥−v(j+1). Since n+1≡4, floor(log_5(n+1))=floor(log_5 n)=:L. Hence v(w_P),v(w_U)≥−L. The factor 5^L occurs in n!, so r≥L≥1. Therefore
v(w_P/f),v(w_U/f)≥2r−L≥1.
Only after this estimate may the normalized moments be discarded. We obtain P*/f≡4 and U*/f≡1.

4. Actual numerator and denominator.

Retaining the correction term gives
X/f≡2·4·4−1·1·1−2·0·1≡1 (mod 5).
Thus X≠0 and v(X)=−2r. For D≠0, rational reduction, without any integrality assumption on X, gives
v(q_n)=max(0,v(D)−v(X))=2r+v(D).
Because D is an integer divisible by 5, this is at least 2r+1. In particular L_5(n)=max(0,2r−v(q_n))=0.

The published claim w3-b2-eventual-d-nonzero-v1 identifies this same D, at the same index, with a nonzero scalar multiple of the matched endpoint and proves eventual nonvanishing. Combining that explicit dependency with the present calculation yields zero five-adic loss for every sufficiently large n≡3 modulo 5. No higher-congruence estimate for v(D) is necessary for this lower-bound conclusion. A claim covering every n≥8 would still require all-index nonvanishing or an explicit nonzero higher congruence.
