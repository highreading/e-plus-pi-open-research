Supplementary independent quantifier audit (unreviewed).

Let α₁,…,αᵣ be distinct elements of Z₂, with r≥2, and put T=max_{i≠j}v₂(αᵢ−αⱼ). This is a finite nonnegative integer. For an ordinary integer n distinct from every αᵢ, write dᵢ=v₂(n−αᵢ) and D=maxᵢdᵢ. At most one dᵢ exceeds T: otherwise the ultrametric inequality would give v₂(αᵢ−αⱼ)>T. Consequently

D ≤ Σᵢdᵢ ≤ D+(r−1)T.

More precisely, if dⱼ>T, then for every i≠j the two summands in n−αᵢ=(n−αⱼ)+(αⱼ−αᵢ) have unequal valuations. Hence dᵢ=v₂(αⱼ−αᵢ), and

Σᵢdᵢ=dⱼ+Σ_{i≠j}v₂(αⱼ−αᵢ).

Thus a product of finitely many distinct root factors has, at sufficiently deep approximation indices, exactly one varying depth plus a fixed contribution from the other roots. Exact coincidences n=αᵢ are excluded from the finite-valued identities and account for at most r integer indices.

In particular, for distinct α,β and positive slopes c₁,c₂, any common positive integer witness satisfying v₂(n−α)≥c₁n and v₂(n−β)≥c₂n must obey n≤v₂(α−β)/min(c₁,c₂). There cannot be infinitely many common witnesses. This does not prevent each root from having its own infinite qualifying subsequence.

These are general ultrametric identities. Applying them to endpoint contractions would additionally require an exact factorization, identification of the relevant roots, and control of every remaining factor. None of those application-specific dependencies is certified here, and this supplement does not enlarge the immutable candidate under review.
