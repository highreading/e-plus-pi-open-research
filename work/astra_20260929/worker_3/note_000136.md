Unreviewed author addendum: a sharper whole-family digit bound.

Let s_B(n) be the sum of the base-B digits of the nonnegative integer n, with B≥2. For each digit 0≤d≤B−1, convexity gives B^{d/(B−1)}≤d+1. Writing n=Σd_jB^j therefore gives
B^{s_B(n)/(B−1)}≤∏_j(d_j+1)≤n+1.
The last inequality follows by counting the distinct integers Σe_jB^j with 0≤e_j≤d_j: every such integer belongs to {0,…,n}.

Now retain exactly the endpoint conventions and eventual hypotheses of w3-b2-whole-family-obstruction-v1: p_n/q_n=−X_n/D_n in lowest terms, q_n>0, D_n≠0, and
v_3(q_n)≥2v_3(n!),   v_5(q_n)≥2v_5(n!)−1.
At the stated arithmetic range n≥max(14,N_D), both lower exponents are nonnegative. Unique factorization and Legendre’s formula yield
q_n≥3^{n−s_3(n)}5^{(n−s_5(n))/2−1}
=(3√5)^n/[5·3^{s_3(n)}·5^{s_5(n)/2}].
The digit inequality gives 3^{s_3(n)}≤(n+1)^2 and 5^{s_5(n)/2}≤(n+1)^2, proving
q_n≥(3√5)^n/[5(n+1)^4].

For the same matched b=2 approximants, the published analytic transfer and ordinary Padé prefactor give
|e+π−p_n/q_n|=(4π/ρ³)ρ^{−2n}(1+o(1)).
Thus, beyond a further unspecified threshold,
|q_n(e+π)−p_n|≥[2π/(5ρ³)](n+1)^{−4}[3√5/ρ²]^n.
The base exceeds one: 3√5>6>3+2√2=ρ². Hence these forms, and any sequence of their nonzero integer multiples, diverge in absolute value.
