For fixed c>0, positive odd m, and residue a modulo m, let E(c;m,a) consist of α∈Z₂ satisfying v₂(n−α)≥cn for infinitely many positive integers n≡a (mod m). Set k_n=ceil(cn) and B_n=n+2^{k_n}Z₂. Then E(c;m,a)=⋂_{N≥1}⋃_{n≥N, n≡a (mod m)}B_n. The ceiling is correct because the valuation is integer-valued, with v₂(0)=∞.

Each tail union is open and dense. Indeed, for any basic disk b+2^hZ₂, coprimality of m and 2^h gives arbitrarily large positive integers n satisfying both n≡a (mod m) and n≡b (mod 2^h). Choose such an n with n≥N and k_n≥h. Then B_n is contained in that basic disk. The countable intersection is therefore a dense Gδ by the Baire theorem for the complete space Z₂. Infinitely many eligible indices necessarily tend to infinity; a single exact equality α=n does not establish membership.

For normalized Haar measure, μ(B_n)=2^{-k_n}≤2^{-cn}. Hence every tail cover has measure at most Σ_{n≥N}2^{-cn}, which tends to zero. This proves Haar nullity without an independence assumption. In the standard dyadic metric, diam(B_n)=2^{-k_n}. For each real s>0, the same tail covers have maximal diameter tending to zero and total s-cost at most Σ_{n≥N}2^{-scn}→0. Thus the s-dimensional Hausdorff measure vanishes for every s>0, proving dimension zero. Density supplies nonemptiness.

These arguments verify the candidate's fixed-parameter theorem. Simultaneous validity for every positive slope and every odd-modulus progression requires an additional countable intersection: intersect E(j;m,a) over positive integers j, positive odd m, and 0≤a<m. Integer slopes suffice by monotonicity, since each real c>0 is bounded above by an integer j. This extension remains residual and is contained in E(1;1,0), so it remains Haar-null and of dimension zero. It does not require the witnessing indices for different progressions or slopes to coincide.

For a strict inequality v₂(n−α)>cn, the correct exponent is floor(cn)+1. The same proofs apply, but the strict and non-strict approximation sets should not be identified merely because their thresholds differ by at most one.
