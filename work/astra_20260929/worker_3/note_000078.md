UNREVIEWED supplementary consequence; not an independently approved claim.

Let α=e+π, ρ=1+√2, and let r_(n,b) be the rational endpoint approximant under the consistent convention r=A_minus(1)/Y=−A_plus(1)/Y. The approved fixed-b transfer and published ordinary Padé prefactor give, for each fixed b≥1,

α−r_(n,b)=(-1)^n C_b ρ^(-2n)(1+o(1)),   C_b=4π/ρ^(b+1).

All statements below concern sufficiently large n, when the relevant endpoints are nonzero. Fix b and set u_n=r_(n,b+2), v_n=r_(n+1,b). Since C_b/ρ²=C_(b+2),

α−u_n=(-1)^n C_(b+2)ρ^(-2n)(1+o(1)),
α−v_n=−(-1)^n C_(b+2)ρ^(-2n)(1+o(1)).

Consequently the rational midpoint m_n=(u_n+v_n)/2 satisfies

α−m_n=o(ρ^(-2n)).

No rate sharper than little-o follows merely by subtracting these two asymptotics. Meanwhile,

v_n−u_n=2(-1)^n C_(b+2)ρ^(-2n)(1+o(1)),

so the two inputs are eventually distinct. This establishes no nonvanishing statement for α−m_n.

Write u_n=a_n/q_n and v_n=c_n/s_n in lowest terms, with positive denominators, and put D_n=lcm(q_n,s_n). Then

m_n=T_n/(2D_n),   T_n=a_n D_n/q_n+c_n D_n/s_n ∈ Z.

Its actual positive reduced denominator is exactly

Q_n=2D_n/gcd(T_n,2D_n).

Also D_n(v_n−u_n) is a nonzero integer eventually, yielding

liminf D_n/ρ^(2n) ≥ 1/(2C_(b+2)) = ρ^(b+3)/(8π).

Thus an upper bound D_n=O(ρ^(2n)), if separately proved, would lie at the natural scale allowed by this elementary lower bound. It would imply Q_n|α−m_n|→0. To turn that shrinking statement into an irrationality argument, one must additionally prove α−m_n≠0 along an unbounded subsequence: under α=p/t, the quantities tQ_n(α−m_n) would then be nonzero integers tending to zero. Without nonvanishing, rationality could instead force eventual exact equality of the midpoints with α.

This is an algebraic consequence of the established asymptotic, not a new denominator estimate or a proof concerning the rationality of α.
