UNVERIFIED INDEPENDENT DERIVATION: INTEGER COEFFICIENTS DO NOT CONTROL DYADIC ROOT DEPTH

Fix any β∈1+2Z₂. There exist digits a_n∈{0,1}, n≥2, such that
G(Y)=Y−1+Σ_{n≥2} a_n 2^{n−1}Y^n
vanishes at β. This is a restricted power series on Z₂, and every coefficient is an ordinary integer.

Construction: put G₁(Y)=Y−1. Inductively suppose G_{n−1}(β) is divisible by 2^{n−1}. Because β^n is odd, exactly one a_n∈{0,1} makes
G_{n−1}(β)/2^{n−1}+a_nβ^n≡0 (mod 2).
Then G_n(Y)=G_{n−1}(Y)+a_n2^{n−1}Y^n satisfies G_n(β)∈2^nZ₂. Uniform convergence gives G(β)=0.

Moreover,
G(Y)=(Y−β)U(Y),
U(Y)=1+Σ_{n≥2}a_n2^{n−1}Σ_{j=0}^{n−1}Y^{n−1−j}β^j.
The displayed series for U converges in the Gauss norm, and U∈1+2Z₂⟨Y⟩. Consequently U(y) is a unit for every y∈Z₂, β is the unique root in Z₂, and
v₂(G(y))=v₂(y−β)
for y≠β. Also G′(y)≡1 (mod 2).

An explicit choice permits extremely deep ordinary-integer approximations. Set b₁=2, n_j=1+Σ_{k=1}^j2^{b_k}, and b_{j+1}=n_j². Then β=1+Σ_{k≥1}2^{b_k} is an odd dyadic integer and
v₂(n_j−β)=b_{j+1}=n_j².
Indeed the first omitted binary digit has position b_{j+1}, and all later positions are larger. The series G constructed above therefore satisfies v₂(G(n_j))=n_j² along an unbounded sequence of positive integers.

For completeness, this β is transcendental over Q: if a nonzero P∈Z[x] of degree d vanished at β, division by X−β in Z₂[X] would give v₂(n−β)≤v₂(P(n)) whenever P(n)≠0. With A the sum of the absolute coefficients, v₂(P(n))≤log₂|P(n)|≤d log₂n+log₂A for n≥1. Only finitely many n_j can be integer roots of P, so the displayed quadratic depth is impossible.

Scope: this does not show that the actual exceptional root is transcendental or admits deep approximation. It proves that ordinary integer coefficients and simple-root factorization alone cannot supply the missing depth bound.
