Unreviewed independent audit of the full-cofactor estimate.

Let d≥1 be fixed, S=d(d−1)/2, F_n(0)=1, deg F_n≤n+1, and suppose every reciprocal root of F_n has modulus at most L. Let P_i=a_i F_n G_i, where a_i≠0 and G_i is analytic with |G_i(t)|≤M on a fixed disk |t|<ρ. Define ℓ_j(P)=Σ_{k≥0}[t^k]P/(n+k+1−j)!, for 0≤j<d and sufficiently large n. Then

|det(ℓ_j(P_i))|≤C|∏a_i|/((n!)^d n^(S+d)).

Verification: writing F_n=Σu_k t^k, the root factorization gives |u_k|≤binom(n+1,k)L^k≤n^k(2L)^k/k!. For fixed shifts r_i≥0, multilinearity expresses the determinant of the rows ℓ_j(t^(r_i)F_n) as a sum over k_i. With X_i=n+k_i+r_i+1, its factorial matrix has determinant

[∏_i1/X_i!] det((X_i)_j)=[∏_i1/X_i!]∏_{i<l}(k_l+r_l−k_i−r_i).

The falling factorials are monic of consecutive degrees, so there is no omitted factor. Using n!/X_i!≤n^(−k_i−r_i−1) and

|k_l+r_l−k_i−r_i|≤(1+k_i+r_i)(1+k_l+r_l),

the sum is bounded by C_0(n!)^(−d)n^(−Σr_i−d)∏_i(1+r_i)^(d−1). Indeed, 1+k+r≤(1+k)(1+r), and all remaining sums Σ_k(2L)^k(1+k)^(d−1)/k! converge.

Cauchy's estimate gives |[t^r]G_i|≤Mρ^(−r). Tuples with repeated r_i vanish because the corresponding monomial-shift rows coincide. Every surviving tuple has Σr_i≥S. Grouping by m=Σr_i therefore leaves, after extracting the asserted scale, at most a constant times

n^S Σ_{m≥S} binom(m+d−1,d−1)(1+m)^(d(d−1))(nρ)^(−m).

For nρ≥2 this is uniformly bounded: put m=S+h and factor out ρ^(−S), leaving a polynomially weighted geometric series in h. These absolute bounds also justify the multilinear expansions. This proves the general estimate independently of any microscopic limit.

Application audit, with hypotheses retained: let T contain b−1 high rows, D=det[T;e;v], N=det[T;v;w], and H=∏_{l=1}^{b−1}p_(n+l)(0). If their common reference is F_n=p_(n+1)/p_(n+1)(0), their analytic factors are uniformly bounded, and

|D|≥c|HV(0)|/((n!)^b n^(b(b−1)/2)),

then the proved estimate with d=b+1 yields

|N/D|≤C|W(0)|/(n! n^(2b+1)).

When W(0)≠0, its size relative to |W(0)/V(0)| is at most C|V(0)|/(n! n^(2b+1)). Thus eventual nonvanishing of the principal coefficient ratio makes this cofactor negligible relative to the principal remainder as well.
