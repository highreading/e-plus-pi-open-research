Unverified independent audit supplement: quantitative reference-polynomial hypotheses.

Scope: this checks the recurrence and microscopic estimates in work/astra_20260929/main/note_000114.md. It is not a registry approval or a verification of the complete matched-endpoint theorem.

The original normalization is p_k(t)=i^k P_k(−i(2t−1))/binom(2k,k), with P_k the Legendre polynomial. Substitution into the Legendre recurrence gives exactly p_0=1, p_1=t−1/2 and
p_(k+1)=(t−1/2)p_k+β_k p_(k−1),  β_k=k²/[4(4k²−1)].
Thus the polynomial indexing in the supplement agrees with the original reference U_n=p_(n+1).

1. Zeros and analytic quotients.

Let T_m=(1/2)I+iS_m, where S_m is real symmetric tridiagonal with off-diagonal entries √β_1,…,√β_(m−1). Characteristic-determinant expansion gives det(tI−T_m)=p_m(t): the squared off-diagonal factor is −β_k, producing the required plus sign in the recurrence. Every eigenvalue of T_m has real part 1/2. Therefore every p_m is nonzero on |t|<1/2, and every reciprocal root has modulus at most 2.

For q_n=p_n/p_(n+1), the last diagonal resolvent entry is q_n. Normality of T_(n+1) implies |q_n(t)|≤(1/2−r)^−1 on |t|≤r<1/2. On r=1/10 this is 5/2, including q_0.

The recurrence gives q_n^−1=t−1/2+β_n q_(n−1). Since β_n≤1/12,
|q_n^−1|≤3/5+(1/12)(5/2)=97/120.
For q_0 the stronger bound is 3/5. Consequently |q_n|≥120/97 uniformly on this disk. Both numerator and denominator polynomials, and the quotient itself, are nonzero there.

2. Quantitative convergence.

Writing d_n=||q_n−q_(n−1)||, subtraction of the reciprocal recurrences gives

d_n≤(25/48)d_(n−1)+(125/8)|β_n−β_(n−1)|.

The constants are respectively (5/2)²/12 and (5/2)³. The successive β differences are summable, so summing the inequality proves Σd_n<∞. Thus q_n converges uniformly to an analytic q satisfying q(t)(t−1/2+q(t)/16)=1.

At zero every q_n is negative: p_k(1)>0 follows by induction, and p_k(0)=(−1)^k p_k(1). The lower bound excludes a zero limit. Hence
q(t)=−1/[a f(t)],  a=(1+√2)/4,
f(t)=[1−2t+√((1−2t)²+1)]/(1+√2),
with the square root positive at zero. In particular f(0)=1 and f′(0)=−√2.

The same subtraction against q gives, for e_n=||q_n−q||,
e_n≤(25/48)e_(n−1)+(125/8)|β_n−1/16|,
where β_n−1/16=1/[16(4n²−1)]. Iteration yields a geometric convolution with O(n^−2) forcing. Splitting the sum at n/2 bounds its early part exponentially and its late part by O(n^−2), proving e_n=O(n^−2). Cauchy's formula gives the same rate for each fixed derivative on any smaller disk.

3. The product estimate.

On a smaller disk define the analytic logarithm L_j(t)=log(q_j(0)/q_j(t)), normalized by L_j(0)=0. Such logarithms exist because the disk is simply connected and the quotients are nonzero. The uniform upper and lower bounds, together with Cauchy bounds for q_j′ and q_j″, give a uniform bound for L_j″. Moreover,
L_j′(0)=−q_j′(0)/q_j(0)=−√2+O(j^−2),  j≥1.
The j=0 term is a fixed finite contribution. Therefore
Σ_(j=0)^n L_j′(0)=−√2(n+1)+O(1).

The exact telescoping product is
p_(n+1)(t)/p_(n+1)(0)=∏_(j=0)^n q_j(0)/q_j(t).
For |z|≤R and sufficiently large n, uniform Taylor remainders now give
Σ_(j=0)^n L_j(z/n)=−√2z+O_R(n^−1).
Exponentiation proves
p_(n+1)(z/n)/p_(n+1)(0)=exp(−√2z)(1+O_R(n^−1)).
This holds on every fixed compact subset of the complex plane. No unjustified exchange between a pointwise quotient limit and a growing product is needed.

4. Coefficients and fixed offsets.

The reciprocal-root bound gives
|[t^j](p_(n+1)(t)/p_(n+1)(0))|≤binom(n+1,j)2^j.
In particular, after division by n^j the bound is 4^j/j! for n≥1, and the microscopic polynomial is bounded by (1+2|z|/n)^(n+1).

For each fixed k≥0, the normalized quotient with p_(n+1+k) is exactly ∏_(j=n+1)^(n+k)q_j(0)/q_j(t). Uniform lower bounds permit division, and the quantitative convergence above gives f(t)^k+O_k(n^−2) on a fixed smaller disk. Every fixed Taylor coefficient has the same rate. These statements are not uniform for growing k.
