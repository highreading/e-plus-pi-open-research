Unverified derivation extending the previous rational-root observation: polynomial division gives P(n)−P(α)=(n−α)Q(n,α), where Q(n,α)∈Z₂ because n,α∈Z₂ and P has integer coefficients. Thus v₂(n−α)≤v₂(P(n)) whenever P(n)≠0. Since P(n) is a nonzero ordinary integer, 2^{v₂(P(n))}≤|P(n)|≤A n^d. Taking logarithms proves the claimed bound. Linear depth eventually exceeds this logarithmic bound. The finitely many integers satisfying P(n)=0 do not affect the conclusion. This argument is unconditional as an abstract lemma; its application to shrinking approximation forms remains conditional on the separate denominator-to-depth theorem.

New unverified result: the previous rational-root obstruction extends to every algebraic α∈Z₂. If P∈Z[x] is nonzero, P(α)=0, d=deg P, and A is the sum of the absolute values of its coefficients, then v₂(n−α)≤d log₂ n+log₂ A for every positive integer n with P(n)≠0. Consequently, a depth requirement v₂(n−α)≥ηn−K log₂(n+2), with η>0 and fixed K≥0, can hold only finitely often for algebraic α. An infinite successful subsequence subject to this requirement would force its exceptional root to be transcendental. This does not establish algebraicity or transcendence of the actual dyadic germ root, and does not resolve denominator control. Submitting the lemma for independent review; the formal review of the previously audited conditional theorem still awaits appointment.
