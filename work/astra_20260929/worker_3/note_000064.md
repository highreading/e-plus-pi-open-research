UNREVIEWED AUTHOR DERIVATION: leading constant for the additional cofactor.

Retain the definitions and hypotheses of worker3-microscopic-reference-and-sharp-cofactor-bound-v1. In particular, F_n(0)=1, |[t^k]F_n|≤(Cn)^k/k!, and F_n(z/n)→exp(−cz) locally uniformly, with c=sqrt(2) in the application. Let P_i=a_i F_n G_(i,n), where the analytic factors are uniformly bounded on one fixed disk and their first d Taylor coefficients converge. Write E=lim det([t^r]G_(i,n))_(i=1,…,d;r=0,…,d−1), and S_d=d(d−1)/2.

For T_j(t^m)=1/(n+m+1−j)!, j=0,…,d−1, the existing upper-bound argument strengthens to
 det(T_j(P_i)) = (∏a_i)(n!)^(−d)n^(−d(d+1)/2) [exp(−dc)(∏_(j=0)^(d−1)j!)E+o(1)].

Here is the additional limit argument. Cauchy–Binet expands the determinant over strictly increasing Taylor shifts r_1<…<r_d. After multiplying by (n!)^d n^(d+S_d), all shifts other than (0,…,d−1) contribute O(1/n), using the previously proved bound and the common analytic coefficient bounds. For fixed shifts, expand each F_n with indices k_i. The exact factorial Vandermonde identity and coefficient convergence give the limiting kernel
 Σ_(k_1,…,k_d≥0) ∏_i [(-c)^(k_i)/k_i!] · Vandermonde(r_1+k_1,…,r_d+k_d).
The coefficient majorant supplies an absolutely summable dominating series, including the polynomial Vandermonde factor. Thus dominated convergence applies.

For j≥0, the polynomial
 M_j(r)=exp(c) Σ_(k≥0) (-c)^k(r+k)^j/k!
is monic of degree j. Multilinearity therefore evaluates the kernel as exp(−dc)det(M_j(r_i))=exp(−dc)Vandermonde(r_1,…,r_d). At r_i=i−1, this Vandermonde equals ∏_(j=0)^(d−1)j!. This proves the displayed leading term without requiring a microscopic convergence rate.

The same argument also checks the leading normalization for Δ_j=T_(j+1)−T_j. After extracting the row denominator (n+x_i+1)!, the jth column is the polynomial (y_i)_(j+1)−(y_i)_j, y_i=n+x_i+1, where the subscript denotes a falling factorial. Its degree is j+1 and its leading coefficient is one. The determinant divided by Vandermonde(y) is a polynomial of degree d whose highest homogeneous part is ∏y_i. Hence its quotient by n^d tends to one for fixed x_i. Polynomial bounds permit the same dominated-convergence argument. Consequently the Δ determinant has leading normalization (n!)^(−d)n^(−S_d), with the same constant exp(−dc)(∏j!)E. This verifies the extra factor n^d and its sign directly.

For the endpoint application set s=ρ^(−2), A=1−s, ρ=1+sqrt(2), and x=f−1. The normalized rows of N tend, in their original order, to
 1,f,…,f^(b−2), A/(A+x), 2/(2+x).
The polynomial rows are absent when b=1. In the x coordinate, eliminating those rows leaves the two columns of degrees b−1 and b. Their determinant is
 [A^(−b)2^(−b)](2−A)=(1+s)/(2^b A^b).
Changing back to t therefore gives
 E_N=(-sqrt(2))^(b(b+1)/2)(1+s)/(2^b A^b),
which is nonzero for every fixed b≥1. For b=1 this is exactly −1.

The denominator coefficient determinant already identified in the registered claim is

Thus E_N/E_V=−2^((1−b)/2), using (1+s)/(1−s)=sqrt(2). The identity D_V=(−1)^(b+1)det(Δ_j(P_i)), with rows P_1,…,P_(b−1),V, then yields
