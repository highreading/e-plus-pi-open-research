Unverified supplementary derivation, independent of the application-specific denominator formulas.

Fix α∈Z₂ and η>0, and let n₁<n₂<⋯ be positive integers satisfying v₂(nⱼ−α)≥ηnⱼ. For consecutive members, the ultrametric inequality gives
v₂(nⱼ₊₁−nⱼ)≥min(v₂(nⱼ₊₁−α),v₂(nⱼ−α))≥ηnⱼ.
Consequently nⱼ₊₁−nⱼ≥2^{⌈ηnⱼ⌉}, and hence nⱼ₊₁≥2^{ηnⱼ}.

Choose N sufficiently large that η2^{ηx}≥x for every x≥N. Once nⱼ≥N, two successive applications yield
nⱼ₊₂≥2^{ηnⱼ₊₁}≥2^{η2^{ηnⱼ}}≥2^{nⱼ}.
Thus each of the two parity subsequences grows at least by repeated application of x↦2ˣ. Defining log*₂X as the number of iterated base-two logarithms required to reach a value at most 1, the counting function satisfies
#{n≤X:v₂(n−α)≥ηn}≤2log*₂X+O_{α,η}(1).
A finite collection of s fixed roots therefore gives at most 2s log*₂X+O(1) qualifying integers. Any eventual depth bound v₂(n−α)≥δn+o(n), with δ>0, implies this conclusion by taking η=δ/2.

If α=a/b is rational in lowest terms with b odd, then for n≠α,
v₂(n−α)=v₂(bn−a)≤log₂|bn−a|≤log₂n+log₂(|a|+|b|).
This cannot reach ηn for arbitrarily large n. The possible equality n=α contributes only one integer. In particular, an exactly identified rational root cannot sustain the required linear depth along an unbounded subsequence.

These deductions check consequences of the depth hypothesis only. They provide no estimate on approximation to the exceptional nonrational dyadic root and no unconditional reduced-denominator bound.

Added an unverified counting consequence of the conditional depth hypothesis: for a fixed dyadic root α and η>0, integers satisfying v₂(n−α)≥ηn have counting function O(log* X).
