Unverified auxiliary audit: binary-digit characterization of linear dyadic depth.

Fix c>0. Let α∈Z₂ have infinitely many nonzero binary digits, at positions 0≤b₁<b₂<⋯, and define the ordinary positive integers s_j=Σ_{i≤j}2^{b_i}. Then

v₂(s_j−α)=b_{j+1}.

Indeed, α−s_j=2^{b_{j+1}} times an odd dyadic integer. This identity concerns convergent dyadic expansions; it does not rely on real convergence.

Every sufficiently large positive integer n satisfying v₂(n−α)≥cn must equal one of the s_j. To prove this, choose n large enough that cn>log₂n, and put t=v₂(n−α). Since α has infinitely many nonzero digits, α≠n and t is finite. The depth assumption implies 2^t>n. The congruence α≡n modulo 2^t therefore identifies n with the least nonnegative residue of α modulo 2^t, which is a finite binary truncation s_j. Its next nonzero digit is precisely at position t.

Consequently, for any fixed modulus m≥1 and residue a, infinitely many positive integers n≡a modulo m satisfy v₂(n−α)≥cn if and only if infinitely many j satisfy both

s_j≡a modulo m,     b_{j+1}≥ceil(c s_j).

Oddness of m is unnecessary for this characterization; it is needed in the candidate's separate density argument. For the strict inequality v₂(n−α)>cn, the exact threshold is instead b_{j+1}≥floor(c s_j)+1. Thus replacing strict by non-strict inequalities without changing the rounding can alter the approximation set.

The characterization also explains index growth directly. After a qualifying truncation s_j, the next binary truncation is

s_{j+1}=s_j+2^{b_{j+1}}≥s_j+2^{ceil(c s_j)}.

Every later qualifying index is at least s_{j+1}. This recovers exponential separation without assuming that consecutive binary truncations both qualify.

For a fixed progression, having infinitely many qualifying indices for every slope c>0 is equivalent to unbounded ratios b_{j+1}/s_j among j with s_j≡a modulo m. Equivalently, it suffices to require infinitely many qualifying indices for each positive integer slope. This confirms why simultaneous slopes can be handled through a countable intersection. For one fixed slope c, a limsup equal to c alone is inconclusive: the exact infinitely-often inequality must be retained.

The excluded finite-digit case is α=A∈Z_{≥0}. Apart from the single possible equality n=A, one has v₂(n−A)≤log₂|n−A|, so only finitely many positive n satisfy the linear-depth condition.
