Conditional denominator-transfer audit and exceptional-depth requirement.

Throughout, n≥3 is odd. Use the definitions in hp_b1_ternary_actual_denominator.md, abbreviating H=H_n, K=K_(n+1), C=mathscr C_n, Qpart=mathscr Q_n. Assume its exact endpoint identity

X/Y=N/Delta,  N=Qpart+2^(n+1)C/(n!)²,
Delta=(n+1)P_(n+1)H−2P_nK.

Let q be the positive reduced denominator of N/Delta. Require Delta≠0, supplied eventually by the cited analytic theorem. This audit concerns the actual rational quotient, so for every prime p,

v_p(q)=max(0,v_p(Delta)−v_p(N)).

1. Integrality and endpoint bounds.

For nonnegative integers m,a, coefficient expansion gives

m![z^m]e^z(1−z+z²/2)^a
=Σ_(b+2c≤m) (−1)^b (m)_(b+2c)(a)_(b+c)/(2^c b!c!).

Every summand is integral: (m)_(b+2c)/(2^c b!c!) equals binom(m,b+2c) binom(b+2c,b) (2c)!/(2^c c!), an integer. Consequently H_k and H'_k(1)/k are integers for k≥1, and so is K_k.

The Legendre polynomial identity gives, in the stated endpoint normalization,

P_k=Σ_(j=0)^floor(k/2) 2^(k−j) binom(k,2j) binom(2j,j).

For j≥1, the central binomial coefficient is even. The j=0 term has valuation k. Therefore v_2(P_k)≥ceil(k/2)+1 for k≥2. Also P_0=1 and P_1=2, so the weaker bound v_2(P_k)≥ceil(k/2) holds for every k≥0.

Apply the weaker bound termwise to Q_k=8Σ_(j=1)^k P_(j−1)P_(k−j)/j. Since v_2(j)≤floor(log_2 k),

v_2(Q_k)≥3+ceil((k−1)/2)−floor(log_2 k).

Put m=floor(log_2(n+1)). Integrality of H,K and evenness of n+1 now imply

v_2(Delta)≥a:=(n+5)/2,
v_2(Qpart)≥b:=(n+7)/2−m.

These estimates do not require exact valuations of H or K.

2. Actual numerator reduction outside the exceptional disk.

Write c=v_2(C), f=v_2(n!)=n−s_2(n). The factorial summand has valuation

t=n+1−2f+c=−n+1+2s_2(n)+c.

The reported germ certificate gives, outside n≡15 (mod 16), c=v_2(n−1) on n≡1 (mod 8), c=2 on n≡3,5 (mod 8), and c=3 on n≡7 (mod 16). In particular c≤m for all such odd n≥3. Hence t=−n+O(log n), whereas b=n/2−O(log n). Eventually t<b, so the ultrametric equality gives v_2(N)=t without a cancellation ambiguity. Thus

v_2(q)≥a−t=2v_2(n!)−(n+1)/2+2−c
=(3n+3)/2−2s_2(n)−c.

This verifies the Section 3 transfer, conditional on the endpoint identity and germ certificate.

3. A bound that also handles possible cancellation on the exceptional disk.

No smallness assumption on c is needed for the weaker universal inequality

v_2(q)≥max(0,a−t−(m−1))
=max(0,(3n+5)/2−2s_2(n)−m−c).                 (*)

Indeed, if t<b, then v_2(N)=t and the stronger bound max(0,a−t) applies. If t≥b, then a−t≤a−b=m−1, and the right side of (*) is zero. If C=0, interpret c=+∞ and the bound as zero. This case split prevents an unjustified use of unique least valuation at unusually deep roots.

4. Necessary depth for a shrinking subsequence.

Assume additionally the reported uniform lower bounds at 5 and 13, whose combined contribution is

log q≥[(1/2)log 5+(1/6)log 13]n+v_2(q)log 2−O(log n).

Together with (*), this implies, for finite c,

log q≥[(3/2)log 2+(1/2)log 5+(1/6)log 13]n−c log 2−O(log n).

For the primitive coefficient form L_n=a_n+q_n(e+π), the cited fixed-b theorem gives log|L_n|=log q_n−c_0 n+o(n), where c_0=2log(1+sqrt(2)). Consequently any unbounded subsequence with L_n→0 must have

c_n≥δn−o(n),
δ=((3/2)log 2+(1/2)log 5+(1/6)log 13−c_0)/log 2>0.

Positivity follows exactly from 2^9·5^3·13=832000>(5/2)^12>(1+sqrt(2))^12. The germ theorem identifies c_n=v_2(n−ν) on n≡15 (mod 16). Since c_n=O(log n) outside that class, a shrinking subsequence must eventually lie in the exceptional class and attain the displayed linear depth there.

Neither the germ certificate nor existence of ν supplies an upper bound for v_2(n−ν) at ordinary integer indices. That remains the precise missing input for this conditional exclusion route; the argument proves no rationality or irrationality assertion.
