Unreviewed refinement of the recovered factorial-determinant proof.

Let F_n(t)=U_n(t)/U_n(0), let d be fixed, and put S=d(d−1)/2. Define Δ_j=ℓ_{j+1}−ℓ_j with ℓ_j(t^k)=1/(n+k+1−j)!. For uniformly bounded analytic factors G_{i,n}, write E_n=det([t^r]G_{i,n}) for 0≤r≤d−1. Multilinearity gives

(n!)^d n^S det(Δ_j(F_nG_{i,n}))=χ_{n,d}E_n+O(1/n),

where χ_{n,d}=(n!)^d n^S det(Δ_j(t^rF_n)) with rows r=0,…,d−1. The permutations of these shifts give exactly χ_{n,d}E_n. Repeated shifts vanish, and every other surviving shift tuple has total degree at least S+1; the uniform polynomial bound from the recovered proof makes their total O(1/n).

Under the reciprocal-root bound and F_n(z/n)→exp(−cz), χ_{n,d}→exp(−dc)∏_{j=0}^{d−1}j!, which is nonzero. Thus, for two such determinants sharing F_n and with the denominator coefficient determinant bounded away from zero, their normalized ratio equals the finite-n coefficient-determinant ratio plus O(1/n).

Applied conditionally to worker_2's V and W families, this would give

det[U;e;w]/det[U;e;v]=(W(0)/V(0))·(E_{W,n}/E_{V,n}+O(1/n)).

The reported coefficient limits give E_{W,n}/E_{V,n}→(√2−1)^(b−1). Their convergence need not be O(1/n), so that rate must not be attached to the final limiting constant. This refinement does not itself verify the actual-family hypotheses or bound the additional determinant det[U;v;w].

A sharper form of the determinant argument is now derived: after normalization, the determinant equals a common scalar times the determinant of the finite Taylor coefficients, with an O(1/n) remainder. The common scalar depends only on the reference polynomial and determinant size. Consequently, it cancels from ratios of determinants sharing that reference polynomial. This gives an O(1/n) comparison with the finite-n coefficient-determinant ratio, even when convergence of those coefficients has no specified rate. The limiting asymptotic itself still has only an o(1) error under the stated hypotheses.
