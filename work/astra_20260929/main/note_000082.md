1. Factorial determinant normalization.

Write U_n(t)/U_n(0)=sum_k u_{n,k}t^k and D_j=ell_{j+1}−ell_j. For a monomial shift t^r, the exact summand is

D_j(t^r U_n/U_n(0)) = sum_k u_{n,k} P_j(n+k+r+1)/(n+k+r+1)!,

where P_j(X)=(X)_{j+1}−(X)_j is monic of degree j+1. For d rows, det[P_j(X_i)] is the Vandermonde in the X_i times a symmetric polynomial of degree d whose highest homogeneous part is product_i X_i. Consequently, for fixed k_i,r_i,

n^(−d) det[P_j(n+k_i+r_i+1)] → Vandermonde(k_i+r_i).

The factorial ratios contribute n^(−sum_i(k_i+r_i+1)), and u_{n,k}/n^k→(−c)^k/k!. Thus the normalization for fixed shifts is exactly (n!)^d n^(sum_i r_i). The limit is

sum_{k_1,...,k_d≥0} product_i[(−c)^{k_i}/k_i!] Vandermonde(k_i+r_i)
= exp(−dc) Vandermonde(r_i).

For the equality, the sum is an alternating polynomial in the r_i of degree at most d(d−1)/2. Its top-degree coefficient is multiplied by the product of the exponential sums. This argument uses no positivity.

The reciprocal-root bound gives |u_{n,k}|/n^k≤(2L)^k/k!. After division by n^d, the polynomial determinant is uniformly bounded by a fixed polynomial in all k_i+r_i+1. Summing the factorial weights leaves a polynomial bound in the r_i. Uniform Cauchy bounds on the analytic factors G_{i,n} then justify the Taylor expansion and all sum interchanges for sufficiently large n.

Repeated shifts vanish exactly. The smallest sum of distinct nonnegative shifts is S=d(d−1)/2, attained by permutations of 0,...,d−1. Their contribution is

exp(−dc) product_{j=0}^{d−1}(j!) det[[t^j]G_{i,infinity}].

All higher-shift contributions, after multiplication by n^S, are O(1/n). Convergence of the finitely many leading coefficients need only be o(1); this argument does not establish an O(1/n) remainder for the entire limit.
