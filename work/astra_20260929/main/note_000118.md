Unreviewed lead derivation: a direct bound for the full cofactor determinant.

Fix d≥1 and S=d(d−1)/2. Let F_n(0)=1, deg F_n≤n+1, and suppose all reciprocal roots have modulus at most L. Let P_i=a_i F_n G_{i,n}, where a_i≠0 and the G_{i,n} are uniformly bounded by M on a fixed disk |t|<ρ. Define
ℓ_{n,j}(P)=Σ_{k≥0}[t^k]P/(n+k+1−j)!,  0≤j≤d−1.
Then, for sufficiently large n,
|det(ℓ_{n,j}(P_i))| ≤ C |∏_i a_i| / ((n!)^d n^(S+d)),
where C depends only on d,L,ρ,M. This upper bound requires no microscopic limit for F_n and no convergence of the G_{i,n}.

Proof. Write F_n=Σ_k u_{n,k}t^k. The root bound gives |u_{n,k}|/n^k≤(2L)^k/k!. For fixed nonnegative row shifts r_i, expand the determinant using X_i=n+k_i+r_i+1. Since the falling factorial (X)_j is monic of degree j,
det((X_i)_j)_(i,j)=∏_(i<l)(X_l−X_i)=∏_(i<l)(k_l+r_l−k_i−r_i).
There is no additional polynomial factor in this identity.

The inequality n!/(n+k+r+1)!≤n^(−k−r−1), together with the factorial coefficient majorant, consequently gives
|(n!)^d n^(Σr_i+d) det(ℓ_{n,j}(t^(r_i)F_n))| ≤ C_0∏_i(1+r_i)^(d−1).
For example, bound each Vandermonde factor by (1+k_i+r_i)(1+k_l+r_l), then sum the factorial weights independently in the k_i. The resulting constants are finite and independent of the shifts.

Expand each G_{i,n} in Taylor coefficients, bounded by Mρ^(−r). A tuple with repeated shifts contributes zero because two monomial-shift rows coincide. Every remaining tuple has Σr_i≥S. The preceding bound therefore bounds the normalized determinant by a constant times
n^S Σ_(m≥S) binom(m+d−1,d−1)(1+m)^(d(d−1))(nρ)^(−m),
which is uniformly bounded for sufficiently large n. Absolute convergence also justifies the expansions. This proves the claimed estimate.

Application to the supplied endpoint identities. Fix b≥1. Let T be the ordered b−1 high rows, e=(1,…,1), and let v,w be the factorial-functional rows of V,W. Put D=det[T;e;v] and N=det[T;v;w]. All polynomial and endpoint rows have the common reference F_n=p_(n+1)/p_(n+1)(0). Write H_n=∏_(l=1)^(b−1)p_(n+l)(0), with empty product one.

Assume the uniform analytic-factor bounds and the nonzero denominator asymptotic supplied by the pending difference-determinant lemma and coefficient-determinant calculation. They give
|D| ≥ c |H_n V(0)| / ((n!)^b n^(b(b−1)/2))
for sufficiently large n. Applying the upper bound just proved with d=b+1 gives
|N| ≤ C |H_n V(0)W(0)| / ((n!)^(b+1) n^((b+1)(b+2)/2)).
Consequently,
|N/D| ≤ C |W(0)|/(n! n^(2b+1)).
When W(0)≠0, division by the scale |W(0)/V(0)| yields the relative bound C|V(0)|/(n! n^(2b+1)).

The displayed source normalization gives
V(0)=(2n+1) binom(2n,n)^2 p_n(1)p_(n+1)(1).
For the stated recurrence, x_k=p_k(1) satisfies x_0=1, x_1=1/2 and x_(k+1)=x_k/2+β_k x_(k−1), with 0<β_k≤1/12. Induction gives 0<x_k≤1. Hence 0<V(0)≤(2n+1)16^n, and the relative bound tends to zero.
