UNVERIFIED LEAD DERIVATION: denominator valuation and forced numerator cancellation on n≡4 modulo five.

Use exactly the definitions of P,U,a,b,f,H_j,J_j,K_j,S,C,W,D,X in the returned published two-chart identity. This note imports its rational endpoint identification, not its large-prime local ideal theorem. Write m=n+1, s=v_5(m), and r=v_5(n!). Assume n≥4 and 5|m.

1. Auxiliary estimates retaining the full valuation of m.

The exact finite expansion of H_m^(j)(1), for j=0,1,2, is
Σ_{u,v≥0} (−1)^u (m)_(u+2v+j)(m)_(u+v)/(2^v u!v!).
At positive integer m, terms beyond the falling-factorial range vanish. The term u=v=0 is respectively 1, m, and m(m−1).

For any other term put R=u+2v and t=u+v>0. After removing the initial factor m from both falling factorials, its valuation is at least
floor((R+j−1)/5)+floor((t−1)/5)−v_5(u!v!).
Indeed, among the factors m−i with 1≤i≤k−1, at least floor((k−1)/5) are divisible by five. Zero terms satisfy the bound automatically.

Set q=floor(t/5) and ε=1 if 5|t, otherwise ε=0. Since u!v! divides t!,
v_5(u!v!)≤q+v_5(q!).
For j=0 the lower bound is at least q−2ε−v_5(q!)≥−1. For j=1,2 it is at least q−ε−v_5(q!)≥0. These inequalities also cover q=0; when ε=1, q≥1 and v_5(q!)≤q−1.

Consequently there are five-adic numbers A,B,E such that
H_m(1)=1+m²A, with v_5(A)≥−1,
H′_m(1)=m+m²B, with B∈Z_5,
H″_m(1)=m(m−1)+m²E, with E∈Z_5.
Because mA∈Z_5, substitution into the exact definitions gives
J_m=2m+m²j, K_m=−2m+m²κ,
where j,κ∈Z_5. Thus
H_m(1)≡1, J_m/m≡2, K_m/m≡−2 modulo five.
These are termwise estimates for every allowed integer m, with no restriction on s.

For example, S/m=(J_m/m)J_m−H_m(1)(K_m/m), and W/m=(J_m/m)J_n−(K_m/m)h_n.

The Legendre endpoint digit identity from the returned five-adic proof is

3. Normalized exponential contractions for n≥9.

Put e_j=j!Σ_{h=0}^j1/h!. Its recurrence gives residues (1,2,0,1,0) at indices congruent to (0,1,2,3,4) modulo five.

Define c_{n,j}=[z^j](1−z+z²/2)^n. The exact Rodrigues contraction becomes
T_0(P)/f=Σ_{j=0}^n (n)_j c_{n,j} e_{2n−j}.
For j≥5, the falling factorial contains n−4 and is divisible by five. For j≤4, the coefficient polynomial has five-adic unit denominators, so it can be evaluated at n=4 modulo five. Its values there are
(c_{4,0},…,c_{4,4})=(1,−4,8,−10,17/2).
The five retained contributions reduce to (1,0,2,0,0). Hence T_0(P)/f≡3 modulo five.

For U, let u_{m,j}=[t^j](1−2t+2t²)^m. The exact formula gives
m T_0(U)/f=2^(1−m)Σ_{d=0}^m ((m−1)!/d!)(m+d)u_{m,m+d}e_{m−1+d}.
For d≤m−1 the factorial quotient is an integer. Since 5|m, Frobenius shows that u_{m,m+d} vanishes modulo five unless m+d is divisible by five. In the latter case the explicit factor m+d supplies the divisibility. Thus every such summand vanishes modulo five.

At d=m, cancel the factorial denominator exactly. The boundary contribution is 4e_{2m−1}, which also vanishes modulo five because 2m−1≡4. Therefore m T_0(U)/f∈5Z_5. This argument retains the boundary and never assumes m is a unit.

4. Moments and the complete numerator.

Let L_0=floor(log_5(n+1)). The integral quotient polynomials in the moment terms have degree at most n, so
v_5(w_P),v_5(w_U)≥−L_0.
For n≥9, r≥L_0. To check the possible power-of-five boundary: if L_0≥2, floor(n/5)≥5^(L_0−1)−1≥L_0; the case L_0=1 is immediate. Consequently
v_5(w_P/f),v_5(w_U/f)≥2r−L_0≥1.
Thus P*/f≡3 and mU*/f≡0 modulo five.

Now divide the complete numerator by fm:
X/(fm)=2(P*/f)(S/m)−(mU*/f)C−2η(W/m).
All three terms are five-adically integral. Their total reduction is
2·3·2−0−2·1·1=10≡0 modulo five.
Hence v_5(X/f)≥s+1, allowing X=0 with valuation +∞.

Since D≠0, the positive reduced denominator q_n of X/D satisfies
v_5(q_n)=max(0,2r+s−v_5(X/f))≤2r−1.
Equivalently, for Z_n=X/[f(n+1)],
v_5(q_n)=max(0,2r−v_5(Z_n)), with v_5(Z_n)≥1.
The missing estimate is an upper bound on v_5(Z_n). An O(log n) bound would suffice for the preserved five-adic loss criterion, but neither this derivation nor finite samples establish it.
