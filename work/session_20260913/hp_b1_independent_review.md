# Independent review of the degree-one endpoint theorem

2026-09-13. Reviewed `hp_b1_endpoint_attempt.md` against the previously verified complex Legendre projection and b=0 identities. Also checked the stated external coincidence theorem directly in Proposition1 of Brändén and Wagner, [A converse to the Grace–Walsh–Szegő theorem](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0809.3225).

**Verdict:** the proof establishes eventual normality and Y>0 for n>=64, and establishes the sign and two-sided exponential estimate for the evaluated remainder divided by Y for n>=512. No substantive gap was found in Sections2–4. One exact kernel formula in the initial ledger was missing `(-1)^k`; the author and reviewer independently identified it, and the author corrected equation(30). The denominator clearing and all main conclusions are unchanged. The reduced endpoint denominator is still an unresolved arithmetic problem, so this result does not decide e+pi.

## 1. Polynomial symbol and root bounds

The polynomial `f_(N,k)=sum_j (-1)^j binom(k,j)z^j/(N+2)_j` is the normalized Laguerre polynomial with parameter N+1. Its monic characteristic polynomial is generated by the stated real tridiagonal matrix: diagonal N+2+2j, off-diagonal squared `(j+1)(j+N+2)`. This is the correct indexing.

In row j, put x=j+1. The sum of the two off-diagonal absolute values is no more than `2 sqrt(x(x+N+1))`; the first summand is smaller and a missing boundary summand only improves the estimate. The lower bound `N+2x-2sqrt(x(x+N+1))` decreases with x. Its value at x=N+1 is `(3-2sqrt2)N+2-2sqrt2`, so the displayed lower bounds are valid for every k<=N+1 and N>=128. The sharper bound with x=n is likewise valid when k<=n and n>=64.

Since all k roots lambda_i of f are positive, Rolle's theorem applied to `z^N f` yields exactly k positive nonzero roots for `Nf+zf'`. Its root at zero has multiplicity N-1 before division and contributes no additional root to g. On [0,6], each lambda_i>12 gives

    sum z/(lambda_i-z)<=12 sum 1/lambda_i<=12.

Thus g/f>=N-12>0 there. The reciprocal-root sum obtained from the constant and linear coefficients is exactly `(N+1)k/(N(N+2))`, at most2. Factoring g and using `log(1-x)>=-2x` for 0<=x<=1/2 proves the two-sided modulus bounds on |z|<=3. The alternative symbol `(N+1)f+zf'` has reciprocal-root sum k/(N+1) and obeys the same safe bounds. The k=0 case is immediate and does not require a matrix argument.

For the specialized disk |z-1|<=1, the roots lambda_i exceed8, the roots of `nf+zf'` exceed4, and their reciprocal sum is at most1. The stated `exp(-4)` and `exp(2)` bounds on |z|<=2 follow.

## 2. Polarization and the sign, not just nonvanishing

For a real polynomial Q with all roots in Re(t)>=1/3, reciprocal roots belong to the closed disk with center3/2 and radius3/2. Its elementary-symmetric expansion gives exactly the factorial-transform polarization displayed in equation(14). The diagonal polynomial is g, with no extra binomial or factorial factor missing.

The source's Proposition1 applies to symmetric multiaffine polynomials on an open or closed circular region, assuming convexity or full total degree. The disks used here are convex, and the polarization is symmetric and multiaffine. Thus all required hypotheses hold, including possible degree drops.

The theorem first supplies the nonzero modulus bound. The additional sign argument is needed and is valid: move each reciprocal root linearly to the disk center, preserving conjugate pairs. Throughout the path the polarization has real coefficients evaluated on a conjugation-invariant multiset, so its value is real. The modulus bound prevents a zero. At the endpoint the value is g evaluated at the positive real center, before its first positive root, hence positive. The sign of the original factorial transform is consequently the sign of Q(0).

Applying the same argument to the p_k disk proves the strict signs of all three transforms ell_0, ell_1 and ell_Delta. This does not assert positivity of the original complex bilinear moment functional.

## 3. Kernel, normality and endpoint size

The monic Christoffel–Darboux identity has the sign shown in equation(5), since the norm ratio is `h_k/h_(k-1)=-alpha_k`. Evaluation at (0,1) gives

    K_n(0,1)=2p_n(1)p_(n+1)(1)/|h_n|>0.

The sign lemma makes every summand in delta positive. The endpoint relation `(1+t0)B0+(1+t1)B1=0` therefore has two positive coefficients and delta>0 for n>=64. It has rank one, B0 cannot vanish for a nonzero solution, and its unique normalized solution has `-1<B1<0`, `Y=delta/(1+t1)>0`.

The explicit large-n cutoff is safe. At n=512, the bound for t1 is at most `e^2*(513)^2*(3/8)^512` using n!>=(n/3)^n. This is much smaller than e^(-20); the ratio of the same displayed majorant at successive indices is below one thereafter. Since Y<t1, the later use of Y<e^(-20) is justified. The weaker lower estimate for Y in equation(19) also follows directly from the delta lower bound, n/(n+1)>=1/2 and 1+t1<2.

The exceptional n=1 endpoint really is zero. It does not fall in any claimed eventual range.

## 4. The actual evaluated remainder

The kernel reproduces the constant polynomial. Therefore subtracting the integral defining H_n from h(t), using

    1/(1-t)-1/(1-s)=(t-s)/((1-t)(1-s)),

and then applying Christoffel–Darboux proves exactly

    Psi_n=(v_n/h_n)(p_(n+1)-a_n p_n)/(1-t).

There is no asymptotic replacement here. The exponential tail at z=1 is ell_B(h), while the tail of C F is `L(C*/(1-t))=-ell_B(H_n)`. Their difference is the complete evaluated remainder in equation(21). The coefficient through degree n is removed by A, and the projection gives the prescribed additional moment cancellations.

The alternating signs of v_k follow from the previously verified Legendre-square Padé identity. Orthogonality gives `v_(k+1)=v_k/2+alpha_k v_(k-1)` for k>=1. Applying it one step later yields

    |a_n|=alpha_(n+1)/(1/2+|a_(n+1)|)<1/6.

The complex symmetric Jacobi matrix in the note has the correct last diagonal entry `1/2+a_n`: its characteristic polynomial is `p_(n+1)-a_n p_n`. Its Hermitian part is the real diagonal matrix, because the purely imaginary symmetric off-diagonal entries cancel under conjugate transpose. For a possibly complex eigenvector, the usual Hermitian Rayleigh quotient identity gives Re(lambda)>=1/3. Nonnormality introduces no exception to this numerical-range argument.

The estimate of Q_n(0) also has the correct sign. It equals `(-1)^(n+1)(p_(n+1)(1)+a_n p_n(1))`; the recurrence gives `p_(n+1)(1)>=p_n(1)/2`, so the term in parentheses lies between two thirds and one times p_(n+1)(1).

Expanding Q_n/(1-t) as `sum_(r>=0)t^r Q_n` is legitimate under both factorial transforms: Q_n has finite degree and the resulting double sum is absolutely convergent. For each r, N=n+r and k=n+1 satisfy k<=N+1, and every summand has the same sign. The precise telescoping sum is

    sum_(r>=0)(n+r)/(n+r+1)!=1/n!.

This proves the lower bound for the full rational transform, not just its initial coefficient. The ell_1 term is bounded by the stated e^7 multiple. Because Y<e^(-20)<e^(-19)/2, it cannot cancel more than half of the ell_Delta lower bound. Finally v_n/h_n is positive, so the resulting sign of R_n(1) is exactly (-1)^n.

Combining the checked bounds gives

    (e^(-14)/6)*epsilon_n
       <=(-1)^n R_n(1)/Y_n
       <=2e^12*epsilon_n,

with the constants in the note. The logarithmic rate follows from the positive p_n(1) recurrence and its limiting dominant root `(1+sqrt2)/4`, together with the exact Legendre norm bounds. No limiting interchange involving a signed tail is required.

## 5. Rational ledger and remaining arithmetic

The corrected exact kernel identity is

    K_n(t,s)=sum_(k=0)^n (-1)^k(2k+1)J_k(t)J_k(s)/2^(2k+1).

The missing sign in the original draft did not change its denominator bound. Since the largest factorial in ell_0 through degree n is (2n+1)!, the stated `T=2^(2n+1)(2n+1)!` clears both projected polynomial coefficients and t_j. The Taylor coefficients of F through n have the stated common denominator d_F. The reconstructed a_j therefore have denominators dividing T*d_F, and D=T^2*d_F clears X and Y in equation(28).

With U=DX and V=DY, the reduced denominator is exactly `|V|/gcd(|U|,|V|)`. This includes every common factor, including any excess introduced by D. Since the primitive endpoint is `q_n R_n(1)/Y_n`, the two-sided analytic estimate precisely identifies the missing input as a bound on this reduced q_n. The note correctly avoids transferring the factorial approximation to e from b=0: the present exponential companion estimate is insufficient for that argument.

This review did not rerun large matrix scans or use numerical evidence to infer a sign. Its principal checks are direct algebraic derivations and verification of every external-theorem hypothesis. The author separately prepared bounded exact checks of the CD and remainder identities; those provide additional normalization checks but are not the logical basis of this all-n result.
