# Exact finite-difference reduction of the unbordered high Smith content

Date: 2026-09-13. Bounded arithmetic continuation by audit_results.

The requested all-index statement that H_n has full row rank modulo
every p>3n is not proved here. The actual high Smith obstruction can,
however, be transferred exactly, including all prime-power exponents,
to an explicit (n-1)-by-(n+1) integer matrix containing only the
arctangent columns. This removes the exponential columns by an
integral row transformation, rather than dividing by a possibly bad
large-prime minor.

The result also gives an exact corresponding reduction for the
extremal degree-d compatibility matrix. It retains every low-A-degree
equation, including the row whose omission previously produced a
spurious candidate prime. No prime or degree scan is used.

## 1. Archive state and the distinction between the two obstructions

The relevant reviewed inputs are raw_large_prime_nullity_and_smith.md
and raw_extremal_no_accessory_compatibility.md. They prove that H_n
has at most one nonunit Smith invariant at p>3n. A row-rank loss
forces a nonzero triple of degree n-1 and Taylor order 3n+1.
The converse is not supplied: an extremal lower-degree triple could
in principle lie in a two-dimensional high kernel generated by it
and its z-multiple. Thus excluding all extremal triples is a
sufficient route to full row rank, rather than an equivalence that
can be silently used backwards.

The newly proved characteristic-zero no-triple-root theorem concerns
the particular endpoint-selected canonical solution. It does not
exclude an unmatched solution of the high equations after reduction
modulo a large prime. Its dyadic Hessian estimate is not a statement
that that Hessian has no large prime factors.

I searched the existing raw-family notes for finite-difference
elimination, Smith content, and annihilation of the exponential
columns. The smaller content-preserving matrix below was not present
in those notes. The existing differential and moment formulations
are related but do not state this integral Smith equivalence.

## 2. A unimodular elimination lemma

Let a,m,L be positive integers with L>=m. Consider a matrix whose
rows are indexed k=a,...,a+L-1. Its first m columns are

    (k)_j=k(k-1)...(k-j+1),  j=0,...,m-1.

Let any additional columns have entries f_j(k). Keep the first m
rows unchanged, and replace the remaining rows by



$$
\Delta^m f_j(a+r)=
\sum_{s=0}^m(-1)^{m-s}\binom ms f_j(a+r+s),
\quad 0\le r<L-m.
\tag{1}
$$



This row transformation has determinant one over Z. In increasing
row order, each new row has coefficient one on its latest original
row a+r+m and uses only preceding rows otherwise. Every first-block
entry in the new rows is zero, since its degree in k is <m.
The resulting block form is



$$
\begin{pmatrix}V&C_0\\0&G\end{pmatrix},
\qquad \det V=\prod_{j=0}^{m-1}j!.
\tag{2}
$$



The determinant follows from the monic falling-factorial columns
and the consecutive Vandermonde. Therefore over any localization
where those factorials are units, invertible row and column operations
give precisely diag(I_m,G). This preserves all remaining Smith
invariants and determinantal ideals, not merely the rank.

## 3. The exact smaller matrix for the actual H_n

Write tau_l=[z^l]arctan(z), with tau_l=0 for even l and
tau_l=(-1)^((l-1)/2)/l for odd positive l. The actual integer
high matrix H_n has rows k=n+1,...,3n and entries



$$
(H_n)_{k,B_j}=(k)_j,\qquad
(H_n)_{k,C_j}=k!\tau_{k-j},\quad 0\le j\le n.
\tag{3}
$$



All k-j are positive. Apply the lemma with a=m=n+1 and L=2n.
Define



$$
\boxed{
(G_n)_{r,j}=\sum_{s=0}^{n+1}(-1)^{n+1-s}\binom{n+1}s
(n+1+r+s)!\,\tau_{n+1+r+s-j},
}
\tag{4}
$$



where 0<=r<=n-2 and 0<=j<=n. Every entry is an integer.
The largest factorial index is 3n. For every p>3n,



$$
\boxed{H_n\ \sim_{\mathbb Z_p}\ \operatorname{diag}(I_{n+1},G_n).}
\tag{5}
$$



For n=1, G_n is the empty matrix and (5) proves full row rank.
For n>=2, let eta_n be the positive gcd of the (n-1)-by-(n-1)
minors of G_n. It is nonzero because the characteristic-zero
high matrix has full row rank. The exact unbordered Smith valuation
e_n(p) in raw_large_prime_nullity_and_smith.md consequently satisfies



$$
\boxed{e_n(p)=v_p(\eta_n),\qquad p>3n.}
\tag{6}
$$



Equivalently, the missing full-rank theorem is precisely that eta_n
has no prime divisor greater than 3n. This is a smaller explicit
integer-content problem; equation (6) does not establish that claim.

The endpoint functional has not been included in (5). It must be
transformed by the same column operations if one studies the
bordered matrix. Its restriction content remains the separate s
in the reviewed formula v_p(delta_n)=e_n(p)+s_n(p).

## 4. The all-A-degree extremal matrix

For the degree-d extremal triple, retain every row
k=d+1,...,3d+3, in columns B_0,...,B_d,C_0,...,C_d. Put m=d+1.
This is a (2m+1)-by-(2m) matrix. The same integral transformation
produces I_m and the (m+1)-by-m matrix



$$
\boxed{
\mathcal G^{(m)}_{r,j}
=\sum_{s=0}^m(-1)^{m-s}\binom ms
(m+r+s)!\,\tau_{m+r+s-j},
\quad 0\le r\le m,\quad0\le j<m.
}
\tag{7}
$$



For p>3d+3=3m, all entries and transformations are legitimate in
Z_p, and full column rank of (7) is equivalent to nonexistence
of an extremal triple. The lowest retained row is k=m, exactly
the condition that A has degree at most d. The largest row index
is 3m, still less than p, even in the allowed case p=3m+1.

There is an equivalent rational differential formulation. Let
D_z denote differentiation, not the polynomial 1+z^2. For C of
degree <m, the r-th entry of (7) times its coefficient vector is



$$
\left[D_z^r\,D_z^m(D_z-1)^m(C(z)\arctan z)\right]_{z=0}.
\tag{8}
$$



The operator D_z^m(D_z-1)^m annihilates both A and B exp(z)
for deg A,deg B<m. Its action on C arctan(z) is a rational
function: the remaining arctangent coefficient is
D_z^m(D_z-1)^m C=0. Formula (8) can alternatively be defined
by the finite sum (7) in characteristic p, so no analytic
exponential series or factorial at or above p is introduced.

## 5. Exact recurrences, and why they do not yet control the content

For general nonnegative m and indices where k>=j+1, put



$$
F^{(m)}_{k,j}=\Delta^m\bigl(k!\tau_{k-j}\bigr).
$$



The construction gives the exact lattice identities



$$
F^{(m+1)}_{k,j}=F^{(m)}_{k+1,j}-F^{(m)}_{k,j},
\tag{9}
$$




$$
F^{(m)}_{k,j+1}
=kF^{(m)}_{k-1,j}+mF^{(m-1)}_{k,j}.
\tag{10}
$$



Equation (10) is stated for m>=1; at m=0 its corresponding
identity is simply the defining column shift without the second term.
Use (k!tau_(k-j-1))=k((k-1)!tau_((k-1)-j))
and the commutator Delta k=k Delta+S, with S the forward shift.
Thus all entries are generated by the scalar factorial-arctangent
sequence y_k=k!tau_k, which obeys



$$
y_{k+2}=-k(k+1)y_k,\qquad k>=1.
\tag{11}
$$



For completeness, the first-column finite differences
f_k=F^{(m)}_{k,0} satisfy the fixed-order recurrence



$$
\begin{aligned}
0={}&f_{k+4}-2f_{k+3}
+[(k+m+2)(k+m+3)+1]f_{k+2}\\
&-2(k+1)(k+m+2)f_{k+1}+k(k+1)f_k,
\qquad k>=1.
\end{aligned}
\tag{12}
$$



To derive it, apply Delta^(m+2) to (11) and use

    Delta^r k(k+1)=k(k+1)Delta^r
       +2r(k+1)S Delta^(r-1)+r(r-1)S^2 Delta^(r-2).

Equations (9)--(12) are exact identities for the actual entry
family. They do not imply a corresponding scalar recurrence for
eta_n: both matrix dimensions and selected row/column sets change
with n, and column updates in (10) use another difference level.
Moreover (12) alone has additional solutions not satisfying the
original second-order sequence and finite-difference initialization.
It must not be used as an invertible replacement for that definition.

The concrete unresolved step is an identity among the maximal minors
of (4), with coefficients in Z[1/(3n)!], generating one. An
equivalent saturated elimination using (7) could prove the stronger
absence of all extremal triples. The bounded-entry recurrences above
have not supplied such an identity or a restricted infinite class.
The all-prime degree-zero and degree-one exclusions in the prior
note therefore remain finite-degree results, without extrapolation.
