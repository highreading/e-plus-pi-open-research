# Independent all-residue certificate at the primes 23 and 43

Date: 2026-09-13. Reviewer: audit_computations.

**FULL PASS.** Every one of the 23 residue classes modulo 23 and every
one of the 43 residue classes modulo 43 has independently verified unit
determinant and exponential-endpoint seeds. The finite input is therefore
complete. Together with the already reviewed positive- and negative-residue
transfer theorems, it proves uniform factorial divisibility of the actual
reduced denominator. No large-degree canonical approximant was constructed,
and no prime outside the two assigned primes was tested.

The target is the 23 and 43 portion of
`raw_predeclared_residue_seed_atlas.json`, generated by
`check_predeclared_residue_seed_atlas.py`. The independent implementation is
`check_uniform_23_43_independent.py`; its full exact output is
`uniform_23_43_independent_certificate.json`. The certificate contains the
SHA-256 digest of the source atlas used for comparison. The independent
implementation does not import any code from the source checker.

## 1. Independent Schur and endpoint algorithms

For a partition lambda and integer background b, the computation evaluates



$$
M_\lambda^{[b]}(1)
 =H_\lambda\det\bigl(h_{\lambda_i-i+j}^{[b]}\bigr),\qquad
 h_d^{[b]}=[z^d]e^z(1+z^2)^b.
$$



The determinant is computed by integer fraction-free Bareiss elimination
on the chosen residue representatives. The hook product is computed
directly from the boxes of the partition. This is independent of the
source checker's normalized Appell-Wronskian/Gaussian-elimination route.
Every complete-function index is less than p, and every hook length is
less than p. All factorial inversions and hook-normalization reductions
are consequently legitimate. Replacing a negative b by its least
nonnegative residue is legitimate for these coefficients: the binomial
index is less than p, so the binomial is a polynomial with a unit
denominator in b modulo p.

For a positive seed k, let D be the rectangle seed and C_j the cofactor
seeds from the reviewed theorem. The scale-free coefficient vector is



$$
B_l=(-1)^{k-l}\binom{k}{l}C_{k-l}
       =D\,b_{k,l}/V_k(1).
$$



The independent endpoint calculation uses the original reconstruction,



$$
U_l=(k+l)!B_l,\quad S(t)=(1+t^2)^kU(t),\quad
 Q_{3k-d}=\binom d k S_d\quad(k\le d\le3k).
$$



Thus Q is exactly the scale-free polynomial
$D\widehat Q_k/V_k(1)$, not a differently normalized cofactor.
Taking the endpoint of its product with the exponential series truncated
at degree 2k gives E. Every factorial involved in that exponential series
has index at most 2k<p. This checks the endpoint without using the source
checker's V-coefficient border formula.

For a negative seed k, put r=p-k-1. From the independently evaluated
cofactors C_i the implementation forms the original positive-index tail



$$
b_i=(-1)^{r-i}\binom ri C_i,
 \qquad
 w_i=\sum_{2u\le k-i}\binom ru
       (r+i+1)^{\overline{2u}}b_{i+2u},
$$





$$
J_h=h!\sum_{i=h}^k\binom{r+i}{r+h}w_i.
$$



It checks J_0=D, and constructs the degree-k Taylor polynomial whose
derivatives at 1 are J_h. It then applies the original endpoint border



$$
D_{r,j}=\sum_{s=0}^{r+j}\binom{r+s}{s}(r+j)_{\underline s}
$$



to this Taylor polynomial. Modulo p, the border is a polynomial of degree
at most k in j: for s<p, its first binomial vanishes when s>k, and for
s\ge p its falling factorial vanishes. Thus this Taylor replacement
preserves the endpoint exactly. This is a different route from evaluating
the source's generalized-negative-binomial coefficients A_{k,h} and their
sum against J_h. All negative J_h are also compared with the source.

The empty positive and negative seeds are handled by their reviewed
k=0 unit theorems; no nonexistent partition row is used.

## 3. Actual reduced-denominator consequence

Retain the actual integer normalization



$$
Z_n=\widehat Q_n(1),\quad
 N_n=Pe_n(1)+4Pa_n(1),\quad
 q_n=|Z_n|/\gcd(|Z_n|,|N_n|).
$$



The independently reviewed transfer theorems, in
`raw_positive_residue_transfer_independent_review.md` and
`raw_negative_residue_transfer_independent_review.md`, show that the unit
seeds make Pe_n(1) a p-unit for every degree n in their residue class.
The global integer polynomial $\widehat Q_n$ has coefficient content
divisible by n!. Therefore



$$
v_p(Z_n)\ge v_p(n!),\qquad
 v_p(Pa_n(1))\ge v_p(n!)-\lfloor\log_p(2n)\rfloor.
$$



If the last lower bound is positive, N_n is a p-unit, so the actual
endpoint gcd removes no p-factor from Z_n. This is the reason the seed
certificate implies a reduced-denominator statement.

For either p=23 or p=43, n\ge2p suffices. Indeed, with
m=\lfloor n/p\rfloor\ge2,



$$
2n\le2p(m+1)-2<p^m,\qquad v_p(n!)\ge m.
$$



The strict inequality holds at m=2 because 6p-2<p^2 for p\ge7;
its induction follows by multiplying the previous right-side bound by
p, which increases faster than the affine expression on the left.
Consequently



$$
\boxed{v_{23}(q_n)=v_{23}(Z_n)\ge v_{23}(n!)\quad(n\ge46),}
$$




$$
\boxed{v_{43}(q_n)=v_{43}(Z_n)\ge v_{43}(n!)\quad(n\ge86).}
$$



These claims hold for all such integer n, in particular all sufficiently
large even n. They are consequences of the all-index transfer theorems
and a complete finite seed certificate, not extrapolations from computed
canonical degrees.

## 4. Rate and scope

Combining the accepted uniform primes 3 and 7 with this certificate,
every even n\ge86 has the simultaneous mandatory divisor



$$
2^{\,n+2\lfloor(n+2)/4\rfloor}
 3^{v_3(n!)}7^{v_7(n!)}23^{v_{23}(n!)}43^{v_{43}(n!)}
 \mid q_n.
$$



Its exponential rate is



$$
\frac32\log2+\frac{\log3}{2}+\frac{\log7}{6}
 +\frac{\log23}{22}+\frac{\log43}{42}
 \approx2.14542,
$$



which remains below the approximation-error threshold
$5\log((1+\sqrt5)/2)\approx2.40606$. Thus these two new uniform primes
are rigorous progress toward a global arithmetic obstruction, but this
particular collection alone does not yet rule out shrinking primitive
forms. The computation makes no claim about the untested primes or any
upper bound on q_n.
