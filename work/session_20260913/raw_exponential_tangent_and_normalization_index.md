# The actual exponential tangent obstruction and a normalization-index bound for norm depth

Date: 2026-09-13. Original bounded continuation by audit_sources.
Independent root review in raw_normalization_index_root_review.md passes
the full proof, retaining the reduced-generic-fiber hypotheses.

The exponential-pair Jacobian has not been proved to be a unit at every actual four-equation root. This note isolates the exact additional linearized pole condition. It also proves a useful alternative to étaleness: when the relevant exponential algebra has reduced generic fiber, the excess norm valuation is at most the index of that algebra in its integral closure. Maximality of this integral order suffices for exact norm depth, even if it is ramified.

Neither generic reducedness nor a small normalization index is established here for the actual family in every degree. An explicit Hermite-leading deformation shows that the already proved finite-free structure, full four-equation rigidity, and scalar quotient do not alone improve the possible local multiplicity of the exponential pair.

## 1. Actual equations and normalization

Retain the reviewed definitions in raw_extremal_four_accessory_equations.md and raw_exponential_pair_jacobian_and_pole_pairing.md. Set


$$
D=1+z^2,\qquad s=3d+2,\qquad p>3d+3,\qquad d\ge2,
$$


and


$$
\begin{aligned}
L={}&zD\partial^3-[(z+s)D-4z^2]\partial^2\\
&+[(2d-2)z^2+\beta z+\gamma]\partial
-d(d-1)z+2d^2(d-1)-d\beta .
\end{aligned}
\tag{1}
$$


Write $P=L^{[1]}$, meaning that $\partial$ is replaced by $\partial+1$. The downward construction gives a monic $B^*(z)$ of degree $d$, with


$$
PB^*=(E_1z+E_0)/d!.
\tag{2}
$$


All divisions used in this construction are units at the stated primes.

Let $\theta=(\beta,\gamma)$ be an actual common zero of $E_1,E_0,R_0,R_1$ over a field $K$ of characteristic $p$. Put $B=B^*(\theta)$. The map


$$
L:\mathcal P_d\longrightarrow\mathcal P_{d-1}
$$


is surjective and has a one-dimensional kernel. Choose a coordinate $j$ in which its cofactor generator is nonzero, and normalize its polynomial generator $C$ by $[z^j]C=1$. This normalization is for the present local calculation; it does not replace the actual integer cofactor normalization in a content statement.

Put


$$
S(z)=(\beta+6d+2)z+\gamma-2d.
$$


The actual pole condition in this chart is


$$
\mathcal R(C):=2C'-SC\equiv0\pmod D.
\tag{3}
$$


The two residues in this chart are the original $R_0,R_1$ divided by the chosen nonzero cofactor coordinate. At a common zero their differentials are therefore scaled by that same unit.

## 2. Exact linearized B/C/residue system

For a parameter direction $(u,v)\in K^2$, define


$$
\dot L=(uz+v)\partial-du,\qquad
\dot P=(uz+v)(\partial+1)-du.
\tag{4}
$$


There is an exponential-pair tangent in this direction if and only if there is a polynomial $\dot B\in\mathcal P_{d-1}$ satisfying


$$
P\dot B+\bigl[(uz+v)(\partial+1)-du\bigr]B=0.
\tag{5}
$$


Indeed differentiating (2) gives this equivalence, including the factors $d!$. The polynomial $\dot B$, when it exists, is unique: the coefficient of $z^{m+2}$ in $Pz^m$ is $m-d$, so $P$ is injective on $\mathcal P_{d-1}$.

For every direction $(u,v)$, independently of (5), there is a unique $\dot C\in\mathcal P_d$ with


$$
L\dot C=-[(uz+v)C'-duC],\qquad [z^j]\dot C=0.
\tag{6}
$$


The forcing has degree at most $d-1$: if $C$ has degree $d$, its possible degree-$d$ term cancels; otherwise the degree bound is immediate. The restriction of $L$ to the subspace with coefficient $j$ zero is an isomorphism, because its one-dimensional kernel is generated by the normalized $C$. This proves existence and uniqueness without a new rank assumption.

The differential of the two pole residues is exactly the remainder


$$
\boxed{\quad
\dot r_0+\dot r_1z
\equiv 2\dot C'-S\dot C-(uz+v)C\pmod D.
\quad}
\tag{7}
$$


All equations (4)–(7) are polynomial identities or finite linear systems. They require no infinite exponential series in characteristic $p$.

Let $T_E=\ker J_{(E_1,E_0)}(\theta)$. Equations (6)–(7) define a linear map


$$
T_E\longrightarrow K[z]/D,\qquad (u,v)\longmapsto \dot r_0+\dot r_1z.
\tag{8}
$$


The reviewed full four-equation transversality theorem proves that (8) is injective. It does not prove $T_E=0$. In particular it is consistent with that theorem that a nonzero exponential tangent is detected by a nonzero pole-residue differential.

An argument establishing that (7) vanishes automatically for every solution of (5) would prove the desired exponential-pair étaleness, by injectivity. No such automatic compatibility identity has been obtained. Polynomial degree bounds justify all three displayed systems but do not by themselves make the remainder in (7) zero. A nonzero remainder is precisely the pole obstruction to extending the exponential tangent to a tangent of the actual triple.

## 3. An exact additional obstruction ideal

Define the actual determinant


$$
J_E=\det\frac{\partial(E_1,E_0)}{\partial(\beta,\gamma)}
$$


with the integer normalization (2). Its explicit bordered determinant and cokernel formulas are in raw_exponential_pair_jacobian_and_pole_pairing.md.

The extra obstruction ideal is


$$
\mathfrak J_d=(E_1,E_0,R_0,R_1,J_E).
\tag{9}
$$


This is an explicit ideal, without an unspecified saturation factor. The reviewed exact full quotient over $R=\mathbb Z_p$ is


$$
R[\beta,\gamma]/(E_1,E_0,R_0,R_1)\cong R/(p^f),
\qquad f=v_p(F_{d+1}).
$$


Consequently, if $j_E$ denotes the image of $J_E$ in this scalar quotient, then


$$
R[\beta,\gamma]/\mathfrak J_d
\cong R/(p^{g}),\qquad
g=\min\{f,v_p(\widetilde j_E)\},
\tag{10}
$$


where $\widetilde j_E$ is any lift of $j_E$ to $R$. The minimum is independent of the lift. Equation (10) includes the zero ring when $g=0$.

Thus proving the desired unit statement is exactly proving $g=0$ at every allowed prime. This note does not prove that assertion. Full four-row Jacobian rank cannot be substituted for it.

## 4. The Hermite-leading data permit the full local multiplicity

The following countermodel concerns the strength of existing algebraic inputs. It is not the actual operator family or its actual residue equations.

Let


$$
P_r=r![t^r]\exp(\beta t+\gamma t^2/2),\quad
P_r=\beta P_{r-1}+(r-1)\gamma P_{r-2},
$$


and fix $h\ge1$. Over $R=\mathbb Z_p$, with the same allowed primes, set


$$
\widetilde E_1=P_{d+1}-p^h,\qquad
\widetilde E_0=\gamma P_d,\qquad
\widetilde R_0=\beta,\qquad\widetilde R_1=\gamma.
\tag{11}
$$


This pair has exactly the actual highest weighted forms for weights
$\operatorname{wt}\beta=1,\operatorname{wt}\gamma=2$. The reviewed monic filtered-basis argument applies unchanged, giving the free basis


$$
\{\beta^a\gamma^b:a+b\le d\}
$$


of rank $D_d=(d+1)(d+2)/2$.

Modulo $p$, the only geometric point of the two-E scheme is the origin. If $\gamma=0$, the first equation gives $\beta=0$. If $\gamma\ne0$, simultaneous vanishing of $P_{d+1},P_d$ propagates backwards through the recurrence, using only units $1,\ldots,d$, to the impossible $P_0=0$. Its local algebra at the origin therefore has the full length $D_d$.

Both E gradients vanish at the origin for $d\ge2$. Every monomial of $P_{d+1}$ has ordinary degree at least $\lceil(d+1)/2\rceil\ge2$, and every monomial of $\gamma P_d$ has degree at least $1+\lceil d/2\rceil\ge2$. But the full four-row Jacobian has rank two, because the two residue rows are the coordinate functions. The full quotient is exactly


$$
R[\beta,\gamma]/(\widetilde E_1,\widetilde E_0,
\widetilde R_0,\widetilde R_1)=R/(p^h).
\tag{12}
$$



Even reducedness of the generic fiber does not eliminate this example. Over a characteristic-zero field, a zero of (11) with $\gamma=0$ has $\beta^{d+1}=p^h\ne0$, and the two E gradients are independent. If $\gamma\ne0$, then $P_d=0$, and
$\partial_\beta P_d=dP_{d-1}\ne0$, by the same backward recurrence. The weighted Euler vector field
$\beta\partial_\beta+2\gamma\partial_\gamma$ annihilates $P_d$ on this locus but takes the value $(d+1)p^h\ne0$ on $P_{d+1}-p^h$. The gradients are again independent. Hence the generic fiber is reduced.

This proves the precise limitation: the Hermite leading forms, the finite-free rank, reduced generic fiber, a unique residue point, full four-equation rank two, and a scalar quotient $R/(p^h)$ still permit exponential local multiplicity $D_d$. A smaller actual multiplicity bound must use an additional identity specific to (1), (3), or their exact polynomial branches.

## 5. A normalization-index alternative to exponential étaleness

Here is a new conditional improvement of the norm-depth estimate.

Let $\mathscr A$ be the actual finite free exponential algebra over $R=\mathbb Z_p$, and $I=(R_0,R_1)$. Suppose $f>0$. Because $R$ is complete, $\mathscr A$ is a product of its finitely many local factors. There is exactly one factor $\mathscr A_*$ whose maximal ideal contains $I$, by the reviewed unique geometric four-equation point. On every other factor $I$ is the unit ideal, so its norm-pencil factor has Gauss valuation zero. Write


$$
r=\operatorname{rank}_R\mathscr A_*,\qquad
\mathscr A_*/I\mathscr A_*\cong R/(p^f).
$$


Assume only for the remainder of this section that
$\mathscr A_*\otimes_R\mathbb Q_p$ is reduced. It is then a product of finite separable fields. Its integral closure is a product of rings of integers,


$$
\mathscr B_*=\prod_\nu \mathcal O_\nu.
$$


It is a finite free $R$-module of rank $r$, contains $\mathscr A_*$, and has finite index


$$
\delta_*=\operatorname{length}_R(\mathscr B_*/\mathscr A_*).
\tag{13}
$$


Let $c$ be the Gauss valuation of the actual norm pencil
$\operatorname{Norm}_{\mathscr A/R}(R_0+tR_1)$, equivalently the large-prime valuation of the correctly cleared scalar carrier.

**Theorem.**


$$
\boxed{\qquad
f\le c\le \min\{rf,\ f+\delta_*\}.
\qquad}
\tag{14}
$$


In particular $\mathscr A_*=\mathscr B_*$ implies $c=f$. The local factor can be ramified over $R$; unramifiedness is not needed for this conclusion.

**Proof of the new upper bound.**
Norms computed on the two lattices $\mathscr A_*,\mathscr B_*$ coincide, since their multiplication operators are conjugate over $\mathbb Q_p$. On a factor $\mathcal O_\nu$, put


$$
m_\nu=\min\{v_\nu(R_0),v_\nu(R_1)\},
$$


using its integer-valued discrete valuation. The ideal generated by the two residues is its uniformizer to exponent $m_\nu$. Multiplicativity of Gauss valuation, or passage through all field embeddings, gives


$$
v_{\rm Gauss}\operatorname{Norm}_{\mathcal O_\nu/R}(R_0+tR_1)
=f_\nu m_\nu
=\operatorname{length}_R\bigl(\mathcal O_\nu/I\mathcal O_\nu\bigr),
$$


where $f_\nu$ is the residue-field degree. This remains true if one of the residues is zero. They cannot both vanish on a component, since $p^f\in I$. Summing gives the exact identity


$$
c=\operatorname{length}_R(\mathscr B_*/I\mathscr B_*).
\tag{15}
$$


There is a natural map


$$
\mathscr A_*/I\mathscr A_*\longrightarrow
\mathscr B_*/I\mathscr B_*.
$$


Its cokernel is $\mathscr B_*/(\mathscr A_*+I\mathscr B_*)$, a quotient of
$\mathscr B_*/\mathscr A_*$, of length at most $\delta_*$. Its image has length at most $f$. Thus (15) is at most $f+\delta_*$.

For completeness, the existing norm lower bound follows by lifting the surjection
$\mathscr A_*\to R/(p^f)$ to a primitive row over $R$. After a unimodular row change, the first row of each residue multiplication matrix is divisible by $p^f$, hence so is the determinant of their pencil. The existing upper bound $rf$ also follows by evaluating $p^f\in I$ in every integral field factor: each conjugate contributes at most $f$ to the Gauss valuation. These give the remaining inequalities in (14). QED.

If the local trace discriminant is denoted by $\Delta_*$, then


$$
v_p(\Delta_*)=
2\delta_*+v_p\operatorname{Disc}(\mathscr B_*/R),
$$


because a change of integral lattice multiplies the discriminant by the square of its determinant. The last term is nonnegative. Therefore


$$
c\le f+\left\lfloor\frac{v_p(\Delta_*)}{2}\right\rfloor.
\tag{16}
$$


If the whole generic exponential algebra is reduced, its explicit monomial-basis trace discriminant can replace $\Delta_*$ to give a weaker global bound. A zero discriminant cannot be used as a finite bound.

## 6. Sharpness and scope of the index term

The index contribution in (14) can really occur, even with reduced generic fiber. For odd $p$, let


$$
\mathscr A=R[x]/(x^2-p^{2h}),\qquad I=(x,p^h).
$$


Then $\mathscr A/I=R/(p^h)$. Its integral closure is $R\times R$, under evaluation at $x=\pm p^h$; the determinant of the basis matrix is $-2p^h$, so $\delta=h$. Moreover


$$
\operatorname{Norm}(x+t p^h)=p^{2h}(t^2-1).
$$


Thus $c=2h=f+\delta$, and the bound is exact. This example explains why a reduced generic fiber alone cannot remove the multiplicity loss.

No all-degree proof of reducedness of the actual local generic E factor, no useful bound for its normalization index, and no unit result for $J_E$ follows from the present work. The two concrete remaining tests are different:

* The actual tangent test is (5)–(7): exclude an exponential tangent whose pole variation is nonzero.
* The norm-depth alternative is to prove reducedness and bound the actual integral-order defect $\delta_*$. This can give exact or nearly exact norm depth even without E-pair étaleness.

Neither conditional improvement supplies the missing Archimedean growth estimate for the actual extremal content, and no claim concerning the rationality of $e+\pi$ is made.
