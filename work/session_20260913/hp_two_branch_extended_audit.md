# Bounded falsification test and the exact three-state obstruction

Date: 2026-09-13. Independent arithmetic continuation after reading
`hp_special_two_branch_attempt.md`, `hp_special_gcd_unit_lift.md`, and
the underlying joint h,E identities. The test range was declared before
execution: every prime 103<=p<=1009, in increasing order, stopping at
the first common root outside +/-1. No larger range was used.

Write

    h_n=H_n(1), E_n=H_n'(1)/n for n>=1, E_0=1,
    J_n(1)=n(h_n+E_n).

Integrality of E_n and the branch valuations are already proved in the
preceding notes; they are not being reproved here.

## 1. Exact finite outcome

All 143 primes in the declared interval have

    {0<=r<p : h_r=J_(r+1)(1)=0 mod p} = {1}.

Thus this bounded test found no counterexample to the proposed
two-branch restriction. It does not prove that restriction.

The primary calculation used the proved four-coordinate h recurrence
and the exact coupling

    E_n=h_n+n h_(n-1)-n(n-1)E_(n-1).

At the predeclared control primes 103,257,509,1009, every value h_n,
H_n'(1), and J_n(1), for 0<=n<=p, was independently recomputed from
successive powers of 1-t+t^2/2 and falling factorials. All matched.
The additional identities proved below were also verified on every
state already generated by this same finite test; this did not extend
its prime or index range.

Code: `check_hp_two_branch_extended_residues.py`.
Complete prime list, root sets, and control flags:
`hp_two_branch_extended_residue_checks.json`.

## 2. A smaller exact joint recurrence

There is a three-state recurrence, simpler than carrying four h
coordinates and a separate E value:

    2h_(n+1) = -3n h_n+n^2 h_(n-1)+n^2 E_n,
    2E_(n+1) = (2-n)h_n+n^2 h_(n-1)-n(n+2)E_n,
                                                        (1)

valid for every n>=1, with initial state (h_1,h_0,E_1)=(0,1,1).

Here is a formal-series proof. Put

    Delta=1+2z-z^2, s=sqrt(Delta),
    w=(1+z-s)/z,
    E(z)=exp(w), G(z)=E(z)/s,
    theta=z d/dz.

These are the previously established exponential generating functions
of E_n and h_n. Direct differentiation, using s^2=Delta, gives

    z^2 E'=(1+z)G-E,
    Delta G'=(1+z)(G-E').                              (2)

Differentiate the first identity and eliminate E' with the second;
equivalently, expand both sides to obtain the exact identity

    2G'=theta^2 E-3theta G+z(theta+1)G.                 (3)

Its z^n/n! coefficient is the first equation (1). Substituting it into
the already proved formula for E_(n+1) gives the second. All operations
are formal differentiation and identities in rational power series;
there is no convergence or division by n assumption.

For S_n=(h_n,h_(n-1),E_n)^T the transition is

    S_(n+1)=T(n) S_n,
    T(n)=1/2 [[-3n,n^2,n^2],
              [2,0,0],
              [2-n,n^2,-n(n+2)]],
    det T(n)=n^3(n+1)/2.                               (4)

Thus for every odd prime p, all transitions from S_1 through S_(p-1)
are invertible over F_p. Every one of those actual states is nonzero.

As a scalar consequence, for n>=2 one may eliminate E_n to obtain

    2(n-1)h_(n+1)
      =-n(n^2+4n-3)h_n
       +n^2(n-1)(1-2n)h_(n-1)
       +n^3(n-1)^2 h_(n-2).                            (5)

For completeness, the elimination identity is

    (n-1)E_n=-(n+1)h_n-2n(n-1)h_(n-1)
                     +n(n-1)^2 h_(n-2).

It follows from (1) at n-1 and the E coupling. The joint system (1)
is preferable modulo p because it avoids the extra scalar division
by n-1.

## 3. The gate is an allowed nonzero projective state

Adding the two equations (1) gives

    J_(r+1)(1)
       =(r+1)((1-2r)h_r+r^2 h_(r-1)-rE_r).              (6)

For 1<=r<=p-2, both r and r+1 are units. Consequently

    h_r=J_(r+1)(1)=0
      iff h_r=0 and E_r=r h_(r-1)
      iff [h_r:h_(r-1):E_r]=[0:1:r].                   (7)

The last equivalence uses the proved nonzero full state. In particular,
at a joint root h_(r-1) is nonzero and

    h_(r+1)=r^2(r+1)h_(r-1)/2,
    E_(r+1)=-h_(r+1),                                  (8)

both nonzero. Two consecutive indices in this interior range therefore
cannot both be common roots.

This is a genuine local restriction, but (7) is a permitted nonzero
state. Invertibility of T(r) does not make it impossible. An additional
invariant, contiguous identity, or global orbit argument would have to
show that the trajectory starting at [0:1:1] never meets [0:1:r] for
2<=r<=p-2. No such argument is established here. The already settled
residue -1 branch is outside this invertibility interval and has not
been reclassified.

## 4. Explicit polynomial and resultant form of the missing exclusion

Fix an odd prime p and work over F_p. For 0<=k<p set

    a_k(X)=(-1)^k sum_(c=0)^floor(k/2)
             (X)_(k-c)/((k-2c)! c! 2^c),               (9)

where (X)_j is the falling factorial. All displayed denominators are
p-units. This is exactly the polynomial [t^k](1-t+t^2/2)^X, obtained
by expanding the two nonconstant terms with the generalized binomial
formula.

Define, reducing polynomial functions modulo X^p-X if desired,

    H_p(X)=sum_(k=0)^(p-1) (X)_k a_k(X),
    E_p(X)=sum_(k=0)^(p-2) (X-1)_k a_k(X).              (10)

For every residue 0<=r<p, these evaluate to h_r and E_r. For r!=0,
the second identity follows by dividing the exact differentiated
coefficient formula by r, since
(r)_(k+1)/r=(r-1)_k. At r=0, a_0(0)=1 and all other a_k(0)=0,
so E_p(0)=1=E_0. Terms with a falling factorial of length at least p
vanish as functions on F_p. Thus no invalid division at r=0 is hidden.

Put

    V_p(X)=H_p(X+1)+E_p(X+1),
    U_p(X)=(X^p-X)/(X^2-1).

For the residue r=p-1 the J prefactor is zero automatically; away
from that residue, its vanishing is V_p(r)=0. Therefore the exact
two-branch assertion at this prime is

    gcd(U_p,H_p,V_p)=1 in F_p[X].                      (11)

Equivalently, the polynomial in an auxiliary indeterminate Y

    R_p(Y)=Res_X(U_p(X), H_p(X)+Y V_p(X))              (12)

must not be identically zero. Indeed U_p is monic and splits into
distinct linear factors at precisely the residues other than +/-1;
hence

    R_p(Y)=product_(r in F_p, r!=+/-1)(h_r+Y V_p(r)).

This product is the zero polynomial if and only if one factor is
identically zero, which is exactly an extra common root. This proof
uses the polynomial ring F_p[Y], not evaluation at just one chosen Y.
The canonical representatives of H_p,E_p have degrees at most p-1;
the definition before reduction also makes every coefficient explicit.

Equations (11) and (12) provide a precise finite-field algebraic target.
They do not prove the desired coprimality. The matrix determinant (4)
only prevents the complete three-state vector from vanishing, whereas
(11) asks for exclusion of a particular one-dimensional state locus.
The existing two-terminal-derivative condition in the preceding note
is another exact expression of this same unresolved compatibility.

## 5. What remains

The odd-part divisibility conjecture, its equivalent all-prime
two-branch exclusion, and the weaker large-prime HP endpoint-gcd
exclusion remain unproved. The extended scan is finite negative
evidence against counterexamples, not positive proof of those claims.

The smaller recurrence is new structural progress: it removes a
redundant coordinate, proves nonzero predecessor and successor values
at any interior common root, and isolates the actual missing orbit
condition (7). A useful next mathematical step would be a rational
invariant or a nonvanishing certificate for (11) that applies to the
explicit initial state. Merely applying recurrence invertibility,
repeating the unit lifts, or classifying the weighted factorial value
at residue -1 would not settle this missing condition.
