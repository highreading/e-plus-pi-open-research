# Literature context for the recurrence-density work

The session's finite-field argument is independently derived, but no
claim of priority in the wider literature is made. The archive search
found no earlier use of progression density for its actual prime slices.

Bell–Burris–Yeats, [On the set of zero coefficients of a function
satisfying a linear differential equation](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1105.6078),
Theorem1.3, proves a characteristic-zero zero-set theorem when a monic
recurrence has polynomial coefficients and its last coefficient is a
nonzero constant. The proof uses p-adic interpolation and Strassman's
theorem. The paper's introduction also records earlier density results
of Bézivin and Methfessel. This is a relevant predecessor, but it does
not state our needed uniform count of congruence zeros for a varying
prime and a prime-dependent initial state. The session's desingularized
recurrence has rational, rather than polynomial, coefficients, so even
the characteristic-zero theorem needs a separate hypothesis check.

Rob Noble's [2011 thesis repository record](https://dalspace.library.dal.ca/items/[session identifier removed])
describes a general nondegeneracy criterion for holonomic zero sets and
its relation to the constant-coefficient eigenvalue criterion. The full
thesis could not be fetched through the available web redirects during
this check. Only its repository abstract has been inspected; no theorem
or proof from the full text is being imported.

The precise additional ingredients retained in our proved finite-field
statement are polynomial observation determinants of bounded degree,
explicit exclusion of zero full states in the actual interval, and
control of the primes for which the determinant becomes identically zero.
These are what let a progression theorem count actual congruence zeros.
The characteristic-zero literature is not a substitute for those steps.
