# Effective content of the actual coordinate adjugate

Status: new paper arithmetic. The earlier global-content paper, exact 13-adic depth theorem, and completed reviews are preserved. No scan, contact solve, or numerical experiment is used. This note works with the exact transformed coordinates of COORDINATE_GLOBAL_COMPANION_CONTENT.md and proves their relation to the smaller cofactor pair below.

## 1. Domain and retained notation

Throughout n=13^(s+1)+3, s>=5, b=3, and the eligibility weight parameter is m=1. In particular n is even. Every approximation-related statement concerns the retained nonempty rational eligible set of positive coordinates j in {1,2,3}. No statement is transferred to coordinate zero or a Gram center.

Put

Q(t)=1-2t+2t^2, N=2n+2,
O=lcm(odd positive integers <=N),
S=diag(1,n+1,(n+1)(n+2)).

Use the actual integer M, Delta=det M, and positive reconstruction matrix

Aplus=[[1,-n-1,n(n+3)],[0,1,-2n-1],[0,0,1]].

For the row a_j of Aplus, put d_j=a_j adj(M) and regard the selector C_j=S d_j^T as a column. The retained endpoint and moment functionals have integer rows U=(U_0,U_1,U_2) and J=(J_0,J_1,J_2), where

K_i=t^n D^n(Q^n t^i)/n!, U_i=K_i(1),
J_i=O calL((K_i-U_i)/(t-1)).

Thus X_j=U C_j, O R_j=J C_j, R_j=L_j/(n!)^2, and B_j=den(R_j/X_j). Define Z with rows O U and J.

In the earlier paper, H is unimodular and

Z H=[[g,0,0],[a,t,0]],
z=H^(-1)C_j, r_j=gcd(|z_0|,|z_1|).

The effective content studied below is exactly this r_j, not the gcd of an unrelated pair. The earlier paper proves log B_j=log|X_j|-log r_j+O(n). Its moment clearer and cancellation identities are retained without reproof.

## 2. An explicit common kernel

Set

A=(n+2)/2, b0=2n+3=4A-1, c0=A(n+1), h=(n+1)(n+2)=2c0,
k=(A,-b0,2A)^T.

The integer vector k is primitive because gcd(A,4A-1)=1. Both U k and J k vanish exactly.

Proof. For F(t)=Q(t)^(n+1), the quadratic selector 2k(t) satisfies

Q^n 2k(t)=(t-1)F'(t)-nF(t).

Indeed (n+1)(t-1)Q'-nQ has coefficients (n+2,-4n-6,2n+4). Taking n derivatives gives

D^n[Q^n 2k]=(t-1)D^(n+1)F.

Its endpoint value at 1 vanishes. Dividing the corresponding K by t-1 leaves t^n D^(n+1)F/n!. The function F has zeros of order n+1 at the two endpoints (1+i)/2 and (1-i)/2. Integration by parts n+1 times against t^n therefore gives zero, with every boundary term zero. This proves the moment assertion as well.

This identifies a common kernel without a matrix solve. The next section proves it is the full rational kernel.

## 3. Rank one is impossible: a closed minor formula

For clarity, introduce Rodrigues polynomials

P_m(t)=D^m[Q(t)^m]/m!,
lambda_m=2^m binom(2m,m),
A_m=P_m(1), v_m=calL(P_m/(1-t)).

Their leading coefficients are lambda_m. Integration by parts proves orthogonality to every polynomial of degree below m. Their squared bilinear norm is

H_m=calL(P_m^2)=(-1)^m 2^(2m+1)/(2m+1).

One direct calculation is H_m=(-1)^m lambda_m calL(Q^m), with

calL(Q^m)=2^(m+1)(m!)^2/(2m+1)!.

The Christoffel-Darboux identity, integrated against 1/(1-t), gives

A_(m+1)v_m-A_m v_(m+1)=(lambda_(m+1)/lambda_m)H_m
=(-1)^m 2^(2m+3)/(m+1).

This identity can also be obtained from the monic three-term recurrence: the adjacent determinant obeys multiplication by the successive norm ratio, starting from calL(1)=2. No positivity of the bilinear norm is assumed.

Differentiating Q^(n+1) once before its remaining n derivatives yields

D^n(t Q^n)/n!=(P_(n+1)+2P_n)/4.

Consequently U_0=A_n and U_1=(A_(n+1)+2A_n)/4. In the moment residual, the polynomial part of t^n/(t-1) has degree n-1 and is orthogonal to both P_n and P_(n+1). Since calL(1/(1-t))=pi, subtraction of pi times the endpoint therefore gives -v_n and -(v_(n+1)+2v_n)/4 respectively. The pi terms cancel in the determinant. Hence the exact rational identity is

U_0 R_1-U_1 R_0=(-1)^n 2^(2n+1)/(n+1),
where R_i=J_i/O.                                                   (1)

In our even-n family it is positive and nonzero. In particular

U_0 J_1-U_1 J_0=O 2^(2n+1)/(n+1) !=0.                            (2)

Thus Z has rank two at every index under consideration, without a scan. The rank-one alternative in the earlier paper remains a valid conditional statement for a hypothetical rank-one map, but it never occurs on this family. Rank zero is also impossible.

Since the cross product of U and J lies in the primitive kernel generated by k, equation (2) determines every minor. Writing Delta_2 for the gcd of the maximal minors, one obtains

Delta_2([U;J])=O 2^(2n+1)/h,
Delta_2(Z)=O^2 2^(2n+1)/h.                                        (3)

These expressions are integers; this also follows directly from the integer cross product and primitivity of k. If d=Delta_1(Z), the earlier residual modulus is now explicitly

D=O^2 2^(2n+1)/(h d^2).

This strengthens its previous size bound to an exact determinantal-divisor formula. It does not determine the content of the actual selector image.

## 4. Identifying the original two transformed coordinates

Define unimodular matrices E and Hstar=E^(-1) by

E=[[b0,A,0],[-2,0,1],[4,1,0]],
Hstar=[[-1,0,A],[4,0,-b0],[-2,1,2A]].

Their determinants are one. The third column of Hstar is exactly k. Therefore the saturated quotient coordinates of C_j are

zeta_0=b0(C_j)_0+A(C_j)_1,
zeta_1=(C_j)_2-2(C_j)_0.                                          (4)

These have exactly the earlier content:

r_j=gcd(|zeta_0|,|zeta_1|).                                       (5)

To verify this for the actual H of the earlier paper, its third column must be either k or -k: it is a primitive integer vector in the now-proved one-dimensional kernel. Thus E H has block form

[[P,0],[*,epsilon]], P in GL_2(Z), epsilon in {1,-1}.

It follows that (zeta_0,zeta_1)^T=P(z_0,z_1)^T. Conversely the original two coordinates are P^(-1)(zeta_0,zeta_1)^T. This proves equality of their gcds by an integral unimodular change, not merely equality of the dimensions of two images.

## 5. Removing the diagonal selector scaling with its exact divisor

The primitive kernel in the unscaled adjugate coordinates is

k_d=(c0,-b0,1)^T.

Let

Qd=[[1,0,c0],[0,1,-b0],[0,0,1]].

This is unimodular. For d_j=(d_0,d_1,d_2), define

w_0=d_0-c0 d_2,
w_1=d_1+b0 d_2.

The exact basis ledger is

E S Qd=[[b0,c0,0],[-2,0,0],[4,n+1,n+1]].                          (6)

In particular

(zeta_0,zeta_1)^T=F(w_0,w_1)^T,
F=[[b0,c0],[-2,0]], det F=h.                                      (7)

The Smith divisors of F are 1,h, because b0 is odd and its entries include -2. The determinant of S is (n+1)h: the factor h acts on the two-dimensional quotient, and the remaining factor n+1 scales the kernel coordinate in (6). Neither factor has been silently discarded.

Put rho=gcd(|w_0|,|w_1|)>0 and (p,q)=(w_0,w_1)/rho. Then

r_j=rho epsilon_j,
epsilon_j=gcd(|b0 p+c0 q|,2|p|), epsilon_j | h.                    (8)

The pair is nonzero since X_j!=0. Formula (8) also covers p=0. A more explicit expression, with delta=gcd(|p|,c0), is

epsilon_j=delta gcd(2,b0(p/delta)+(c0/delta)q).

Its proof uses gcd(p,q)=1 and gcd(p/delta,c0/delta)=1. Thus this last change of integral basis has only a known polynomial-size content cost.

Also, exactly,

X_j=U_0 w_0+(n+1)U_1 w_1,
O R_j=J_0 w_0+(n+1)J_1 w_1.                                     (9)

## 6. The actual smaller cofactor pair

All quantities in this section come from the same actual matrix M and reconstruction row; no surrogate adjugate is introduced.

Set

Mtilde=M Aplus^(-1), Nmat=Qd^T Mtilde.

Both basis changes are integral unimodular, and det Nmat=Delta. Since

adj(Nmat)=Aplus adj(M) Qd^(-T),

the row i=j-1 of adj(Nmat) is exactly

(w_0,w_1,d_2).                                                   (10)

Let k<l be the two column indices different from i. Define

nu_j=gcd(|Nmat_(2,k)|,|Nmat_(2,l)|).

This gcd is positive. If both entries vanished, both w_0 and w_1 would vanish by their cofactor formulas, contradicting X_j!=0. Write

alpha=Nmat_(2,k)/nu_j, beta=Nmat_(2,l)/nu_j,
gcd(|alpha|,|beta|)=1.

The two reduced cofactors are

P_j=(-1)^i (Nmat_(1,k) beta-Nmat_(1,l) alpha),
Q_j=(-1)^(i+1) (Nmat_(0,k) beta-Nmat_(0,l) alpha).                 (11)

Then

(w_0,w_1)=nu_j(P_j,Q_j).                                         (12)

This factors a genuine, explicitly specified common divisor before attempting the unresolved gcd. The third cofactor is

D_j=d_2=(-1)^(i+2) det Nmat_[rows 0,1;columns k,l].

Let sigma_j=gcd(|P_j|,|Q_j|). Multiplying the two-by-two map in (11) by its adjugate and using primitivity of (alpha,beta) shows

sigma_j | D_j.                                                  (13)

For D_j=0 this divisibility is interpreted in its usual sense; a stronger conclusion for that case is given below.

The raw row content is

kraw_j=gcd(|d_0|,|d_1|,|d_2|)
      =gcd(|w_0|,|w_1|,|D_j|)
      =gcd(nu_j sigma_j,|D_j|).                                 (14)

It divides |Delta| by adj(M)M=Delta I and primitivity of a_j. Equation (13) also implies sigma_j | kraw_j. Conversely kraw_j divides nu_j sigma_j. Therefore the known integer

kappa_j=kraw_j/gcd(kraw_j,nu_j)

divides sigma_j. Define the smaller integer pair

Pbar_j=P_j/kappa_j, Qbar_j=Q_j/kappa_j,
G_j=gcd(kraw_j,nu_j), tau_j=gcd(|Pbar_j|,|Qbar_j|).

There is now an exact capped gcd:

tau_j | G_j,
tau_j=gcd(G_j,|Pbar_j|,|Qbar_j|).                                (15)

Indeed sigma_j=kappa_j tau_j divides kraw_j=kappa_j G_j. Combining every factor yields the promised content factorization

r_j=nu_j kappa_j tau_j epsilon_j,                                (16)

where the primitive pair used in (8) is

(p,q)=(Pbar_j,Qbar_j)/tau_j.

This accounts for the original effective content without multiplying in selector coefficient content a second time. In particular the previously defined eta_j=content(C_j) already divides the product in (16); it is not an additional independent factor.

If D_j=0, (14) gives kraw_j=nu_j sigma_j, so kappa_j=sigma_j and tau_j=1. For nonzero D_j one also has tau_j | |D_j|/kappa_j. These are exact alternatives, not generic coprimality assumptions.

Useful uniform bounds following from (16) are

nu_j kappa_j <= r_j <= h nu_j kraw_j,
|X_j|/(h nu_j kraw_j) <= |X_j|/r_j <= |X_j|/(nu_j kappa_j).        (17)

## 7. Companion cancellation after this reduction

Put

Vmat=[[O U_0,O(n+1)U_1],[J_0,(n+1)J_1]].

Equation (2) gives

det Vmat=O^2 2^(2n+1).                                           (18)

Let dV=Delta_1(Vmat), D_V=O^2 2^(2n+1)/dV^2, and Vprim=Vmat/dV. From (9), (12), and (15),

(O X_j,O R_j)=nu_j kappa_j tau_j dV Vprim(p,q)^T.

If (Theta_j,Phi_j)^T=Vprim(p,q)^T, the actual companion denominator is

B_j=|Theta_j|/e_j,
e_j=gcd(|Theta_j|,|Phi_j|)=gcd(D_V,|Theta_j|,|Phi_j|),
e_j | D_V.                                                       (19)

The determinant argument for the last divisibility uses the primitive pair (p,q). It is valid even if Phi_j=0. Here Theta_j!=0 because X_j!=0.

For comparison with the original universal divisors, (6)-(7) imply

d | dV | h d,
Delta_2(Vmat)=h Delta_2(Z).

Thus changing to this cofactor pair introduces exactly the quotient determinant h, while the first divisor changes by an overlapping factor dividing h. One must not multiply the two normalized residual moduli as independent cancellation factors.

Equations (17)-(19) give genuine all-prime bounds, for example

O|X_j|/(dV nu_j kraw_j D_V) <= B_j
                              <= O|X_j|/(dV nu_j kappa_j).        (20)

The quantities on the right are determined from the actual adjugate and its transformed third row. These bounds retain all prime factors. They need not be sharp enough to yield a favorable asymptotic rate.

Every prime dividing D_V is either 2 or at most 2n+2. At a prime outside this support, v_p(B_j)=v_p(Theta_j); such primes are not assumed absent from Theta_j or from the earlier cofactor contents.

## 8. An explicit uniform global upper bound

Here is a coarse numerical-rate bound that follows without evaluating any of the unresolved gcds. It is included to distinguish an actual global bound from only a local factorization.

Set

M0=4*20^n,
Hn=3*5^n (n+2)!,
Kn=(n+2)^2 Hn,
Ln=(n+3)^2 Kn.

The retained universal coefficient bound gives |U_i|<=M0. Cauchy's coefficient estimate on |z|=1 gives

|[z^r] exp(z)(1-z+z^2/2)^n| <= e(5/2)^n,

hence every entry of M has modulus at most Hn. Direct inversion yields

Aplus^(-1)=[[1,n+1,n^2+1],[0,1,2n+1],[0,0,1]].

Its column sums are at most (n+2)^2, so Mtilde has entries bounded by Kn. The top two rows of Nmat have this bound. Its third row is c0 times the first row of Mtilde minus b0 times its second row plus its third row; c0+b0+1 <=(n+3)^2. Thus that row is bounded by Ln.

Formula (11) gives

max(|P_j|,|Q_j|)<=2 Kn Ln/nu_j.

Using (9), and canceling the common factor nu_j kappa_j tau_j before taking the denominator, proves

B_j <= 2 O(n+2) M0 Kn Ln/(nu_j kappa_j tau_j)
    <= 72 O (n+2)^5 (n+3)^2 500^n ((n+2)!)^2.                    (21)

In particular

log B_j <= 2n log n+O(n),                                         (22)

uniformly on the stated family and in eligible j. The first line of (21) retains the actual content saving; the second is a completely explicit bound depending only on n. It is much too large to establish the subfactorial companion budget sought in the larger project. No new positive exponential lower rate for B_j is established here.

## 9. The remaining prime-power question and its precision

The unresolved content has been reduced to the exact pair (Pbar_j,Qbar_j) in (11)-(15), with its known cap G_j. This pair consists of contractions of the first two rows of the actual transformed M against its primitive third-row two-vector. It is not an arbitrary pair of integers.

For a prime ell set

a=v_ell(nu_j kappa_j), g=v_ell(G_j), t=v_ell(tau_j).

Then 0<=t<=g, and for 1<=r<=g,

t>=r iff Pbar_j=Qbar_j=0 mod ell^r
       iff w_0=w_1=0 mod ell^(a+r).                              (23)

Thus tau_j is decided by the reduced pair modulo ell^g. If both entries vanish to this level, the exponent is exactly g because tau_j divides G_j. No extra digit is required merely to distinguish that capped case. If g=0, no residual gcd is possible at this stage.

To obtain the primitive pair (p,q) modulo ell^k after tau_j has been determined requires the original w pair modulo ell^(a+t+k), followed by division by the exact known factors and their unit parts. An uncapped valuation determination by searching for the first nonzero coordinate would instead require ell^(a+t+1).

The basis factor epsilon_j is decided using (8); its exponent is capped by v_ell(h). The last output cancellation in (19) is decided from the primitive pair modulo ell^(v_ell(D_V)). Computing the exponent of Theta_j itself requires its own first nonzero digit and is not supplied by that capped cancellation calculation. In particular primes outside D_V still require the valuation of Theta_j.

These precision statements include ell=2. Every matrix and reduced cofactor used here is integral. No division by a nonunit has been performed without explicitly accounting for the precision loss.

The available elimination identities explain why no better rate has been proved. Equation (13) forces divisibility by a determinant, while (14)-(15) constrain the remaining content to an overlap of kraw_j and nu_j. They do not determine whether Pbar_j and Qbar_j vanish simultaneously at intermediate powers of those overlap primes. The orthogonal-polynomial identity determines the universal two-row determinant, but it imposes no such vanishing on these exponential Toeplitz cofactors. The known 13-adic theorem does not fill that gap at other primes. No randomness or unit assumption is justified.

## 10. Relation to the completed local theorem and final scope

The completed theorem v_13(X_j)=0 and v_13(B_j)=s+1 is retained as a dependency, not rederived. Since nu_j kappa_j tau_j divides X_j by (9), all three factors are 13-units on this family. The earlier identity X_j=g_U r_j u_j likewise makes r_j a 13-unit. Thus the new effective-content question is not a missing replay of the already settled 13-layer.

New proved results are: the explicit common kernel; the closed nonzero universal minor and exact determinantal divisors; equality of the new quotient gcd with the original r_j; the complete diagonal/basis ledger; the actual cofactor factorization (16) with capped remaining gcd (15); all-prime bounds (20); and the explicit coarse upper bound (21).

The rank-one branch is now excluded on the entire requested family by a paper identity. The rank-two effective content is substantially reduced but not estimated at a favorable asymptotic scale. In particular log B_j=o(n log n), an exponential upper window for B_j, and any new positive exponential lower rate remain unproved. The exact smaller unresolved pair and the precision needed after removing each known content factor are specified above. No conclusion about Gram centers, shrinking primitive forms, or rationality of e+pi is asserted.
