# Factorial-scaled coefficients for canonical numerator lifting

Status: new main-agent author proof, not independently reviewed. This continues CANONICAL_HIGH_DENOMINATOR_STAGE.md and the last automatically preserved coefficient-block deduction. No computation or seed replay is claimed.

The new point is that the nonunit leading coefficients of the raw coefficient recurrence can be removed from the actual matrix and exponential contractions. This improves the lifting representation; it supplies no bound on the resulting numerator valuation.

## 1. Raw coefficient recurrence and sharp block truncation

Write

    b_s(n)=[t^s](2-2t+t^2)^n,
    b_(-1)=0, b_0=2^n.

Differentiating the defining polynomial gives exactly

    2(s+1)b_(s+1)
      =2(s-n)b_s+(2n-s+1)b_(s-1).                 (1)

For the fixed b=3 construction, the needed coefficients have s<=n+2. Let

    sqrt(2n+2)<p<=n,
    n=ap+r, 3<=r<=p-3.

Among the steps computing b_0,...,b_(n+2), the leading coefficient in (1) is a nonunit modulo p exactly at s=jp-1, 1<=j<=a. It then has valuation one. Using only (1) modulo p^k at that step does not determine b_(jp) modulo p^k: one would need a higher-precision right side or additional information. This is a limitation of that recurrence representation, not an intrinsic impossibility of computing the contractions.

The unscaled canonical formulas are

    M_ij=sum_s b_s(n)(n+i)_(j+s),
    Ecal_i=sum_s b_s(n)(n+2)_(2-i+s)Dcal_(2n+i-s),

with the ranges in the stage paper. All factorial factors lie below p^2. Thus

    v_p((n+i)_(j+s))
       =a-floor((n+i-j-s)/p),
    v_p((n+2)_(2-i+s))
       =a-floor((n+i-s)/p).

Consequently, modulo p^k, the sums may be truncated respectively at

    s<=min(2n,n+i-j,r+i-j+(k-1)p),
    s<=min(2n,n+i,r+i+(k-1)p).                     (2)

Terms beyond these cutoffs vanish individually. At k=a+1 the windows already include the full sums. A first-residue short window must not be extrapolated to arbitrary lifting depth.

## 2. A recurrence with leading coefficient one

Define the factorial-scaled integer coefficient

    C_s(n)=s! b_s(n), C_(-1)=0, C_0=2^n.

Multiplying (1) by s!/2 gives

    C_(s+1)=(s-n)C_s
       +[ns-s(s-1)/2]C_(s-1).                    (3)

The bracket is an integer for every integer n,s. Equation (3) has leading coefficient one and works modulo every positive integer. There are no exceptional modular steps at multiples of p.

Recovering b_s alone from C_s would still require division by s!, with the corresponding precision loss. The next identities show that such recovery is unnecessary for the actual canonical contractions.

## 3. Exact division-free contractions

For all indices in the original sums,

    (n+i)_(j+s)/s!
       =(n+i)_j binom(n+i-j,s),
    (n+2)_(2-i+s)/s!
       =(n+2)_(2-i) binom(n+i,s).

Both right sides are integers. Substituting C_s=s!b_s gives

    M_ij=(n+i)_j
       sum_(s=0)^min(2n,n+i-j) C_s binom(n+i-j,s), (4)

    Ecal_i=(n+2)_(2-i)
       sum_(s=0)^min(2n,n+i)
          C_s binom(n+i,s)Dcal_(2n+i-s).           (5)

Equations (3)-(5) are exact identities over the integers. Binomial coefficients are integers evaluated before reduction; they may alternatively be generated by Pascal addition. No factorial inverse modulo p is used.

Therefore C_s modulo p^k is sufficient to determine M and Ecal modulo p^k. There is no need for p^(k+floor(s/p)) precision merely to recover the raw b_s. The factorial already present in each weight absorbs that division.

This resolves a representation-level obstacle in the previous raw recurrence. It does not make the resulting sums nonzero modulo p, control cancellations among their terms, or bound a final gcd.

In the large-prime range of Section 1, v_p(C_s)>=floor(s/p). Hence s>=kp contributes zero modulo p^k. The sharper original cutoffs (2) remain valid and can be used with (4)-(5); the factorial-scaled form does not invalidate them.

## 4. Endpoint data and integer contraction

The remaining forcing entries are

    J_0=P_n,
    J_1=P_n/2+P_(n+1)/4,
    J_2=P_(n+2)/8.

A division-free finite formula is

    P_t=sum_(j=0)^floor(t/2)
       binom(t,2j)binom(2j,j)2^(t-j).              (6)

It follows by expanding CT_x(2+x+2/x)^t. For the odd primes considered here, only the fixed powers 2,4,8 in J require inversion, and these are units.

The sequence Dcal_j satisfies Dcal_j=jDcal_(j-1)+1, also without division. Thus (3)-(6), followed by integer adjugates and contractions, determine the unprimitive endpoint-corrected numerator

    S_n=z_n^T Ecal_n+d_n Delta_n x_(n,0)

modulo p^k. This computation requires no inverse of M modulo p, even when its determinant is zero modulo p. Normality is needed for the rational center at the actual integer index, not for these polynomial congruence operations.

No computation is executed in this note. The formulas specify exact finite data for a subsequent proof or calculation; they are not an efficiency claim for enormous n.

## 5. Primitive normalization still has a real precision cost

For the same center,

    S_eff=h S_n/g,
    g=gcd(z_n), h=det(Krec^T Omega Krec).

Restrict to primes p not dividing dh, as in the deep-valuation target. Put u=v_p(g). Then

    v_p(S_eff)=v_p(S_n)-u.

Thus testing p^K dividing S_eff requires S_n modulo p^(K+u), together with the exact value of u. Establishing exact depth K requires precision through p^(K+u+1). Determining u itself requires identifying the first nonzero coordinate after dividing z_n by its common p-power.

The factorial-scaled recurrence removes the extra division cost of the raw coefficient algorithm. It does not remove this genuine normalization requirement. In particular, knowing S_n modulo p^K without knowing u cannot decide the requested primitive depth.

## 6. Remaining arithmetic question

The stage paper proves that a geometric denominator would require v_p(S_eff) of order at least sqrt(n) at some p>sqrt(2n+2), and aggregate cancellation of almost all double-factorial mass. Equations (3)-(6) give a modularly regular representation of the relevant sums at any prescribed precision.

What remains unresolved is whether their specific contracted cancellations can reach those depths, after removal of the actual selector content. A recurrence with unit leading coefficient determines residues from its initial data; it does not, by itself, bound the valuation of a selected nonlinear contraction. No propagation theorem excluding the necessary high-depth zeros has been obtained.

The next useful step is a parameter-uniform identity or valuation restriction for those contractions, not another first-layer support scan. This is the main numerator direction and does not duplicate the children's current residual, phase-crossing, exceptional-deficit, or coordinate-content assignments. The e+pi problem remains OPEN.
