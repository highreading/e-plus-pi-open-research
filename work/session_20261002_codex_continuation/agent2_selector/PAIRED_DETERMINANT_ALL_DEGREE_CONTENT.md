# All-degree content and carry for the actual paired determinant

Author theorem, 2026-10-02. The fresh archive/primary-paper gate and the exact M22 normalization are recorded in `PAIRED_DERANGEMENT_DETERMINANT_CONTENT_PROGRESS.md`. Additional primary searches on Hankel divisibility, finite differences and local Smith form opened Eberly–Giesbrecht–Villard, *On Computing the Determinant and Smith Form of an Integer Matrix*, https://perso.ens-lyon.fr/gilles.villard/BIBLIOGRAPHIE/PDF/detsmith.pdf, Introduction equation(1). Smith form and its determinant product are established background. The new object is the complete rational beta-weighted projected pair, and the exact recurrences below supply its content rather than assuming it from a row clearer.

## 1. Actual coefficient pair and response reduction

Fix k>=2, n=2k−1, and root's primitive integer q_n(y), with degree n and the rho-orthogonality constraints. Set Q=q_n(−1), and define beta and R as in the progress note. Then

    det H=A+S B,
    A=det R,
    B=Q det D,
    D_ij=beta[y^(i+j)(1+y)^2q_n(y)]   (0<=i,j<k−1).

The unimodular basis1,(1+y),y(1+y),... proves this without dividing by any determinant. In particular zeros of either output coefficient do not affect the divisibility assertions.

Let C be the least positive common denominator of A and B. C is odd. Let

    G=gcd(CA,CB).

If B!=0, the actual center denominator is

    q_actual=|CB|/G.                         (1)

The theorem below bounds G itself. It applies to the actual pair after q_n is made primitive and does not remove an arbitrary normalization of q_n.

## 2. A dyadic moment recurrence

Write beta_r=−F_r+L_r, with F_r=(2r)! and

    L_0=0,
    L_(r+1)+L_r=4/(2r+1).                  (2)

Every L_r is in4 Z_2. Let E denote forward shift in r, Delta=E−1, and f_r=1/(2r+1). Direct subtraction proves for every d>=0

    Delta^d f_r = (−2)^d d! /
                  product_(j=0)^d (2r+2j+1).       (3)

All factors in the denominator are odd. Therefore

    v2(4 Delta^d f_r)=2+d+v2(d!)

uniformly for all r>=0. The factorial term satisfies

    E^m F_r=0 mod2^a for every r>=0

whenever v2((2m)!)>=a.

Define

    m_a=min{m>=0 : v2((2m)!)>=a},
    d_a=min{d>=0 : 2+d+v2(d!)>=a}.

For a=1,2, the logarithmic term vanishes modulo2^a, so beta obeys the monic recurrence E^(m_a) beta=0 mod2^a, of order m_a. For a>=3, equations(2),(3) show

    E^(m_a) Delta^(d_a)(E+1) beta=0 mod2^a.       (4)

Its order is m_a+d_a+1. Since any integer polynomial in E commutes with these operators, the same recurrence holds after multiplying the beta functional by ANY integer polynomial q(y). No orthogonality, coefficient-size estimate or congruence for q is needed here.

For the D moment sequence, multiplication by(1+y)^2 eliminates one factor(E+1) from the required recurrence: for a>=3,

    E^(m_a) Delta^(d_a) D_sequence=0 mod2^a.      (5)

Indeed its L part is(E+1)^2 q_n(E)L=4(E+1)q_n(E)f; its F part is still killed by E^(m_a). For a=1,2 it has recurrence E^(m_a). Thus its orders are one smaller than(4) at every a>=3.

## 3. From a monic recurrence to determinant content

Elementary lemma: suppose a sequence z_s in Z_2 obeys a monic recurrence of order r modulo2^a for all s>=0. The t-square Hankel matrix (z_(i+j)) has at least max(t−r,0) Smith invariant factors divisible by2^a.

Proof: if r<t, apply the recurrence consecutively to its columns r,...,t−1, keeping the original columns by a triangular unimodular column transformation. The transformed last t−r columns lie in2^a Z_2^t. The image of the matrix modulo2^a is consequently generated by at most r columns. A diagonal Smith matrix has one nonzero cyclic image summand for each invariant factor of valuation<a. Hence at most r such invariant factors exist. This argument also covers singular matrices, interpreting a zero Smith factor as valuation infinity.

Adding this bound over all a gives a determinant valuation, because for each Smith factor s, v2(s)=sum_(a>=1)1_(2^a|s).

Set the explicit finite integer

    F(k)= sum_(a=1)^2 max(k−1−m_a,0)
         +sum_(a>=3) max(k−m_a−d_a−1,0).          (6)

Every summand is eventually zero. Apply the lemma to R, which has size k and the recurrence(4), and to D, which has size k−1 and(5). The size decrease and recurrence-order decrease cancel for every a>=3. At a=1,2 use the weaker of the two bounds. Since Q is an integer,

    v2(A)>=F(k),       v2(B)>=F(k).              (7)

No assertion about v2(Q), positivity, or nonzero is used. Since C is odd,

    2^F(k) divides G=gcd(CA,CB).                 (8)

This is a shared factor of the ACTUAL full coefficient pair. In particular, if B!=0,

    q_actual <= |CB|/2^F(k).                    (9)

The complete primitive linear form is(CA+CB S)/G, so both its coefficient height and its full signed residual are scaled by the same actual G. This content theorem alone does not prove that the primitive residual is small.

Legendre's formula yields

    m_a=a/2+O(log(a+2)), d_a=a/2+O(log(a+2)),
    F(k)=k^2/2+O(k log(k+2)).                   (10)

Thus the universally removable dyadic factor has logarithm (log2)k²/2+O(k log k), or (log2)n²/8+O(n log n). The exact small values F(k), k2..8, are0,1,3,6,9,13,18. These are lower bounds; they do not claim the larger observed content2,8,16,30,46,66,88 is explained completely.

## 4. Odd-prime orthogonality forces a polynomial divisor

Let p be an odd prime and n>=p+1. Then

    (y−1)^p(y+1) divides q_n(y) modp.           (11)

Proof: the derangement factorial expansion gives, for integer r,

    D_(2r) = sum_(j=0)^(p−1) (−1)^j (2r)_(j) modp,

where (x)_(j) is the falling factorial. This polynomial in r has degree p−1 and leading coefficient 2^(p−1)=1 in F_p. Call it P(r). The exact orthogonality says

    q_n(E)P(s)−(−1)^s Q=0 modp     (0<=s<n).

Compare s=0 and s=p, using polynomial periodicity and the oddness of p; this forces2Q=0 and hence Q=0 modp. Now q_n(E)P(s) vanishes at every residue s0,...,p−1, so it is the zero polynomial. The cyclic difference space generated by P has dimension p: Delta^j P has distinct degrees p−1−j with nonzero leading coefficients for j0,...,p−1. Its annihilator is exactly(E−1)^p. Therefore(y−1)^p divides q_n modulo p, while Q=0 separately supplies y+1. These factors are coprime for odd p, proving(11). The assertion allows any degree drop of q_n modulo p.

Consequently every odd prime<=n−1 divides Q. This is a coefficient fact, not common output content: the beta denominators may destroy the desired cancellation.

## 5. Exact odd-prime carry map and final denominator

Let M=4n−3 and L=lcm{odd integers<=M}. Set the integer matrix T=L R, d=det T and J=u^T adj(T)u. Then EXACTLY

    A=d/L^k, B=Q J/L^(k−1),
    center=−d/(L Q J),
    q_actual=|L Q J|/gcd(d,L Q J),              (12)

whenever B!=0. All row-clearing content is retained in this full final gcd. In particular, at any odd prime p,

    v_p(q_actual)=max(0,v_p(L)+v_p(Q)+v_p(J)−v_p(d)). (13)

To compute the carried first residue, let a=v_p(L)>0 and ell=L/p^a, a p-unit. For0<=r<=2n−1 define

    K_(p,a)(r)=sum over positive odd u with p^a u<=2r−1
                (−1)^(r−(p^a u+1)/2) /u in F_p.     (14)

Every u in this sum is<p, since p^(a+1)>M, so all inverses exist. The factorial term is annihilated by L modulo p, and precisely the denominators with valuation a survive. Thus

    T_ij=4 ell sum_t q_t K_(p,a)(i+j+t) modp.        (15)

This is the complete rational beta carry, including both the factorial part's disappearance at this precision and the entire arctangent remainder. It is not an uncancelled factorial estimate.

If the determinant of the matrix on the right of(15) is a p-unit, then v_p(d)=0 and(13) gives

    v_p(q_actual)=a+v_p(Q)+v_p(J)>=a+v_p(Q).        (16)

Therefore a nonzero carried determinant certifies surviving denominator cost, rather than a shared-content gain. When it vanishes, higher carries and J must be retained; no uniform gain is inferred. Equation(11) alone cannot settle(13).

## 6. Scope

Equations(6)–(10) prove an infinite-degree shared dyadic factor of the complete projected coefficient pair. Equations(11)–(16) identify the exact all-degree odd-prime divisor/carry interface, with the ACTUAL final denominator. They do not prove a global odd-content rate, explain the full finite dyadic exponents, establish nonzero B, or give a small primitive linear form. Those unresolved statements are not replaced by generic Gram positivity or a prime scan.

## 7. Complete higher carry, including the factorial boundary

For any precision b>=1, the preceding top carry has an exact refinement. Keep p^a||L, ell=L/p^a and write j=p^v u with p∤u. For an index r in the actual range define

    K_v^(c)(r)=sum_(positive odd u, p∤u, p^v u<=2r−1)
                   (−1)^(r−(p^v u+1)/2) u^(-1) modp^c.

All inverses are p-adic units. Directly grouping the complete odd denominators gives

    L beta_r = −p^a ell (2r)!
               +4 ell sum_(v=0)^a p^(a−v) K_v^(b−a+v)(r)
               modp^b,                               (17)

where only terms with a−v<b occur. The factorial term is retained whenever its valuation is below b. Equivalently, replace v by a−c, c0,...,min(a,b−1), to obtain a layer-by-layer carry expansion. Each layer uses inverses modulo p^(b−c), so lower layers are not lost by reducing every reciprocal modulo p.

Apply(17) to the finite q_t convolution before taking det T or adj(T). Determinants and the contraction J are polynomial expressions in all entries, hence their residues modulo p^b follow exactly. This determines the three valuations v_p(d),v_p(J),v_p(Q) entering the ACTUAL denominator(13), including any shared-content cancellation. It is an all-depth carry identity; it does not assert that the carried determinants are nonzero or that their zeros provide favorable arithmetic density.
