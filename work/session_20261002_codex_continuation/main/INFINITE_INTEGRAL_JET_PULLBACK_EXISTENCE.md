Root original deduction,2026-10-02. Complete author proof with a finite exact prefix certificate. Independent review pending. This is a new analytic construction theorem, not a solution of the rationality of e+pi. Research remains active.

## 2. Precise results

Set R=53/20=2.65. There exists a real-coefficient phi holomorphic on |w|<R, with

    phi(0)=0, phi(1)=1,
    phi^(k)(0) in Z for every k>=0,
    phi(w) != 1+i,1-i throughout that disk.       (1)

Moreover, for EVERY real r with1<r<R, there is an entire integral-Hurwitz POLYNOMIAL P fixing0 and1 such that

    G(w)=4 arctan(P(w)/(2-P(w)))

has its branch analytic at0 continued throughout a neighborhood of the closed disk |w|<=r, all G^(k)(0) integral, and G(1)=pi. In particular the supremum of available polynomial composition radii is at least2.65. No single explicitly displayed polynomial with radius2.65 is asserted.

Combined with the current exact sixth-jet obstruction, the supremum over this real endpoint-fixing integral-Hurwitz class lies between2.65 and2.67. The lower theorem is an existence/convergence construction; the upper theorem excludes the exact disk2.67. Neither theorem addresses the primitive denominator in a mixed approximation.

## 3. Schur constants and their integral lattice

Use the normalized universal cover p:D->Omega, Omega=C minus{1+i,1-i}, with p(0)=0,p'(0)=a>0 and p(t)=1 along the real path. Its local inverse xi has xi'(0)=1/a. These constants and the real branch are established in the preceding notes.

Lift phi as phi=p composed with f, and write h(z)=f(Rz)=z g0(z). Then g0(1/R)=Rt. For successive real Schur constants b_k define

    g_k(z)=(g_(k-1)(z)-b_k)/[z(1-b_k g_(k-1)(z))].

At order k, let T_k=g_(k-1)(s), s=1/R. Fixing the earlier integral phi derivatives, the new constant is affine in the kth derivative j_k:

    b_k=B_k+F_k j_k,
    F_k=R^k/[a k! product_(l<k)(1-b_l^2)]>0.     (2)

The coefficient of j_k in xi composed with phi is1/(a k!), the scaling contributes R^k, and each previous Schur division contributes1/(1-b_l^2). All lower-jet contributions are included in the real number B_k. Thus no assumption on their size is necessary.

The update formulas are

    T_(k+1)=R(T_k-b_k)/(1-b_k T_k),
    F_(k+1)=R F_k/[(k+1)(1-b_k^2)].              (3)

For |b_k|<1, the necessary and sufficient compatibility of the two prescribed real values of g_(k-1) is the usual Schwarz-Pick inequality |(T_k-b_k)/(1-b_kT_k)|<=s. Every finite compatible sequence has an interpolant: set the final Schur function equal to its prescribed constant endpoint and reverse the Schur transformations.

## 5. The infinite rounding invariant

Assume k>=9, |T_k|<=1/4 and0<F_k<=1/40. Choose an integer j_k nearest to(T_k-B_k)/F_k. Then

    |b_k-T_k|<=F_k/2,
    |b_k|<=1/4+1/80=21/80<1.

Consequently1-b_kT_k>=1-(21/80)(1/4)=299/320. The endpoint update satisfies

    |T_(k+1)|
       <=R(1/40)/[2(299/320)]
       =53/1495 <1/4.                           (5)

It also satisfies Schwarz-Pick since |T_(k+1)|<1. The affine spacing contracts:

    F_(k+1)/F_k
       <=R/[10(1-(21/80)^2)]
       =1696/5959 <1.                           (6)

Thus the invariant holds for all later k. Ties in nearest-integer choice can be resolved either way. No estimate of the offset B_k, no probability assumption, and no finite numerical extrapolation enter this induction. Every derivative jet can be chosen integral forever.

## 6. Holomorphic realization of all jets

For each finite N>=8, choose the jets by the rule above, set the last Schur function equal to T_(N+1), and reverse all N Schur steps. This produces a real Schur function g0,N, hence h_N(z)=z g0,N(z), and

    f_N(w)=h_N(w/R), phi_N=p composed with f_N.

They satisfy f_N(0)=0,f_N(1)=t, and their first N phi jets equal the chosen integers. Schwarz's lemma gives |f_N(w)|<=|w|/R. On every compact subdisk |w|<=r<R, their images therefore stay in the compact disk |z|<=r/R<1. By Montel, a subsequence f_N converges locally uniformly to a holomorphic f taking values insideD, with f(0)=0,f(1)=t. The derivative f'(0)=1/a prevents constancy.

The composition phi=p composed with f is consequently holomorphic, real, endpoint-fixing and omits the two punctures. Its kth derivative is the eventually fixed integer j_k, because compact convergence implies convergence of derivatives. This proves (1). No entire-phi claim is made at this stage.

## 7. Conversion to entire endpoint-fixing Hurwitz polynomials

Write phi(w)=sum_(j>=1) j_j w^j/j!, with every j_j integer. For M>=1 set

    P_M(w)=sum_(j=1)^M j_j w^j/j!
              +[1-sum_(j=1)^M j_j/j!]w^(M+1).   (7)

This fixes0 and1. Its first M derivatives are j_j, and its derivative of order M+1 is

    (M+1)[M!-sum_(j=1)^M j_j M!/j!],

an integer. Every higher derivative is zero. Thus P_M is entire integral-Hurwitz. Equivalently it is expressed in the endpoint basis w^m(1-w)/m! with integer coefficients.

For r<r2<R, Cauchy bounds give uniform convergence P_M->phi on |w|<=r: the ordinary Taylor tail is O((r/r2)^(M+1)), while the endpoint correction has the same bound because phi(1)=1 and its omitted Taylor tail at1 is O(r2^(-M-1)). The compact image phi(|w|<=r) has a strictly positive distance from the two punctures. Hence every sufficiently large M makes P_M omit both punctures on the closed disk. Being a polynomial, its nearest puncture preimage is strictly outside that disk, so the composition Taylor radius is strictly greater than r.

## 8. All-order composition integrality and the endpoint value

For F(w)=4 arctan(w/(2-w)), F'(w)=2/(1-w+w^2/2). If D_n=F^(n)(0), then D1=D2=2 and

    D_(n+1)=n D_n-binom(n,2)D_(n-1), n>=2.

This proves every D_n integral. The integer Bell-polynomial composition formula then gives every derivative of F composed with P_M integral. The real path P_M([0,1]) backtracks only on the real axis, which contains no puncture; integrating the rational derivative fixes its endpoint value at F(1)=pi. Apparent singularity of the arctangent expression at P_M=2 is a branch-coordinate artifact and does not obstruct this continuation.

This completes the polynomial existence theorem. Adding exp(w) gives a function with integral jets and endpoint e+pi, but its finite Taylor radius still provides no factorial-scale denominator cancellation. The main problem remains open.
