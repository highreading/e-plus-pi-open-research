Exact observation: with N fixed, raw kernels at n+4 are (1-w)^4 times the kernels at n. Actual projected kernels have a linear multiplier that also changes. An integer combination of K consecutive n-indices therefore has a multiplier of degree at most 4(K-1)+1 relative to the first raw kernel. Unless it vanishes identically, its origin zero order is at most that degree. For fixed K the resulting origin-pole factorial clearer can improve by only a bounded number of factorial factors, with dyadic valuation O_K(log N), rather than an N-sized decrease. This is a bound on the pole-removal mechanism, NOT a bound on the final primitive content.

For two ACTUAL projected kernels F,F' at n,n+4, the origin-leading-pole cofactor combination is

    F*=m1' F-m1 F'.

Its common multiplier is

    m1 m1'[1-(1-w)^4]
       -w[m0 m1'-m0' m1(1-w)^4],

which has a zero at w=0. Since each actual kernel has A(F)=R(F), their combination preserves that equality. Its exact target coefficient is U*=m1' U-m1 U', and both endpoint/exponential outputs combine linearly. Nonzero U* and its full evaluated content remain to be controlled.

For ANY integral combination with U*!=0, define E*=N!B(F*) and Pi*=4 Im P*(a). With the appropriate full primitive clearer O, the final denominator is exactly

    q*=N! O |U*|/gcd(N! O U*, O E*+N! O Pi*).

Individual E values are odd, but their linear combination need not be. Thus the old compulsory q2 lower bound cannot be transferred to F*. To remove an N-sized dyadic factor one needs an actual high-depth congruence for E*+N!Pi*, together with U*, and weight/cofactor heights. Bounded pole removal alone does not supply it. This is the live content mechanism, retained as an interface only.
