## Objects, normalizations, and premises

H_n has high rows k=n+1,…,3n, B/C degrees≤n, and size 2n×(2n+2). X_n has rows k=n,…,3n, B/C degrees<n, and size (2n+1)×2n. Entries are (k)_j and k!τ_(k−j), with τ_a=[z^a]arctan z. J_n is H augmented by the endpoint row C(1)−4B(1); it is not H. D and F are the positive maximal-minor contents of H and X respectively.

This report uses ηᴴ for the high-matrix difference content of 2163, θ for X difference content, 𝖹 for the matrix, and Z for the endpoint scalar. In 2089, δ_r is one deleted-row minor of 𝖹; the J content in 2192 is written δᴶ. The unit ηᵘ=δ_n/h in 1986 differs from ηᴴ. These superscripts only disambiguate this report and do not alter the source manuscripts.

## R1: at most one nonunit factor for H and two for J

This corresponds to 2192 §§1–4, lines 18–217; its Wronskian premises are in 2188 §2 and 2189 §§2–3. They are independently checked here without inheriting historical PASS labels.

Take p>3n and K=F_p, using only the exponential and arctangent Taylor polynomials E_T and F_T through degree 3n. Every denominator is a unit. For a nonzero triple in ker H, the low polynomial A is uniquely reconstructed and R=A+BE_T+CF_T=O(z^(3n+1)). Both B and C are nonzero. If C=0, division by k! makes the B equations a falling-factorial polynomial of degree≤n vanishing at 2n>n distinct field elements. If B=0, the C equations split by parity into signed Cauchy blocks: denominators lie in 1,…,3n, row/column parameters are distinct mod p, and each block has enough rows, so each has full column rank. Either case forces the zero triple.

Set D(z)=1+z². The denominator-cleared Wronskian is



$$
\mathcal N=\det\begin{pmatrix}
D^2A&B&C\\
D^2A'+DC&B+B'&C'\\
D^2A''+2DC'-D'C&B+2B'+B''&C''
\end{pmatrix}.
$$



Set a=A/C, b=B/C, k=a′+1/D, and l=b′+b. Successive subtraction of row multiples corresponding to the C column gives



$$
\mathcal N=D^2C^3\{k(l+l')-k'l\}.
$$



If l=0, the nonzero rational function b satisfies b′/b=−1, contradicting its logarithmic derivative being O(1/z) at infinity. If k=0, there is also a contradiction: over the algebraic closure, 1/(1+z²) has nonzero simple-pole residues, whereas a rational derivative has no simple-pole terms. If the bracket vanishes, (k/l)′/(k/l)=1 gives the same contradiction at infinity. Hence N is nonzero. This proof permits additional constant functions in characteristic p and does not assume that derivative zero forces an ordinary constant.

Subtract E_T times the B column and F_T times the C column from the first column. The remaining three terms have orders at least 3n+1, 3n, and 3n−1 at zero, so z^(3n−1) divides N. Only Taylor derivative errors through these orders are used; (3n+1)! is not used. The boundary p=3n+1 is permitted.

The top terms cancel on expansion, giving deg N≤3d+2, with coefficient b_d(a_dc_(d−1)−a_(d−1)c_d+c_d²); missing coefficients are zero. This is a polynomial coefficient identity and remains valid for degenerate leading coefficients. No nonzero kernel triple of degree≤n−2 exists. A nonzero triple of degree≤n−1 must have N=κz^(3n−1), κ≠0, so b_(n−1)≠0. This linear functional is injective on that slice, giving slice dimension≤1.

The ordinary determinant of any three kernel triples of degree≤n becomes divisible by z^(3n+1) after replacing the A column by R. Its original degree is at most 3n, so it vanishes identically. The degree-n leading-coefficient map has rank≤2 and kernel dimension≤1, yielding



$$
2\le\dim\ker H_n\le3,\qquad
\operatorname{rank}(H_n\bmod p)\ge2n-1.
$$



If the dimension is 3, the kernel is generated by T,zT,S with deg T=n−1. T has no nonconstant common factor. If such a factor has degree m and order h≤m at zero, division leaves a nonzero N with order at least 3n−1−h and degree at most 3n−1−3m, forcing 3m≤h, a contradiction.

The primary-reviewed nonzero endpoint system makes H full row rank in characteristic zero. H therefore has at most one nonunit factor over Z_p. Let its valuation be e. After eliminating a unit block of size 2n−1, the remaining part of J is



$$
\begin{pmatrix}p^e&0&0\\a&b&c\end{pmatrix},\qquad(b,c)\ne(0,0).
$$



Set s=min(v_p(b),v_p(c)) and r=min(e,v_p(a),v_p(b),v_p(c)). The minimum entry and 2×2-minor valuations give Smith exponents r and e+s−r, and v_p(δᴶ)=e+s. Thus J has at most two nonunit factors, unlike H. Here s is the content of the endpoint row on H's integral free kernel; a affects the distribution between the two factors. Rank identifies which factors are nonunits without bounding e or s.

## R2: integral finite differences retain complete depth

This corresponds to 2163 §§2–4, lines 42–166. The global X-content identity is also in supporting manuscript 2129 §1, lines 13–48.

Retain the first m rows and replace every remaining row by an adjacent mth difference. Each new row has coefficient 1 on its newest original row and uses only earlier original rows. The transformation U is integral lower triangular with diagonal 1 and det U=1. The m falling-factorial columns have degree<m and vanish after differencing. The leading block V is a consecutive Vandermonde with det V=∏_(j<m)j!. Therefore



$$
UM=\begin{pmatrix}V&C_0\\0&G\end{pmatrix}.
$$



When this factorial product is a p-unit, legitimate row/column elimination yields diag(I_m,G), retaining all remaining Smith factors and their full depths. Taking m=n+1 for H and m=n for X, for p>3n,



$$
H\sim\operatorname{diag}(I_{n+1},G_H),\quad
X\sim\operatorname{diag}(I_n,G_X),\quad
v_p(D)=v_p(\eta^{H}),\quad v_p(F)=v_p(\theta).
$$



At n=1, G_H has size 0×2. By convention the order-zero maximal minor is 1, so ηᴴ_1=1. Manuscript 2163 explicitly defines content for n≥2; 2154/55 complete this convention, which is not an error.

For X, a stronger identity holds over the integers. Maximal minors use all 2n columns. Omitting one of the first n rows makes the B-column rank insufficient and the minor zero; the other minors equal det V times an order-n minor of G_X. Thus F=(∏_(j<n)j!)θ holds over Z. This global identity cannot automatically be copied to H, whose maximal minors also select columns.

The entry recurrence in 2163 §5 is valid. The identity Δ^m(kf(k−1))=kΔ^mf(k−1)+mΔ^(m−1)f(k) gives the column shift. Applying Δ^(m+2) to y_(k+2)+k(k+1)y_k=0 and expanding the three-term Leibniz rule gives the original formula (12). The source correctly does not promote this entry recurrence to a cross-n content recurrence.

## R3: complete transfer of high valuation to F without circularity

This corresponds to 2154 §§1–5 and 2155 §§1–5.

First use the actual nonzero endpoint system reviewed by the primary agent. If X had a nonzero right-kernel triple T in characteristic zero, its degree would be≤n−1, and contact conditions including the lowest row k=n would give order 3n+1. Then (z−1)T has degree≤n, the same contact order, and both B/C vanish at 1, contradicting the endpoint system. Hence X has full column rank. This argument does not infer endpoint nonvanishing backwards from a later factorization of F.

By R1, if e=v_p(D)>0, the sole nonunit factor of H is p^e. First remove the unit row factors k!. The last row of the Smith row transformation gives primitive w with wH⁰=0 modp^e. The vectors (0,w) using H's j columns and (w,0) using its j+1 columns annihilate adjacent row ranges of X respectively. If w_r is the earliest nonzero coordinate modp, the two-row minor in columns r,r+1 is −w_r² modp, a unit. The two rows can therefore be completed to a GL_(2n+1)(Z_p) row transformation.

After transformation, both first rows of X are entrywise divisible by p^e. Every choice of 2n rows contains at least one of them, so every maximal minor contains p^e. A unit row transformation preserves the maximal-minor ideal. Consequently,



$$
v_p(D)\le v_p(F),\qquad
v_p(\eta^H)\le v_p(\theta),\quad p>3n.
$$



This uses the complete e; a left null vector modp alone is insufficient. The dependency order is primary-reviewed endpoint nonvanishing → characteristic-zero full rank of X; R1 → number of nonunit H factors; R2 → small-matrix identities; then the shift argument here. Saturation in 2290 is not a premise. Literal mutual citations do not force circularity in this proof.

## R4: saturation of the augmented shared core

This corresponds to the complete manuscript 2290. Support used in §4 is 1995 §§2–4, lines 59–200.

Over any field, the contact module A_M={(A,B,C):A+BE+CF∈z^M K[z]} has basis (z^M,0,0), (−E,1,0), (−F,0,1), with determinant z^M. When leading vectors are dependent, appropriate monomial multiples eliminate the top-degree leading term of a row involved in the relation. Invertible row operations reduce the total degree; repetition yields independent leading vectors. Row degrees d_1≤d_2≤d_3 satisfy



$$
d_1+d_2+d_3=M,\qquad
\dim\{T:\deg T\le D\}=\sum_i\max(0,D-d_i+1).
$$



Take M=3n+1 and p>3n. R1's dimension bound≤1 for the degree-(n−1) slice forces d_1≥n−1, d_2≥n, and d_3≤n+2. The only possibilities are (n,n,n+1), (n−1,n+1,n+1), and (n−1,n,n+2), so the degree-(n+1) slice has dimension exactly 5.

For B/C of degree≤n+1, the low-degree conditions uniquely recover A. The remaining high rows n+2,…,3n form C_n^+, with 2n+4 columns, 2n−1 rows, and kernel dimension 5. Hence it has full row rank and maximal-minor content ζ that is a unit for every p>3n. This excludes every depth of ζ, not just its first layer.

The divisibility in 1995 follows by expansion along the omitted low row or three newly added rows, giving ζ_n|gcd(D_n,D_(n+1)). A Schur form in a unit-pivot chart directly gives v_p(ζ)=min_c v_p(E_(ρ,c)). If the old core loses one rank, at least one new column restores that row, so a complete shared-row unit pivot can be chosen for H_(n+1). This does not make the old H's final-row pivot a unit or make the two-new-column 2×2 pivot a unit. Manuscript 2290 explicitly preserves the distinction and asserts neither reverse divisibility nor unit status for D/eta.

## R5: exact cancellation of F and the physical n! in actual q

This corresponds to 2123 §§1, 3–6 and 1986 §§2–3.

Fix u_k=(−1)^(k−n)det X[delete row k], and w=u/F. Primitive integral w gives W=z^n(z−1)^nV with primitive integral V. The reversed factorial map gives



$$
\widehat Q(z)=\sum_{k=n}^{3n}\frac{k!}{n!}w_kz^{3n-k},
\qquad\mathcal E=\det[H;B(1);C(1)]=(-1)^nFZ.
$$



The sign can be checked independently from B=(z−1)b+β and C=(z−1)c+γ. The total column-transformation sign is (−1)^n, and the remaining high rows are adjacent differences of the nonfactorial X. Cauchy–Binet gives F∑k!w_k/∏k!; restoring the row factorials yields exactly the displayed formula, without omitting n!.

Write the Pe/Pa coefficients with j=3n−t≥n. Their contributions are respectively (j!/n!)binom(k,j)w_k and (j!/n!)binom(k,j)(k−j−1)!(−1)^((k−j−1)/2)w_k, both integers. Cross-product endpoints isolate −A_E(1) and −A_F(1), so the actual chosen endpoint (1,4) gives



$$
N=Pe(1)+4Pa(1),\quad A(1)=-N/Z,\quad
q=|Z|/\gcd(|Z|,|N|).
$$



Under the original augmented convention,



$$
\Delta_B=(-1)^{n+1}FZ,\quad
\Delta_A=(-1)^n n!FN,\quad
\gcd(|\Delta_A|,n!|\Delta_B|)=n!F\gcd(|N|,|Z|).
$$



Thus every depth of F cancels in reduction of actual q, while n! belongs only to the augmented-row normalization. This is not a source error: 1986 already prints it at lines 133–147, and this batch confirms it independently. Bounds for F may still support estimates for E/F=Z, but F cannot bypass the Z/N endpoint relation and be treated directly as q.

For p>3n, the coefficient content c_Q of Qhat is a unit, so



$$
v_p(d^{II})=v_p(Z),\quad
v_p(\mathcal E)=v_p(\theta)+v_p(Z),\quad
v_p(q)=v_p(Z)-\min(v_p(Z),v_p(N)).
$$



In formula (24) of 2123, v_p(Z)=v_p(E) is equivalent to v_p(F)=v_p(θ)=0 and is sufficient for full rank of H. The source does not assert necessity. R3/R4 do not prove this stronger condition.

## R6: scalar and Cauchy congruences and two depth identities

This corresponds to the complete manuscripts 2089 and 2119, with support from 2129 §§1–2.

Set L=lcm(1,…,3n), 𝖹_(rj)=L(G_X)_(rj)/(n+r)!, δ_r=det𝖹[delete row r], Θ=gcd|δ_r|, c_r=(2n)!/(n+r)!, h=gcd|c_rδ_r|, and R=(2n)!/n!. Matrix integrality holds entrywise. Row scaling and all minors in R2 give F=(∏_(j<n)j!)·(∏_(r<n)(n+r)!)/L^n·h. Taking gcd in fractional ideals is legitimate and does not assume that L^n divides the factorial product.

Signed left cofactors transform by U^(−T) under U. Returning to the original rows gives formula (5) of 2089. Define



$$
\mathscr P(z)=\sum_{r,s=0}^n(-1)^{r+s}\binom ns\delta_r
\frac{(n+r+s)!}{(n+r)!}z^{2n-r-s},\qquad K=\mathscr P(1).
$$



Complete transport gives hQhat=R𝒫. The first n+1 high-degree coefficients are related to (δ_0,…,δ_n) by an integral triangular matrix with diagonal ±1, so the content of 𝒫 is exactly Θ. Therefore



$$
c_Q=R\Theta/h,\quad h/\Theta\mid R,\quad hZ=RK,\quad
d^{II}=|K|/\Theta.
$$



These are complete integer identities. The small-prime depths of h and Θ may differ; neither h=Θ nor h=1 may be assumed in advance.

The integral identity binom(n,s)(n+r+s)!/(n+r)!=n(s−1)!binom(n−1,s−1)binom(n+r+s,s) gives 𝖹=(−1)^n𝖢+n𝖠, where 𝖢_(rj)=Lτ_(n+r−j) and 𝖠 is integral. It also gives K≡det[𝖢|1] modn. If p^ν divides n, the ordered Smith exponents of the two matrices satisfy



$$
\min(e_i,\nu)=\min(a_i,\nu).
$$



This is a truncated-depth identity. Put 𝖢 into Smith form and eliminate pivots with a_i<ν; the remaining block is still entrywise divisible by p^ν. Deeper exponents with a_i≥ν are not determined. The high-precision shifted sum in 2119 retains s≤min(n,p(h−ν)); it is valid but supplies no final depth upper bound.

For an odd prime p dividing n, set T=p^floor(log_p(3n)) and b=n mod T. Modp, only Taylor-index terms that are odd multiples of T survive. Grouping by residue mod T gives blocks equal to a common nonzero unit times (τ_(C+u−v)). The parity Cauchy subblocks have maximal possible rank. There are min(b,T−1−b) column-deficient blocks, so rank(𝖹 modp)=n−min(b,T−1−b).

In the saturated family n=mp^ν with ν≥1 and 3m<p, b=0 and the minor from the first n rows is a unit. Thus δ_n, h, and Θ are all p-units. The weight identity then gives the complete depth



$$
v_p(F)=\frac{2n(n-m)}{p-1}-2\nu n.
$$



The general first-layer rank formula gives only a lower bound on the valuation of Θ and must not be confused with this complete saturated identity.

The left kernel of the unique nonsquare remainder block gives the ordinary monic imaginary Legendre polynomial Q_m. Hence K≡(−1)^nδ_nQ_m(1) modp, and



$$
v_p(c_Q)=\frac{n-m}{p-1},\quad
v_p(Z)=\frac{n-m}{p-1}+v_p(K),\quad
v_p(d^{II})=v_p(K).
$$



When Q_m(1) is a unit, K is a unit. Otherwise only a next-layer lower bound is initially available. The conservative conclusion at m=4,p=17 in 2089 is correct; the later 2085 gives the exact depth for ν≥2.

## R7: the modp² identity for every m and a seed-nonvanishing criterion

This corresponds to 2085 §§1–6. The Gauss and K/A bridge is in supporting manuscript 2083 §§1–4, 6. The Mellit–Vlasenko Dwork unit-function branch does not enter the main nonvanishing proof below; a function value is not treated as a coefficient ray.

Let f=2+z+2/z and A_m=CT f^m. Integral Frobenius gives f^p≡f(z^p) modp. Successive pth-power lifting yields A_(mp^r)−A_(mp^(r−1))∈p^rZ for every m≥1 and r≥1. The ray limit therefore exists and agrees with A_mp modp².

The independent specialized finite-log proof in 2085 §2 is valid. Set l(X)=∑_(j=1)^(p−1)X^j/j, α+β=1, and αβ=X. Both sides have degree less than p. Differentiation and zero constant terms give



$$
\sum_{j=1}^{(p-1)/2}\binom{2j}{j}X^j/j
=l(\alpha)+l(\beta)\quad\text{in }\mathbb F_p[X].
$$



In the étale algebra Z_p[i], take α=(1+i)/2 and β=(1−i)/2. Their pth-power sum is the rational number u=(2/p)2^(−(p−1)/2). From u≡1 modp and u²=(1+pq_p(2))^(−1), one obtains u≡1−pq_p(2)/2 modp². The finite sum at X=1/2 is therefore congruent to q_p(2). This independent proof includes p=3 and does not misuse an external record restricted to p≥5.

G=(f^p−f(z^p))/p is an integral Laurent polynomial. Its only exponents divisible by p are p,0,−p, with coefficients 0,g_0,2q_p(2), where g_0≡q_p(2) modp. For every m, expand



$$
f^{mp}\equiv f(z^p)^m+mpGf(z^p)^{m-1}\pmod{p^2}
$$



Take constant terms and use f(2/z)=f(z), which gives A_m=2A_(m−1)+4[z]f^(m−1), to obtain



$$
A_{mp}\equiv A_m\left(1+\frac{mp}{2}q_p(2)\right)\pmod{p^2}.
$$



The bracket is a unit. No division by A_m or assumption m<p is required. If the seed valuation is 0 or 1, the limit is nonzero with unchanged valuation. In the K/A bridge for saturated n, the first-n-row Cauchy determinant and binom(2n,n) are units, so K≡U_nA_n modp^ν. When e<ν, v_p(K)=e follows: a unit seed works for every ν≥1, and a seed with valuation exactly 1 works for ν≥2. The identity A_4=136=8·17 proves the latter without rerunning the old order-68 certificate.

The integral H(t) and Witt expansion in §6 are also valid. In the quadratic equation with constant term 1, the multiplier of each new coefficient is 1, so H has integral coefficients. The unique factorization H=∏(1−b_dt^d) gives A_n=∑_(d|n)d b_d^(n/d). For p not dividing m, write d=ep^j. At fixed j the powers converge to Teichmüller representatives, and the j≥J tail is uniformly divisible by p^J, permitting interchange. Terms in the limit sum may cancel; no nonvanishing theorem for higher-valuation seeds, unasserted in the source, is obtained.

## R8: the actual N is a unit, and two finite interfaces

This corresponds to 1986 §§4–6. In complete cofactor transport, c_r for r<n contains p while c_n=1, giving W(z)≡ηᵘz^(2n)(z−1)^n modp. Since p divides n, surviving k are multiples of p. In Pe, j!/n! contains p for j≥n+p; for n<j<n+p, Lucas gives binom(k,j)=0 modp. Only j=n remains. The identity [y^n]W(1+y)=ηᵘ gives Pe(1)≡ηᵘ.

In Pa, only j=n remains as well. If n>p, k≥2n makes (k−n−1)! contain p, so Pa(1)≡0 and N is a unit. At the boundary n=p, only the Pa term k=2p survives. Wilson gives Pa(1)≡2χ_pηᵘ and N≡(1+8χ_p)ηᵘ. The unique exception for p>3 is 7, as stated in the source.

The integer program written for this review reconstructs only all cofactors, Pe/Pa, complete endpoints, and final gcd at n=7,25, and checks K/h/Θ by the second difference-based path. It does not rerun primary reviews 1..6, the 718 residue classes, or saddle arguments.
