## A1 continuation: a fixed-$3$ divided-basis calculation for the actual signed even compression

I do **not** obtain the requested valuation theorem for the actual odd part of the center denominator. I do obtain a fixed-prime, growing-index result that identifies an exact obstruction to a straightforward factorial-survival argument:

> On every regular index $n=4^j+1$, the signed derangement Gram matrix in the factorial-divided basis constructed below is integral at $3$, but its reduction has **exactly one radical direction**.

The calculation uses the actual even compression and the negative mass at $-1$. It is not a transfer of the ordinary consecutive-index derangement determinant. Moreover, the radical has zero constant coordinate in an endpoint-vanishing basis. Thus a unit-Gram argument cannot determine the orthogonal endpoint or the final center denominator. The next required information is a specific lift of this radical, not the rank of the undivided moment matrix.

I give the derivation and an exact two-by-two lifting reduction. The rational arctangent terms are retained separately in the final center pair; no integrality claim for those terms is inferred from the derangement calculation.

---

## 1. The actual even compression admits a different factorial-divided lattice

Write


$$
\mu(F)=\int_0^\infty e^{-t}F((1-t)^2)\,dt,
\qquad
\rho(F)=\mu(F)-F(-1).
$$


Thus $\mu(y^s)=D_{2s}$, exactly as in the supplied construction.

Use the monic integral basis


$$
h_0(y)=1,\qquad
h_{d+1}(y)=(y+1)(y-1)^d\quad(d\ge0),
$$


and put


$$
\psi_0=1,\qquad
\psi_{d+1}=\frac{h_{d+1}}{d!}.
\tag{1}
$$


Every nonconstant basis member vanishes at the negative mass.

The relevant compression identity is


$$
y-1=t(t-2),\qquad y+1=t^2-2t+2
\quad\text{when }y=(1-t)^2.
\tag{2}
$$


Consequently, if $F\in\mathbb Z[y]$, then


$$
\mu\bigl((y-1)^sF(y)\bigr)
$$


is an integer linear combination of factorials $(s+a)!$, $a\ge0$. In particular,


$$
s!\mid \mu\bigl((y-1)^sF(y)\bigr).
\tag{3}
$$



For


$$
G_{ij}=\rho(\psi_i\psi_j),
$$


equation (3) gives


$$
G_{d+1,e+1}\in\mathbb Z,
$$


because $(d+e)!/(d!e!)$ is integral. It also gives $G_{0,d+1}\in\mathbb Z$, and $G_{00}=0$.

This proves integrality over $\mathbb Z$, not merely over $\mathbb Z_3$.

### Exact scale, without a saturation assumption

The change from $1,y,\ldots,y^{n-1}$ to $h_0,\ldots,h_{n-1}$ is integral unit triangular. Thus


$$
\det\bigl(\rho(y^{i+j})\bigr)_{i,j<n}
=
\left(\prod_{d=0}^{n-2}d!\right)^2\det G_n.
\tag{4}
$$



The lattice generated by the $\psi_i$ contains the ordinary integral polynomial lattice, with exact index


$$
\prod_{d=0}^{n-2}d!.
$$


This accounts for the entire scale in (4). It does **not** assert that this is a maximal integral lattice for the moment pairing; further local enlargement or determinant content remains possible.

## 2. Exact normalized moment residues at $3$

Define the integers


$$
b_s=\frac{\mu((y-1)^s)}{s!}.
$$


Expanding (2) gives the finite identity


$$
b_s
=
\sum_{\ell=0}^{s}
\binom{s}{\ell}(-2)^{s-\ell}\frac{(s+\ell)!}{s!}.
\tag{5}
$$



Modulo $3$, every term for which the interval
$\{s+1,\ldots,s+\ell\}$ contains a multiple of $3$ vanishes. Since $-2\equiv1\pmod3$, this leaves only the following possibilities:

* If $s\equiv0\pmod3$, terms with $\ell\ge3$ vanish, and the terms $\ell=1,2$ have a factor $3$ in $\binom{s}{\ell}$. Hence $b_s\equiv1$.
* If $s\equiv1\pmod3$, terms with $\ell\ge2$ vanish, and the $\ell=1$ term is $s(s+1)\equiv2$. Hence $b_s\equiv0$.
* If $s\equiv2\pmod3$, all terms with $\ell\ge1$ vanish. Hence $b_s\equiv1$.

The small cases $s=0,1$ are included directly in (5). Therefore, at **every** $s\ge0$,


$$
\boxed{b_s\equiv(1,0,1)_{s\bmod3}\pmod3.}
\tag{6}
$$



Now let


$$
c_s=\frac{\mu((y+1)(y-1)^s)}{s!},\qquad
e_s=\frac{\mu((y+1)^2(y-1)^s)}{s!}.
$$


Using $y+1=2+(y-1)$,


$$
c_s=2b_s+(s+1)b_{s+1},
$$




$$
e_s=4b_s+4(s+1)b_{s+1}+(s+1)(s+2)b_{s+2}.
$$


Substitution of (6) gives


$$
\boxed{c_s\equiv2,\qquad e_s\equiv-s\pmod3.}
\tag{7}
$$



Because the negative mass vanishes on every $h_{d+1}$, the complete signed Gram residues are therefore


$$
\boxed{
G_{00}=0,\quad
G_{0,d+1}\equiv2,\quad
G_{d+1,e+1}\equiv
-(d+e)\binom{d+e}{d}\pmod3.
}
\tag{8}
$$


The entry $G_{00}=0$, rather than $\mu(1)=1$, is essential below.

## 3. Growing-index theorem: precisely one radical on the regular family

Let $N=n-1$ be the number of nonconstant coordinates. First take $N=3M$, and write


$$
d=3D+a,\qquad e=3E+b,\qquad 0\le a,b<3.
$$


Lucas’s theorem applied to (8) gives the tensor factorization


$$
E_{3M}=B_M\otimes A,
\tag{9}
$$


after grouping coordinates by $D,a$, where


$$
B_M(D,E)=\binom{D+E}{D}\pmod3
$$


and


$$
A=
\begin{pmatrix}
0&2&1\\
2&2&0\\
1&0&0
\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{10}
$$


Indeed, a carry in the lowest ternary digit makes the binomial coefficient zero. Without a carry, the factor $-(d+e)$ is $-(a+b)$.

The matrix $A$ has determinant $1$ in $\mathbb F_3$. Also,


$$
B_M=P_MP_M^T,\qquad
P_M(D,a)=\binom Da,
\tag{11}
$$


by Vandermonde’s identity. Since $P_M$ is unit triangular,


$$
\det B_M=1.
$$


Thus $E_{3M}$ is invertible for every $M\ge1$.

Let $\mathbf1$ denote an all-ones vector of the appropriate size. Directly solving $Ax=\mathbf1$ gives


$$
x=(1,1,2)^T,\qquad
\mathbf1^TA^{-1}\mathbf1=1.
$$


Since $\mathbf1=P_Me_0$, equation (11) likewise gives


$$
\mathbf1^TB_M^{-1}\mathbf1=1.
$$


Therefore


$$
\mathbf1^TE_{3M}^{-1}\mathbf1=1.
\tag{12}
$$



### Add the coordinate $d=3M$

Now take


$$
N=3M+1.
$$


Let


$$
b_D=\binom{M+D}{D},\qquad 0\le D<M,
$$


and let $a_0=Ae_0=(0,2,1)^T$. The cross-vector between the new coordinate and the first $3M$ nonconstant coordinates is


$$
x=b\otimes a_0.
$$


Its own norm modulo $3$ is zero. Moreover,


$$
x^TE_{3M}^{-1}x
=(b^TB_M^{-1}b)(a_0^TA^{-1}a_0)=0,
\tag{13}
$$


because $a_0^TA^{-1}a_0=A_{00}=0$.

For the constant coupling, Vandermonde gives


$$
b=P_Mv,\qquad v_a=\binom Ma.
$$


Hence


$$
\mathbf1^TB_M^{-1}b=v_0=1,
$$


and


$$
\mathbf1^TA^{-1}a_0=1.
$$


It follows that


$$
\mathbf1^TE_{3M}^{-1}x=1.
\tag{14}
$$



Eliminate the invertible block $E_{3M}$ from the **signed** full Gram matrix. The two remaining coordinates are the constant and $d=3M$. Equations (12)–(14) give their Schur complement:


$$
\begin{pmatrix}
0&2\\
2&0
\end{pmatrix}
-
\begin{pmatrix}
2\mathbf1^T\\ x^T
\end{pmatrix}
E_{3M}^{-1}
\begin{pmatrix}
2\mathbf1&x
\end{pmatrix}
=
\begin{pmatrix}
2&0\\
0&0
\end{pmatrix}
\pmod3.
\tag{15}
$$



Thus:

### Fixed-$3$ theorem
For every $M\ge1$, the normalized signed Gram matrix of size


$$
n=3M+2
$$


has corank exactly one modulo $3$.

In particular, since $4^j\equiv1\pmod3$,


$$
\boxed{
\operatorname{corank}_{\mathbb F_3}\overline{G_n}=1
\quad\text{for every }n=4^j+1,\ j\ge1.
}
\tag{16}
$$



This is an infinite-index theorem established by the explicit compression and Lucas factorization, not finite extrapolation.

## 4. What the radical says—and what it does not say

The radical in (15) is represented by the last coordinate after eliminating $E_{3M}$. In the original divided coordinates it has:

* constant coordinate zero;
* final coordinate one;
* preceding nonconstant coordinates $-E_{3M}^{-1}x$.

Thus its representing polynomial vanishes at $y=-1$.

This has a concrete consequence. A factorial-divided unit-Gram proof of integrality of the monic orthogonal polynomial cannot work on this regular family: the divided Gram is not a unit. Its one singular direction is also endpoint-vanishing, so merely locating that direction does not determine the endpoint value $Q_n(-1)$.

Neither of the following follows from (16):

* bounded $3$-depth of the actual center denominator;
* survival of factorial $3$-depth after the final endpoint gcd.

The theorem isolates the exact missing lift.

### An exact two-by-two lifting reduction

Retain the actual rational divided Gram, not only its residue. Partition it as


$$
G_n=
\begin{pmatrix}
E&U\\
U^T&W
\end{pmatrix},
$$


where $E$ comprises the first $3M$ nonconstant coordinates and $W$ comprises the constant and final coordinates. We proved that $E$ is a $3$-adic unit.

Set


$$
S=W-U^TE^{-1}U=
\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\b&c\end{pmatrix}.
$$


Then


$$
a\equiv2,\qquad b\equiv c\equiv0\pmod3.
$$


The exact scalar


$$
\delta_n=c-\frac{b^2}{a}
\tag{17}
$$


satisfies


$$
\det G_n=\det(E)\,a\,\delta_n,\qquad
v_3(\det G_n)=v_3(\delta_n)\ge1.
\tag{18}
$$


The real nonsingularity of the signed moment matrix gives $\delta_n\ne0$.

For the orthogonal degree-$n$ polynomial, let


$$
\omega_i=\rho(\psi_i\psi_n).
$$


Eliminate the same $E$-block from this mixed vector:


$$
\xi=\omega_W-U^TE^{-1}\omega_E.
$$


Then the last coefficient of $\eta=G_n^{-1}\omega$ is exactly


$$
\eta_{\rm last}
=
\frac{\xi_{\rm last}-(b/a)\xi_{\rm const}}{\delta_n}.
\tag{19}
$$


The constant coefficient is


$$
\eta_{\rm const}
=\frac{\xi_{\rm const}-b\eta_{\rm last}}a.
\tag{20}
$$


Since $h_n$ is monic and its divisor in (1) is $(n-1)!$, the monic orthogonal polynomial has endpoint


$$
P_n(-1)=-(n-1)!\,\eta_{\rm const}.
\tag{21}
$$



Equations (17)–(21) locate both possible lift cancellations. Controlling only $v_3(\delta_n)$ would still not control (21); the mixed numerator and the factor $b$ must also be retained.

## 5. The complete endpoint pair, including arctangent poles

The preceding lattice is a lattice for $\rho$, not a proof of integrality for the rational arctangent functional. At $3$, denominators $3,9,27,\ldots$ occur in that functional and cannot be discarded.

For the actual primitive $Q_n$, retain


$$
L_s=4\sum_{a=1}^{s}\frac{(-1)^{s-a}}{2a-1},
$$




$$
R_{ij}=\sum_{t=0}^n Q_{n,t}
\left(-(2(i+j+t))!+L_{i+j+t}\right).
$$


In the endpoint basis, write


$$
R_{\rm end}=\begin{pmatrix}a_R&b_R^T\\b_R&H\end{pmatrix}.
$$


With


$$
\ell=\operatorname{lcm}(1,3,\ldots,4n-3),\quad
t=\ell a_R,\quad z=\ell b_R,\quad K=\ell H,
$$


put


$$
A=t\det K-z^T\operatorname{adj}(K)z,\qquad
B=\ell Q_n(-1)\det K.
$$


The **actual final gcd and denominator** remain


$$
g=\gcd(|A|,|B|),\qquad
q=\frac{|B|}{g},\qquad
p=-\frac{\operatorname{sgn}(B)A}{g}.
\tag{22}
$$


In particular,


$$
v_3(q)=\max\{0,v_3(B)-v_3(A)\}.
\tag{23}
$$


No quantity in (18) or (21) can replace (23).

The whole evaluated error is


$$
q(e+\pi)-p
=
\frac{\operatorname{sgn}(B)}g\bigl(A+B(e+\pi)\bigr)
=
\frac{q}{Q_n(-1)}
\int_0^1
Q_n(x^2)F_n(x^2)^2
\left(e^x+\frac4{1+x^2}\right)\,dx.
\tag{24}
$$


On $n=4^j+1$, the supplied exact dyadic theorem may be reused as author mathematics: it gives $B\ne0$, $v_2(q)=n+2$, and therefore distinct centers. Consequently at most one error in (24) vanishes. This does not establish a shrinking-error estimate.

Likewise, the direct determinant budget remains


$$
q|e+\pi-p/q|
\le
\frac{\ell^k(7M_n)^kD_k}{g},
\qquad
M_n=\max_{[0,1]}|Q_n|,
$$


with the definitions in A4. The new fixed-$3$ Gram theorem supplies no presently proved gain for the $g$ in this bound.

## Handoff

### (1) New result and proof status

**Proved here:** an integral factorial-divided lattice for the actual signed even compression; its exact determinant scale; the all-index normalized moment residues (6)–(8); and the theorem


$$
\operatorname{corank}_{\mathbb F_3}\overline{G_{4^j+1}}=1
\quad(j\ge1).
$$


The radical is explicitly endpoint-vanishing. Equations (17)–(21) give an exact lift reduction.

**Not proved:** any quantitative valuation of the actual $q$ at $3$, any bounded-depth theorem for that denominator, or any decision on the irrationality of $e+\pi$.

### (2) Exact remaining bottleneck

First, one must control the coupled quantities


$$
\delta_n,\qquad
\xi_{\rm last}-(b/a)\xi_{\rm const},\qquad
\xi_{\rm const}-b\eta_{\rm last}
$$


on an infinite regular index class. Second, their consequence for the primitive $Q_n$ must be passed through the **complete** rational matrix, including all arctangent $3$-power poles, and through (22). The present calculation proves why the simplest unit-Gram shortcut fails; it does not prove factorial-depth survival false.

### (3) One bounded computation request

**Input:** $n=17$, $p=3$, the defining derangement moments, and the actual primitive $Q_{17}$.

**Requested exact output:**

1. Construct the divided Gram (1), verify its integral entries, and verify the factorization and corank in (9)–(16).
2. Compute the exact scalars in (17)–(20), reporting their $3$-valuations and first nonzero $3$-adic units.
3. Verify (21) against the actual monic orthogonal polynomial.
4. Construct the complete $9\times9$ rational endpoint matrix, retaining every $L_s$, and report
   

$$
v_3(A),\quad v_3(B),\quad v_3(g),\quad v_3(q).
$$



This is one fixed normalization-and-lift check. It would verify whether the first radical lift already explains the native $n=17$ endpoint depth and whether that depth survives the complete endpoint gcd. It would not establish an infinite valuation theorem.
