# A3, Turn 5 — Actual resonant residues at $d=2$: a complete force recurrence, sharper valuations, and a genuine height obstruction

## 1. Executive summary

Let


$$
S=e+\pi,\qquad M=1+\sqrt2,\qquad d=2,\qquad b=3.
$$


All indices below are original indices. The contact matrix remains $3\times3$, with rows and columns $0,1,2$, and reconstruction retains coordinates $0,1,2,3$.

I reuse the accepted complete producer, the exact endpoint gcd decomposition, and A4 Turn 9’s acceptance of the fixed-$d$ whole-error contrast. I do not revisit the already excluded fixed-weight families.

The new results are as follows.

### 1.1 A complete scalar recurrence for the actual companion force

The actual row-scaled companion force can be generated by a third-order recurrence with an explicit polynomial forcing term. It includes both the exponential and logarithmic/arctangent contributions. It does not replace the companion by its exponential part.

For fixed $n$, define


$$
\mathscr H(z)=\frac{e^z+f(z)}{1-z},
\qquad
f(0)=0,\qquad f'(z)=\frac2{Q(z)},
\qquad Q(z)=1-z+\frac{z^2}{2}.
$$


Put


$$
A_n(z)=Q(z)^n\mathscr H^{(n)}(z),
\qquad
a_N^{(n)}=N![z^N]A_n(z).
$$


Then the actual contact force is


$$
w_i=a_{n+i}^{(n)},\qquad i=0,1,2.
$$



An exact recurrence for $a_N^{(n)}$ is derived in Section 3. It supplies the actual residue at arbitrary selected factorial depths without discarding the complete force.

### 1.2 The first force needs only three consecutive $\tau$-values

For the actual first force,


$$
\frac{z}{(n!)^2}
=
\begin{pmatrix}
\tau_n\\[1mm]
\dfrac{n+1}{2}(\tau_n+\tau_{n+1})\\[2mm]
\dfrac{(n+1)(n+2)}2\tau_{n+2}
\end{pmatrix}.
$$


This gives a particularly small factorial-stripped formula for the actual moving rational residue $\Theta$.

### 1.3 Exact companion valuations at $3$ and $7$

Let


$$
s=v_p(n),\qquad m=2v_p(n!),\qquad
K_p(n)=m-\left\lfloor\log_p(2n+2)\right\rfloor.
$$


Define the convergent $p$-adic factorial sum


$$
\mathfrak D_p=\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}(-1)^r r!.
$$


A new congruence for the **actual** companion endpoint is


$$
\boxed{
v_0\equiv-2n\mathfrak D_p
\pmod{p^{\min(2s,K_p(n))}}.
}
\tag{1.1}
$$



Consequently, on the requested progressions:


$$
\boxed{
v_3(v_0)=v_3(n)\qquad(15\mid n),
}
\tag{1.2}
$$


and


$$
\boxed{
v_7(v_0)=v_7(n)\qquad(105\mid n).
}
\tag{1.3}
$$


At $5$,


$$
\boxed{
v_5(v_0)\ge v_5(n)+1\qquad(15\mid n).
}
\tag{1.4}
$$


Moreover,


$$
\boxed{
v_5(v_0)=v_5(n)+1
\qquad(15\mid n,\ 25\mid n).
}
\tag{1.5}
$$



Thus the original lower bound $v_p(v_0)\ge v_p(n)$ is now an exact valuation at $3$ and $7$, and is sharpened at $5$. The branch $v_5(n)=1$—which includes $n=15,30,105,210$—still requires further actual digits.

### 1.4 A genuine actual height obstruction

Write


$$
t_n=\frac{n^2}{2}+\frac{1-3\sqrt2}{2}n.
$$


Suppose a reduced weight $a/k$ lies in a fixed-width window


$$
\left|\frac ak-t_n\right|\le C
\tag{1.6}
$$


and simultaneously cancels enough at every selected prime to force


$$
a\equiv k\pmod{n_{\mathcal P}},
\qquad
n_{\mathcal P}:=\prod_{p\in\mathcal P}p^{v_p(n)}.
$$


Put $r=n/n_{\mathcal P}$. Then


$$
\boxed{
k\ge c_C\frac{\sqrt n}{r},
\qquad
\max(|a|,k)\ge c'_C\frac{n^{5/2}}r
}
\tag{1.7}
$$


for sufficiently large $n$.

This applies, in particular, to simultaneous resonant cancellation at $3,5$, or at $3,5,7$, on subfamilies whose complementary cofactor $r$ is bounded. It rules out integer and bounded-denominator threshold weights there. It is an obstruction for the actual residue, not an abstract rational-array example and not another uniqueness theorem.

It does **not** rule out all polynomial-height or subexponential-height resonant weights.

### 1.5 What remains unresolved

The report does not determine whether weights of height $n^A$, for some fixed $A\ge 5/2$, can achieve exponentially large $H_{\rm gcd}$ in the actual threshold window along infinitely many original indices.

It does prove that simultaneous deep local cancellation and a nonzero improved whole error are compatible at an exponential presentation-height scale. That compatibility alone gives no favorable primitive-denominator estimate.

The unresolved arithmetic question is now more concrete: obtain a short reconstruction—or an actual dual-lattice height certificate—for the residue produced by the scalar recurrence and the factorial-stripped $3\times3$ formulas below.

No tools were executed.

where

$$
s_n=

x=C^{-1}z,\qquad y=C^{-1}w.

The actual endpoints remain


$$
u_0=-s_n^Tx,\qquad u_3=x_2,

\boxed{

## 3. A new exact recurrence for the complete companion force

The producer coefficients satisfy


$$
\mathscr H(z)=\sum_{\ell\ge0}\eta_\ell z^\ell
=\frac{e^z+f(z)}{1-z},
\qquad f'(z)=\frac2{Q(z)}.
\tag{3.1}
$$


Consequently,


$$
(1-z)\mathscr H'(z)-\mathscr H(z)
=e^z+\frac2{Q(z)}.
$$


Differentiating $n$ times gives


$$
(1-z)\mathscr H^{(n+1)}(z)
-(n+1)\mathscr H^{(n)}(z)
=e^z+2\left(\frac1Q\right)^{(n)}(z).
\tag{3.2}
$$



Define


$$
R_n(z)=Q(z)^{n+1}\left(\frac1Q\right)^{(n)}(z).
$$


These are polynomials satisfying


$$
\boxed{
R_0=1,\qquad
R_{n+1}=Q R_n'-(n+1)Q'R_n.
}
\tag{3.3}
$$


Their degree is $n$.

With


$$
A_n=Q^n\mathscr H^{(n)},
$$


equation (3.2) becomes


$$
(1-z)Q A_n'
-\bigl[n(1-z)Q'+(n+1)Q\bigr]A_n
=e^zQ^{n+1}+2R_n.
\tag{3.4}
$$


The two polynomial coefficients simplify to


$$
(1-z)Q=1-2z+\frac32z^2-\frac12z^3
$$


and


$$
n(1-z)Q'+(n+1)Q
=1+(n-1)z-\frac{n-1}{2}z^2.
$$



Let


$$
a_N^{(n)}=N![z^N]A_n(z),
$$


and let


$$
\mathcal B_N^{[n+1]}
=N![z^N]\bigl(e^zQ(z)^{n+1}\bigr).
$$


Coefficient extraction in (3.4) proves:

> **Complete-force recurrence.**
> 

$$
> \boxed{
> \begin{aligned}
> a_{N+1}^{(n)}
> ={}&(2N+1)a_N^{(n)}
> +\frac{N(2n+1-3N)}2a_{N-1}^{(n)}\\
> &+\frac{N(N-1)(N-n-1)}2a_{N-2}^{(n)}
> +\mathcal B_N^{[n+1]}
> +2N![z^N]R_n.
> \end{aligned}
> }
> \tag{3.5}
>
$$


> Here
> 

$$
> a_0^{(n)}=\mathcal W_n,
>
$$


> and negative-index terms are zero. The actual contact force is
> 

$$
> \boxed{w_i=a_{n+i}^{(n)},\qquad i=0,1,2.}
> \tag{3.6}
>
$$



This recurrence is an exact reorganization of the complete producer. The logarithmic/arctangent contribution occurs both in the initial value $\mathcal W_n$ and in $R_n$. Neither may be omitted.

### 3.1 Exact terminal forcing

The leading coefficient of $R_n$ is


$$
[z^n]R_n=(-1)^n\frac{(n+1)!}{2^n}.
\tag{3.7}
$$


Indeed, this follows inductively from (3.3). Hence


$$
2n![z^n]R_n
=(-1)^n2^{1-n}n!(n+1)!.
\tag{3.8}
$$


At an eligible odd prime dividing $n$, this terminal forcing has valuation exactly


$$
2v_p(n!).
$$


For $N>n$, the polynomial forcing is zero.

This is useful at the requested factorial depths, but it does not authorize deleting earlier polynomial forcing.

## 4. Factorial stripping and a small exact formula for $\Theta$

### 4.1 Three consecutive $\tau$-values produce the first force

Recall


$$
t_i=[z^{n+i}]\frac{Q(z)^n}{(1-z)^{n+1}}.
$$


Use the change of variable


$$
z=\frac{x}{1+x}.
$$


Then


$$
\frac{Q(z)}{z(1-z)}
=x^{-1}+1+\frac x2=:\mathcal L(x),
$$


and the coefficient integral gives


$$
t_i=\operatorname{CT}_x\,
\mathcal L(x)^n x^{-i}(1+x)^i.
\tag{4.1}
$$



Write $a_j=[x^j]\mathcal L(x)^n$. The symmetry of $\mathcal L$ gives


$$
a_{-j}=2^j a_j.
$$


Therefore


$$
\tau_{n+1}=a_0+2a_1,
$$


and


$$
\tau_{n+2}=2a_0+4a_1+2a_2.
$$


Substituting in (4.1),


$$
\boxed{
t_0=\tau_n,\qquad
t_1=\frac{\tau_n+\tau_{n+1}}2,\qquad
t_2=\frac{\tau_{n+2}}2.
}
\tag{4.2}
$$



Thus, with $f_n=(n!)^2$,


$$
z=f_n\bar z,
\qquad
\boxed{
\bar z=
\begin{pmatrix}
\tau_n\\[1mm]
\dfrac{n+1}{2}(\tau_n+\tau_{n+1})\\[2mm]
\dfrac{(n+1)(n+2)}2\tau_{n+2}
\end{pmatrix}.
}
\tag{4.3}
$$


The accepted recurrence


$$
(r+1)\tau_{r+1}=(2r+1)\tau_r+r\tau_{r-1}
$$


can be used in exact rational arithmetic. Modular implementations must not invert $r+1$ when it is a nonunit.

### 4.2 Factorial-stripped endpoint determinants

Put


$$
\Delta=\det C,
$$




$$
X=-s_n^T\operatorname{adj}(C)\bar z,
\qquad
Z=e_2^T\operatorname{adj}(C)\bar z,
$$




$$
Y=\Delta-s_n^T\operatorname{adj}(C)w,
\qquad
R=e_2^T\operatorname{adj}(C)w.
\tag{4.4}
$$


Then


$$
u_0=f_nX/\Delta,\qquad u_3=f_nZ/\Delta,
$$




$$
v_0=Y/\Delta,\qquad v_3=R/\Delta.
\tag{4.5}
$$



At every eligible prime,


$$
\Delta,\ X,\ Z,\ R
$$


are units, while $Y$ is divisible by $p$. Consequently


$$
XR-ZY
$$


is a unit.

The actual moving residue has the exact formula


$$
\boxed{
\Theta_{n,2}
=-\frac{\mathcal V}{J}
=\frac{XR}{XR-ZY}.
}
\tag{4.6}
$$


In particular,


$$
\boxed{
\Theta_{n,2}-1=\frac{ZY}{XR-ZY},
\qquad
v_p(\Theta_{n,2}-1)=v_p(v_0).
}
\tag{4.7}
$$



The squared factorial has been removed **before** modular division. Thus computing $\Theta\bmod p^\ell$ from (4.6) does not require carrying an artificial extra $m_p$ digits.

These determinant expressions determine endpoint ratios and residues. They are not substitutes for the least actual clearer $d_B$ in a global denominator estimate.

## 5. A rigorous near-factorial precision strip

Split the complete companion force into its exponential and logarithmic/arctangent parts:


$$
w=w^{\exp}+w^{\log}.
$$


For $N=n+i$, a logarithmic summand can be written as


$$
2q_j\,N!\,n!\binom{2n+i-j}{n}
\frac{\alpha_{r-1}}r.
$$


For $p>2$, $p\mid n$, and $i=0,1,2<p$,


$$
v_p(N!)=v_p(n!).
$$


Also $r\le2n+2$. Hence


$$
\boxed{
w_i^{\log}\in
p^{K_p(n)}\mathbb Z_{(p)},
\qquad
K_p(n)=2v_p(n!)
-\left\lfloor\log_p(2n+2)\right\rfloor.
}
\tag{5.1}
$$



Since $C^{-1}$ is $p$-integral,


$$
v_0-v_0^{\exp}\in p^{K_p(n)}\mathbb Z_{(p)},
\tag{5.2}
$$


and the analogous assertion holds for $v_3$.

Therefore:

* through depth $K_p(n)$, the complete companion residues can be computed from the exponential companion;
* between $K_p(n)$ and $m_p$, the complete logarithmic contribution must be restored;
* the missing strip has width only $O(\log n)$, but its digits can decide an exceptional reconstruction.

This is a precision theorem about the complete force. It is not a claim that the logarithmic force is globally irrelevant.

## 6. Sharper actual valuations of $v_0$

### 6.1 The exponential factorial polynomial

For a nonnegative integer $L$, define


$$
F(L)=\sum_{t=0}^{L}L^{\underline t}.
$$


This is the exponential part of $\mathcal W_L$.

Let


$$
s=v_p(n).
$$


The accepted contact congruence, with the exponential force alone, gives


$$
C\equiv(i^{\underline j})_{0\le i,j\le2}\pmod{p^s},
\qquad
y^{\exp}\equiv
\begin{pmatrix}1\\1\\1\end{pmatrix}
\pmod{p^s}.
\tag{6.1}
$$


Write


$$
e=y^{\exp}-\mathbf1,\qquad f=w^{\exp}-C\mathbf1.
$$


Thus


$$
e\in p^s\mathbb Z_{(p)}^3,\qquad Ce=f.
$$



Because the first row of $C$ is congruent to $(1,0,0)$ modulo $p^s$,


$$
f_0\equiv e_0\pmod{p^{2s}}.
$$


Also


$$
1-s_n^T\mathbf1=-n^2
$$


and


$$
s_n^Te\equiv e_0\pmod{p^{2s}}.
$$


It follows that


$$
\boxed{
v_0^{\exp}\equiv-f_0\pmod{p^{2s}}.
}
\tag{6.2}
$$



### 6.2 The nonconstant $Q^n$-terms disappear one level further

Expanding the first row exactly,


$$
f_0=
\sum_{j=0}^{n}
q_j n^{\underline j}
\left[
F(2n-j)-1-(n-j)^2
\right].
\tag{6.3}
$$


Here the polynomial $1+(n-j)^2$ is exactly


$$
\sum_{r=0}^{2}(n-j)^{\underline r}.
$$


No endpoint row has been replaced.

For $j\ge1$, differentiating $Q^n$ gives


$$
jq_j=n[z^{j-1}]Q'Q^{n-1},
$$


so


$$
v_p(q_j)\ge s-v_p(j).
$$


Moreover,


$$
n^{\underline j}
=n(-1)^{j-1}(j-1)!\binom{n-1}{j-1},
$$


and hence


$$
v_p(q_jn^{\underline j})
\ge
2s+v_p((j-1)!)-v_p(j).
\tag{6.4}
$$



For an odd prime $p$,


$$
v_p((j-1)!)-v_p(j)\ge0
$$


except possibly at $j=p$, where it equals $-1$.

At $j=p$, the bracket in (6.3) is divisible by $p$:


$$
F(2n-p)\equiv1\pmod p,
\qquad
1+(n-p)^2\equiv1\pmod p.
$$


Thus every $j\ge1$ term in (6.3) is divisible by $p^{2s}$. Consequently


$$
f_0\equiv F(2n)-1-n^2\pmod{p^{2s}}.
\tag{6.5}
$$



### 6.3 The remaining linear coefficient

For each $t\ge1$,


$$
x^{\underline t}
=(-1)^{t-1}(t-1)!\,x+x^2P_t(x),
\qquad P_t\in\mathbb Z[x].
$$


Applying this at $x=2n$,


$$
F(2n)-1
\equiv
2n\sum_{r=0}^{2n-1}(-1)^rr!
\pmod{p^{2s}}.
$$


The omitted tail of $\mathfrak D_p$ has valuation at least $s$, since every factorial $r!$, $r\ge2n$, contains the full $p$-part of $n$. Therefore


$$
F(2n)-1-n^2
\equiv2n\mathfrak D_p\pmod{p^{2s}}.
$$


Combining this with (6.2), (6.5), and the complete-force strip proves


$$
\boxed{
v_0\equiv-2n\mathfrak D_p
\pmod{p^{\min(2s,K_p(n))}}.
}
\tag{6.6}
$$



This proof explicitly uses the exterior $+1$. Removing it would change the starting identity $1-s_n^T\mathbf1=-n^2$.

### 6.4 Consequences at $3,5,7$

The required factorial sums are small exact calculations:


$$
\mathfrak D_3\equiv1-1+2=2\pmod3,
$$




$$
\mathfrak D_7
\equiv1-1+2-6+24-120+720
=620\equiv4\pmod7.
$$


Hence


$$
v_0/n\equiv2\pmod3,
\qquad
v_0/n\equiv6\pmod7,
\tag{6.7}
$$


whenever the precision in (6.6) exceeds $s$.

On $15\mid n$, and on $105\mid n$, respectively, this gives the exact valuations


$$
v_3(v_0)=v_3(n),\qquad
v_7(v_0)=v_7(n).
$$



At $5$,


$$
\mathfrak D_5\equiv20\pmod{25}.
\tag{6.8}
$$


Indeed, terms $r\ge10$ vanish modulo $25$, and


$$
\sum_{r=0}^{9}(-1)^rr!=-326980\equiv20\pmod{25}.
$$


Thus $v_5(\mathfrak D_5)=1$.

For $s=1$, (6.6) proves only


$$
v_5(v_0)\ge2.
$$


For $s\ge2$, the available precision exceeds $s+1$, so


$$
\boxed{
v_5(v_0)=s+1,\qquad
\frac{v_0}{5n}\equiv2\pmod5.
}
\tag{6.9}
$$



The exact $5$-valuation for $s=1$ is not determined by this argument. In particular, it must not be replaced by the guess $w_5=2$ in the proposed four probes.

Every quantity in this lift is an actual producer quantity. Its use is not a Diophantine theorem about a fixed number.

## 8. An actual height obstruction inside the threshold window

The accepted whole-error contrast implies


$$
\Lambda_{n,2}
=t_n+O(1),
\qquad
t_n=\frac{n^2}{2}+\frac{C_2n}{2},
\qquad C_2=1-3\sqrt2.
\tag{8.1}
$$



Here is a new obstruction that uses the actual local congruences.

> **Threshold-height theorem.**  
> Fix $C\ge0$, and let
> 

$$
> \left|\frac ak-t_n\right|\le C,\qquad
> \gcd(a,k)=1,\qquad k>0.
>
$$


> Let $\mathcal P$ be a selected eligible prime set, and suppose cancellation is deep enough that
> 

$$
> a\equiv k\Theta\pmod{p^{\ell_p}},
> \qquad
> \ell_p\ge v_p(n),\qquad p\in\mathcal P.
>
$$


> Set
> 

$$
> n_{\mathcal P}=\prod_{p\in\mathcal P}p^{v_p(n)},
> \qquad r=n/n_{\mathcal P}.
>
$$


> Then, for sufficiently large $n$,
> 

$$
> \boxed{
> k\ge
> \sqrt{\frac{n}{2(6\sqrt2+1)(C+1)}}\,\frac1r.
> }
> \tag{8.2}
>
$$


> In particular,
> 

$$
> \boxed{
> \max(|a|,k)\gg_C\frac{n^{5/2}}r.
> }
> \tag{8.3}
>
$$



### Proof

Since


$$
\Theta\equiv1\pmod{p^{v_p(n)}},
$$


the local conditions imply


$$
a-k=n_{\mathcal P}b=\frac nr\,b
$$


for an integer $b$. Therefore the rational number


$$
x=\frac{2b-rnk}{rk}
$$


satisfies


$$
x=\frac{2(a/k)-2}{n}-n.
$$


Writing $a/k=t_n+\delta$, $|\delta|\le C$, gives


$$
x=C_2+\frac{2(\delta-1)}n.
$$


Hence


$$
|x-C_2|\le\frac{2(C+1)}n.
\tag{8.4}
$$



Let $P/Q$ be the reduced form of $x$. Its denominator satisfies


$$
Q\le rk.
$$


The conjugate of $C_2$ is


$$

The integer

For sufficiently large $n$, (8.4) gives

|x-C_2'|\le6\sqrt2+1.

Consequently

\frac1{(6\sqrt2+1)(rk)^2}

which proves (8.2).

Finally $a/k\ge n^2/4$ for sufficiently large $n$, so

|a|\ge \frac{n^2k}{4}.

This proves (8.3). ∎

## 9. Simultaneous local cancellation can coexist with a nonzero improved whole error

There is no purely local incompatibility between deep cancellation at all selected primes and a nonzero improved whole error.

This can be proved without asserting that the resulting reduced weights are short.

Let


$$
L=\prod_{p\in\mathcal P}p^{m_p},
\qquad
r_L\equiv\Theta\pmod L.
$$


The accepted threshold expansion supplies constants $C_0,N$ such that

|\Lambda_{n,2}-t_n|\le C_0\qquad(n\ge N).
$$


Set


$$

$$


Choose an integer $A_*$ in the progression


$$
A_*\equiv r_LK\pmod L
$$


nearest to


$$

$$


Then


$$
\left|\frac{A_*}{K}-(t_n+C_0+2)\right|
\le\frac{L}{2K}<\frac12.
\tag{9.1}
$$


Thus


$$
\frac32
<
\frac{A_*}{K}-\Lambda_{n,2}
<
2C_0+\frac52.
\tag{9.2}
$$



Reduce $A_*/K$ to $a/k$. Since $K$ is coprime to $L$, the reduction factor is also coprime to $L$, so


$$
a\equiv k\Theta\pmod L.
$$


All selected factorial prime powers cancel from the actual primitive denominator:


$$
\boxed{v_p(q_\lambda)=0,\qquad p\in\mathcal P.}
\tag{9.3}
$$


The selected part of the final shared gcd is exactly


$$
\boxed{
\prod_{p\in\mathcal P}p^{\max(m_p-w_p,0)}.
}
\tag{9.4}
$$


In particular, the $3$-part is exponentially large:


$$
v_3(H_{\rm gcd})=m_3-v_3(n).
$$



The whole error is nonzero by (9.2). Indeed, the exact whole-error identity is


$$
\boxed{

=-e_3\alpha_{n,2}(\lambda-\Lambda_{n,2}),
}
\tag{9.5}
$$


where


$$
e_3=(-1)^{n+1}4\pi M^{-2n-3}(1+O(n^{-1})),
$$




$$
\alpha_{n,2}=\frac2{n^2}(1+O(n^{-1})).
$$


Hence the constructed weights satisfy


$$
\boxed{
|c_\lambda-S|\asymp \frac{M^{-2n-3}}{n^2},
\qquad c_\lambda-S\ne0.
}
\tag{9.6}
$$



This is a whole-error statement, including the complete factorial residual.

### 9.1 Why this does not yet advance to an irrationality construction

The presentation height is


$$
\max(|A_*|,K)=O(n^2L).
$$


Reduction could lower it, but controlling that reduction is part of the short-reconstruction problem, not something established here.

\boxed{

\le

\tag{9.7}

The exact denominator remains (2.5). Equation (9.7) does not give a sufficient asymptotic upper bound for it.

\asymp q_\lambda\frac{M^{-2n-3}}{n^2}.
}
\tag{9.8}
$$


No conclusion that this tends to zero follows.

Thus:

* simultaneous cancellation and nonzero analytic improvement **can coexist**;
* this argument establishes that coexistence only at an exponential presentation-height scale;
* it does not establish a short favorable reconstruction or a small whole primitive form.

## 10. A useful actual height certificate beyond uniqueness

The remaining moving-residue question can be attacked by an exact dual certificate.

Let


$$
r_L\equiv\Theta\pmod L,
$$


and consider weights in


$$
|\lambda-t_n|\le C,\qquad 1\le k\le K.
$$


Choose integers $u,v$, not both zero, satisfying


$$
ur_L+v\equiv0\pmod L.
\tag{10.1}
$$


Put


$$
\beta=ut_n+v.
$$



> **Actual dual obstruction.**  
> If
> 

$$
> |\beta|>|u|C
> \tag{10.2}
>
$$


> and
> 

$$
> K\bigl(|\beta|+|u|C\bigr)<L,
> \tag{10.3}
>
$$


> then no weight in the specified window and denominator range satisfies
> 

$$
> a\equiv kr_L\pmod L.
>
$$



### Proof

For such a weight,


$$
ua+vk\equiv0\pmod L.
$$


But


$$
ua+vk=k\bigl(\beta+u(\lambda-t_n)\bigr).
$$


Condition (10.2) makes this integer nonzero, while (10.3) makes its absolute value less than $L$. That contradicts its divisibility by $L$. ∎

This is not a uniqueness statement. One certified pair $(u,v)$ proves **absence** throughout the specified height window.

All comparisons can be made exactly in $\mathbb Q(\sqrt2)$. The pair $(u,v)$ must be obtained from the actual residue (4.6); a generic lattice-shape assertion would not suffice.

A concrete follow-on lemma is therefore:

> **Actual dual-vector lemma sought.**  
> Along a specified original-index family, produce $(u_n,v_n)$ satisfying (10.1) and (10.2), with
> 

$$
> |u_nt_n+v_n|+C|u_n|
> \le e^{o(n)}
>
$$


> for an exponentially large selected modulus $L_n$.
>
> This would exclude every subexponential denominator bound $K_n$ for which the product in (10.3) remains below $L_n$.

The recurrence in Section 3 and the stripped residue in Section 4 make this an actual finite-arithmetic problem. No such infinite-family dual-vector estimate is proved here.
