# An obstruction to integral certificate descent for the original mixed rectangle

## 1. Main conclusions and scope

The rationality or irrationality of $e+\pi$ remains unresolved. The results below concern the arithmetic of the **original, unweighted** compact rectangles.

The principal new result is a disproof of the proposed integral certificate descent.

Let


$$
\mathcal W_k
=
(2^{15}3^2\Lambda_k)^k
\left(\prod_{j=0}^{k-1}j!\right)^4
$$


be the accepted certificate for the weighted stack. Then


$$
\boxed{\mathcal W_k\notin I_{2k-1}(Y_k)\qquad(k\ge18).}
\tag{1.1}
$$


In particular, the proposed descent is false at **every original index**


$$
k=K_u=9^{18+32u},\qquad u\ge0.
$$



The obstruction is an explicitly evaluated binary divisibility of **every original maximal minor**. It does not use weighted equations, enlarge the physical rectangle, or assume an interpolation pivot is a unit.

More precisely, write


$$
S(a)=\sum_{r=0}^{a-1}v_2(r!),\qquad s_2(n)=\text{the number of ones in the binary expansion of }n,
$$


and let $\epsilon_k\in\{0,1\}$ be the parity of $k$. Every maximal minor of $Y_k$ is divisible by $2^{\nu^Y_k}$, where


$$
\boxed{
\nu^Y_k
=
k^2-3k+3-\epsilon_k+4S(k)+2s_2(k-1).
}
\tag{1.2}
$$


Consequently,


$$
\boxed{
\nu^Y_k-v_2(\mathcal W_k)
=
k^2-18k+3-\epsilon_k+2s_2(k-1).
}
\tag{1.3}
$$


The right side is positive for every $k\ge18$.

A related bound is proved for the original $Z_k$, **with its contact atom retained**. These results give genuine original-family lower divisors, not estimates for an augmented matrix.

I also obtain an evaluated, nonzero **divisor-size bound** for the original contents on the supplied nonvanishing domain:


$$
\boxed{
1\le \mathscr R_k,\mathscr L_k\le
\mathcal U_k,
\qquad
\mathcal U_k
=
(2k-1)!\,4^k10^{k-1}\Lambda_k^{k-1}
\prod_{i=0}^{k-1}(2k+4i)!.
}
\tag{1.4}
$$


This is an ordinary upper bound for the actual integer divisors. It is **not** a claim that either content divides $\mathcal U_k$, and it is not a replacement Bézout certificate of the strength originally requested. Its leading size is


$$
\log\mathcal U_k=4k^2\log k+O(k^2),
\tag{1.5}
$$


which is insufficient for the decisive combined-content target.

Finally, a new auxiliary finite calculation at the boundary prime


$$
(k,p)=(4,23),\qquad p=6k-1,
$$


gives actual unit maximal minors


$$
\boxed{
\det Z_4[0,\ldots,6]\equiv16,\qquad
\det Y_4[:,0,\ldots,6]\equiv17\pmod{23}.
}
\tag{1.6}
$$


This establishes exactly that finite normality check. It is not a uniform theorem.

## 3. Integral finite differences of the complete contact return

The first ingredient is an integral divisibility statement for the original sequence.

Let $\Delta y_n=y_{n+1}-y_n$.

### Lemma 3.1

For all $n,r\ge0$,


$$
\boxed{2^r r!\mid \Delta^r u_n,}
\tag{3.1}
$$


and hence


$$
\boxed{2^{r+1}r!\mid\Delta^r\sigma_n.}
\tag{3.2}
$$



#### Proof

The recurrence for $a_d$ gives the polynomial-moment representation


$$
a_d=\int_0^\infty e^{-t}(1-t)^d\,dt.
$$


Indeed, integration by parts gives $a_0=1$ and $a_d=1-da_{d-1}$. Therefore


$$
u_n=\int_0^\infty e^{-t}(t-1)^{2n}\,dt.
$$


Taking a finite difference in $n$ multiplies the integrand by


$$
(t-1)^2-1=t(t-2).
$$


Thus


$$
\Delta^r u_n
=
\int_0^\infty e^{-t}(t-1)^{2n}t^r(t-2)^r\,dt.
\tag{3.3}
$$



Expand


$$
t^r(t-2)^r
=
\sum_{i=0}^r\binom ri(-2)^{r-i}t^{r+i}.
$$


Because $(t-1)^{2n}$ has integer coefficients,


$$
M_{n,r+i}
=
\int_0^\infty e^{-t}t^{r+i}(t-1)^{2n}\,dt
$$


is an integer multiple of $(r+i)!$.

For each $i$,


$$
\frac{\binom ri\,2^{r-i}(r+i)!}{2^r r!}
=
\frac{(r+i)!}{2^i i!(r-i)!}
=
\binom{r+i}{2i}(2i-1)!!\in\mathbb Z.
\tag{3.4}
$$


Here $(-1)!!=1$. Every summand in (3.3) is therefore divisible by $2^r r!$, proving (3.1).

Finally,


$$
\sigma_n=u_{n+1}+u_n=2u_n+\Delta u_n.
$$


Consequently,


$$
\Delta^r\sigma_n=2\Delta^r u_n+\Delta^{r+1}u_n
$$


is divisible by $2^{r+1}r!$. ∎

This proof evaluates only integrals of polynomials, hence finite factorial sums. When applied to the original rectangles, its highest polynomial degree is at most $6k-4$. It does not introduce an additional moment.

### Lemma 3.2 — all minors of the original contact-return block

Every $d\times d$ minor of the finite Hankel block $(\sigma_{m+j})$ is divisible by


$$
\boxed{
A_d=2^{d^2}\left(\prod_{r=0}^{d-1}r!\right)^2.
}
\tag{3.5}
$$



#### Proof

For finite row and column ranges,


$$
\sigma_{m+j}
=
\sum_{r=0}^m\sum_{s=0}^j
\binom mr\binom js\,\Delta^{r+s}\sigma_0.
\tag{3.6}
$$


By Lemma 3.1,


$$
\frac{\Delta^{r+s}\sigma_0}{2^{r+s+1}r!s!}\in\mathbb Z.
$$


Thus the finite block factors as


$$
2P_{\rm row}D_{\rm row}BD_{\rm col}P_{\rm col}^{\,T},
\tag{3.7}
$$


where $P$ denotes the finite Pascal matrix,


$$
D_{rr}=2^r r!,
$$


and $B$ is integral.

Cauchy–Binet expresses every $d$-minor as an integer sum of products containing $d$ distinct diagonal entries from each $D$. Since


$$
2^r r!\mid2^{r+1}(r+1)!,
$$


each such product is divisible by


$$
\prod_{r=0}^{d-1}2^r r!.
$$


The outside factor $2$ contributes $2^d$. Their product is precisely (3.5). ∎

The divisions in (3.7) have been proved integral. They are not declarations that the displayed factors are the actual contents.

For later use, define


$$
C(d)=v_2(A_d)=d^2+2S(d).
\tag{3.8}
$$

## 4. The contact atom in $Z_k$ is retained

The corresponding argument for $c_n=u_n-w_n$ must retain the atom. It gives a slightly different bound.

### Lemma 4.1

Every $d\times d$ minor of the finite block $(c_{m+j})$ is divisible by


$$
\boxed{
B_d
=
2^{d(d-1)}
\left(\prod_{r=0}^{d-2}r!\right)^2
\qquad(d\ge1).
}
\tag{4.1}
$$


The product is empty when $d=1$.

#### Proof

The finite double-difference transform gives


$$
\Delta^{r+s}c_0
=
\Delta^{r+s}u_0-(-2)^{r+s}.
$$


After factoring $2^r$ from transformed row $r$ and $2^s$ from transformed column $s$, the remaining matrix has the form


$$
K_{rs}=U_{rs}-(-1)^r(-1)^s,
\tag{4.2}
$$


where


$$
U_{rs}=\frac{\Delta^{r+s}u_0}{2^{r+s}}
$$


is an integer multiple of $r!s!$, by Lemma 3.1.

The second term in (4.2) has rank one. Expanding a $d$-minor of $K$ in that term leaves only:

* a $d$-minor of $U$; and
* terms consisting of a $(d-1)$-minor of $U$ times two atom coordinates.

Every such term is divisible by


$$
\left(\prod_{r=0}^{d-2}r!\right)^2.
$$


The outside row and column powers of $2$ contribute at least


$$
2^{2(0+\cdots+d-1)}=2^{d(d-1)}.
$$


The finite Pascal matrices are integral, so Cauchy–Binet preserves this divisibility for every original minor. ∎

Thus the atom has not been suppressed. Its rank-one contribution is exactly why the factorial product in (4.1) stops at $d-2$, rather than $d-1$.

## 5. Evaluated binary divisibility of the complete return block

Write


$$
g_n=f_{n+1}+f_n,\qquad b_n=\frac4{2n+1},
$$


so that the complete return is


$$
\boxed{\tau_n=-g_n+b_n.}
\tag{5.1}
$$



We now work in $\mathbb Z_{(2)}$. All denominators $2n+1$, and also $\Lambda_k$, are units there. This localization is used only to prove a binary divisibility of the original integer columns $\Lambda_k\tau_n$; it does not remove $\Lambda_k$ from any all-prime normalization.

### 5.1 Factorial contribution

For all original row and column indices,


$$
g_{m+j}
=
(2m)!(2j)!
\binom{2m+2j}{2m}
\bigl((2m+2j+2)(2m+2j+1)+1\bigr).
\tag{5.2}
$$


Therefore every $d$-minor of $(g_{m+j})$ is divisible by


$$
\left(\prod_{r=0}^{d-1}(2r)!\right)^2.
$$


Its binary valuation is


$$
F(d)=2\sum_{r=0}^{d-1}v_2((2r)!)
=d(d-1)+2S(d),
\tag{5.3}
$$


using


$$
v_2((2r)!)=r+v_2(r!).
$$



### 5.2 Arctangent forcing contribution

For distinct row indices $m_1,\ldots,m_d$ and column indices $j_1,\ldots,j_d$, the Cauchy determinant formula gives


$$
\left|
\det\left(\frac4{2m_i+2j_h+1}\right)_{i,h=1}^d
\right|
=
\frac{
4^d2^{d(d-1)}
\left|\prod_{i<h}(m_h-m_i)\prod_{i<h}(j_h-j_i)\right|
}{
\prod_{i,h}(2m_i+2j_h+1)
}.
\tag{5.4}
$$


Every denominator is odd.

For distinct integer indices, the Vandermonde product is divisible by


$$
\prod_{r=0}^{d-1}r!,
$$


as follows by changing from powers to the integer-valued basis $\binom{x}{r}$. Hence every $d$-minor of $(b_{m+j})$ has valuation at least


$$
F(d)+2d.
\tag{5.5}
$$



This is the contribution of the full $4/(2n+1)$ forcing. It has not been discarded.

### 5.3 Mixing the two contributions

Expand a $d$-minor of $\tau=-g+b$ by the choices of factorial and forcing columns, then expand along the chosen factorial columns. If $s$ columns use $g$ and $t=d-s$ use $b$, every term has valuation at least


$$
F(s)+F(t)+2t.
$$


Thus every $d$-minor of the original scaled return block has valuation at least


$$
E(d)=\min_{s+t=d}\bigl(F(s)+F(t)+2t\bigr).
\tag{5.6}
$$



This minimum can be evaluated, rather than left as an optimization problem.

### Lemma 5.1

If $d=2h$, then


$$
E(d)=2F(h)+2h.
$$


If $d=2h+1$, then


$$
E(d)=F(h+1)+F(h)+2h.
$$


Equivalently,


$$
\boxed{
E(d)=
2\left\lfloor\frac{d^2}{4}\right\rfloor
+2S\!\left(\left\lfloor\frac d2\right\rfloor\right)
+2S\!\left(\left\lceil\frac d2\right\rceil\right).
}
\tag{5.7}
$$



#### Proof

The successive differences of $F$ are


$$
F(s+1)-F(s)=2v_2((2s)!),
$$


which are nondecreasing.

For $t=d-s$, the change in the expression in (5.6) when $s$ increases by one is


$$
2v_2((2s)!)-2v_2((2t-2)!)-2.
\tag{5.8}
$$


It is negative when $s<t$. Once $s$ passes the balanced choice, it is nonnegative. For even $d$, a possible equality merely gives a second minimizing choice; it does not change the minimum. This proves the stated evaluations. Substitution of (5.3) gives (5.7). ∎

## 6. New lower divisors of the actual maximal-minor contents

A maximal minor of $Y_k$ has one of two forms:

* $k-1$ contact-return columns and $k$ right-return columns;
* $k$ contact-return columns and $k-1$ right-return columns.

Laplace expansion along the contact-return columns, using Lemma 3.2 and Section 5, gives the respective valuation bounds


$$
C(k-1)+E(k),\qquad C(k)+E(k-1).
$$


The first is smaller. Indeed,


$$
C(k)-C(k-1)=2k-1+2v_2((k-1)!),
$$


whereas the evaluated increments of $E$ are


$$
E(k)-E(k-1)=
\begin{cases}
2v_2((k-2)!)+2,&k\text{ even},\\
2v_2((k-1)!),&k\text{ odd}.
\end{cases}
$$


The difference is positive for $k\ge2$.

We have proved the following.

### Theorem 6.1 — original unweighted content divisibility

For every $k\ge2$, every maximal minor of $Y_k$ is divisible by $2^{\nu^Y_k}$, where


$$
\boxed{
\nu^Y_k=C(k-1)+E(k).
}
\tag{6.1}
$$


Every maximal minor of $Z_k$ is divisible by $2^{\nu^Z_k}$, where


$$
\boxed{
\nu^Z_k=k(k-1)+2S(k-1)+E(k-1).
}
\tag{6.2}
$$



In particular, whenever the contents are nonzero,


$$
\boxed{
v_2(\mathscr L_k)\ge\nu^Y_k,\qquad
v_2(\mathscr R_k)\ge\nu^Z_k.
}
\tag{6.3}
$$



The $Z_k$ bound follows from Lemma 4.1 and the fact that every maximal minor contains all $k$ contact columns and all $k-1$ return columns.

### Physical-boundary audit

All operations above are on the finite original blocks.

* For $Y_k$, the largest double difference used is
  

$$
\Delta^{3k-3}\sigma_0,
$$


  requiring $u_{3k-2}$.
* For $Z_k$, the contact differences require at most $c_{3k-2}$.
* The largest return index is $3k-3$.
* The largest factorial is $(6k-4)!$.
* The largest odd denominator is $6k-5$.

No row, moment, or terminal correction has been added.

### 6.1 Closed evaluation of $\nu^Y_k$

Put


$$
T(a)=\sum_{r=0}^{a-1}s_2(r).
$$


Legendre’s formula gives


$$
S(a)=\frac{a(a-1)}2-T(a).
$$


Writing $h=\lfloor k/2\rfloor$, $t=\lceil k/2\rceil$, equations (6.1) and (5.7) yield


$$
\nu^Y_k
=
3(k-1)^2-2\bigl(T(k-1)+T(h)+T(t)\bigr).
\tag{6.4}
$$


The binary identities


$$
T(k)=T(h)+T(t)+h,\qquad
T(k)-T(k-1)=s_2(k-1)
$$


then give


$$
\boxed{
\nu^Y_k
=
k^2-3k+3-\epsilon_k+4S(k)+2s_2(k-1),
}
\tag{6.5}
$$


as asserted in (1.2).

These are evaluated factorial and binary-digit formulas, not unevaluated determinants.

## 7. Disproof of the proposed integral descent

Because $\Lambda_k$ is odd,


$$
v_2(\mathcal W_k)=15k+4S(k).
$$


Subtracting this from (6.5) gives


$$
\boxed{
\nu^Y_k-v_2(\mathcal W_k)
=
k^2-18k+3-\epsilon_k+2s_2(k-1).
}
\tag{7.1}
$$


For $k\ge18$, the right side is strictly positive.

But Theorem 6.1 says


$$
I_{2k-1}(Y_k)\subseteq 2^{\nu^Y_k}\mathbb Z.
$$


Therefore $\mathcal W_k$, which has smaller binary valuation, cannot belong to this ideal.

### Theorem 7.1



$$
\boxed{
\mathcal W_k\notin I_{2k-1}(Y_k)\qquad(k\ge18).
}
\tag{7.2}
$$



This proof does not require a prior full-rank theorem: if all maximal minors vanished, the ideal would be zero and the conclusion would remain true.

For a small scalar illustration of the proved formula,


$$
v_2(\mathcal W_{18})=742,\qquad \nu^Y_{18}=749.
$$


This is not an $18$-th-index determinant computation. It is the direct evaluation of the preceding closed formulas.

At the original indices $K_u$, which are all odd and exceed $18$, the forced excess is


$$
K_u^2-18K_u+2+2s_2(K_u-1)>0.
$$



### Meaning of the obstruction

The proposed descent fails for a stronger reason than the absence of a known construction of Bézout coefficients:

> Such integer Bézout coefficients do not exist.

The weighted stack and the original rectangle have genuinely different integral content behavior. Moreover, the lost binary payment is quadratic in $k$, not a fixed small adjustment.

This does **not** disprove good-prime normality. The prime responsible here is $2$, not a prime $p>6k-4$. It instead demonstrates concretely why large-prime normality, even if eventually proved, would not itself establish the proposed all-prime certificate.

## 8. An evaluated nonzero divisor-size bound for the original contents

A replacement integer Bézout certificate of comparable strength is not proved here. There is, however, an evaluated upper bound for the actual nonzero contents, obtained directly from the original columns.

### 8.1 A complete cancellation identity

From the moment representation,


$$
u_n=\frac1e\left(f_n+I_n\right),
\qquad
I_n=\int_0^1 e^t t^{2n}\,dt.
\tag{8.1}
$$


Therefore


$$
e\sigma_n+\tau_n
=
I_n+I_{n+1}+\frac4{2n+1}.
$$


Define


$$
d_n=e\sigma_n+\tau_n.
$$


Then


$$
\boxed{
d_n
=
\int_0^1 e^t(1+t^2)t^{2n}\,dt+\frac4{2n+1},
\qquad
0<d_n<\frac{10}{2n+1}\le10.
}
\tag{8.2}
$$


Both the factorial contribution and the full $4/(2n+1)$ correction have been included.

Elementary estimates from (8.1) give


$$
|c_n|\le4(2n)!,
\qquad
|\sigma_n|\le4(2n+2)!.
\tag{8.3}
$$



### 8.2 A nonzero $Y_k$ minor with all contact-return columns

The first $k$ rows of the contact-return block form a positive definite Hankel matrix over $\mathbb R$. Indeed, for a nonzero real polynomial $P$ of degree at most $k-1$,


$$
\sum_{i,j}P_iP_j\sigma_{i+j}
=
\int_0^\infty
e^{-t}\bigl((t-1)^2+1\bigr)
P\bigl((t-1)^2\bigr)^2\,dt>0.
\tag{8.4}
$$


Thus those $k$ columns are independent over $\mathbb Q$.

On the supplied nonvanishing domain, $Y_k$ has row rank $2k-1$ over $\mathbb Q$. Consequently its $k$ contact-return columns can be extended to a column basis using $k-1$ return columns. There is therefore a nonzero maximal minor containing all contact-return columns and omitting one return column.

In that minor, replace each retained right column by


$$
\Lambda_k\tau_{m+j}+e\Lambda_k\sigma_{m+j}
=\Lambda_k d_{m+j}.
\tag{8.5}
$$


This preserves the determinant over $\mathbb R$.

These are **not integer row or column operations**, and no integral-content conclusion is inferred from them. They are used only to bound the absolute value of an original integer minor.

There are now $k$ factorial-sized columns and $k-1$ bounded columns.

### 8.3 The corresponding $Z_k$ cancellation

In $Z_k$, for each $j<k-1$, replace the return column by


$$
\Lambda_k\tau_{m+j}
+
e\Lambda_k\bigl(c_{m+j}+c_{m+j+1}\bigr)
=
\Lambda_kd_{m+j}.
\tag{8.6}
$$


Both contact columns used on the left already belong to $Z_k$. The last one is $j+1=k-1$, so no extra column or moment is introduced.

Again there are $k$ factorial-sized columns and $k-1$ bounded columns.

### 8.4 Evaluating the factorial product

The function $n\mapsto(2n)!$ is log-convex. For ordered row indices and ordered shifts, the largest product of factorial bounds is attained by pairing them in the same order.

For $Y_k$, the largest possible $k$ row indices are


$$
k-1,\ldots,2k-2,
$$


and the large-column shifts are $0,\ldots,k-1$, with an additional $+1$ in the factorial argument.

For $Z_k$, the largest possible $k$ row indices are


$$
k,\ldots,2k-1,
$$


and there is no additional $+1$.

Both cases give the same product:


$$
\prod_{i=0}^{k-1}(2k+4i)!.
\tag{8.7}
$$


Its last factorial is exactly $(6k-4)!$.

Expanding the determinant has $(2k-1)!$ terms. Equations (8.2), (8.3), and (8.7) therefore prove:

### Theorem 8.1 — evaluated original-content upper bound

Whenever $H_{1,k}\ne0$,


$$
\boxed{

$$

$\mathscr L_k\mid\mathcal U_k$.

Stirling’s formula gives


$$

$$


One weak but explicit prime-exclusion consequence is that a prime dividing $\mathscr L_k$ must satisfy


$$

$$


and similarly for $\mathscr R_k$. This leaves an enormous finite prime range and does not address the desired threshold $6k-4$.
