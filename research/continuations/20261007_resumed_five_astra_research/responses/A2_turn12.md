## 2. The original research objects that a transfer must preserve

Write


$$
\alpha=e+\pi.
$$


The accepted approximation strategy requires integers $p_n,q_n$, with $q_n>0$, such that


$$
0<|q_n\alpha-p_n|\longrightarrow0
$$


on **one and the same infinite set of original admissible indices**.

The irrationality implication is elementary: if $\alpha=A/B$ were rational, every nonzero $q_n\alpha-p_n$ would have absolute value at least $1/B$.

The difficulty is producing the required integer sequence after every actual normalization and cancellation. If the final, fully paid integer scalar pair is $(A_n,B_n)$, $B_n\ne0$, then


$$
G_n=\gcd(|A_n|,|B_n|),\qquad
q_n=\frac{|B_n|}{G_n},\qquad
p_n=\frac{\operatorname{sgn}(B_n)A_n}{G_n}.
$$


Consequently,


$$
q_n\alpha-p_n
=
\frac{\operatorname{sgn}(B_n)}{G_n}(B_n\alpha-A_n).
$$


Both the gcd and the error in this formula are the **final, whole quantities**. A prime-specific gain, a column-content gain, or a partial source estimate cannot be substituted for them.

### 3.1 Family017: interpolation, integer determinants, and the mixed-source barrier

#### Advertised method and theorem

The abstract claims that the irrationality exponent of $\pi$ is $2$. The coordinator additionally identifies the proposed mechanism as separated multivariable interpolation and an integer determinant.

The promising component is not the conclusion about $\pi$ by itself. It is a possible interpolation theorem whose constants, nonvanishing conditions, and arithmetic normalization might survive in a construction containing both exponential and logarithmic data.

#### Precise outstanding obligation it could address

A useful transfer would have to construct or control a nonzero integer determinant for the **mixed** source, while proving:

1. exact contact identities for all complete corrected columns;
2. nonvanishing at the original indices;
3. every rational and factorial clearing cost;
4. a whole-error estimate after the same normalization;
5. an arithmetic comparison strong enough for the actual final primitive denominator.

For A2, the most concrete point of contact is the proposed logarithmic charge determinant and its missing complete exponential counterpart. A theorem about a logarithmic interpolation family alone does not supply that counterpart.

#### Decisive transfer hypotheses

Before application, one must verify that:

- the actual exponential source belongs to the theorem’s permitted function class;
- interpolation estimates are uniform over the centers and dimensions actually used;
- no independence or nonvanishing hypothesis already assumes the desired mixed-period conclusion;
- the theorem treats the finite contact range and physical endpoint, rather than an infinite or boundary-free surrogate;
- the integer determinant is integer **after all** original divisions;
- the determinant and error bounds apply on the same infinite original subfamily.

#### Primary proofs needed

Inspect the complete proofs of:

- the center-uniform interpolation theorem;
- the separation and dimension hypotheses;
- determinant nonvanishing;
- denominator clearing and coefficient-height bounds;
- the final passage from determinant size to the irrationality-exponent statement;
- any auxiliary theorem used to cover all sufficiently large denominators.

No such proof is included here.

#### A rigorous obstruction to the direct-theorem shortcut

Knowing the irrationality exponent of each summand does not determine that of their sum—or even whether their sum is irrational.

More specifically, irrationality exponent is invariant under rational translation and sign. If $r=a/b\in\mathbb Q$, then approximating


$$
\beta=r-\xi
$$


by $p/q$ approximates $\xi$ by


$$
\frac{aq-bp}{bq}
$$


with the same absolute error. The reduced denominator is at most $bq$. Passing to any slightly smaller exponent absorbs the fixed factor $b$; applying the same argument in reverse gives


$$
\mu(r-\xi)=\mu(\xi).
$$



Thus the hypothetical relation


$$
e+\pi=r\in\mathbb Q
$$


would imply $\mu(\pi)=\mu(e)$, not contradict it. In particular, a value $2$ for both exponents is compatible with that hypothetical relation.

**Conclusion:** even a verified Family017 theorem would not directly settle $e+\pi$. Its interpolation machinery needs a new mixed-source transfer.

### 3.2 Family022: distinguish deterministic overlap estimates from metric conclusions

#### Exact claimed quantifier

The supplied abstract states, for every fixed shift $\gamma$ and every fixed finite-valued $\psi$,


$$
\sum_{q\ge1}\frac{\phi(q)\psi(q)}q=\infty
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\|qx-\gamma\|<\psi(q)
\ \text{infinitely often for almost every }x.
$$



This is not a theorem for every $x$. Nor does the wording establish one exceptional set valid simultaneously for every $\gamma$ and every $\psi$.

#### What could be reusable

The useful targets are internal deterministic estimates concerning:

- overlaps of shifted approximation sets;
- gcd dependence;
- sums over exceptional divisor configurations;
- uniformity in the real shift;
- separation of arithmetic and measure-theoretic steps.

Such estimates might help only if the actual A2 scalar sequences can be substituted into their hypotheses with explicit control. Existing gcd/overlap methods are already in the archive; only a stronger, specifically compatible estimate would be new.

#### Precise transfer obstacle

The actual scalar numerator and denominator are generated by finite inverse matrices and complete forcing. They are not arbitrary denominators sampled with Lebesgue measure. A transfer needs a deterministic estimate for those exact scalar sequences, including the final ALL-prime gcd.

Even an overlap estimate valid for all integer denominators does not automatically estimate the content of the particular scalar cofactors produced here.

#### Two rigorous reasons the metric conclusion is insufficient

**Fixed exceptional values.** A null exceptional set may contain $e+\pi$.

**Nonzero error is not supplied.** Let $\gamma=0$ and


$$
\psi(q)=\frac1{4\log(q+1)}.
$$


Then


$$
\sum_{q\ge1}\frac{\phi(q)\psi(q)}q
\ge
\frac14\sum_{q\ge1}\frac1{q\log(q+1)}
=\infty.
$$


At the rational point $x=0$, however,


$$
\|qx\|=0
$$


for every $q$. The approximation conclusion is compatible with rationality and supplies no nonzero linear form.

There is also an index-set mismatch. The explicitly displayed A2, A3, and A5 index sets have $O(\log X)$ members up to $X$. A density-one property of unrestricted integers can fail at every one of those indices.

#### Primary proofs needed

Request the proofs establishing:

1. the precise approximation events and treatment of unrestricted numerators;
2. the pairwise overlap bounds, including large-gcd configurations;
3. arithmetic exceptional-set estimates and their dependence on $\gamma$;
4. truncation for arbitrary finite-valued $\psi$;
5. the second-moment/Borel–Cantelli conclusion and exact quantifier order.

**Inspection status here:** none of these sections is supplied. The global ongoing reading remains ongoing.

### 3.3 Family090: a potentially useful certificate technique, with a factorial-cost obstruction

#### Exact claimed ingredients

Across its four abstracts, Family090 advertises:

- sharp Gaussian Fourier minorants built from an atomic interpolation certificate;
- positive mixtures and heat-kernel comparison;
- a radial Schwartz certificate with global sign conditions proved using interval arithmetic and analytic estimates;
- a direct Voronoi-cell comparison, also with rigorous interval arithmetic.

The first and third ingredients are the most plausible reusable techniques.

#### Possible outstanding obligation

For an explicitly new mixed construction, a certificate method could potentially provide:

- a sign-definite representation of the **whole** evaluated error;
- global, rather than sampled, positivity;
- a finite exact certificate plus an analytic tail bound.

It does not address the final primitive denominator. Moreover, the original route already has accepted analytical results at their stated scope, so Family090 is not a reason to repeat those calculations.

#### Decisive transfer hypothesis

One must first prove an exact representation of the complete factorial-plus-pole functional in the proposed certificate framework, with controlled support, total variation or coefficient norm, and all reconstruction factors.

The following elementary bound shows why this requirement is substantial.

### Proposition 1. Finite compact-representation cost

Fix an admissible moment index $m$ in the **original finite moment range**, a number $R>0$, and a finite signed or complex measure $\mu$ supported on $[-R,R]$. Suppose its $2m$-th moment equals the complete prescribed moment


$$
\int x^{2m}\,d\mu(x)
=
-\frac{3^h}{4}(2m)!+P_m,
$$


where $P_m$ is the actual pole contribution. Then


$$
\boxed{\quad
\|\mu\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
\ge
\frac{\left|-\frac{3^h}{4}(2m)!+P_m\right|}{R^{2m}}
\ge
\frac{\bigl(\frac{3^h}{4}(2m)!-|P_m|\bigr)_+}{R^{2m}}.
\quad}
$$



#### Proof

By total variation,


$$
\left|\int x^{2m}\,d\mu(x)\right|
\le
\int |x|^{2m}\,d|\mu|(x)
\le R^{2m}\|\mu\|_{\mathrm{TV}}.
$$


Substitute the full moment and apply the reverse triangle inequality. ∎

For example, **if additionally**


$$
|P_m|\le \frac{3^h}{8}(2m)!,
$$


then


$$
\|\mu\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
\ge
\frac{3^h(2m)!}{8R^{2m}}.
$$


For fixed $R,h$, this is factorial rather than merely exponential growth. Indeed, $(2m)!\ge m^m$.

This proposition does not assert the additional pole bound in the original functional. Its first bound retains the pole term exactly. It also does not extend the finite moment range.

**Consequence for transfer:** a compact representation is not automatically a cheap representation. If support or coefficient norm grows, that growth must enter the whole-error estimate and arithmetic normalization.

#### Primary proofs needed

Before adopting Family090 techniques, inspect:

- construction and exactness of the atomic interpolation certificate;
- proof of global sign conditions;
- analytic tail estimates beyond the finite certified region;
- dependence of constants on interpolation order and scale;
- which arguments require nonnegative completely monotone potentials or positive measures;
- whether signed data can be treated without losing the claimed bounds.

**Classification:** promising certificate technique; no direct theorem implication.

## 4. A proved prime-$29$ obstruction to content and saturation shortcuts

The A2 scope explicitly warns that a logarithmic column-content bound does not bound the full norm valuation. The following construction proves a stronger algebraic warning, including the final gcd.

### Proposition 2. Saturated primitive frames do not control norm valuation or primitive scalar denominator

For every integer $k\ge1$, there is an integer $t$ and primitive integer vectors


$$
v=(1,t,0),\qquad
w_0=(1,t,1),\qquad
w_1=(1,0,1)
$$


such that:

1. $29^k\mid Q$, where $Q=v^Tv=1+t^2$;
2. every displayed column has content $1$;
3. both matrices $[v,w_0]$ and $[v,w_1]$ have gcd of $2\times2$ minors equal to $1$;
4. the least simultaneous denominator clearer is $1$;
5. with $P_i=v^Tw_i$,
   

$$
\gcd(Q,P_0)=Q,\qquad \gcd(Q,P_1)=1.
$$


   Hence $P_0/Q$ has primitive denominator $1$, whereas $P_1/Q$ has primitive denominator $Q$.

All gcd statements are over **all primes**.

#### Proof: arbitrarily deep norm divisibility

Since


$$
12^2+1=145=5\cdot29,
$$


start with $t_1=12$. Suppose $29^k\mid t_k^2+1$, and put


$$
a_k=\frac{t_k^2+1}{29^k}.
$$


Choose $c_k\in\{0,\ldots,28\}$ satisfying


$$
a_k+2t_kc_k\equiv0\pmod{29},
$$


and define


$$
t_{k+1}=t_k+c_k29^k.
$$


This choice exists uniquely because $t_k\equiv12\pmod{29}$, so $2t_k$ is a unit modulo $29$. Expanding,


$$
t_{k+1}^2+1
=
29^k\bigl(a_k+2t_kc_k+c_k^2\,29^k\bigr),
$$


which is divisible by $29^{k+1}$.

Thus $t=t_k$ gives the first assertion.

#### Proof: contents, minors and final scalar gcd

Every column has a coordinate equal to $1$, so each content is $1$, and all entries are already integral.

For $[v,w_0]$, the three $2\times2$ minors are


$$
0,\quad1,\quad t.
$$


For $[v,w_1]$, they are


$$
-t,\quad1,\quad t.
$$


Both minor gcds are $1$.

Finally,


$$
P_0=v^Tw_0=1+t^2=Q,\qquad
P_1=v^Tw_1=1.
$$


The claimed ALL-prime gcds and primitive denominators follow. ∎

### Scope of this obstruction

This is an explicit auxiliary family, **not** the original A2 forced family. It proves that the following information alone is insufficient:

- primitive columns;
- completely paid integral coordinates;
- least clearer $1$;
- saturated $2\times2$ minors;
- even knowledge of the entire norm $Q$.

The pairing with the complete second column can change the final primitive denominator from $1$ to $Q$.

It does not prove that either behavior occurs in the original A2 construction. The missing ingredient must use the actual forcing and reconstruction, not only abstract frame invariants.

Nor are these auxiliary fractions approximants to $e+\pi$: their whole errors do not tend to zero. They are counterexamples to an algebraic transfer principle, not an alternative irrationality construction.

### A concrete follow-on lemma

A source-dependent norm bound must exclude deep isotropy for the actual normalized vector.

If the archived full norm is $V_u^TV_u$, let $C_u$ be the **actual** integer content of that complete vector and write


$$
V_u=C_uV_u^{\mathrm{prim}}.
$$


Then exactly


$$
v_{29}(V_u^TV_u)
=
2v_{29}(C_u)
+
v_{29}\!\left((V_u^{\mathrm{prim}})^TV_u^{\mathrm{prim}}\right).
$$



A concrete next lemma for any proposed logarithmic norm bound is therefore:

> **Complete-force modular exclusion lemma.** On a specified infinite subset of the original A2 progression, prove, for an explicit constant $K$, that the actual primitive vector satisfies
> 

$$
> (V_u^{\mathrm{prim}})^TV_u^{\mathrm{prim}}
> \not\equiv0
> \pmod{29^{K(1+\lfloor\log_{29}n\rfloor)}}.
>
$$



This would give the corresponding explicit logarithmic upper bound for the residual norm valuation. Proposition 2 proves that column content and minor saturation cannot establish this lemma by themselves.

**Status:** open. The full archived vector definition must first identify the precise norm and content involved. Even this lemma would not, by itself, settle the ALL-prime denominator and whole-error problem.

## 5. Exact arithmetic cost of the proposed A2 logarithmic charge

The proposed identity


$$
D=-\frac{2^{n+1}(n!)^2}{b!}
$$


is under independent review. The arithmetic of its right-hand side can nevertheless be evaluated rigorously.

### Proposition 3. ALL-prime valuation of the proposed charge expression

For integers $n\ge b\ge1$, define


$$
D_*=-\frac{2^{n+1}(n!)^2}{b!}.
$$


Then $D_*$ is a nonzero integer and, for every prime $\ell$,


$$
\boxed{
v_\ell(D_*)
=
(n+1)\mathbf1_{\ell=2}
+
2\sum_{j\ge1}\left\lfloor\frac n{\ell^j}\right\rfloor
-
\sum_{j\ge1}\left\lfloor\frac b{\ell^j}\right\rfloor.
}
$$


Equivalently,


$$
\boxed{
v_\ell(D_*)
=
(n+1)\mathbf1_{\ell=2}
+
\frac{2n-b-2s_\ell(n)+s_\ell(b)}{\ell-1},
}
$$


where $s_\ell$ denotes the base-$\ell$ digit sum.

#### Proof

Since $b!\mid n!$, the displayed expression is integral and nonzero. Taking valuations and applying Legendre’s formula gives the first expression.

For completeness, if $N=\sum_i a_i\ell^i$, then


$$
\sum_{j\ge1}\left\lfloor\frac N{\ell^j}\right\rfloor
=
\sum_i a_i(1+\ell+\cdots+\ell^{i-1})
=
\frac{N-s_\ell(N)}{\ell-1}.
$$


Substitution gives the second formula. ∎

### 5.1 Specialization to the original A2 parameters

Here


$$
n=2001b=29\cdot69b.
$$


Therefore


$$
s_{29}(n)=s_{29}(69b),
$$


and


$$
\boxed{
v_{29}(D_*)
=
\frac{4001b-2s_{29}(69b)+s_{29}(b)}{28}.
}
$$



This is an exact digit formula, not an unnamed factorial sum.

For the original exponent


$$
E(u)=249005515+574312172u,
$$


one has


$$
249005515\equiv3\pmod{28},
\qquad
574312172\equiv0\pmod{28}.
$$


Thus, at every original admissible index,


$$
b=3^{E(u)}\equiv3^3=-2\pmod{29},
\qquad v_{29}(n)=1.
$$



Moreover, using


$$
s_{29}(N)\le28\bigl(1+\lfloor\log_{29}N\rfloor\bigr),
$$


we obtain


$$
v_{29}(D_*)=\frac{4001}{28}b+O(\log b).
$$



### Interpretation

**Conditional on the charge identity**, the actual logarithmic charge has a linearly large $29$-adic valuation in this normalization. It is emphatically not a $29$-adic unit.

This calculation identifies a normalization cost that a determinant transfer must retain. It does **not** determine:

- the complete exponential residue;
- cancellation in the mixed charge;
- the auxiliary scalar clearer;
- the actual final scalar gcd;
- the primitive denominator;
- the whole evaluated approximation error.

No gain at another prime has been discarded: Proposition 3 retains every prime, including the additional $2^{n+1}$ payment.
