## 3. Verification of the rotation alternative

Write $u$ for the rotation budget and


$$
\epsilon=e^{-10u}.
$$


Dirichlet pigeonholing gives


$$
\alpha=\frac a\ell+\theta,\qquad
(a,\ell)=1,\qquad
1\le \ell\le \sqrt N,\qquad
|\theta|\le \frac{2}{\ell\sqrt N}.
$$



The statement concerns


$$
\|n\alpha-y\|\le \Delta,
\qquad
\Delta\le e^{-C_1u},
\qquad
N\ge e^{C_1u}.
$$


The constants must be chosen in this order: first the absolute rational-approximation constant $C_2$, then $C_1$ sufficiently larger.

### 3.1 Large $\ell$

Suppose $\ell>N^{1/30}$. For a tested divisor $k\le N^{1/500}$, set $n=km$.

In a block of $\ell$ consecutive $m$'s:

- the rational parts form a grid with multiplicity $(k,\ell)$;
- the grid mesh is $(k,\ell)/\ell$;
- the drift is $O(k/\sqrt N)$.

Consequently the count is


$$
\frac{2\epsilon N}{k}
 +O\!\left(\frac N\ell+\sqrt N+\ell\right)
 =
\frac{2\epsilon N}{k}+O(N^{.99}).
$$


Here the generous exponent $.99$ is uniform over all the stated tested divisors.

For the prime count, the Selberg square uses $Z=N^{1/5000}$. Its tested least common multiples are at most $Z^2<N^{1/500}$, and the coefficient bound gives a total discrepancy


$$
O(Z^4N^{.99}).
$$


The sieve main term is $O(\epsilon N/\log N)$. Since


$$
Z^4N^{.99}=N^{.9908},
$$


both errors are smaller than the required $e^{-u}$ bounds once $C_1$ is sufficiently large.

This is a uniform estimate for all primes in the dyad. No summation over individual primes is being inferred from a pointwise assertion.

### 3.2 Intermediate $\ell$

Suppose


$$
\epsilon^{-10}<\ell\le N^{1/30}.
$$


In windows of length $H\asymp N^{1/4}$, the drift is $O(N^{-1/4})$, which is absorbed into the $\epsilon$-width for the stated choice of $C_1$.

The number of permitted invertible residues is


$$
O(\epsilon\phi(\ell)+\tau(\ell)).
$$


The elementary estimates used in the source imply


$$
\frac{\tau(\ell)}{\phi(\ell)}
 \ll \ell^{-3/4}
 \ll \epsilon^{15/2},
$$


so this is $O(\epsilon^{1/2}\phi(\ell))$.

The progression sieve has main term


$$
O\!\left(\frac{H}{\phi(\ell)\log N}\right)
$$


and error $O(Z^4)$. The latter is uniformly absorbed because


$$
\frac{H}{\phi(\ell)\log N}
 \ge \frac{N^{1/4-1/30}}{\log N}.
$$


Summation over windows and permitted residues gives the stated saving.

### 3.3 Small $\ell$

Suppose $\ell\le \epsilon^{-10}$.

If


$$
|\theta|N>\Delta\epsilon^{-20},
$$


then, on each residue class modulo $\ell$, the continuous return set has $O(|\theta|N+1)$ components and total length


$$
O\!\left(\Delta N+\frac{\Delta}{|\theta|}\right).
$$


Enlarging to $H$-windows costs at most


$$
O\!\left((\Delta+|\theta|H)N+
          \frac{\Delta}{|\theta|}+H\right)
 \ll \epsilon N.
$$


The preceding progression estimate applies separately to each residue's selected windows. This remains valid although those windows depend on the residue.

If the displayed drift inequality fails, then


$$
\ell\le e^{100u},
\qquad
|\theta|\le \frac{\Delta e^{200u}}N,
$$


which is the rational alternative.

The cases $\Delta=0$ are also covered: isolated return points are covered by the same window enlargement, while $\theta=0$ is already the exact rational alternative.

**Status:** this verifies `ar:rotation` at its stated scope.

## 4. Simultaneous weighted switching: the finite reindexing argument

The main issue is not the rotation estimate itself. It is how its exceptional set is charged after summing over all available switches.

### 4.1 A finite weighted reindexing principle

Suppose an original finite configuration has a factor $h$, and stripping it gives a residual configuration $\omega$. Assume:

1. after stripping, the rotation slope and target are fixed as $h$ varies in a dyad $[H,2H)$;
2. the original weight is bounded by
   

$$
\frac{B(\omega)}h;
$$


3. every original failure is included in the reindexed failure sum.

If the integer rotation alternative fails for at most $Ce^{-u}H$ integers in the dyad, then


$$
\sum_{\substack{h\in[H,2H)\\\text{failure}}}
 \frac{B(\omega)}h
 \le C e^{-u}B(\omega).
$$


For prime switches, the corresponding estimate is


$$
\sum_{\substack{p\in[H,2H)\\\text{failure}}}
 \frac{B(\omega)}p
 \le C e^{-u}\frac{B(\omega)}{\log H}.
$$



Summing these inequalities over residual configurations and tested dyads bounds the **union** of all failures. An original configuration may be counted many times in this upper bound; that causes no difficulty.

This is the required replacement for an invalid union bound over individual primes.

### 4.2 Verification in `dir:ultra-switch`

At a prime, put


$$
a=\nu_p(d_i),\qquad b=\nu_p(T),\qquad c=\nu_p(Ud_j).
$$


Then


$$
\nu_p(f_0)=\min(a+b,c),
\qquad
\nu_p(\sigma_0)=\max(a+b-c,0).
$$


If $p^k\mid(d_i,\sigma_0)$, then


$$
k\le a,\qquad k\le a+b-c
$$


whenever $k>0$. Hence


$$
\min(a-k+b,c)=\min(a+b,c).
$$


Thus removing $h\mid(d_i,\sigma_0)$ really does leave $f_0$ unchanged.

After fixing $d_i/h,d_j$, the determinant condition becomes a rotation in $h$, with fixed slope


$$
\frac{\sigma_0\alpha_i}{h},
$$


fixed target modulo one, and unchanged tolerance.

Moreover,


$$
\frac{f_0}{d_id_j}
 =\frac1h\,\frac{f_0}{(d_i/h)d_j}.
$$


The factor $1/h$ is exact; it is not a probabilistic heuristic.

The unrestricted residual-mask sum is controlled locally. At equal centre exponents,


$$
\sum_{a,b\ge0}p^{-\max(a,b)}
 =\frac{1+p^{-1}}{(1-p^{-1})^2}
 =1+O(p^{-1}).
$$


At an exponent mismatch $g$, the source's sum is bounded by


$$
(1+g)^C(1+C/p).
$$


Thus the aggregate residual cost is


$$
\exp(CY_0)\tau(UT)^C.

The tested dyads are finite and number at most $\exp(O(Y_0))$. Because $Y_0=O(X_0)$ in these birth regimes, a budget $D'X_0$ with $D'$ sufficiently large absorbs both costs.

The rotation hypotheses also hold:

\Delta\ll e^{-.9Q_0X_0};

- choosing $Q_0$ sufficiently large against $C_1D'$ supplies the tolerance inequality.

This establishes a simultaneous exceptional estimate of the form asserted in the source before the centre-kernel summation.

## 5. New lemma: transported birth failures retain the current $1/q$

This is the additional derivation needed to connect the static calculation to the current table.

### Lemma 1 — Orbit-invariant switching charge

Let $q^\nu$ be a currently unprocessed common tail prime power. Let


$$
\mathcal G=\langle x\mapsto x+1/q^\nu\rangle
$$


act on a finite labelled table per unit length.

Assume:

1. the pre-update table, its raw weights, and the tested geometric query transport equivariantly under $\mathcal G$;
2. a specified plus label succeeds on one residue class of the translation parameter modulo $q$;
3. the switching-failure predicate is invariant under $\mathcal G$;
4. all stripped switching factors are prime to $q$.

Then the failure charge restricted to that plus success fibre is exactly $1/q$ of its unrestricted orbit charge. If two plus successes are required, it is either $0$ or $1/q$, according to whether their success colours are incompatible or compatible. In particular it is at most $1/q$.

These assertions remain true with any nonnegative equivariant weights and with matched or unpaired portions.

#### Proof

Write the orbit parameter as $t\bmod q^\nu$. The plus numerator changes by a unit modulo $q$, so its success indicator has the form


$$
S_\xi(t)=\mathbf1_{\{t\equiv c_\xi\pmod q\}}.
$$


The invariant failure predicate and transported weight are constant on the orbit. Hence


$$
\frac1{q^\nu}\sum_{t\bmod q^\nu} S_\xi(t)=\frac1q.
$$


For two sources,


$$
\frac1{q^\nu}\sum_t S_\xi(t)S_\eta(t)
 =
 \begin{cases}
  1/q,&c_\xi=c_\eta,\\
  0,&c_\xi\ne c_\eta.
 \end{cases}
$$


Multiplication by the invariant nonnegative charge proves the assertion. Summation over complete finite label orbits preserves it. Stabilizers merely repeat each orbit element equally. ∎

### 5.1 Validation in the supplied table objects

The necessary invariance is not an extra stochastic hypothesis.

- A currently unprocessed $q^\nu$ lies in the pre-$q$ core.
- Every earlier decision preserves translation by $1/q^\nu$.
- The same-location ancestor construction transports earlier birth data along this orbit.
- Simultaneous translation preserves the physical distance query.
- The rational-alternative failure predicate depends on the fixed masks, phases, slope, target, and tolerance. It does not depend on the choice of numerator representative along this joint translation orbit.
- A birth tag or processed low factor being switched is distinct from the current unprocessed tail prime. Stripping it leaves the $q$-translation mechanism intact.

The argument should be applied **before** discarding the numerator data and reindexing the switched factor. It then leaves an external factor $1/q$ in every dyadic exception estimate of Section 4.

This proves the promised transported-fibre extension at the scope of the table chronology and the previously established same-location rescaling identities. It does not authorize applying it to an arbitrary conditioning event. A condition that is not invariant under the pre-$q$ orbit would require a different argument.

## 6. Compatibility and the actual denominator after the whole signature

A successful switch gives a rational approximation to a slope. Multiplying back by the switched integer does not enlarge its denominator.

For side $i$, write the resulting reduced rational as


$$
A_i=\frac nq,\qquad (n,q)=1.
$$


The determinant defines its partner:


$$
A_j=A_i+\sigma_0z_i-\tau_0z_j.
$$


The partner has the same denominator bound and approximates $\tau_0\alpha_j$, because the original cohit error is at most $\Delta$.

Two candidates obtained from different switches differ by


$$
O(\Delta e^{C D'X_0})
$$


and have denominators at most $e^{C D'X_0}$. If they were distinct, their difference would be at least the reciprocal product of those denominators. The hierarchy $Q_0\gg D'$, with the absolute constants included, rules this out.

Thus all successful switches give the same coupled pair.

### 6.1 Exact division formula

For every positive integer $H$,


$$
\operatorname{den}\!\left(\frac{A_i}{H}\right)
 =\frac{qH}{(n,H)}.
$$


This formula is valid even if $q$ and $H$ have common prime factors. In particular, if $(n,H)=1$, the denominator is $qH$, not a smaller quantity obtained by cancelling factors between $q$ and $H$.

A switched prime larger than the allowed denominator bound must therefore divide $n$.

### 6.2 Assembly over all factors

Set


$$
B_i=(d_i,\sigma_0).
$$


The source correctly proves


$$
\sigma_0/B_i\mid T.
$$



To bound the denominator after division by $B_i$, separate:

1. prime-power excess beyond the first power;
2. distinct primes dividing $n$;
3. distinct primes not dividing $n$.

The good-mask condition pays for item 1. Item 2 cancels. Every sufficiently large prime in item 3 would itself be a prime switch, so none can remain. If the product of the smaller primes in item 3 reached the lower divisor-switch cutoff, a first-crossing subproduct would lie between that cutoff and its square. Its switch would force a denominator exceeding the allowed bound, a contradiction.

Consequently the uncancelled squarefree product is below the cutoff, up to the stated endpoint factor. Finally $\sigma_0/B_i\mid T$ pays for the remaining centre-ratio factor.

This proves the full-signature denominator bound. It is stronger than merely saying that each individual switched prime divides the numerator: the prime-power excess and the whole uncancelled small-prime product have both been paid.

The same reasoning applies in the deeper-medium box, where:

- the switched whole factor $c_0$ supplies its denominator bound directly;
- the unswitched $c_0$ is below its cutoff;
- the bad subproduct of $b_0$ is controlled by first crossing;
- the primes of $m_i$ cancel;
- $T_0\le\eta^{-\vartheta}$ is paid separately.

No denominator is being inferred from a radical alone.

### Lemma 2 — Exact one-prime fluctuation and rational reduction

Fix a finite realized prime update. Let its residual-scaled plus portions be indexed by $\xi$. Let

- $c_\xi\in\mathbb Q_{\ge0}$ be the portion's raw mass times its after-mask probability;
- $c_\xi^{\rm col}\in\mathbb Z/p\mathbb Z$ be its success colour;
- $b_\xi$ be its signed endpoint vector:
  

$$
b_\xi=
  \begin{cases}
  e_{\xi,+}-e_{\xi,-},&\text{matched},\\
  e_{\xi,+},&\text{unpaired plus}.
  \end{cases}
$$



Unpaired minus portions have no centred fluctuation.

Define


$$
v_c=\sum_{c_\xi^{\rm col}=c}c_\xi b_\xi,
\qquad
\bar v=\frac1p\sum_{c\bmod p}v_c.
$$


Then the complete centred update at orbit colour $t$ is


$$
