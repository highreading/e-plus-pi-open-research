### 2.3 The unchanged success criterion

Write $\alpha=e+\pi$. The approximation route must produce integers $p_n,q_n$, with $q_n>0$, such that


$$
0<|q_n\alpha-p_n|\longrightarrow0
$$


on **one and the same infinite set of original admissible indices**.

The familiar implication is rigorous: if $\alpha=a/b\in\mathbb Q$, with $b>0$, then every nonzero value


$$
q_n\alpha-p_n=\frac{a q_n-bp_n}{b}
$$


has absolute value at least $1/b$.

The difficulty is constructing the required primitive integer forms—not this final implication.

If a completely cleared construction gives integers $P_n,Q_n$, then its final reduction uses


$$
g_n=\gcd(|P_n|,|Q_n|)
$$


over **all primes**, and


$$
q_n=\frac{|Q_n|}{g_n},\qquad
p_n=\frac{\operatorname{sgn}(Q_n)P_n}{g_n}.
$$


Its whole reduced error is


$$
q_n\alpha-p_n
=\frac{\operatorname{sgn}(Q_n)}{g_n}(Q_n\alpha-P_n).
$$


Neither a component error nor a denominator before this final gcd is an adequate replacement.

## 5. Detailed assessment of the best candidates

## 5.1 Rank 1: Family116 — exact identity verification, not scalar nonvanishing

### Claimed method

The three abstracts claim deterministic matrix hitting constructions for:

1. bounded-size division-free noncommutative formulas in positive characteristic;
2. bounded-size division-free noncommutative formulas in characteristic zero;
3. admissible noncommutative rational formulas over $\mathbb Q$, using polynomial-size hitting lists.

The rational-formula claim includes an important domain condition: the formula must have a defined rational-matrix evaluation.

### Outstanding obligation it might address

This could help verify a **proposed bounded algebraic identity** for a finite adjoint, a return term, a source decomposition, or a corrected determinant formula.

For example, after an actual new finite-matrix identity is conjectured, it may be useful to test the difference of its two sides without expanding every intermediate expression.

That is a verification aid. It does not by itself prove:

* the identity for an unbounded sequence of matrix dimensions;
* divisibility at all primes;
* nonvanishing at the fixed original parameters;
* a bound on the primitive denominator.

### Decisive transfer obstruction

Noncommutative formal nonvanishing is weaker than nonvanishing under our scalar specialization.

A complete elementary example is


$$
F(X,Y)=XY-YX.
$$


With


$$
X=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\0&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
Y=\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\1&0\end{pmatrix},
$$


one obtains


$$
F(X,Y)=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&-1\end{pmatrix}\ne0.
$$


Thus the formula is nonzero as a noncommutative polynomial. Nevertheless,


$$
F(x,y)=xy-yx=0
$$


for every pair of scalar numbers $x,y$.

Even in a commutative setting, a nonzero polynomial such as $X-a$ can vanish at the prescribed specialization $X=a$.

Therefore a hitting theorem cannot replace the actual nonvanishing argument.

For formulas with inverses there is a second obstruction: one must prove that every inverse is defined in the original finite object. A legal matrix evaluation elsewhere does not prove that the original scalar denominator is nonzero.

### Primary proof material needed

Before adoption, inspect the portions proving:

* the exact formula model and size convention;
* the hitting property with extension-field coefficients;
* characteristic dependence;
* the rational-formula admissibility/domain lemma;
* bit-height and inverse-domain bounds;
* how formula identities are distinguished from identities in a specialized quotient algebra.

**Classification:** likely reusable finite-verification technique, conditional on manuscript verification. **No direct theorem implication.**

## 5.2 Rank 2: Family076 — energy bounds for finite polynomials

### Claimed method and result

The abstracts claim increasingly strong constructions of real Littlewood polynomials


$$
P(z)=\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}\varepsilon_j z^j,\qquad \varepsilon_j\in\{-1,1\},
$$


including uniform upper and lower bounds on the entire unit circle and, in the strongest version, ultraflatness through every sufficiently large length.

The signs may be chosen separately at each length. That quantifier matters.

The abstracts do not reveal the construction’s proof mechanism. It would be inappropriate to assign it an uninspected discrepancy, probabilistic, or interpolation argument.

### What the energy conclusion would mean

Define the aperiodic correlations


$$
c_k=\sum_{j=0}^{N-1-k}\varepsilon_{j+k}\varepsilon_j,
\qquad 1\le k<N.
$$


On the unit circle,


$$
|P(e^{it})|^2
=N+\sum_{k=1}^{N-1}c_k(e^{ikt}+e^{-ikt}).
$$


Orthogonality of the exponentials gives


$$
\frac1{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}|P(e^{it})|^4\,dt
=N^2+2\sum_{k=1}^{N-1}c_k^2.                 \tag{5.1}
$$



If


$$
\sup_{|z|=1}|P(z)|\le(1+\varepsilon)\sqrt N,
$$


then, using $\frac1{2\pi}\int |P|^2=N$,


$$
\frac1{2\pi}\int |P|^4
\le (1+\varepsilon)^2N^2.
$$


Consequently,


$$
2\sum_{k=1}^{N-1}c_k^2
\le (2\varepsilon+\varepsilon^2)N^2.          \tag{5.2}
$$


This validates the stated kind of merit-factor consequence, assuming the asserted uniform bound.

It does **not** validate the asserted construction.

### Specific possible application

A compatible version of such an estimate might control a complete finite polynomial encoding of an archimedean error or a correlated contact term.

The required transfer would have to identify the polynomial’s coefficients with the **actual complete forced coefficients**, including endpoint corrections, rather than with freely chosen signs.

### Why the present construction is incompatible

Our current objects do not have independent sign freedom. Changing a coefficient may change:

* the forcing equation;
* the physical terminal condition;
* the actual column content;
* the least simultaneous clearer;
* the final gcd;
* the whole error.

Moreover, small correlation energy does not imply large arithmetic gcd, and a positive modulus lower bound for a polynomial does not imply nonvanishing of a signed integral against the complete factorial-logarithmic functional.

A replacement that drops the factorial contribution and keeps only a compact pole kernel would be invalid.

### Primary proof material needed

Inspect:

* the sign-selection construction;
* the uniformity through all lengths;
* the treatment of $z=1$ and $z=-1$;
* the lower-envelope proof;
* the energy calculation and quantitative losses;
* any extension to prescribed coefficients, weighted coefficients, or constrained signs.

**Classification:** analytical inspiration and a possible reusable estimate only after a constrained-coefficient transfer. **No current application.**

## 7. New rigorous arithmetic result for the actual endpoint factorial factor

This section uses no unverified catalogue theorem.

Let


$$
N_n=n!,\qquad
\Lambda_n=\operatorname{lcm}(1,2,\ldots,n),
$$


and for any integer $\zeta$ define


$$
B_n(\zeta)=\frac{N_n}{\gcd(N_n,\zeta)},
$$


with $\gcd(N_n,0)=N_n$.

For the endpoint stream, substitute the **actual** $\zeta=\zeta_j$. No replacement of the projected residual is involved.

### 7.1 An elementary exponential bound for $\Lambda_n$

We first establish


$$
\log\Lambda_n<4n\log2\qquad(n\ge2).          \tag{7.1}
$$



Write


$$
\psi(x)=\sum_{p^a\le x}\log p=\log\Lambda_{\lfloor x\rfloor}.
$$


For an integer $m\ge1$, Legendre’s formula gives


$$
v_p\binom{2m}{m}
=\sum_{a\ge1}
\left(
\left\lfloor\frac{2m}{p^a}\right\rfloor
-2\left\lfloor\frac{m}{p^a}\right\rfloor
\right).
$$


Every summand is nonnegative. If $m<p^a\le2m$, that summand equals $1$. Hence


$$
\psi(2m)-\psi(m)
\le \log\binom{2m}{m}
\le 2m\log2.
$$


Summing at dyadic values yields


$$
\psi(2^k)\le(2^{k+1}-2)\log2.
$$


Choose $k$ minimally with $n\le2^k$. Since $2^k<2n$,


$$
\psi(n)\le\psi(2^k)<4n\log2,
$$


proving (7.1).

### 7.2 Fixed-prime and bounded-multiplicity cancellation cannot pay a factorial

**Proposition 7.1.**  
Fix a finite set of primes $S$ and an integer $K\ge0$. Suppose an integer $\zeta_n$ satisfies


$$
v_p\bigl(\gcd(n!,\zeta_n)\bigr)\le K
\qquad\text{for every }p\notin S.            \tag{7.2}
$$


No bound is imposed on the cancellation at primes in $S$, beyond the automatic bound from $n!$.

Then


$$
\log B_n(\zeta_n)
\ge n\log n
-\left(1+\sum_{p\in S}\frac{\log p}{p-1}
       +4K\log2\right)n+1.                 \tag{7.3}
$$


In particular,


$$
\log B_n(\zeta_n)=n\log n-O_{S,K}(n),
$$


so this cancellation pattern cannot yield a subfactorial factor
$\exp(o(n\log n))$.

**Proof.** Put $d_n=\gcd(n!,\zeta_n)$. Legendre’s formula gives


$$
v_p(n!)=\sum_{a\ge1}\left\lfloor\frac{n}{p^a}\right\rfloor
\le\frac{n}{p-1}.
$$


Using (7.2),


$$
\begin{aligned}
\log d_n
&\le \sum_{p\in S}v_p(n!)\log p
  +K\sum_{p\le n}\log p\\
&\le n\sum_{p\in S}\frac{\log p}{p-1}
  +K\log\Lambda_n\\
&< n\sum_{p\in S}\frac{\log p}{p-1}
  +4Kn\log2.
\end{aligned}
$$


Also,


$$
\log(n!)=\sum_{k=1}^n\log k
\ge\int_1^n\log x\,dx
=n\log n-n+1.
$$


Subtracting proves (7.3). The upper bound
$\log B_n\le\log(n!)=n\log n+O(n)$ completes the stated asymptotic. ∎

**Application at the original indices.** The proposition applies without alteration to


$$
n=15^r\quad\text{or}\quad105^r,\qquad r\ge2,
$$


and to any admissible choice of $j$, provided hypothesis (7.2) holds for the actual $\zeta_j$.

It proves a limitation of a proposed cancellation mechanism. It does **not** assert that the actual $\zeta_j$ satisfies (7.2).

In particular:

* arbitrarily deep cancellation at finitely many fixed primes is insufficient by itself;
* a growing radical, with uniformly bounded multiplicity elsewhere, is insufficient for the factorial payment;
* the required improvement must involve substantial multiplicities at a growing collection of primes, or a different fully paid correlated mechanism.

This statement concerns A3’s factorial factor. It does not transfer conclusions about A1’s radical or A5’s binary return to A3.

### 7.3 An exact all-prime divisibility certificate

For a fixed integer $K\ge1$, define the explicit integer


$$
J_{n,K}
=
\prod_{p\le n}
p^{\max\{v_p(n!)-K\lfloor\log_p n\rfloor,\,0\}}.
$$



**Proposition 7.2.**
For every integer $\zeta$,


$$
J_{n,K}\mid\zeta
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
B_n(\zeta)\mid\Lambda_n^K.                  \tag{7.4}
$$



Consequently, either condition implies


$$
\log B_n(\zeta)<4Kn\log2.
$$



**Proof.** Fix a prime $p\le n$, and put


$$
a=v_p(n!),\qquad
\ell=v_p(\Lambda_n)=\lfloor\log_p n\rfloor,\qquad
c=v_p(\zeta).
$$


For $\zeta=0$, take $c=+\infty$. Then


$$
v_p(B_n(\zeta))=a-\min(a,c).
$$


Thus


$$
v_p(B_n(\zeta))\le K\ell
$$


is equivalent to


$$
c\ge\max(a-K\ell,0).
$$


Taking this equivalence over every prime proves (7.4). The exponential bound follows from (7.1). ∎

For $K=1$, $\Lambda_n\mid n!$, so the certificate simplifies to


$$
J_{n,1}=\frac{n!}{\Lambda_n}.
$$


Therefore


$$
\boxed{
\frac{n!}{\Lambda_n}\mid\zeta_j
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\frac{n!}{\gcd(n!,\zeta_j)}\mid\Lambda_n.
}                                                        \tag{7.5}
$$



This is stronger than merely asking for an unspecified subfactorial bound, but it is concrete and exactly checkable.
