# Paid finite-resolvent contractions and the remaining arithmetic obstruction

## Abstract

The parent proposal can be completed, at the scope of the explicitly retained producer reset, coefficient and endpoint theorem. Its essential point is not a support assertion about the entire actual inverse. Instead, one constructs four integral polynomial multipliers, projects each in the **original finite basis**, and pays the one possible LOW inverse loss at each comparison with the sourced resolvent.

This gives


$$
\boxed{T^TE_{\rm act}^{-1}T\in3^{20}M,\qquad
\mathcal Q=3T^TE_{\rm act}^{-1}T\in3^{21}M.}
$$


The result uses the actual producer representative modulo $3^{20}$ stated conditionally in A4 turn2; it is not a theorem about arbitrary perturbations of the core.

Together with the accepted complete-core and linear bounds, it gives


$$
\boxed{S_{\rm act}\in3^{21}M.}
$$


The first potentially nonzero normalized residual is consequently


$$
\Upsilon_{21}=-S_{\rm act}/3^{21},
\qquad
\boxed{\Upsilon_{21}\equiv-S_c/3^{21}\pmod3.}
$$


Neither this leading core operator nor its distinguished endpoint contraction is evaluated by the present argument.

The new local divisibility does not establish irrationality of $e+\pi$. The remaining tasks include relative determinant/cofactor nonvanishing and control of the **actual** contents, multiplier, least simultaneous clearer, all-prime gcd and whole same-index error.

## 3. A support-preserving integral projection

Define the projection


$$

$$


along $\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb Z_3}F$. Thus every $H\in\mathcal V$ has a unique decomposition

\tag{3.1}
$$


The matrices $C,D$ are integral.

This is not projection by an unexamined inverse. It is the integral basis projection just verified. Its connection to the core inverse follows from complete orthogonality:


$$
G_c(W,H)=E_cC.
\tag{3.2}
$$

\tag{3.3}

Suppose an integral polynomial $p$ has, modulo $3^{20}$, a representative

\operatorname{supp}f\subseteq I_\Omega(w).

Provided $D-s\ge48$, define

\tag{3.4}

Equations (1.1) and (3.3) give

The degree increases by at most $19$:

### What the projection actually does
