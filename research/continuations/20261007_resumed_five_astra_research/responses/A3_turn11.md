# Sparse alignment, birth prescriptions, and chronological partitions: a proof audit for A3, Turn 11

## 1. Scope and conclusions

The objective remains an unconditional determination of whether $e+\pi$ is rational or irrational. **No such determination follows from the present packet.**

This report audits the three newly supplied sections—`prescriptions.tex`, `sparse.tex`, and `partitions.tex`—against the conditional hypotheses isolated in Turn 10. It does not repeat the Turn 10 transfer identity or the variance budget as if they were new results.

The principal conclusions are as follows.

1. **The birth prescriptions establish the required upper first-mass bound.** In particular,
   

$$
M_{\mathrm{birth}}\le W.
$$


   The hard-mean and soft-loss arguments are valid under the stated scale inequalities. The good-mask purge supplies the divisor bound subsequently needed in the difficult intrinsic history comparison.

2. **The geometric chronological-partition argument is sound under explicit, checkable inputs.** It really preserves inherited equivalence relations after intermediate connecting labels die. It simultaneously provides:
   - containment of surviving inherited classes;
   - separation of surviving old cells within each child;
   - intrinsic occupied-cell span;
   - the stronger span at a high-type newborn insertion;
   - padding;
   - equivariance of each realized partition, not just invariance in distribution.

3. **The no-switch train estimate can be proved directly and uniformly in the past history.** I give the complete quantile argument, including atomic phase laws, and an elementary divisor-selection lemma underlying the no-switch signature bound.

4. **The sparse proof is not yet independently closed by this packet.** Its elementary identities, local neutralization factors, padding calculation, and prime-window calculation are correct. Its remaining substantive imported input is a **simultaneous, weighted, fiberwise switching-and-reconstruction estimate**, including the divisor-assembly conclusion. This is more than an individual rotation estimate. Its exact required scope is specified below.

5. **The partition cost proof also imports one indispensable table result:** the single-row product upper fields with their chronological averaging and core-period properties. The supplied Turn 10 report does not contain the full statement and proof of that result. The present proof uses it in a precise way; it cannot be replaced by generic residue independence.

6. No contradictory identity or counterexample was found in the supplied elementary and geometric arguments. This is not a certification of omitted sections. In particular, the additional medium birth deletion and the direct switching lemmas have not been newly checked here.

The new reusable results proved below are:

## 3. Audit of the complete supplied birth prescriptions

## 3.1 The good-mask purge

Let $J_p=\nu_p(d)$, truncated at $\nu_p(L)$, under the unrestricted deficit law. Its tail is


$$
\Pr(J_p\ge j)=p^{-j}.
$$



### Excess prime powers

For $0<\eta<1$,


$$
\mathbb E\,p^{\eta(J_p-1)_+}=1+O_\eta(p^{-2+\eta}),
$$


uniformly in the finite exponent of $p$ in $L$. Consequently


$$
\mathbb E\prod_p p^{\eta(J_p-1)_+}
$$


is bounded uniformly over every finite centre prime support.

Thus


$$
\Pr\!\left(\prod_p p^{(J_p-1)_+}>e^{C_{\rm mask}B}\right)
\ll_\eta e^{-\eta C_{\rm mask}B}.
$$


Choosing $C_{\rm mask}$ so that $\eta C_{\rm mask}>2$ gives the required saving.

In particular, if


$$
p>e^{JB},\qquad J>C_{\rm mask},
$$


then a good mask cannot have $J_p\ge2$. This justifies the squarefree-deficit treatment in the tagged and later ranges. It does **not** say that the centre itself is squarefree.

### Initial-segment divisor count

For every fixed positive integer $M$,


$$
\mathbb E(J_p+1)^M\le 1+\frac{C_M}{p}.
$$


The constant is uniform in the finite exponent boundary. Hence, on a prime segment $\mathcal S$,


$$
\mathbb E\,\tau(d_{\mathcal S})^M
\le
\exp\left(C_M\sum_{p\in\mathcal S}\frac1p\right).
$$



The ordering in the source is important and valid:

1. choose $s'>0$;
2. choose $M$ with $Ms'>3$;
3. choose $s_{\rm low}>0$ with
   

$$
Ms'-C_Ms_{\rm low}>2.
$$



Then


$$
\Pr\bigl(\tau(d_{\mathcal S})>e^{s'B}\bigr)\le e^{-2B}
$$


for every applicable initial segment. Since the divisor count increases with the segment, testing the largest allowed initial segment suffices.

This is exactly the kind of bound used later for


$$
\tau(c_{\le Y'})\le e^{s'B_j}.
$$


It is a statement about a divisor of a retained original good mask, not about a newly sampled adaptive mask.

### Large-prime harmonic cost

Independence gives


$$
\log\mathbb E\exp\left(
2B\sum_{\substack{p\mid d\\p>d_0B}}\frac{(\log p)^{0.3}}p
\right)
\le
\sum_{p>d_0B}
\frac{\exp(2B(\log p)^{0.3}/p)-1}{p}.
$$



The integer-sum comparison in the source is sufficient:

- for $d_0B<p\le B^2$, the contribution is at most
  

$$
O_{d_0}\!\left(\log B\,\exp(C_{d_0}(\log B)^{0.3})\right)=o(B);
$$


- for $p>B^2$, it is
  

$$
O\!\left(B\sum_{n>B^2}\frac{(\log n)^{0.3}}{n^2}\right)=o(B).
$$



There is no unaccounted factor involving the size of the centre’s prime support. The failure probability is therefore


$$
e^{-2B+o(B)}.
$$



### High-type occupancy

If $X$ counts omitted primes in the last dense bin, then


$$
\mathbb E e^{-X}
\le e^{-c_1\kappa Y_d}.
$$


For a sufficiently small fixed occupancy constant,


$$
\Pr(X<c\kappa Y_d)\le e^{-c_2\kappa Y_d}.
$$


Taking $D_0$ after $\kappa$, with $c_2\kappa D_0>2$, gives the required bound when $Y_d>D_0B$.

Since the retained original centre density satisfies


$$
\delta_L\ge e^{-.64B},
$$


all four failure probabilities are $o(\delta_L)$, uniformly as the minimum band tends to infinity.

**Conclusion.** The good-mask purge is valid at its stated unrestricted-law and original-assignment scope.

## 3.3 Tagged reconstruction and model separation

$$

their numerator cross-difference has absolute value at most $2Q^3$. A prime exceeding $2Q^3$ cannot divide a nonzero such difference.

The two size comparisons needed are:

## 3.4 Hard means and soft losses

At one active exclusion, the residue mean is $R_p=1-1/p$. With two distinct exclusions it is at least $1-2/p$. The ratio to the baseline is


$$
\frac{1-2/p}{1-1/p}=1-\frac1{p-1}.
$$



Only distinct small-broad and narrow models can create this second exclusion. Their additional narrow hard primes satisfy


$$
p>e^{c\epsilon_sB}.
$$


On medium type, primes of $V$ are at most $e^{JB}$. The reciprocal sum over this interval is bounded uniformly in $B$, with a bound depending on the already fixed parameters. Consequently the product of the displayed ratios is bounded below by a positive constant.

This proves


$$
c_{\rm hard}R(VP_M/m)
\le \mathbb E(\text{hard weight})
\le R(VP_M/m).
$$



The soft-loss argument correctly works under the normalized hard measure. For a model $n_0/q$,

- primes dividing $q$ cannot divide $qz+n_0$;
- above $H_v^{\epsilon_s}$, the model’s own hard exclusion removes the valuation;
- below that threshold, conditioning on one other exclusion enlarges geometric valuation tails by at most an absolute factor.

Thus


$$
\mathbb E_{\rm hard}\log(qz+n_0,V)
\ll
\sum_{p\le H_v^{\epsilon_s}}\frac{\log p}{p-1}
\ll \epsilon_s\log H_v,
$$


and


$$
\Pr_{\rm hard}\bigl((qz+n_0,V)>H_v^{h_s}\bigr)
\ll \epsilon_s/h_s.
$$



Averaging the failed-hard-mass inequality over anchors **before normalizing** avoids an unnecessary factor $c_{\rm hard}^{-1}$. That is a genuine advantage of the supplied proof.

## 4. Exact finite arithmetic for target history counts

The source-mask sum is compatible with the finite exponent boundaries. The elementary local sum from Turn 10 can be reused; it need not be recalculated experimentally.

There is, however, a useful exact evaluation of the target moment that was only bounded in the source.

### Proposition 4.1 — Exact truncated deficit moment

Let $p$ be prime, $v\ge t\ge0$, and let $J\in\{0,\ldots,v\}$ satisfy


$$
\Pr(J\ge j)=p^{-j}\qquad(1\le j\le v).
$$


Then


$$
\boxed{
\mathbb E\bigl[p^{\min(t,J)}(J+1)\bigr]
=
(t+1)+\left(1-\frac1p\right)\frac{t(t+1)}2
+\sum_{h=1}^{v-t}p^{-h}.
}
\tag{4.1}
$$



#### Proof

For any function $f$ on $\{0,\ldots,v\}$,


$$
\mathbb Ef(J)
=f(0)+\sum_{j=1}^{v}(f(j)-f(j-1))p^{-j}.
$$


Take $f(j)=p^{\min(t,j)}(j+1)$.

For $1\le j\le t$,


$$
(f(j)-f(j-1))p^{-j}
=1+\left(1-\frac1p\right)j.
$$


For $j>t$, it equals $p^{t-j}$. Summation gives (4.1). ∎

For $t\ge1$, this implies the convenient uniform bound


$$
\mathbb E\bigl[p^{\min(t,J)}(J+1)\bigr]\le (t+1)^2.
$$


For $t=0$,


$$
\mathbb E(J+1)=1+\sum_{h=1}^{v}p^{-h}=1+O(1/p).
$$



If the divisor-count factor is absent, the exact formula is simpler:


$$
\mathbb E p^{\min(t,J)}=1+t(1-1/p)\le t+1.
$$



Consequently the source’s estimate can be given the explicit form


$$
\mathbb E\bigl[(T,c)\tau(c_{\le Y'})\bigr]
\le
\tau(T)^2
\prod_{\substack{p\text{ in the source segment}\\p\nmid T}}
\left(1+\frac1{p-1}\right).
$$


The harmonic exponent has an absolute coefficient. It does not acquire a dependence on $N_0$, the realized history, or a later tiny divisor exponent.

### Why the conditional target currency matters

The identity


$$

$$


is the correct baseline currency.

The main term of the target-grid count can retain the target assignment. The unrestricted error incurs

## 5.3 A deterministic no-switch lemma

The source’s no-switch conclusion has an elementary core.

### Lemma 5.1 — Divisor availability

Let $D\ge1$, and let $H\ge2$. Suppose no divisor of $D$ lies in $[H,H^2]$, and no prime divisor of $D$ exceeds $H$. Then $D<H$.

#### Proof

If $D\ge H$, multiply prime factors of $D$, with their multiplicities, until the product first reaches $H$. The previous product is $<H$, and the last prime factor is at most $H$. The resulting divisor lies in $[H,H^2]$, a contradiction. ∎

A rounded dyadic cutoff changes $H$ by at most a fixed factor and causes no difficulty.

Moreover,


$$
\frac{s_0}{(d,s_0)}\mid T,
\qquad
\frac{t_0}{(e,t_0)}\mid U.
$$


Thus, if the tested-divisor system covers the range in Lemma 5.1,


$$
s_0<TH,\qquad t_0<UH.

With $H=\exp(O(D''Y))$, the component-path bounds on $U,T$ give the claimed


$$
s_0,t_0\le \exp(\exp(h_*Y))

once the stated parameter margins and minimum height hold.

This proves the no-switch implication without an appeal to an unnamed divisor-assembly sum. The stronger **successful-switch reconstruction** is a different obligation.

## 6. A complete atomic quantile-train lemma

The quantile argument can be closed without any regularity assumption on the distribution of $a\gamma\bmod1$.

### Lemma 6.1 — Quantile marks and thin determinant arcs

Let $\mu$ be any probability measure on $\mathbb R/\mathbb Z$, possibly atomic. Choose a cut at $0$, include $0$ as a mark, and include generalized inverse quantiles at increments $k/N$. Repeated marks are retained only once.

Then every connected component of the complement of the marks has $\mu$-mass at most $1/N$.

Suppose an admissible set is a union of at most $s$ circular arcs, each of diameter at most $d$. If the endpoint test accepts every point within distance $\rho>d$ of a mark, then the mass of admissible points failing the test is at most


$$
\frac{2s}{N}.
$$


The factor $2$ permits splitting arcs at the cut.

#### Proof

If a complementary component had mass $>1/N$, its cumulative distribution would cross one of the levels $k/N$ inside that component. A generalized inverse quantile would then be a mark there, a contradiction.

An admissible arc meeting a mark consists entirely of points within its diameter $d<\rho$ of that mark. Therefore an arc contributing a failing point is disjoint from all marks and lies in a complementary component. Splitting at $0$ gives at most $2s$ components, each of mass at most $1/N$. ∎

Every atom of mass $>1/N$ is necessarily marked. No absolute continuity or density bound is needed.

### Application to the sparse no-switch branch

For fixed $b,d,e,q$, the determinant condition restricts $a\gamma\bmod1$ to at most $s_0$ arcs. Dividing the determinant tolerance by $s_0$ gives the correct phase radius


$$
O\!\left(V'r\exp(-\tfrac12e^{\sigma_gY})\right).
$$


Their diameters are smaller than


$$
V'r\exp(-.4e^{\sigma_gY})
$$


for sufficiently large $Y$.

Take


$$
N=\left\lceil\exp(\exp(2h_*Y))\right\rceil.
$$


In the no-switch case,


$$
s_0,t_0\le\exp(\exp(h_*Y)).
$$


Thus failure at either endpoint has product-law probability at most


$$

## 9. A deterministic history-safe partition lemma

The following formulation isolates the reusable geometric result.

### Proposition 9.1 — History-safe equivariant partitioning

Consider a finite labelled configuration on the circle, all of one exact width $r$. Assume:

1. old children have fixed cyclic cells, and each surviving old class lies in one old cell;
2. each occupied old cell has actual arc length $<1/4$;
3. old-child boundary padding is at least $g_D>0$;
4. each positive-length inherited class is connected by historical edges whose closed connecting segments belong to a fixed stored danger family;
5. that stored family is not reduced when labels die, and every connected component of its union has length $<1/4$;
6. all objects are equivariant under translation by a current period $T$;
7. after preliminary history tests, every required intrinsic segment of length $s_x$, and every required old-child gap segment of length $g_D$, has at least half its length outside all hulls relevant to that test;
8. a microinterval length $a$ divides $T$, and each tested length $s$ satisfies $s+a<T$.

Choose one uniform point in each microinterval of a period and replicate periodically. Skip points in inherited closed hulls. Then, after the stated pointwise and old-cell purges:

- surviving inherited classes are not split;
- a new cell contains surviving points from at most one old cell of each child;
- each surviving point $x$ lies in a cell of length at most $2s_x$;
- every surviving point is padded by the chosen distance $\delta$;
- the realized partition and all discards are equivariant.

Conditional on the preliminary configuration, the expected discarded mass is bounded by the sum of:



$$
\frac{2\delta}{a}\,M^0
$$


for raw-line padding,



$$
\sum_x 2e^{-s_x/(2a)}\,m_x^0
$$


for intrinsic tests, and



$$
\sum_C 2e^{-g_{D(C)}/(2a)}\,M_C^0
$$


for old-cell gap tests.

#### Proof

**Inherited hulls.**  
A historical chain remains a connected geometric chain even after intermediate labels disappear, because its stored segments remain. It lies in one connected component $K$ of the stored danger union.

The surviving class also lies in one short old cell $C$. Since the lengths of $C$ and $K$ sum to less than $1$, choose a cut outside $C\cup K$. In the resulting real interval, the segment between the extreme surviving positions lies in both $C$ and $K$. It is the inherited short hull.

This argument does not require the historical chain to stay inside the current old cell. It uses the permanent stored cover and the shortness of both sets.

**Probability of finding a line.**  
Let $A$ be the available subset of a tested segment of length $s$. Distinct periodic copies of one microinterval cannot both meet the segment because $s+a<T$. Hence


$$
\Pr(\text{no candidate in }A)
=
\prod_J\left(1-\frac{|A\cap J|}{a}\right)
\le e^{-|A|/a}
\le e^{-s/(2a)}.
$$


Arbitrary fragmentation of $A$ causes no loss.

**Intrinsic span.**  
An unskipped line in each directional segment of length $s_x$ places the containing cell between two lines at total distance at most $2s_x$.

**Old-child separation.**  
Consecutive preliminary occupied old cells have an intervening gap of length at least $2g_D$. A successful directional test supplies an unskipped line in the first $g_D$ portion of that gap. These lines are chosen from the fixed preliminary gaps and are never removed. Thus separation persists even if some intermediate cells subsequently become empty.

**No splitting.**  
No retained boundary lies in an inherited closed hull. Removing labels restricts an equivalence class but does not create new boundaries inside its hull.

**Mass accounting.**  
For each old cell,


$$
\text{later cell-purge loss}\le \mathbf1_{E_C}M_C^0.
$$


Taking expectation conditional on the preliminary configuration gives


$$
\mathbb E(\text{loss in }C)\le \Pr(E_C)M_C^0.
$$


There is no conditioning on outcomes of other tests and therefore no illicit independence assumption.

Raw-line padding follows from the expected number of candidate lines in a length-$2\delta$ neighborhood, which is $2\delta/a$.

**Equivariance.**  
The candidate configuration is replicated with period $T$, and all hull, distance, and cell predicates commute with that translation. Therefore every realized output is equivariant. ∎

A finite realization with loss at most twice the displayed conditional bound exists by Markov’s inequality. Applying this selection chronologically, or one height at a time, gives a deterministic construction with the same summable order of loss. No assertion about every jitter realization is needed.
