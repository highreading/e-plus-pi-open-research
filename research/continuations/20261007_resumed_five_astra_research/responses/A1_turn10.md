# The complete second core jet and an evaluated precision-$28$ radical observation

## Abstract

The complete second corrected-column jet can be recovered from the finite LOW/HIGH equations. With the physical HIGH endpoint still $Y_m$, the result is


$$
\boxed{

-y^{H/3+i}+\delta_{i,\nu-1}q_{d+1}(y)\pmod3,
}
$$


where $q_b$ is the actual monic quotient in


$$
y^b=r_b+x^Dq_b,\qquad \deg r_b<D.
$$


The term $-y^{H/3+i}$ is a bulk term. It comes from the complete next HIGH force, not from the terminal return. The term $q_{d+1}$ comes from the finite terminal inverse and its LOW feedback.

These actual jets have substantially narrower support than the previously available generic second-jet allowance. Their entire total-order-two Gram contribution vanishes modulo $3^{28}$. This also proves, for the actual core columns, the proposed stationary refinement


$$
\boxed{

\qquad

T_i=x^D(y^i-3\delta_{i,\nu-1}q_d).

The proof does not infer this bound from precision-$18$ support.

$$

Consequently, on adjacent endpoint-annihilating sums, the complete normalized core observation is

=[y^{C-u-v}](y+1)^2(y-1)^{N_0},

## 3. Paying the total-order-two contribution

We now show that the actual second jet is harmless at precision $28$, although the generic second-jet support estimate was not strong enough to show this.

### Lemma 3.1 — Two complete moment vanishings

For every integral $P$ with $\deg P\le2D$,


$$
\mathcal M((y+1)x^HP)\in3^{26}\mathbb Z_3,
\tag{3.1}
$$


and


$$
\boxed{
\mathcal M((y+1)x^Hy^{H/3}P)\in3^{26}\mathbb Z_3.
}
\tag{3.2}
$$



#### Proof

The unshifted assertion follows from the established depth-$26$ low-degree moment evaluation.

For the shifted assertion, the top extraction is absent because


$$
\frac{4H}{3}+2D<\frac{3H-1}{2}
$$


for sufficiently large original tuples.

At a lower layer $t$, $0\le t\le24$, the functional weight is $3^{t+1}$. The required support of $x^H$ is therefore on


$$
H/3^{24-t}.
$$


Combining this with the pole grid $H/3^t$ and the shift $H/3$, the common grid spacing is at least


$$
H/3^{24}=3\Omega.
$$


Every pole is on its odd half-grid. But


$$
2D<\frac{3\Omega-1}{2}
$$


throughout the adjacent window, for sufficiently large tuples. Thus every extraction vanishes. Layers $t\ge25$ already have weight divisible by $3^{26}$. The factorial term vanishes at the same precision. ∎

### 3.1 Application to every order-two term

The order-two terms in the corrected-column Gram product are


$$
\phi_{i,2}y^j+y^i\phi_{j,2}
+\delta_i\delta_jq_d^2.
$$


By (2.9), these are sums of:

* $y^{H/3}$ times polynomials of the original low degree;
* local terms involving $q_{d+1}$;
* the terminal square $q_d^2$.

After multiplication by $x^D(\beta+3y)$, the local polynomial in each case has degree at most $2D$. Lemma 3.1 therefore gives


$$
\boxed{
\text{The complete total-order-two contribution is }0\pmod{3^{28}}.
}
\tag{3.3}
$$



This includes the bulk second jet and the terminal terms.

### 3.2 Higher digit orders

The established digitwise precision-$20$ representatives may be chosen with the jets (2.8), while keeping the supplied nested support bounds. For total order $s\ge3$, the width is


$$
\left(2+\frac s2\right)D+1.
$$


At $s=3$, the relevant common grid is $3\Omega$, and


$$
\frac72D+1<\frac{3\Omega-1}{2}.
$$


The later grids grow faster than the width until the retained $\Omega_{20}$-grid controls the estimate. Hence the supplied complete half-grid argument gives


$$
\boxed{
\text{All total orders }s\ge3\text{ vanish modulo }3^{28}.
}
\tag{3.4}
$$



This argument remains within the available precision-$20$ support theorem; it does not extend that theorem to arbitrary precision.

### Lemma 5.1 — Complete low-degree moment modulo $3^{28}$

For every integral polynomial $P$ with $\deg P\le2D$,


$$
\boxed{
\mathcal M((y+1)x^HP)
\equiv
3^{26}\bigl(4[y^c]P+3[y^{c_0}]P\bigr)
\pmod{3^{28}}.
}
\tag{5.1}
$$



#### Proof

The top extraction is absent by degree; the factorial term vanishes.

At layer $t=0$, only the denominator $H$ occurs. Modulo $3^{27}$, the relevant coefficients of $x^H$ lie on the $\lambda$-grid. Since $\deg P<5\lambda/2$, only the coefficients $P_{c_0}$ and $P_c$ can occur.

For a signed coefficient $C_H(k)=[y^k](y-1)^H$, the exact identity


$$
C_H(k)=\frac Hk\prod_{s=1}^{k-1}\left(1-\frac Hs\right)
\tag{5.2}
$$


holds because $H$ is odd.

At


$$
k=\frac{H-\Omega}{2},
$$


division by $3^{25}$ gives, modulo $9$,


$$
\frac{H/k}{3^{25}}\equiv-2,
\qquad
\prod_{s=1}^{k-1}\left(1-\frac Hs\right)\equiv1-3=-2.
$$


Only $s=H/3$ contributes a nontrivial factor modulo $9$. Hence


$$
C_H(k)/3^{25}\equiv4\pmod9.
$$


At $k=(H-\lambda)/2$, similarly,


$$
C_H(k)/3^{26}\equiv1\pmod3.
$$


After the layer weight $3$, these give the two terms in (5.1).

At layer $t=1$, only $\alpha=1,5$ can produce an interior coefficient of $x^H$. Both normalized coefficients are $1$ modulo $3$; their units satisfy


$$
1+5^{-1}=0\pmod3.
$$


Thus this entire layer cancels modulo $3^{28}$.

For $2\le t\le24$, the common grid spacing is at least $9\lambda$. Its half-spacing exceeds $2D$, so all extractions vanish.

At $t=25$, only the four coefficients


$$
0,\ H/3,\ 2H/3,\ H
$$


of $x^H\bmod9$ can occur. The associated normalized denominator units are all $1\bmod9$, and all four denominators lie inside the original cutoff. Their coefficients sum to zero modulo $9$, so this layer cancels.

At $t=26$, the bottom and upper-end extractions for $P_{c_0}$ have opposite signs and equal units modulo $3$. They cancel. The potential index $c$ belongs to a denominator divisible by an additional $3$, so it is not an $\alpha$ prime to $3$ in this layer. Higher layers vanish by their weights. ∎

The coefficient $4$ in (5.1) is a genuine next-digit contribution. It cannot be replaced by $1$ before the complete normalized restriction has been assembled.
