# A2 Turn 5 — An all-depth boundary decomposition, a corrected source interface, and the remaining content-transfer obstruction

## Abstract

The global objective remains unresolved: the supplied work does not prove either rationality or irrationality of $e+\pi$.

This report closes the recovered fixed-digit tail issue without relying on the unreviewed strength of the proposed extension $Le_i\in29^3$. It also establishes an all-depth result that changes the formulation of the growing obligation.

The principal conclusions are:

1. **The actual normalized tail is divisible by $29$.** Using the retained exact finite inverse modulo $29$, I prove directly that
   

$$
Le_i\equiv0\pmod{29}\qquad(0\le i\le434).
$$


   Together with the recovered factorial divisibility of the actual first force, this proves
   

$$
V=L\bar w\in29\mathbb Z_{29}^{b+1}.
$$


   Thus the displayed tail feedback vanishes on every content stratum. This does not certify the stronger, previously proposed $29^3$-bound through row $231$, but it independently proves the consequence for which that extension was needed.

2. **The physical terminal admits an exact, $29$-integral orthogonal separation at every depth.** There is an explicit integer vector $t$, with $t_b=1$, such that
   

$$
t^TZ_w=0,\qquad t^TY=W_b,
$$


   and
   

$$
t^Tt\equiv8\pmod{29}.
$$


   Consequently the projection
   

$$
P=I-\frac{tt^T}{t^Tt}
$$


   is $29$-integral and
   

$$
\Delta(\bar f)
   =\bar Z^T(6PY-29\bar Z)
$$


   exactly. No terminal contribution has been discarded: its normal component is separated by an exact identity.

3. **The proposed whole-column content-transfer law has an additional necessary terminal condition.**
   

$$
v_{29}(Y)\ge c+3
$$


   requires
   

$$
v_{29}(W_b)\ge c+3.
$$


   The retained information gives only $v_{29}(W_b)\ge c+2$. No original-family counterexample to the stronger condition is established here. The exact decomposition shows, however, that a *tangential* content-transfer law for $PY$ is the more relevant target for the mixed contraction.

4. **An explicit non-Gram sufficient obligation is obtained.** A unitriangular integer transformation reduces the desired vector congruence
   

$$
6PY-29\bar Z\in29^K\mathbb Z_{29}^{b+1},
   \qquad K=c+4+\nu,
$$


   to original contact-coordinate congruences involving the complete finite inverse and a boundary sequence generated by a first-order recurrence. These congruences are sufficient for the required whole-defect alignment. They are not proved in this report.

5. **The archived actual-source Cartier interface extends to every precision.** The extension explicitly handles denominator exponents $h\ge29$, where the earlier formula requiring $h<29$ cannot simply be reused. It gives paid, finite source directions at arbitrary precision and identifies the actual original-word invariants evaluating their coefficients. It does not establish the missing relation between those coefficients and the complete second response.

The genuinely growing content-transfer or relative-source theorem remains open. So does the all-prime estimate for the actual primitive denominator.

Summing the latter binomial by the finite hockey-stick identity yields


$$
\theta^{(i)}_j

## 3. An exact all-depth treatment of the physical terminal

The next result uses the original reconstruction matrix and its full terminal row. It does not use a fixed carry guard.

Define


$$
\omega_j=j!W_j=\frac{N!}{(N-j)!},
$$


and the integer vector


$$
\boxed{
t_j=\frac{\omega_b}{\omega_j}
=\frac{(N-j)!}{(N-b)!},
\qquad 0\le j\le b.
}
\tag{3.1}
$$


In particular, $t_b=1$.

### Proposition 3.1 — Exact finite normal vector

One has


$$
t^T\mathcal R=0.
\tag{3.2}
$$


Consequently,


$$
\boxed{
t^TZ_w=0,\qquad t^TY=W_b.
}
\tag{3.3}
$$



#### Proof

The coefficient of $\theta_k$ in $t^T\mathcal R\theta$ is


$$
-t_kW_k+t_{k+1}(k+1)W_{k+1}.
$$


But


$$
(k+1)W_{k+1}=(N-k)W_k,
\qquad
t_k=(N-k)t_{k+1}.
$$


The coefficient is therefore zero.

The second identity in (3.3) uses the physical terminal exactly:


$$
t^TY=t^T\mathcal R\psi+W_bt_b=W_b.
$$


∎

An immediate all-prime consequence is that whenever the physical column is integral at a prime $\ell$,


$$
v_\ell(Y)\le v_\ell(W_b).
\tag{3.4}
$$


No assertion about the final gcd $g_B$ follows merely from this content bound.

### 3.1 The normal vector has unit norm at $29$

Put


$$
\tau=t^Tt.
$$


For $r=b-j$,


$$
t_{b-r}=(N-b+1)(N-b+2)\cdots(N-b+r).
\tag{3.5}

\tag{3.6}

Therefore $t_{b-r}\equiv(5)^{\overline r}\pmod{29}$, and it vanishes modulo $29$ for every $r\ge25$.

t_{b-r}^2&
1&25&1&20&4&5&7&6&23&1&22&20&16

$$
\boxed{\tau\equiv8\pmod{29}.}
\tag{3.7}

This small exact arithmetic evaluation has an all-depth consequence: division by $\tau$ is paid at $29$ at every precision.

### Theorem 3.2 — All-depth orthogonal separation

Define


$$
P=I-\frac{tt^T}{\tau},
\qquad
Y^\parallel=PY.
\tag{3.8}
$$


Then:

1. $P$ is a $p$-integral orthogonal projection;
2. $PZ_w=Z_w$;
3.
   

$$
Y=Y^\parallel+\frac{W_b}{\tau}t;
   \tag{3.9}
$$


4.
   

$$
\boxed{
   v_p(Y)=\min\{v_p(Y^\parallel),v_p(W_b)\};
   }
   \tag{3.10}
$$


5. for the normalized first column $\bar Z=Z_w/C_n$,
   

$$
\boxed{
   \Delta(\bar f)
   =6\bar Z^TY^\parallel-p\,\bar Z^T\bar Z
   =\bar Z^T(6Y^\parallel-p\bar Z).
   }
   \tag{3.11}
$$



#### Proof

Equations (3.7)–(3.8) show that $P$ is integral and


$$
P^2=P,\qquad P^T=P,\qquad Pt=0.
$$


Equation (3.3) gives $PZ_w=Z_w$ and (3.9).

If $Y\in p^a$, then both integral projections $PY$ and $(I-P)Y$ are in $p^a$. Conversely, if both projected components are in $p^a$, so is their sum. Since $t_b=1$ and $\tau$ is a unit,


$$
v_p\!\left(\frac{W_b}{\tau}t\right)=v_p(W_b).

This proves (3.10).

Finally, $\bar Z^Tt=0$, so the normal component contributes exactly zero to the mixed contraction. The norm term remains unchanged. ∎

This is not a boundary omission. The whole terminal $W_be_b$, including its interaction with the reconstructed contact column, is present in (3.9)–(3.11).

Also, division by $\tau$ is **only a local analytic operation** here. It does not replace the actual integer columns, their contents, their least simultaneous clearer, or their all-prime primitive normalization.

\begin{cases}

## 5. An explicit finite-coordinate obligation, not an unevaluated Gram pairing

The projection above can be combined with a unitriangular integer transformation of the original finite reconstruction.

For $0\le k\le j<b$, define


$$
C_{jk}
=\frac{(N-k)!}{(N-j)!}.
\tag{5.1}
$$


Thus $C_{jj}=1$, and $C$ is an integer lower-unitriangular matrix.

Define a $(b+1)$-dimensional integer transformation $E$ by


$$
(Ev)_j=\sum_{k=0}^jC_{jk}v_k\quad(0\le j<b),
\qquad
(Ev)_b=t^Tv.
\tag{5.2}
$$


Its determinant is $1$, so $E$ and $E^{-1}$ are integral at every prime.

### Proposition 5.1 — Exact finite diagonalization

For every contact vector $\theta$,


$$
\boxed{
E\mathcal R\theta
=
(-W_0\theta_0,\ldots,-W_{b-1}\theta_{b-1},0)^T.
}
\tag{5.3}
$$



#### Proof

Set $u_j=W_j\theta_j$. Since


$$
jW_j=(N-j+1)W_{j-1},
$$


the contact reconstruction is


$$
(\mathcal R\theta)_j
=(N-j+1)u_{j-1}-u_j.
$$


Solving this triangular recurrence gives


$$
u_j=-\sum_{k=0}^jC_{jk}(\mathcal R\theta)_k.
$$


The last coordinate is Proposition 3.1. ∎

In particular, the exact physical content satisfies


$$
v_p(Z_w)=\min_{0\le j<b}v_p(W_j\theta_j),
\tag{5.4}
$$


and


$$
v_p(Y)
=
\min\!\left\{
v_p(W_b),\
\min_{0\le j<b}v_p(W_j\psi_j)
\right\}.
\tag{5.5}
$$


These are exact content identities for the physical columns, not evaluations of their values.

Define

It is unnecessary to leave this as an unevaluated sum: it is the integer sequence


$$
\boxed{

\quad(0\le j<b),

\tag{5.6}

with

These recurrences use the exact original finite endpoint.

\tag{5.8}

Hence the tangential content-transfer law (4.4) is equivalent to the explicit congruences


$$
\boxed{

No division by $W_j$ has been introduced.

For a digit $d\in\{0,\ldots,p-1\}$, let


$$
\Lambda_{d,0}\!\left(\sum a_{a,b}t^az^b\right)
=

### Theorem 6.1 — Paid Cartier closure at arbitrary precision

Fix $r\ge1$. Consider states


$$
S(t,z)
=
\sum_{h=1}^r
p^{h-1}\frac{P_h(t,z)}{Q(t,z)^h}
\pmod{p^r},
\tag{6.3}
$$


with


$$
\deg_tP_h\le h,\qquad
\operatorname{supp}_zP_h\subset[-h,h].
\tag{6.4}
$$


This module is closed under every $\Lambda_{d,0}$.

More explicitly, put


$$
m=\left\lceil\frac hp\right\rceil.
$$


Then


$$
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
\Lambda_{d,0}
\left(p^{h-1}\frac{P_h}{Q^h}\right)
\equiv
\sum_{k=0}^{r-h}
&(-1)^k
\binom{m+k-1}{k}
p^{h-1+k}\\
&\cdot
\frac{
\Lambda_{d,0}\!\left(
P_hQ^{pm-h}V_Q^k
\right)
}{Q^{m+k}}
\pmod{p^r}.
\end{aligned}
}
\tag{6.5}
$$



#### Proof

Since $pm-h\ge0$,


$$
\frac1{Q^h}
=
\frac{Q^{pm-h}}{(Q^p)^m}
=
\frac{Q^{pm-h}}
     {(Q(t^p,z^p)+pV_Q)^m}.
$$


The integral binomial expansion gives


$$
(1+X)^{-m}
=
\sum_{k\ge0}(-1)^k\binom{m+k-1}{k}X^k.
$$


After multiplication by $p^{h-1}$, terms with $k>r-h$ vanish modulo $p^r$. Applying Cartier and extracting the factors in $t^p,z^p$ gives (6.5).

The numerator before Cartier has $t$-degree at most


$$
h+(pm-h)+pk=p(m+k),
$$


and $z$-support inside


$$
[-p(m+k),p(m+k)].
$$


After Cartier, it satisfies (6.4) for denominator exponent $m+k$.

Finally,


$$
m+k\le h+k\le r,
$$


and


$$
h-1+k\ge m+k-1.
$$


Thus every output coefficient carries the required power of $p$. ∎

For $h<p$, $m=1$, and this specializes to the archived formula. For $h\ge p$, the factor $Q^{pm-h}$ and the binomial coefficient in (6.5) are essential.

### 6.1 Initialization with arbitrary growing source indices

$$


Therefore


$$

}
\tag{6.6}
$$


initial steps suffice to enter the bounded module.

The state dimension is

\boxed{

=\frac{r(4r^2+15r+17)}6.
}
\tag{6.7}
$$



After all actual base-$p$ digits of $n$ have been read, the accepting functional is


$$

\sum_{h=1}^r p^{h-1}[z^0]P_h(0,z)\pmod{p^r}.
\tag{6.8}
$$



This evaluates the actual $J_i(n)$ after the **whole actual word** is supplied. A low original prefix gives only a functional on the unread suffix.

### 6.2 Exactly which first-source directions are paid

For a requested source precision $p^a$, define


$$
\mu_i(n)
=
v_p\!\left(\frac{(n+i)!}{n!}\right)
=
\sum_{r\ge1}
\left(
\left\lfloor\frac{n+i}{p^r}\right\rfloor
-\left\lfloor\frac n{p^r}\right\rfloor
\right).
\tag{6.9}
$$


Then


$$
\mu_i(n)\ge v_p(i!).
$$



The directions with $\mu_i(n)\ge a$ vanish modulo $p^a$. For the remaining directions, put


$$
u_i(n)=p^{-\mu_i(n)}\frac{(n+i)!}{n!}\in\mathbb Z_p^\times.
$$


Their actual normalized coefficients are


$$
\boxed{
\bar f_i
=
p^{\mu_i(n)}u_i(n)\frac{J_i(n)}{J_0(n)}
\pmod{p^a}.
}
\tag{6.10}
$$


Thus $J_i(n)$ is required only modulo


$$
p^{a-\mu_i(n)}.
$$



Every division in (6.10) is paid:

- the power of $p$ is removed using its exact valuation;
- $J_0(n)=C_n$ is a retained unit.

The surviving directions are supported within


$$
0\le i<\min(b,pa).
\tag{6.11}
$$


This is a sufficient factorial cutoff, not a claim that every listed coefficient is nonzero.

Equations (6.5), (6.8), and (6.10) identify the original-word invariant evaluating each paid source coefficient. They do not assign arbitrary values to the unread suffix.
