## Operation and exact conditional bridge

Let F(z)=exp(z)+4 arctan(z), on its real branch, with F(0)=1 and F(1)=S. Since F'>0 on the real axis, the operation

    T(z)=F^(-1)(1+S-F(z))

## Exact jet field, retaining both actual laws

Put u(z)=T(z)-1 near z=0. The arctangent addition formula, with the branch fixed at the origin, gives

    F(1+u)-S=e(exp(u)-1)+4 arctan(u/(2+u)).

Consequently u is the local solution of

    e(exp(u)-1)+4 arctan(u/(2+u))=1-F(z), u(0)=0.

The right side has rational Taylor coefficients. The left side has coefficients in Q(e) and invertible linear term e+2. Formal inversion therefore gives every Taylor coefficient of T in Q(e). The first derivative is exactly

    T'(0)=-5/(e+2).

It is transcendental: if it were algebraic, then e=-5/T'(0)-2 would be algebraic, contrary to the known transcendence of e. Moreover this derivative recovers e, so the field generated by ALL Taylor coefficients of T at 0 is exactly Q(e), not merely a subfield.

At the other endpoint put z=1+t and use

    F(T(1+t))-1=-e(exp(t)-1)-4 arctan(t/(2+t)).

The inverse of F(z)-1 at 0 has rational coefficients and nonzero linear term 5, so all endpoint-1 jet coefficients also belong to Q(e). Their first derivative is -(e+2)/5 and again generates that field.

These statements are UNCONDITIONAL. The constant pi cancels from the local endpoint equations. Thus assuming S rational does not reduce either endpoint jet field: both are already exactly Q(e). The operation retains both exact exponential and arctangent laws throughout; no deformation or altered endpoint density is used.
