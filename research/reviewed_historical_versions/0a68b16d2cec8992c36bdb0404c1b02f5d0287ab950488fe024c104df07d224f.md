# Trailing pairing blocks and partial primitive-denominator survival

Author: Main. Status: conditional author proof; independent review pending. This stage extends Stage 11 from the single deepest Smith line to one trailing filtered subspace. It proves no occurrence theorem for the actual residue condition and no irrationality conclusion.

## 1. Actual normalization and inherited local reduction

Use the canonical diagonal family m=k, k>=2, from DIAGONAL_LOCAL_CONTENT_REDUCTION_STAGE10.md and SMITH_PIVOT_ROOTS_AND_CENTERED_INVARIANT_STAGE11.md. With h=y+1 and indices 0<=i,j<k,

    M_ij=mu(h^(i+j)),       G_ij=mu(h^(k+i+j)),
    J_ij=nu_k(h^(i+j)),     B_ij=R_k(h^(i+j)),
    D_ij=R_k(h^(k+i+j)),    C=M-J,
    beta(T)=det[[C,G],[B+TJ,D]].

R_k is the complete rational remainder, retaining both endpoints. In particular its high-degree formula is not substituted into B. The complete physical clearer is delta, I_j=delta beta_j are integers, g=gcd(I_0,...,I_k)>0, and P is the primitive polynomial oriented to have positive leading coefficient

    A=|I_k|/g.

Fix a prime p>6k dividing det G=F*_k. All blocks are p-integral, and delta and det J are p-units for the specified canonical clearer. Set

    a=v_p(det G), d=v_p(g), v_p(A)=a-d.

Stage 10 gives a p-unit congruence splitting G into a unit block and an r-dimensional nonunit symmetric block E. After a further p-unit congruence, since p is odd,

    E=diag(p^alpha_1 epsilon_1,...,p^alpha_r epsilon_r),
    1<=alpha_1<=...<=alpha_r,
    H_j=sum_(l=1)^j alpha_l, H_0=0, H_r=a,

with every epsilon_l a unit. All congruences are also applied to the effective matrix. For the reversed polynomial one has exactly

    chi(z)=z^k beta(1/z)=u(z)det[E-zQ(z)],

where u is a unit of Z_p[[z]] and Q has p-integral formal coefficients. If W is the complete congruence matrix and Z its final r columns, the constant term before the final diagonal congruence is

    Q(0)=Z^T C J^(-1) D Z.

No symmetry of this effective matrix is assumed. The complete low block B remains present in the higher coefficients of Q. The earlier local reduction has received a limited review; its historical moment identities remain inherited inputs.

## 2. One trailing-block theorem

Choose 0<=i<r, with alpha_i<alpha_(i+1) when i>0. Define

    s=r-i,
    b=alpha_(i+1),
    c=alpha_i if i>0, and c=0 if i=0.

Thus b>c. In the diagonal coordinates assume that the trailing principal block

    Q(0)[{i+1,...,r},{i+1,...,r}]

is nonsingular modulo p. No condition is imposed on the determinant of the full Q(0) unless i=0.

Then the following statements hold for the actual complete coefficients and primitive polynomial:

1. v_p(I_(k-s))=H_i.
2. Exactly s roots of beta, counted with algebraic multiplicity, have denominator valuation at least b. Their denominator valuations sum to a-H_i. Every remaining root has denominator valuation at most c. For a nonzero root alpha, denominator valuation means max(0,-v_p(alpha)); for a zero root it means zero.
3. d<=H_i and v_p(A)>=a-H_i.
4. Every root multiplicity is at most max(s,k-s). In particular 0<s<k excludes a pure linear power. If k is even and s=k/2, no root has multiplicity greater than k/2.

The root statements concern an algebraic closure of Q_p. They do not assert that any root is rational over Q or simple within either group.

## 3. Exact coefficient survival

Write f(z)=det[E-zQ(z)] and f_j=[z^j]f. The minor argument of Stage 11 gives

    v_p(f_j)>=H_(r-j), 0<=j<=r.

A degree-j contribution selects at most j columns from zQ, leaving a minor of E of size at least r-j. This argument applies even though Q is a formal series.

In degree s=r-i, terms selecting fewer than s Q columns have valuation strictly greater than H_i, since they leave more than i positive-depth pivots. Terms selecting exactly s columns use only Q(0). The unique subset of i diagonal E entries with minimum valuation is {1,...,i}, by the strict gap. For i=0 this is the unique empty subset. Consequently

    p^(-H_i) f_s
      =(-1)^s (product_(l=1)^i epsilon_l)
        det Q(0)[{i+1,...,r},{i+1,...,r}] modulo p.

This is nonzero by hypothesis, so v_p(f_s)=H_i.

For j<s, v_p(f_j)>=H_(r-j)>H_i. Therefore multiplication by u(z) cannot cancel the surviving degree-s coefficient: all contributions from earlier f_j have greater valuation, and u(0) is a unit. Multiplication by delta is also by a p-unit. This proves

    v_p([z^s]chi)=v_p(I_(k-s))=H_i.

The complete coefficient ideal is used throughout; no residual block content replaces g.

## 4. Newton-polygon separation and root-weight identity

Write c_j=[z^j]chi, treating absent coefficients as zero with valuation infinity. The same minor bounds survive multiplication by u:

    v_p(c_j)>=H_(r-j), 0<=j<=r.

For j<s,

    H_(r-j)>=H_i+(s-j)b.

For s<j<=r,

    H_(r-j)>=H_i-(j-s)c,

because the j-s omitted shallow depths are at most c. For j>r the latter inequality follows from integrality: H_i<=ic and j-s>i, with the i=0 case immediate. Together with the exact coefficient from Section 3 these bounds give

    v_p(c_s)=H_i,
    v_p(c_j)>=H_i+(s-j)b for j<s,
    v_p(c_j)>=H_i-(j-s)c for j>s.

The two affine bounds form a convex broken line with slopes -b and -c, since b>c. It lies below every coefficient point and meets the coefficient point at (s,H_i). Thus (s,H_i) is a vertex of the Newton polygon of chi. Every slope to its left is at most -b, and every slope to its right is at least -c.

Since v_p(c_0)=a, the first s reciprocal roots have valuations at least b and total valuation a-H_i. All other reciprocal roots have valuation at most c. Reciprocation proves the stated separation and weight identity for beta. If beta has zero roots, chi has degree less than k; those zero roots belong to the remaining group and satisfy its denominator bound.

Also d<=v_p(I_(k-s))=H_i, proving v_p(A)>=a-H_i. A repeated root cannot occupy both disjoint valuation groups, so its multiplicity is at most the size of its group. This proves the multiplicity assertions.

This argument does not require d=0. Further negative-valuation roots can occur in the remaining group when c>0, and they account for any extra survival beyond a-H_i.

## 5. Intrinsic filtered-subspace formulation

In the original coordinates let

    L_b={x in Z_p^k : Gx belongs to p^b Z_p^k},
    V_b=image of L_b in F_p^k.

In diagonal Smith coordinates, a coordinate of depth alpha<b must be divisible by p^(b-alpha), while coordinates of depth at least b are unrestricted. Therefore V_b has dimension s and is the span of precisely the trailing directions selected above. The unit directions of G contribute nothing to V_b.

Define the actual bilinear form modulo p by

    H(x,y)=x^T C J^(-1) D y.

It is well-defined because every entry is p-integral and J has unit determinant. The hypothesis of Section 2 is exactly nonsingularity of H restricted to V_b. Changing a basis of V_b changes the matrix by a congruence, multiplying its determinant by the square of a nonzero determinant. Changing lifts does not alter its residue. Thus the condition is intrinsic to the actual filtered subspace, despite possible nonsymmetry of H.

The case s=1 recovers the unique deepest-line condition of Stage 11. The case i=0 recovers full constant-pairing nondegeneracy. Intermediate strict gaps give partial survival even when the full pairing is singular.

## 6. Conditional global consequence and approximation interface

For each prime p>6k dividing F*_k, consider all strict depth breaks passing the restricted-pairing condition. Define w_p to be the largest value a-H_i among those breaks, or zero if none passes. Count each prime once. Then

    log A >= sum_(p>6k) w_p log p.

Only primes dividing F*_k contribute, so this is a finite sum. The inequality omits, rather than estimates, all small-prime contributions. No assumption of independence between primes or nested residue tests is used.

For any k for which beta(S)!=0, with S=e+pi, put

    R_k=|beta(S)/beta_k|>0.

Here R_k denotes a scalar response ratio, not the rational functional used in the block definitions. The exact normalization gives

    |P(S)|=A R_k.

Writing W_k=sum w_p log p therefore yields

    log|P(S)| >= W_k+log R_k.

For a fixed positive integer v, a necessary condition for this degree to satisfy |P(S)|<v^(-k) is

    W_k < -k log v-log R_k.

This is a conditional obstruction to successful primitive small values, not a construction of them. It preserves the true gcd and the actual response. The analytic nonvanishing range and response estimates must be supplied by their separate proved hypotheses when this interface is applied.

## 7. Remaining gaps and evidence status

No infinite family of actual pairs (k,p) satisfying a useful trailing-block condition is proved here. No sufficient total lower bound on W_k is established. Conversely, failure of a tested constant pairing does not prove large common content: higher coefficients of Q can still survive. Small primes require their separate clearer and endpoint accounting.

This stage proves a conditional local and global interface. It does not establish the favorable content upper bound on A needed for small values, nor exclude all degrees from producing such values. S=e+pi remains unresolved.
