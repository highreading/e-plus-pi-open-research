# Exceptional rational factors: denominator and coefficient-gcd tests

Author: Main. Status: author derivation, not independently reviewed. This document saves the deductions following DOMINANT_RATIONAL_FACTOR_CONTENT_BOUND_STAGE3.md. No actual all-degree coefficient-content estimate or irrationality theorem is asserted.

## 1. Hypotheses and the inherited Stage 3 theorem

Let P be a primitive integer polynomial of degree m>=1, with positive leading coefficient A. Suppose all its complex roots, counted with multiplicity, lie in |z-x|<=R, where x is real and 0<2R<1. Set

    L=log(1/(2R))>0.

The elementary theorem proved in Stage 3 says that if

    log A<mL/2,

then P has a unique rational root p/q among those with q<exp(L/2), in reduced form with q>0. Its exact multiplicity e exceeds m/2, and

    log A >= (m-e)L+(2e-m)log q.                       (1)

The theorem uses discriminants of nonlinear irreducible factors and the nonzero resultant of qT-p with its complementary factor. It does not assume squarefreeness, real-rootedness, or a rational value of x. The complementary factor may be constant.

## 2. Stronger denominator and multiplicity consequences

Rewrite (1) as

    log A >= m log q+(m-e)(L-2log q).

Since q<exp(L/2), the second term is nonnegative. Therefore

    q^m<=A.                                          (2)

Also q>=1 and 2e-m>0, so (1) implies

    e>=m-log A/L.                                    (3)

If alpha=log A/(mL)<1/2, then the exceptional multiplicity is at least (1-alpha)m and q<=exp(alpha L). Thus a smaller degree-normalized leading coefficient forces a larger fraction of the polynomial degree into one rational linear factor.

For the actual short-stack primitive polynomial, write

    I_j=delta_(k,m) beta_j,
    g=gcd(I_0,...,I_m)>0,
    A=|I_m|/g,

where delta_(k,m) is the complete physical clearer. Suppose an inherited complete every-root bound supplies R with 0<2R<1 and the leading coefficient satisfies the above strict threshold. Since P(S)!=0 for S=e+pi in the inherited analytic range,

    0<|qS-p|<=A^(1/m) R,
    e>=m-log A/L.                                    (4)

The nonzero assertion uses the actual complete evaluation, not a numerical approximation. Neither (2) nor (4) establishes that the actual coefficient threshold holds.

## 3. Compatibility between exceptional rational factors

Take two primitive polynomials satisfying Section 1 with common center x, degrees m,n, leading coefficients A,B and radii R,T. Suppose both are below their respective strict leading-coefficient thresholds. Let their exceptional roots be p/q and p'/q'. If these rational numbers differ, reduced-fraction separation gives

    1<=qq'|p/q-p'/q'|
      <=A^(1/m) B^(1/n)(R+T).                        (5)

Thus

    A^(1/m) B^(1/n)(R+T)<1                          (6)

forces equality of their exceptional rational roots. This deduction uses no coprimality premise and only the two upper root radii.

Consequently, consider an infinite sequence of actual primitive polynomials P_j, each with P_j(S)!=0, radii R_j tending to zero, and each below its strict threshold. It is impossible for

    A_j^(1/m_j) A_(j+1)^(1/m_(j+1)) (R_j+R_(j+1))<1

at every sufficiently large successive pair. Otherwise the exceptional rational roots all agree from some index onward. Their distances from S tend to zero, forcing that fixed rational number to equal S. Every polynomial in that tail would then vanish at S, contradicting its inherited nonzero evaluation.

This is a conditional sequence obstruction. No actual sequence satisfying all the coefficient hypotheses has been established. In particular it does not produce an individual bound on each actual final gcd.

## 4. Exact coefficient divisibility from a repeated rational factor

Write

    P(T)=A_0+...+A_m T^m,
    P(T)=(qT-p)^e Q(T),

where gcd(p,q)=1, q>0, and Q is integral. Gauss's lemma justifies the integral primitive factorization. For every integer r with 0<=r<e,

    q^(e-r) divides gcd(A_m,...,A_(m-r)),
    p^(e-r) divides gcd(A_0,...,A_r).                  (7)

For the second divisibility one may use |p| when describing positive gcds. The application below has p>0.

Proof: a term contributing to the coefficient of T^(m-j) uses a coefficient of (qT-p)^e at a drop i<=j from its highest degree. That coefficient contains q^(e-i), hence q^(e-j). Every summand therefore has this divisor. For j<=r it is divisible by q^(e-r). Expanding at the constant end gives the corresponding p^(e-j) divisor. The same proof applies when Q is constant; missing coefficients are zero and do not affect divisibility.

In particular, when e>=2,

    q^(e-1) divides H_top=gcd(A_m,A_(m-1)),
    p^(e-1) divides H_bottom=gcd(A_0,A_1).             (8)

These are necessary conditions for the stated rational factor, not sufficient conditions for its existence.

## 5. A test excluding dominant rational factors near S

Now take x=S=e+pi and assume

    R<min(S-5,6-S),    0<2R<1.

The familiar elementary bounds 5<S<6 make this a positive-radius condition. Every rational root then lies in (5,6). A reduced fraction p/q in that interval has q>=2 and p>=11.

For m>=2, a rational root of multiplicity e>m/2 has

    e>=floor(m/2)+1.

Equations (8) therefore force

    H_top>=2^floor(m/2),
    H_bottom>=11^floor(m/2).                          (9)

It follows that either of the strict tests

    H_top<2^floor(m/2),
    H_bottom<11^floor(m/2)                            (10)

excludes every dominant rational linear factor. Stage 3 then proves

    log A_m >= (m/2)log(1/(2R)).                      (11)

There is a full profile version: for 0<=r<=floor(m/2), a dominant rational factor forces

    gcd(A_m,...,A_(m-r))>=2^[floor(m/2)+1-r],
    gcd(A_0,...,A_r)>=11^[floor(m/2)+1-r].             (12)

A strict violation of any one of these necessary inequalities also excludes that factor. The pair tests in (10) are the r=1 case.

## 6. Exact transfer to the actual cleared coefficient vector

For the actual polynomial, all coefficients have a common orientation sign and satisfy A_j=+/- I_j/g with the same sign for every j. Therefore

    H_top=gcd(I_m,I_(m-1))/g,
    H_bottom=gcd(I_0,I_1)/g,

and, more generally,

    gcd(A_m,...,A_(m-r))=gcd(I_m,...,I_(m-r))/g,
    gcd(A_0,...,A_r)=gcd(I_0,...,I_r)/g.               (13)

Thus the tests retain the complete final coefficient gcd g. They do not replace it by a row clearer, a guaranteed divisor, or an intermediate rectangular content. Every endpoint and basis factor already included in the physical integer coefficients remains included. No basis transformation is made in this argument.

With the inherited radius

    R=exp[-ck+U(k,m)], c=4log(1+sqrt(2)),

the conclusion (11), when its exact radius and gcd hypotheses hold, reads

    log(|I_m|/g)>=(m/2)[ck-U(k,m)-log2].              (14)

Here U is the explicit inherited upper-root loss recorded in REVERSE_COEFFICIENT_ROOT_BOUND_STAGE2.md. The later child report announcing an improved upper bound is pending examination and is not used in this document.

## 7. Remaining gap and evidence scope

No uniform bound for the normalized coefficient gcds in (13) has been proved. Guaranteed divisibility of individual raw coefficients does not by itself establish the upper bounds required by (10). The denominator and multiplicity consequences likewise apply only after establishing an actual leading-coefficient threshold.

Even when (14) applies, its content saving is of order km in the inherited small-pole regime, while the leading raw scale is 4k^2 log k. It does not close the global primitive-content requirement or decide the rationality of S.
