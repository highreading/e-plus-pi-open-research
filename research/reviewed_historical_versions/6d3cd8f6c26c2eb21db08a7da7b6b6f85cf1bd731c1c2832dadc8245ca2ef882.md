# Finite-module forced content and the complete characteristic polynomial

Author: Main. Status: author proof within the canonical diagonal construction; independent review pending. This consolidates source record [session identifier removed] and adds a bordered-determinant description. No global growth estimate or irrationality theorem is asserted.

## 1. Actual objects and prime restrictions

Use m=k>=2, h(y)=y+1, and 0<=i,j<k:

 M_ij=mu(h^(i+j)), G_ij=mu(h^(k+i+j)),
 J_ij=nu_k(h^(i+j)), C=M-J,
 B_ij=R_k(h^(i+j)), D_ij=R_k(h^(k+i+j)),
 beta(T)=det[[C,G],[B+TJ,D]].

Moment definitions and the complete rational functional R_k are those of DIAGONAL_LOCAL_CONTENT_REDUCTION_STAGE10.md. Both endpoints and every period jet remain. G and C are symmetric; G is integral and det G=F*_k>0. B is the COMPLETE low rational block, not its high-degree replacement. Monomial-to-h changes are unimodular.

Put b_k=oddpart binom(2k-2,k-1), L_n=lcm(1,...,n), E_clear=b_k*2^(3k+2)*L_k*L_(6k), delta=b_k^k*E_clear^k. The inherited complete integer vector is I_j=delta beta_j. Set g=gcd(I_0,...,I_k)>0 and A=|I_k|/g, the positive leading coefficient of the primitive polynomial P. For p>6k all blocks are p-integral, J and delta are p-units, and

 beta_k=(-1)^k det J det G,
 a=v_p(det G), d=v_p(g), n=v_p(A)=a-d.

Enlarging delta by p would change d; this equality uses the specified clearer. Work over O=Z_p, F=Q_p, L=O^k, with len denoting O-module length. Stage 14 supplies exactly

 H=J^(-1)(DG^(-1)C-B), beta(T)/beta_k=det(T Id-H).       (1)

G^(-1) is over F; no nonunit pivot is silently canceled. Analytic nonvanishing at S is unnecessary for the local algebra.

## 2. Reachable and killed fractional directions

The finite module M_G=G^(-1)L/L has length a. Define

 R=(L+G^(-1)CL)/L,
 K_D={x+L in M_G:Dx in L},
 K_C={x+L in M_G:Cx in L}.

They are well-defined since G,C,D are integral over O. Also G(L+G^(-1)CL)=GL+CL is contained in L, so R is a submodule of M_G.

The homomorphism R -> F^k/L, x+L -> J^(-1)Dx+L, has kernel R intersect K_D and image N_H=(L+HL)/L. Indeed J^(-1)L=L and B is integral. Hence

 N_H is isomorphic to R/(R intersect K_D),
 ell:=len(N_H)=len(R)-len(R intersect K_D).             (2)

Including J^(-1) identifies the image exactly with N_H; omitting it gives the isomorphic denominator module of DG^(-1)C. This specifies the output-lattice identification implicit in the source note.

Set c=a-len(R), b=len(K_D), h_p=a-ell. The exponent h_p is distinct from h(y). Then

 h_p=c+len(R intersect K_D),
 max(c,b)<=h_p<=min(a,c+b).                            (3)

For the lower b bound, len(R intersect K_D)=len(R)+b-len(R+K_D)>=b-c. All other bounds follow by inclusion. No pairing-nonsingularity hypothesis is used.

## 3. Maximal-minor interpretations

Multiplication by G identifies R with (GL+CL)/GL. Therefore c=len(L/(GL+CL)), equivalently the minimum valuation of ALL k-by-k minors of [G C], not just det C.

The map F^k/L -> F^(2k)/O^(2k), x+L -> (Gx,Dx)+O^(2k), has kernel K_D. The tall matrix [G;D] has full column rank over F. Its integral Smith form shows that this kernel is a direct sum of O/p^s_i O, for its k Smith depths s_i. Consequently

 b=min valuation of the k-by-k minors of [G;D].         (4)

Denominators prime to p do not change these statements. This stack is not the historical wide right block.

## 4. Perfect linking pairing

On M_G define <x+L,y+L>=x^TGy modulo O. It is well-defined since Gx,Gy are integral. If x pairs integrally with every G^(-1)v, v in L, then x^Tv is integral for every v, hence x lies in L. This finite pairing is perfect. Finite O-modules and their Hom(-,F/O) duals have equal length, so nondegeneracy identifies the two.

For r=G^(-1)Cv, symmetry of G,C gives <r,x>=v^TCx modulo O. Thus

 R^perp=K_C, R=K_C^perp, len(K_C)=c.

Let s be the length of the image of K_D -> Hom_O(K_C,F/O), y -> (x -> <x,y>). Its kernel is K_D intersect R. Thus

 h_p=c+b-s.                                           (5)

In particular 0<=s<=min(c,b), with h_p<=a. The linking image cannot be discarded: c+b>=a does not imply R subset K_D. No symmetry of H is assumed.

## 5. Complete coefficient content

For any H over F, Smith form after a scalar clearing gives U,V in GL_k(O) with UHV diagonal, entries p^t_i times units and possibly zeros. The t_i are integers. The quotient (L+HL)/L has length ell=sum max(0,-t_i); zero entries contribute zero.

Now U(T Id-H)V=TUV-UHV. Multiply each row with t_i<0 by p^(-t_i). This makes the entire polynomial matrix integral. Its determinant is an O-unit times p^ell det(T Id-H). This proves p^ell det(T Id-H) in O[T], including when H is singular. Left/right Smith transformations are not claimed to be similarity transformations.

Primitivity of P means the minimum p-valuation of its coefficients is zero. Hence the least scalar p-power clearing P/A=beta/beta_k is p^n. Equation (1) implies n<=ell. Consequently

 d>=h_p,
 d=h_p+rho_p, rho_p:=ell-n>=0.                         (6)

h_p is forced by the actual C/G/D finite modules. rho_p measures additional cancellation of coefficients of the complete characteristic polynomial. B does not affect ell but may affect rho_p. It must remain in H; h_p is not identified with the final d.

An exact sufficient condition is

 R subset K_D iff DG^(-1)C is integral iff ell=0.

It implies n=0,d=a. The converse from n=0 to ell=0 is not asserted: a fractional matrix can have integral characteristic polynomial.

## 6. Global direction and small-prime gap

For fixed k set Q_struct=product_(p>6k) p^h_p. Only primes dividing det G contribute. Then

 Q_struct divides g,
 A_large divides (det G)_large/Q_struct,
 A_large=(det G)_large/[Q_struct*product_(p>6k)p^rho_p]. (7)

Here large means support on primes greater than 6k. This is an upper-denominator bound, in the direction needed for small values. No growing-k lower estimate for Q_struct or control of A_small follows.

For k>=131072 in the inherited nonvanishing range, retain T_k(v)=v^(-k)/|beta(S)/beta_k|, S=e+pi. The inequality

 A_small*(det G)_large/Q_struct<T_k(v)

is sufficient for |P(S)|<v^(-k), but is not proved. Unknown rho_p can only improve this upper bound. No small-prime contribution is assumed harmless.

## 7. New bordered-determinant description

Put X=DG^(-1)C. For subsets I,J of {0,...,k-1} of equal size j, let D_I be those rows of D and C_J those columns of C, and set

 E_(I,J)=det[[G,C_J],[-D_I,0_(j by j)]].

For j=0 use det G. The Schur formula over F gives exactly

 E_(I,J)=det G * det(X_(I,J)).                          (8)

The determinants are integral over O directly from their entries. For any rational matrix X, the minimum valuation among minors of ALL orders, including the empty minor 1, is the sum of its negative Smith depths. This follows from Smith form; Cauchy-Binet and the inverse integral transformations preserve each minor ideal. The minimum is attained by choosing precisely the negative depths, or the empty minor if none is negative.

The quotient length for X equals ell by Section 2, so

 h_p=min_(0<=j<=k, |I|=|J|=j) v_p(E_(I,J)).            (9)

Zero determinants have infinite valuation; j=0 ensures a finite minimum. This is a division-free ideal describing structural forced content. It uses all such bordered determinants, not just horizontal or vertical maximal-minor content. No numerical value or uniform estimate is asserted.
