# A sharper lower root-distance bound from the complete coefficients

Author: Main, current continuation. Status: author derivation from the explicitly attributed analytic inputs below; not independently reviewed. This strengthens the lower bound in ROOT_ANNULI_AND_ACTUAL_RESULTANTS_STAGE1.md. The earlier bound remains valid. No irrationality conclusion or uniform final-content estimate is established.

## 1. Actual family and inherited estimates

Historical source prefix: work/session_20261002_codex_continuation/.

Use the actual polynomial beta_(k,m)(s)=det[C;R+sV] from main/GENERAL_POLE_RANK_M_PRIMITIVE_INTERFACE.md. Assume k>=131072 and 1<=m<=k. All endpoint terms and jets are retained. Write

    beta(S+t)=sum_(l=0)^m b_l t^l, S=e+pi.

The source analytic theorems give b_0!=0 and b_m!=0. The primitive polynomial differs from beta by a nonzero scalar, so every coefficient ratio and root bound below applies unchanged after the FULL moment clearer and final coefficient gcd. This argument makes no basis change and introduces no additional arithmetic index.

Put

    ell=log(1+sqrt(2)), c=4ell,
    a=1/(48e^4), C=4/a, L0=exp(-C),
    kappa_m=[4^(m-1)/binom(2m-2,m-1)]^m.

Let F*=det M_[mu(y+1)^k,k], and let

    I_l=det G_[sigma_m(y+1)^(2l),k-l], 0<=l<=k,

with I_k=1. These I_l are compact insertion determinants, NOT the integer polynomial coefficients used elsewhere in the arithmetic notation.

The complete product source agent3_analysis/GROWING_POLE_ORDER_SHORT_COMPLETE_PRODUCT.md gives

    |b_0|>=L0 F* I_0,
    |b_m|<=kappa_m F* I_m.                              (1)

The second bound follows directly from its exact highest-coefficient integral and F(x,(-1)^m)<=F*. The middle-coefficient source agent3_analysis/GROWING_POLE_ALL_ROOT_LOCALIZATION.md gives, for 1<=l<m,

    |b_l|<=F* I_l B_(k,m,l)/l!,
    B_(k,m,l)=2^[l(m+l-2)] k^[2l(m-l)] exp(3l).          (2)

Both estimates concern the COMPLETE actual polynomial. No positivity sign for a middle coefficient is assumed.

## 2. An upper bound for compact insertion ratios

The exact successive-insertion identity gives

    I_l/I_0=product_(r=0)^(l-1)
                 K_[sigma_m(y+1)^(2r),k-r](-1),         (3)

where K_(w,n)(-1) is the value reproducing kernel on polynomials of degree below n.

For gamma=y^(-1/2)dy on [0,1], the inherited pointwise measure comparison is sigma_m>=(1/2)gamma. Also (1+y)^(2r)>=1. The minimum-norm characterization of the value kernel therefore gives

    K_[sigma_m(y+1)^(2r),k-r](-1)
        <=2 K_(gamma,k-r)(-1).

The complete product source proves K_(gamma,n)(-1)<=n^2 exp(c n). Consequently, for every 1<=l<=m,

    (1/l)log(I_l/I_0)
        <=c k-2ell(l-1)+2log k+log2.                   (4)

This uses the same actual compact measure sigma_m throughout all insertions. It does not replace its pole order m by l.

## 3. Uniform bounds on every normalized coefficient

For a nonzero b_l with 1<=l<m, (1), (2), and (4) imply

    (1/l)log|b_l/b_0| <= c k+E_l,

where

    E_l=2(m-l+1)log k+(m+l-1)log2
          -2ell(l-1)+3+C/l-(log(l!))/l.                (5)

Zero coefficients satisfy the corresponding exponential bound automatically.

The first middle-index bound is

    E_1=2m log k+m log2+3+C.

Subtracting this expression from (5) gives

    E_l-E_1=(l-1)(-2log k+log2-2ell)
                 +C/l-C-(log(l!))/l <=0,              (6)

because k>=1 and 2ell>log2. Thus every middle coefficient has the same convenient majorant E_1; no factor proportional to m is lost by dividing a product of all root distances.

For the leading coefficient, (1) and (4) give

    (1/m)log|b_m/b_0|
       <=c k+2log k+log2-2ell(m-1)
                         +(log kappa_m)/m+C/m.        (7)

The inherited bound kappa_m<=(2m-1)^m and the elementary inequality

    2m-1<=3^(m-1)<=(3+2sqrt(2))^(m-1)

show that the sum -2ell(m-1)+(log kappa_m)/m is nonpositive. The first inequality follows by induction, including equality at m=1. Hence (7) is also bounded by c k+E_1. This includes m=1, when no middle indices exist.

We have proved, for all 1<=l<=m,

    |b_l/b_0| <= exp[l(c k+E_1)].                      (8)

## 4. Reverse coefficient argument and every-root lower bound

Let M=exp(c k+E_1). If |t|<=1/(2M), equation (8) gives

    sum_(l=1)^m |b_l t^l/b_0|
        <=sum_(l=1)^m 2^(-l)=1-2^(-m)<1.

The nonzero constant term cannot be canceled. Thus no root lies in this closed disk. In particular, EVERY complex root alpha of the actual polynomial satisfies the convenient non-strict bound

    log|alpha-S| >= -c k-W(k,m),                       (9)

where

    W(k,m)=2m log k+(m+1)log2+3+C.

The proof actually gives strict exclusion at the displayed radius. Multiplicities and nonreal roots cause no difficulty. The estimate is valid throughout 1<=m<=k, although its sharp asymptotic usefulness depends on m.

Unlike the Stage 1 lower bound, W has order O(m log(k+1)+1), with an absolute implied constant. Therefore

    m log k=o(k)

implies the one-sided bound log|alpha-S|>=-c k-o(k), uniformly over every root.

## 5. Improved two-sided annulus

The inherited every-root upper bound is

    log|alpha-S|<=-c k+U(k,m),

with

    U(k,m)=2m log k+(c+log4+3)(m-1)
             +(m-1)(2m-3)log2
             +C0+log(6m)+C+log2,
    C0=log[32pi^2(3+2sqrt(2))^2].

In particular U=O(m^2+m log(k+1)+1). Combining this with (9) proves

    exp(-c k-W(k,m))<=|alpha-S|<=exp(-c k+U(k,m)).       (10)

For EVERY integer sequence m=o(sqrt(k)), both U and W are o(k), since log k/sqrt(k) tends to zero. Hence uniformly over all roots,

    log|alpha-S|=-4k log(1+sqrt(2))+o(k).               (11)

This extends the sufficient m=o(k^(1/3)) regime in Stage 1. For example, m=floor(k^(2/5)) is now included. It does not establish the corresponding two-sided asymptotic for proportional m or for m comparable to sqrt(k).

## 6. Actual coprimality with asymmetric pole growth

Let P=P_(k,m) and Q=P_(K,n) be the actual primitive integer polynomials, with positive leading coefficients A and B. Both dimensions satisfy the analytic threshold. Define

    t=exp[-c(K-k)+W(k,m)+U(K,n)].

When t<1, every root beta of Q and alpha of P satisfies

    |beta-S|/|alpha-S|<=t<1.

Their root sets are disjoint. Thus Res(P,Q) is a nonzero integer, without any generic-coprimality assumption.

For fixed eta>0, put K=ceil((1+eta)k). The sufficient asymptotic conditions are now

    m log k=o(k),   n=o(sqrt(K)).                      (12)

Indeed W(k,m)=o(k), U(K,n)=o(K)=o(k), and log t=-c eta k+o(k). Notice the asymmetry: only a LOWER root-distance bound for P and an UPPER bound for Q are needed. The outer polynomial may have pole order substantially larger than sqrt(k). A concrete choice is m=floor(k^(3/4)), n=floor(K^(2/5)), for sufficiently large k. Both remain within the inherited range m<=k, n<=K.

The exact resultant comparison from Stage 1 consequently applies in this larger range:

    (1-t)^(mn) B^m |P(S)|^n
      <=|Res(P,Q)|
      <=(1+t)^(mn) B^m |P(S)|^n.                       (13)

For completeness, factor alpha-S from each cross difference in

    |Res(P,Q)|=A^n B^m product_(alpha,beta)|alpha-beta|.

Then A^n product_alpha|alpha-S|^n=|P(S)|^n, proving (13). Its upper bound and integer nonvanishing imply

    B^(1/n)|P(S)|^(1/m)>=1/(1+t).                      (14)

All leading coefficients in these formulas are AFTER their own full final gcds. If the raw integer polynomials have contents g and h, their resultant still obeys Res(I,J)=g^n h^m Res(P,Q), up to orientation signs. No factor has been removed from the arithmetic interface.

Under (12), the inherited complete root-product estimate also gives

    log A/m+log B/n >=4k log(1+sqrt(2))-o(k),            (15)

because its degree-normalized error is O(m+log k+log(m+1)+1), which is o(k). This remains a paired bound, not a uniform bound for every individual primitive leading coefficient.

## 7. Remaining limitation and evidence scope

The sharper annulus removes a loss in the previous root-product division argument and supplies larger actual coprimality ranges. Equations (13)-(15) still do not control the final coefficient contents at the leading 4k^2 log k scale. A later polynomial with a large primitive leading coefficient remains compatible with a small earlier primitive value. No small-value selection or irrationality theorem follows.

All derivations here are symbolic consequences of the named inherited complete coefficient, compact kernel, and nonzero estimates. No new execution, numerical root evidence, independent review, or external source access is claimed. Stage 1 remains a valid weaker result; its stated sufficient regimes are strengthened, not refuted, by this note.
