# Exterior Smith bounds and repeated primitive factors

Author: Main. Status: author proof from the canonical complete diagonal construction; no independent review of this stage is claimed. The results require no effective-pairing nondegeneracy. They constrain actual root and factor denominators but do not estimate the final coefficient content sufficiently to resolve S=e+pi.

## 1. Actual construction and normalization

Use the canonical diagonal family m=k>=2, with h=y+1 and indices 0<=i,j<k:

    M_ij=mu(h^(i+j)),       G_ij=mu(h^(k+i+j)),
    J_ij=nu_k(h^(i+j)),     B_ij=R_k(h^(i+j)),
    D_ij=R_k(h^(k+i+j)),    C=M-J,
    beta(T)=det[[C,G],[B+TJ,D]].

R_k is the complete rational functional, retaining both endpoints. In particular B is the complete low block, not the high-degree integral formula. The monomial-to-h changes have determinant one. These are the exact objects of DIAGONAL_LOCAL_CONTENT_REDUCTION_STAGE10.md.

Let delta be the specified complete physical clearer, I_j=delta beta_j, g=gcd(I_0,...,I_k)>0, and orient the primitive polynomial P to have positive leading coefficient

    A=|I_k|/g.

The exact leading coefficient is beta_k=(-1)^k det J det G, with det G=F*_k>0. Fix p>6k. The specified delta and det J are p-units, and every displayed block is p-integral. Write

    a=v_p(det G), d=v_p(g), n=v_p(A)=a-d.

All assertions below concern this large-prime range. Small primes require separate accounting. The prime restriction is not removed by changing a clearer.

Let the Smith valuations of the integer matrix G, INCLUDING its unit pivots, be

    0<=gamma_1<=...<=gamma_k.

Put

    Sigma_0=0,
    Sigma_j=gamma_(k-j+1)+...+gamma_k, 1<=j<=k.

Thus Sigma_k=a and Sigma_j is nondecreasing. This notation avoids confusion with the physical low block B.

Stage 14 proves directly, by swapping column blocks and taking the Schur complement over Q, that

    beta(T)/beta_k=det(T Id-H),
    H=J^(-1)(D G^(-1) C-B).                         (1)

No integral invertibility of G is presumed. Its inverse is used over Q with all Smith denominators retained. Equation (1) includes B exactly.

## 2. Every exterior power has a Smith denominator bound

For 1<=j<=k,

    p^Sigma_j wedge^j H is integral over Z_p.        (2)

Equivalently, each j by j minor of H has valuation at least -Sigma_j.

Proof. Write H=X G^(-1)Y+Z, where X=J^(-1)D, Y=C and Z=-J^(-1)B are integral over Z_p. Expand a selected j by j determinant by choosing, in each column, its contribution from X G^(-1)Y or Z. If exactly l columns come from the former, Laplace expansion along those columns expresses the term as a sum of l by l minors of X G^(-1)Y multiplied by integral minors of Z, with signs.

Cauchy–Binet, applied twice, expresses each selected l by l minor of X G^(-1)Y as an integral linear combination of l by l minors of G^(-1). This remains valid for nonconsecutive column and row subsets. For l=0 the selected minor is one.

Smith normal form over Z_p gives G=U diag(p^gamma_i) V with U,V invertible over Z_p. Its inverse therefore has every l by l minor of valuation at least -Sigma_l. Since l<=j and Sigma_l<=Sigma_j, every determinant term has valuation at least -Sigma_j. Addition cannot lower that bound. This proves (2).

The argument uses all Smith depths, not just j times the largest depth. It assumes neither symmetry of H nor invertibility of the effective radical pairing.

As a coefficient consequence, because characteristic coefficients are sums of principal minors,

    v_p(I_(k-j))>=a-Sigma_j, 0<=j<=k.               (3)

The complete clearer is a p-unit, and v_p(beta_k)=a, so no normalization factor is omitted. Formula (3) alone is not an upper bound on d.

## 3. Weak majorization of root denominator valuations

For a nonzero root alpha of P define its denominator valuation as max(0,-v_p(alpha)); a zero root has denominator valuation zero. List these numbers in descending order, with algebraic multiplicity:

    lambda_1>=...>=lambda_k>=0.

Then

    sum_(i=1)^j lambda_i<=Sigma_j, 1<=j<=k,
    sum_(i=1)^k lambda_i=n.                          (4)

Proof of the inequalities. Over an algebraic closure, a matrix can be triangularized. Hence the eigenvalues of wedge^j H are the products of j eigenvalues of H, with multiplicities, even if H is not diagonalizable. Integrality in (2) implies that every such nonzero product has valuation at least -Sigma_j.

If at least j roots have negative valuation, take the product corresponding to the j largest lambda_i. Its valuation is minus their sum, proving the bound. If only t<j roots have negative valuation, their sum is bounded using wedge^t H, and Sigma_t<=Sigma_j finishes the argument. The case t=0 is immediate. Zero roots never enter a selected product of negative-valuation roots.

Proof of the total identity. The coefficients of P are integers and primitive, so their minimum p-adic valuation is zero. Reverse P. Its constant coefficient has valuation n; the first coefficient of valuation zero ends its negative-slope Newton-polygon portion at height zero. The horizontal lengths count exactly the reciprocal roots of positive valuation, and their valuation sum is n. Reciprocation gives the last equality in (4). Missing highest coefficients of the reversed polynomial correspond to zero roots of P and do not affect this sum.

In particular, every collection of j roots has denominator weight at most Sigma_j. The largest-pivot cap from Stage 14 is the j=1 instance. If t is the number of roots with negative valuation and n>0, then

    t>=min{j:Sigma_j>=n}.                            (5)

These are actual-family statements without constant-pairing or residue-squarefreeness hypotheses. Their numerical strength still depends on the actual Smith depths and n.

## 4. Rational roots with multiplicity

Suppose u/q is a reduced rational root of exact multiplicity e, q>0. Gauss's lemma gives P=(qT-u)^e F with F primitive integral, up to orientation. Put nu=v_p(q). The e copies of the root have denominator weight e nu. Equations (4), applied to them and to the remaining k-e roots, give

    e nu<=min(n,Sigma_e),
    n-e nu<=Sigma_(k-e).

Consequently

    max(0,ceil((n-Sigma_(k-e))/e))
        <=nu<=floor(min(n,Sigma_e)/e).               (6)

An empty interval excludes a rational root of multiplicity e. If n>Sigma_(k-e), such a root must have p in its denominator. For e=1 the upper bound includes the largest-Smith cap; for higher e it can improve the bound obtained by multiplying that single-pivot cap by e.

The lower bound uses the complement, which also consists of actual eigenvalues of H. It is not inferred from a hypothetical pairing nondegeneracy assumption. The unknown complete content remains present through n=a-d.

## 5. Repeated factors of arbitrary degree

More generally, let F be primitive in Z[T], of degree f>=1, with positive absolute leading coefficient A_F. Suppose

    P=F^e R,
    e>=1, ef<=k,

where R is integral and primitive; these properties follow from Gauss's lemma when the divisibility is initially over Q[T]. The absolute leading coefficients satisfy A=A_F^e A_R. Let u_F=v_p(A_F).

The primitive Newton-polygon identity used in Section 3 applies to F itself. Its roots have total denominator weight u_F. The ef roots belonging to its e copies consequently contribute e u_F; the complementary roots contribute n-e u_F. Thus

    e u_F<=min(n,Sigma_(ef)),
    n-e u_F<=Sigma_(k-ef),                            (7)

and

    max(0,ceil((n-Sigma_(k-ef))/e))
       <=u_F<=floor(min(n,Sigma_(ef))/e).             (8)

No splitting of F over Q_p is required. Its roots are considered in an algebraic closure, and their valuation sum is an integer because it equals v_p(A_F). The statement also permits zero roots and reducible primitive F. If ef=k, the complement has degree zero and (7) requires n=e u_F exactly.

## 6. Global determinantal-divisor formulation

Let Delta_j^G denote the positive gcd of all j by j minors of G, with Delta_0^G=1. This notation is unrelated to the Legendre basis conversion factors in earlier stages. Since G is nonsingular and integral, its integer Smith invariants exist and divide successively. Define

    T_j(G)=det G/Delta_(k-j)^G, 0<=j<=k.

This is the positive product of the j largest integer Smith invariants; T_0=1 and v_p(T_j)=Sigma_j.

For an integer N>0 write N_large for its part supported on primes greater than 6k. The rational-root bound gives

    q_large^e divides gcd(A,T_e(G))_large.            (9)

The A divisibility follows from Gauss's lemma, and the T_e divisibility follows from (6). More generally, (7) gives

    (A_F)_large^e divides gcd(A,T_(ef)(G))_large.    (10)

For e=f=1, this recovers the largest-integer-Smith restriction of Stage 14. For repeated factors it uses the relevant whole Smith tail, rather than a power of the largest invariant.

The complementary bound gives a further finite divisibility interval. For primes p>6k dividing det G define

    ell_p=max(0,ceil((n_p-Sigma_(k-ef),p)/e)),
    u_p=floor(min(n_p,Sigma_(ef),p)/e),

where n_p=v_p(A) and Sigma_(j),p is the local sum of the j largest Smith depths. If a factor F as above exists, ell_p<=u_p at every such prime, and

    product_p p^ell_p divides (A_F)_large,
    (A_F)_large divides product_p p^u_p.             (11)

At every other prime greater than 6k, a=0 and n=0, so A_F has valuation zero there. Thus the products are finite and include all relevant large primes. They make no assertion about small-prime divisibility.

## 7. What remains missing

The new results are the all-exterior-power denominator estimate, the unconditional-within-construction weak majorization, and two-sided repeated-factor valuation constraints. They apply to the complete actual polynomial with its true primitive normalization.

They do not bound the actual determinant-minor products T_j(G) favorably as k grows, and do not estimate n=v_p(A) without the final gcd. They supply necessary arithmetic restrictions on repeated factors, not an occurrence theorem for useful residues, a favorable infinite sequence of primitive values, or an exclusion of every degree in the construction.

No Archimedean root radius is asserted here. In particular the shrinking annuli in the slow-pole regime are not transferred to m=k. No analytic nonvanishing assumption is needed for this purely algebraic stage.

The initial exterior-power derivation is preserved in Main note [session identifier removed]. This document adds the complementary bound for arbitrary repeated primitive factors and its global divisibility interval. No computation or independent review is claimed. S=e+pi remains unresolved.
