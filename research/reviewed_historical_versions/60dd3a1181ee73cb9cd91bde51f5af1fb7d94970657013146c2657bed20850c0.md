Generic root-product and resultant identities are classical. The specialization here supplies an individual lower root-distance bound and an infinite coprimality regime for the actual polynomials.

Set

    ell=log(1+sqrt(2)), c=4 ell,
    a=1/(48 e^4), C=4/a,
    C0=log(32 pi^2 (3+2sqrt(2))^2),
    kappa_m=[4^(m-1)/binom(2m-2,m-1)]^m.

In particular log kappa_m=O(m log(m+1)) and kappa_m>=1. Write the roots as alpha_1,...,alpha_m and r_i=|alpha_i-S|. Every r_i is positive because beta(S)!=0.

The complete product identity is

    log product_i r_i = -log kappa_m-log det K_(sigma_m,k,m)+epsilon,
    |epsilon|<=C.

The source's upper inverse-Gram bound gives

    log product_i r_i
      >= -c k m + 2 ell m(m-1)
           -2m log k-m log 2-log kappa_m-C.             (1)

The every-root upper bound gives

    log r_i <= -c k+U(k,m),                           (2)

where the explicit positive quantity U is

    U(k,m)=2m log k+(c+log 4+3)(m-1)
           +(m-1)(2m-3)log 2
           +C0+log(6m)+C+log 2.

For m=1 the potentially negative factor 2m-3 is multiplied by m-1=0. Thus U>0 throughout the stated range.

## 2. Every-root LOWER bound

Define

    D(k,m)=log kappa_m+2m log k+m log 2+C,
    V(k,m)=D(k,m)+(m-1)U(k,m).

Dropping the nonnegative term 2 ell m(m-1) in (1) gives

    sum_i log r_i >= -c k m-D(k,m).

For any selected index i, subtract the upper bound (2) for each of the other m-1 roots:

    log r_i
      =sum_j log r_j-sum_(j!=i) log r_j
      >=-c k m-D(k,m)-(m-1)(-c k+U(k,m))
      =-c k-V(k,m).

Consequently EVERY root of the actual polynomial satisfies

    exp(-c k-V(k,m)) <= |alpha_i-S|
                               <= exp(-c k+U(k,m)).    (3)

This proof neither assumes simple roots nor real roots. Multiplicities are counted in both inherited product and upper bounds. It does not infer a lower bound for an individual root from the product alone: the upper bounds for all other roots are essential.

With absolute constants, including the fixed C,

    U(k,m)=O(m^2+m log(k+1)+1),
    V(k,m)=O(m^3+m^2 log(k+1)+1).

Therefore, along every integer sequence m=m(k)>=1 satisfying

    m=o(k^(1/3)),

both U and V are o(k). Indeed m^3=o(k), and m^2 log(k+1)=o(k) follows since log(k+1)/k^(1/3) tends to zero. Uniformly over ALL roots of each polynomial,

    log|alpha_i-S|=-4k log(1+sqrt(2))+o(k).             (4)

This includes growing m. It sharpens the inherited one-sided individual localization in this narrower regime. It does not extend an individual lower bound to m proportional to k.

## 3. Explicit coprimality between separated dimensions

Take two actual polynomials

    P=P_(k,m), Q=P_(K,n),

where k,K>=k0, 1<=m<=k, and 1<=n<=K. Let their positive leading coefficients be A and B. Define

    t=exp[-c(K-k)+V(k,m)+U(K,n)].

If t<1, then every root beta_j of Q is strictly closer to S than every root alpha_i of P, because

    |beta_j-S|/|alpha_i-S|<=t<1.                       (5)

Thus P and Q have no common complex root, so their resultant is a NONZERO integer. This is a proved coprimality conclusion for the actual family under an explicit numerical parameter inequality. There is no unproved generic-coprimality premise.

In particular, fix any eta>0 and put K=ceil((1+eta)k). If m=o(k^(1/3)) and n=o(K^(1/3)), then

    log t=-c eta k+o(k),

so t<1 eventually. Hence the two actual primitive polynomials are coprime for every sufficiently large member of this sequence. A simple concrete growing choice is m=floor(k^(1/4)), n=floor(K^(1/4)); only the sufficiently large range is asserted without a further explicit threshold calculation.

For each fixed pole order, this also gives coprimality at sufficiently large proportionally separated dimensions. It does not assert coprimality at consecutive dimensions or between different pole orders at the same dimension.

## 4. Complete resultant comparison, including primitive content

For t<1, use the exact classical root formula

    |Res(P,Q)|=A^n B^m product_(i,j)|alpha_i-beta_j|.

Factoring alpha_i-S from each cross difference gives

    |Res(P,Q)|
      =B^m |P(S)|^n product_(i,j)
           |1-(beta_j-S)/(alpha_i-S)|.

By (5),

    (1-t)^(mn) B^m |P(S)|^n
       <=|Res(P,Q)|
       <=(1+t)^(mn) B^m |P(S)|^n.                     (6)

Since the resultant is a nonzero integer, the UPPER bound in (6) implies

    B^(1/n) |P(S)|^(1/m) >= 1/(1+t),                 (7)

or equivalently

    log A/m+log B/n
      >= -(1/m)log|beta_(k,m)(S)/beta_(k,m),m|
                                                   -log(1+t). (8)

The direction in (7) matters: the lower bound for the resultant alone would not imply it. The complete first polynomial value, rather than an isolated coefficient or a raw determinant entry, occurs here.

The inherited upper bound for the root product, from equations (21)-(22) of the complete product source, makes (8) explicit:

    log A/m+log B/n
      >= c k-(2 ell+log 2)(m-1)-2log k-C0-log(6m)
             +(log kappa_m)/m-C/m-log(1+t).            (9)

For the proportional separation and small-pole regime of Section 3 this becomes

    log A/m+log B/n >= 4ell k-o(k).                    (10)

At least one of the two degree-normalized primitive leading coefficients therefore has logarithm at least 2ell k-o(k). This is a paired lower bound; it is not a lower bound of that size for every single member.

To display the final common contents explicitly, let the second polynomial have raw leading integer J_n and final coefficient gcd h. Then A=|I_m|/g and B=|J_n|/h. If

    Eprod=|beta_(k,m)(S)/beta_(k,m),m|,

equation (8) is EXACTLY the content bound

    log g/m+log h/n
      <= log|I_m|/m+log|J_n|/n
                           +(log Eprod)/m+log(1+t).    (11)

Both raw integers use their own full physical clearers. The exact homogeneity identity is also

    Res(I,J)=g^n h^m Res(P,Q),

up to harmless global signs from orientation. Thus g^n h^m divides the nonzero integer Res(I,J). No assertion of equality of these contents is made.

## 5. What this does and does not close

The new mathematical interfaces are the two-sided individual annulus (3), its uniform shrinking regime (4), actual-family coprimality at proportionally separated dimensions, and the complete resultant comparison (6). All preserve complex roots, multiplicities, full endpoint moments, and final primitive contents.

The resulting arithmetic restriction does NOT supply the needed global content estimate. The inherited raw-leading scale is

    log|I_m|=4k^2 log k+O(k^2)

uniformly in the source range. Equation (11) subtracts a quantity of order k from the DEGREE-NORMALIZED combined logarithmic content, while the raw-leading contributions have order k^2 log k/m. It therefore does not yield a bound log g<=(4-epsilon)k^2 log k for a fixed positive epsilon, nor does it prove or exclude the content needed for shrinking primitive values.

Likewise (7) is compatible with small values of P(S) when the later leading coefficient B is sufficiently large. The analytic rational-target criterion still requires |P(S)|<v^(-m) with P(S)!=0 for every fixed hypothetical rational denominator v. Neither coprimality nor (7) establishes this.

A useful next question is whether an ACTUAL recurrence or determinant identity gives a substantially sharper upper bound on these same nonzero resultants than the root-factor estimate alone, with every forced content factor retained. Such an identity is presently unproved. No numerical resultant scan is warranted merely by the generic inequality.
