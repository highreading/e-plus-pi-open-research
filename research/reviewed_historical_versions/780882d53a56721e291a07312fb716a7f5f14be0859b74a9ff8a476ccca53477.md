# Exceptional dimensions: density and multiplicative separation

Author: Main. Status: symbolic author derivation from the explicit inputs below; not independently reviewed. This saves the preceding density-zero deduction and strengthens its local separation statement. The rationality of S=e+pi remains unresolved.

## 1. Actual family and evidence scope

For every sufficiently large integer k choose an integer m_k with 1<=m_k<=k. Assume

    m_k log k=o(k).

Use the actual short-stack polynomial beta_(k,m_k) and complete clearer delta_(k,m_k) from the historical main/GENERAL_POLE_RANK_M_PRIMITIVE_INTERFACE.md, under work/session_20261002_codex_continuation/. Define

    I_(k,j)=delta_(k,m_k) beta_(k,m_k),j,
    g_k=gcd(I_(k,0),...,I_(k,m_k))>0,
    P_k=the primitive integer polynomial with positive leading coefficient,
    A_k=|I_(k,m_k)|/g_k.

Every coefficient uses its full physical clearer. The complete coefficient gcd g_k is retained. No basis change is made.

The inherited complete analytic theorem supplies degree m_k and P_k(S)!=0 for k>=131072. Main's REVERSE_COEFFICIENT_ROOT_BOUND_STAGE2.md supplies the lower root-distance bound. Its new implications received a limited review in Child 4's report, conditional on the inherited analytic estimates. The same report contains an ORIGINAL upper-bound refinement:

    work/parallel_batch_01/agents/c1e91660/350ce822/ROOT_BOUND_REVIEW_AND_EXTENSION_STAGE2.md.

The upper refinement is an author proof, not independently reviewed. Main has checked the exact Taylor weights, factorial Vandermonde estimate, surviving l! normalization, radius h/(k^2+2k), and the l=0 and m=1 cases before using it here. This does not independently certify the historical hypotheses.

Set

    c=4 log(1+sqrt(2)),
    a=1/(48e^4), C=4/a,
    C0=log[32pi^2(3+2sqrt(2))^2].

For every root alpha of P_k, including multiplicities,

    exp(-ck-W_k)<=|alpha-S|<=exp(-ck+U_k),             (1)

where

    W_k=2m_k log k+(m_k+1)log2+3+C,

and the improved upper loss is

    U_k=2log k+(c+log4)(m_k-1)+C0+log(6m_k)+C
        +(m_k-1)[log(m_k+1)+log(k^2+2k)+1]+log2.

There is an absolute constant C_* such that both losses are at most

    C_*[m_k log(k+1)+1].                              (2)

In particular U_k,W_k=o(k). Put R_k=exp(-ck+U_k) and

    L_k=log(1/(2R_k))=ck-U_k-log2.

Then L_k>0 eventually and L_k/k tends to c.

## 2. Cheap leading coefficients force an actual rational factor

Fix a real constant tau with 0<tau<c/2. Define

    E_tau={k: log A_k<=tau k m_k}.

For every sufficiently large k in E_tau,

    log A_k<m_k L_k/2.

The general polynomial theorem in DOMINANT_RATIONAL_FACTOR_CONTENT_BOUND_STAGE3.md, together with the denominator deduction in EXCEPTIONAL_FACTOR_DENOMINATORS_AND_COEFFICIENT_GCD_STAGE4.md, gives a reduced rational root

    r_k=p_k/q_k, q_k>0,

with multiplicity greater than m_k/2 and

    q_k<=A_k^(1/m_k)<=exp(tau k).                     (3)

This is the unique rational root with denominator below exp(L_k/2). It differs from S because P_k(S)!=0. The full annulus (1) applies to r_k. No rationality assumption about S is used.

For completeness, the denominator inequality follows from the exact factor-resultant bound

    log A_k>=(m_k-e)L_k+(2e-m_k)log q_k
           =m_k log q_k+(m_k-e)(L_k-2log q_k),

whose final term is nonnegative. This argument also covers a constant complementary factor and m_k=1.

## 3. Exact alternatives for two exceptional dimensions

Let k<l be sufficiently large members of E_tau.

If r_k=r_l, applying the lower annulus at k and upper annulus at l gives

    c(l-k)<=W_k+U_l.                                 (4)

If r_k differs from r_l, reduced-fraction separation and (3) give

    1<=q_k q_l |r_k-r_l|
      <=exp[tau(k+l)](R_k+R_l).                      (5)

These alternatives are exact necessary inequalities. They retain all primitive coefficients through A_k and A_l.

A useful global asymptotic form follows. For any fixed epsilon with 0<epsilon<c, all sufficiently large j satisfy U_j,W_j<=epsilon j. In the equal-root case, (4) yields

    l/k<=(c+epsilon)/(c-epsilon).                    (6)

In the distinct-root case,

    R_k+R_l<=2 exp[-(c-epsilon)k],

because l>k. Taking logarithms in (5) gives

    l/k>=(c-epsilon)/tau-1-log2/(tau k).              (7)

Thus two exceptional dimensions cannot have an intermediate ratio bounded away both from 1 and from c/tau-1. Along any sequence of exceptional pairs with k tending to infinity, the equal-root pairs have l/k tending to 1; distinct-root pairs satisfy

    liminf l/k>=c/tau-1>1.                           (8)

This does not assert that the exceptional set is finite. The same root can occur in a short interval of dimensions, and different exceptional roots can occur at widely separated dimensions.

## 4. A stronger local interval statement

Choose any fixed lambda satisfying

    1<lambda<c/tau-1,

or equivalently tau(1+lambda)<c. This is a larger allowed interval range than the sufficient condition 2tau lambda<c in the preceding author note.

For N sufficiently large, every exceptional dimension in [N,lambda N] has the SAME rational root. To see this, choose epsilon>0 with tau(1+lambda)<c-epsilon. If k<l belong to that interval and their roots differ, (5) implies

    1<=2 exp[tau l-(c-epsilon-tau)k]
      <=2 exp[-(c-epsilon-tau(1+lambda))N],

which is impossible for large N. The case of zero or one exceptional member is automatic.

Let H_N be the largest pole order among the integer dimensions in this interval. For two exceptional members, (4) and (2) give

    l-k<=C_lambda[H_N log(N+1)+1],                   (9)

with a constant independent of N and of the pole-order choices. Taking the first and last members proves that their number is at most the right side of (9) plus one. This uses the shared rational root; no assumption that every intervening dimension is exceptional is made.

## 5. Quantitative dyadic count and density zero

Define

    M_N=max{m_k:N<=k<=2N, k integer}.

Cover [N,2N] by a fixed finite number of intervals whose endpoint ratios are at most lambda. Apply the local result to the exceptional members of each interval, restricting the last interval to [N,2N]. Endpoint rounding changes the count only by a bounded amount. Equation (9) gives

    #{k in E_tau:N<=k<=2N}
        =O_tau(M_N log(N+1)+1).                      (10)

The condition m_k log k=o(k) implies M_N log(N+1)=o(N), uniformly over the dyadic interval. Hence

    #{k in E_tau:N<=k<=2N}=o(N).                     (11)

Summing over dyadic intervals proves

    #{k in E_tau:1<=k<=X}=o(X).                      (12)

One justification of the final step is to fix any positive eta, choose a threshold beyond which every dyadic count is at most eta times its lower endpoint, sum the resulting geometric series, and then let eta tend to zero. The finitely many earlier indices contribute O(1).

Therefore E_tau has natural density zero for every fixed 0<tau<2log(1+sqrt(2)). The result applies to any pole-order function indexed by every sufficiently large integer k and satisfying the stated growth condition. It is not a density assertion relative to an arbitrarily preselected sparse subsequence.

## 6. Exact content formulation and limitations

Because A_k=|I_(k,m_k)|/g_k, membership in E_tau is exactly

    g_k>=|I_(k,m_k)| exp[-tau k m_k].                 (13)

Thus (13) holds only at a density-zero set of dimensions. Outside that exceptional set the actual primitive leading coefficient exceeds exp(tau k m_k). This is a statement about frequency, not a pointwise lower bound at every dimension.

The stronger pair alternatives (4)-(8) describe the remaining possibility: exceptional outputs may cluster at nearly equal dimensions with one rational root, and distinct exceptional roots require multiplicatively separated dimensions. An infinite sparse sequence is still possible under these bounds.

The inherited raw-leading logarithm is 4k^2 log k+O(k^2). The saving furnished here has order km_k, which is much smaller in the present regime. It does not supply the primitive values required to beat v^(-m_k) for every hypothetical rational denominator v. It also does not exclude a sparse subsequence with favorable primitive values.

No irrationality conclusion, all-degree odd-content estimate, numerical calculation, or independent review of this Stage 5 proof is claimed. The result combines actual analytic localization with classical rational separation and the explicitly proved dominant-factor implication.
