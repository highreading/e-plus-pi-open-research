## Exact candidate adjacent-size identity

Write P_k(t)=I_{k,0}+t I_{k,1}, L_k=lcm(1,3,...,6k-5), D=L_{k+1}, rho=D/L_k. The old 2k-square physical core, but with all lower rows cleared by D, has determinant d_k(t)=rho^k P_k(t). Append the two columns 2k,2k+1 and the two rows C_k,D(R_k+tV_k). In the row order (old core rows,C_k,D(R_k+tV_k)) the enlarged determinant is (-1)^k P_{k+1}(t).

For a in {C,R} and b in {0,1}, let B_ab(t) be the (2k+1)-minor consisting of the complete old core, appended row a at shift k, and appended column 2k+b, in that order. Each is an integer affine polynomial, since the response has rank one. Desnanot--Jacobi gives

 det(B_ab(t))=(-1)^k rho^k P_k(t)P_{k+1}(t).

This is fraction-free, including zero-core cases. Gauss's content lemma would give

 content(det(B_ab(t)))=rho^k G_k G_{k+1}.

All four auxiliary affine minors must be retained. This is not yet a closed recurrence for them or a uniform gcd bound.

## Why scalar order four is not four-column closure

Let v_j be a physical column with k rows in each block, and J=diag(0,...,k-1,0,...,k-1). The inherited recurrence gives

 sum_(ell=0)^4 q_ell(j+J)v_(j+ell)=0.

Its coefficients are diagonal matrices depending on the row index, not scalars. Taking a determinant with fixed other columns therefore generates row-weighted-column minors. The first missing data are det[U,J^m v_j], rather than another scalar moment recurrence.

The algebra Q[J] has dimension k and minimal polynomial product_(i=0)^(k-1)(x-i). Thus the natural weight closure grows with k. This observation only excludes a universal fixed-dimensional row-weight module: it does NOT rule out special nonlinear identities among the actual determinants. A precise scoped obstruction and all singular factors still need to be written and checked.
