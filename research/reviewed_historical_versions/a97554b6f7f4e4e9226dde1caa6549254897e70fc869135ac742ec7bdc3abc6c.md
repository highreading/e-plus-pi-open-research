## 2. Division-free addendum and the first 3-adic block

The final addendum is incorporated explicitly:

    e_0=0,
    e_(r+1)=(2r+2)(2r+1)e_r+2r(r+1),
    c_r=e_r+(1-(-1)^r)/2,
    b_r=((2r+2)(2r+1)+1)e_r+2r(r+1)+1.

Here e_r=(D_(2r)-1)/2. Substitution in the derangement recurrence proves the first equation, and the remaining two recover the signed and endpoint moments. Only additions and multiplications are required. In particular generation modulo any power of 3 never requires a nonunit inverse.

Modulo 3, the multiplier (2r+2)(2r+1) takes values 2,0,0 at r=0,1,2 modulo 3, while 2r(r+1) takes values 0,1,0. Thus e_r has period (0,0,1), and

    c_r: 0,1,1,1,0,2 (period 6),
    b_r: 1,2,2 (period 3).

The c recurrence is

    c_(r+4)+c_(r+3)-c_(r+1)-c_r=0 modulo 3.          (1)

Its polynomial is R(y)=(y^3-1)(y+1)=(y-1)^3(y+1) over F_3. The leading 4 by 4 c Hankel determinant is 2 modulo 3: the displayed period gives the matrix

    [[0,1,1,1],[1,1,1,0],[1,1,0,2],[1,0,2,0]],

whose determinant is 5. The earlier draft incorrectly stated 7; the corrected determinant is still a unit modulo 3, as required. Consequently rho_0 modulo 3 is nondegenerate on the quotient F_3[y]/(R). This is an actual-moment assertion, including the endpoint atom.

For context the division-free recurrence also proves e_(r+s)=e_r and b_(r+s)=b_r modulo s for s=3^d. At r=s-1 the next state e_s is 0 modulo s, and the coefficients subsequently repeat modulo s. Since s is odd, c_(r+s)-c_r=(-1)^r modulo s; hence (y^s-1)(y+1) annihilates the c functional modulo 3^d. This higher-depth annihilation does not assert nondegeneracy of the associated larger quotient: that missing unit claim cannot be imported from the first block.

## 3. Forced endpoint divisibility after the actual primitive normalization

For every integer polynomial P satisfying rho_0(P y^i)=0 for i=0,1,2,3,

    R divides P modulo 3.                            (2)

Proof: divide P by the monic R over F_3. By (1), multiples of R pair to zero against every monomial. The remainder of degree below 4 therefore pairs to zero against 1,y,y^2,y^3. The invertible displayed Hankel matrix makes that remainder zero.

Apply this to the ACTUAL primitive monomial Q_0. For every k>=4,

    Q_0=(y-1)^3(y+1) A modulo 3,
    Q_0(-1)=0 modulo 3.                              (3)

In particular the endpoint-unit premise at p=3 is impossible after monomial primitive normalization. This is stronger than the fact that unnormalized endpoint determinants vanish modulo 3.

There is also a quantified overestimate in the previous norm/endpoint divisor. Descending row operations using (1) on the bordered determinant U_j make its last j-4 moment rows divisible by 3. Hence every coefficient of U_j is divisible by 3^(j-4) for j>=4. Orthogonality gives

    H_j H_(j+1)t_j/g=rho_0(Q_0 U_j),

so

    v_3(M(t)/g)>=k-4.                               (4)

Together with (3), for every k>=5,

    3g divides M_end(t).                             (5)

Thus the actual norm/endpoint bound systematically has at least one spare factor of 3 in this range; it cannot identify g exactly at this prime. This does not claim the spare factor is exactly 3.

For completeness, b periodicity similarly gives 3^(j-3)|B_j for j>=3. This lower bound is not substituted for a primitive projection bound.

## 4. Exact bookkeeping of norm-forced support and endpoint cancellation

Write gamma=v_3(g), tau_j=v_3(t_j), nu_j=v_3(H_j H_(j+1)), and beta_j=v_3(B_j), with infinity for a zero t_j. Since t is primitive, min tau_j=0. The preceding pairing identity gives the actual support restriction

    tau_j+nu_j>=gamma+j-4,  k<=j<2k.                 (6)

In particular, a unit coordinate t_j can occur only if nu_j>=gamma+j-4. This is a necessary restriction, not a determination of support or of its nonzero residues. The exact cofactor accounting is

    tau_j=v_3(C_j)-v_3(d_E),
    v_3(d_E)=min_j v_3(C_j).                          (7)

Dividing the raw reduced matrix or its cofactor vector by a guessed common 3-power would not determine t.

Put mu=min_j(tau_j+beta_j) and J_min={j:tau_j+beta_j=mu}. The endpoint sum has valuation at least gamma+1 by (3). Therefore if mu<gamma+1, its leading residue MUST cancel:

    sum_(j in J_min) (-1)^j
       (t_j/3^tau_j)(B_j/3^beta_j)=0 modulo 3.        (8)

More precisely the entire sum divided by 3^mu vanishes modulo 3^(gamma+1-mu). If mu>=gamma+1, the endpoint divisibility already holds termwise and no leading cancellation follows. The distinction is necessary. The norm-support restriction (6) supplies neither mu nor the unit residues in (8).

These statements answer part of the requested cancellation question: cancellation is forced whenever endpoint summands reach below the primitive endpoint floor; otherwise their valuations suffice. What is unresolved is which case the ACTUAL normalized cofactors occupy, and how much cancellation persists beyond that floor. Positivity of B_j provides no answer over F_3.

## 5. The conversion index has a bounded denominator depth

Expand Q_0=sum_(j<2k) alpha_j ell_j over Q. Define

    d=-min_(j<2k) v_3(alpha_j).

Since Q_0 is primitive in monomial coordinates and mathcal B has integer entries, the minimum is at most 0. The extraction formula

    alpha_j=(4j+1)lambda(Q_0 ell_j)/16^j

shows d<=floor(log_3(8k-3)): every odd denominator in the integral is at most 8k-3. Thus d>=0 and the exact conversion identities are

    v_3(theta_L)=v_3(Delta_(2k))-d,
    v_3(zeta_W)=d+min_(j<k-1)v_3(alpha_j),
    v_3(Lambda)=gamma+v_3(Delta_(2k))
                           +min_(j<k-1)v_3(alpha_j). (9)

These track Lambda, the monomial content and the entire conversion index separately. They are not a replacement of theta_L by a unit.

For low j<k-1, the actual lower equation gives

    alpha_j=(4j+1)f((y+1)Q_0 ell_j)/(4*16^j),         (10)

so every low alpha_j is 3-integral. This uses the full lower functional K; it is not a statement about raw signed moment determinants.

## 6. Ternary Legendre collapse and the infinite subfamily k=3^a

For a nonnegative integer n, let h(n) be the sum of 3^r over ternary digits of n equal to 1. Then

    ell_j(y)=y^(h(2j)/2) modulo 3.                    (11)

The exponent is an integer because 2j is even and hence has an even number of ternary digits equal to 1. To prove (11), use

    2^n P_n(x)=sum_r binom(n,r)^2 (x-1)^(n-r)(x+1)^r.

The binomial congruences from (1+z)^3=1+z^3 in characteristic 3 separate the ternary digits, giving P_n(x)=product_r P_(n_r)(x^(3^r)) modulo 3. Here P_0=1, P_1=x and P_2=1 modulo 3. Also 4^j=1 modulo 3. This proves the collapse. In particular the exponent in (11) is never 1.

Now set k=s=3^a with a>=3, and m=(3s-1)/2. Multiplying the ACTUAL lower equation K(Q_0 y^i)=0 by 3^(a+1) and reducing modulo 3 gives

    [y^(m-i)]Q_0=0 modulo 3,  0<=i<=s-2.            (12)

Indeed 3s is the only odd denominator of valuation a+1 among 1,...,6s-5, and the factorial contribution is integral. Hence the central monomial coefficients with indices

    (s+3)/2 <= r <= (3s-1)/2

all vanish modulo 3. This statement holds after primitive monomial normalization.

For every j<2s, let r_j=h(2j)/2. If 2j<3s, then r_j<=(3s-1)/4<=s-2. If 2j>=3s, then r_j>m. Therefore the coefficient of y^m in Q_0 ell_j vanishes modulo 3: in the first case it is a constrained central coefficient from (12), and in the second it is zero for degree reasons.

The only possible odd denominator with valuation a+1 in lambda(Q_0 ell_j) is again 3s, now because the maximum denominator is 8s-3<9s. Its numerator has just been shown divisible by 3. Thus every alpha_j has valuation at least -a, proving

    0<=d<=a,  k=3^a, a>=3.                          (13)

This is an unconditional bound on the ACTUAL conversion depth in this infinite subfamily, using the lower matching equations as well as the Legendre index structure. It is not yet an unconditional upper bound on v_3(zeta_W), because the low coefficients in (9) may all have positive valuation.

The exact basis carry costs in this subfamily are

    v_3(Delta_(2s))=a*s+ceil(s/4),
    v_3(Delta_s)=floor(a*s/2),
    v_3(Delta_(s-1))=floor(a*s/2)-a,
    v_3(L)=a+1.                                     (14)

For the first two identities sum floor(4j/3^r)-2floor(2j/3^r) over j. For r<=a a full residue block of length 3^r contributes (3^r-1)/2. The single remaining r=a+1 contribution is ceil(3s/2)-ceil(3s/4) when j<2s, and s-ceil(3s/4) when j<s. These simplify to (14). Finally v_3 binom(4s-4,2s-2)=a for a>=2 by the a ternary carries when doubling 2s-2, giving the third identity. In particular theta_L retains the large cost

    a*s+ceil(s/4)-a <= v_3(theta_L)
                         <=a*s+ceil(s/4).            (15)

## 7. A concrete conditional projection bound and its missing premise

Let q_1=[y]Q_0. Modulo 3 the factorial functional obeys

    f((y+1)P)=2[y]P

for every integer polynomial P: its constant moment is 1+2=0 modulo 3, its linear moment is 2+24=2, and all higher moments vanish. Apply this to (10), then use (11) and the fact r_j is never 1:

    alpha_j=2(j+1)q_1 * 1_(r_j=0) modulo 3,
                                    0<=j<k-1.        (16)

In particular alpha_0=2q_1 modulo 3. Thus

    min_(j<k-1)v_3(alpha_j)=0 iff q_1 is a 3-unit.     (17)

Combining (13) and (17) gives the NEW CONDITIONAL bound

    if k=3^a, a>=3, and 3 does not divide [y]Q_0,
    then v_3(zeta_W)=d<=a.                           (18)

If 3 divides q_1, all low alpha_j are divisible by 3 and v_3(zeta_W)>=d+1. No upper bound for their excess depth is proved here.

The missing premise in (18) concerns the ACTUAL reduced cofactors with all normalizations included:

    q_1=(sum_j C_j U_j'(0))/(d_E*g).

To establish the unit condition one must prove

    v_3(sum_j C_j U_j'(0))=v_3(d_E)+gamma.            (19)

The endpoint support and norm restrictions in Section 4 do not prove (19). Nor do the first-layer equations determine it: modulo 3, both R(y) and y^2 R(y) satisfy the four upper equations and the central-coefficient zero conditions (12) for s>=27, while their linear coefficients respectively are a unit and zero. These are explicit candidates for the FIRST-LAYER CONGRUENCES ONLY, not actual rational kernel polynomials or counterexamples to (18)'s possible premise. Higher-depth, saturated lower constraints and the normalized cofactors must distinguish them.

This is the precise stopping point: obtaining a raw determinant valuation law or restating the old sandwich would not settle (19), the higher low-output cancellation when it fails, or the endpoint residue data (8).
