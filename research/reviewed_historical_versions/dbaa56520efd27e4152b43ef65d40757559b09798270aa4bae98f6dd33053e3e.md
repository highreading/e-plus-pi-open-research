# Diagonal root operator: explicit bound and failure of the small-reference strategy

Author: Child 3. Status: author derivation, not independent verification. This is a bounded root-operator step for canonical m=k, not another endpoint determinant asymptotic. S is the actual e+pi. No numerical root scan or new computation is used.

## 1. Outcome

For k>=131072, the COMPLETE root matrix H admits an explicit, although growing, all-root disk

    rho(H-SI) <= R_k,
    log R_k = 2k log k + O(k).                              (1)

An elementary finite formula for R_k is given in (18). This is not a shrinking disk and does not yield arithmetic separation.

More importantly, the natural positive compact-Gram similarity has the exact form

    L^(1/2)(H-SI)L^(-1/2) = -A0 + N,
    A0=L^(1/2)J^(-1)L^(1/2),                               (2)

where A0 is real symmetric but indefinite. It is NOT a small reference operator. For d=k-1>=64,

    rho(A0)=||A0|| >= 4^d/[144 d^2(d+1)^2].                 (3)

This is a Rayleigh-quotient bound, not a trace asymptotic. Thus a genuinely small additive remainder cannot be used to infer that every root is close to S by perturbing this reference. In fact, an explicit small-remainder hypothesis implies a far root; see (21).

A second exact obstruction is

    det(I-Y)=theta_k in [exp(-192e^4),1],
    ||I-Y|| >= exp(-192e^4/k),                              (4)

where Y is defined in Section 3 and -A0(I-Y) is the operator in (2). Consequently the submultiplicative estimate ||A0|| ||I-Y|| cannot prove a small diagonal disk either. The full operator might still enjoy cancellations unavailable to these estimates. No lower bound on the spectral radius of the actual H-SI is asserted without the explicit remainder hypothesis in (21).

The task stops after this quantitative operator reduction. The exact remaining requirement is control of the FULL product A0(I-Y), or a different structured localization argument, rather than positivity of one Gram or smallness of a determinant.

Put h=y+1, d=k-1. All matrices here have indices 0<=i,j<k. The exact canonical pencil is

    beta(T)=det[[C,G],[B+TJ,D]],
    M_ij=mu(h^(i+j)), G_ij=mu(h^(k+i+j)),
    J_ij=nu_k(h^(i+j)), C=M-J,
    B_ij=R_k(h^(i+j)), D_ij=R_k(h^(k+i+j)).                (5)

Here mu is the pushforward of exp(-t)dt, t>=0, by y=(1-t)^2. The complete endpoint functional is

    nu_k(f)=sum_(r=0)^d a_r f^(r)(-1),
    a_r=2^r binom(d,r)(2(d-r)-1)!!/(2d-1)!!.

The complete rational remainder R_k contains BOTH the factorial endpoint contribution and the rational-kernel endpoint contribution. In particular the low block B in (5) is not dropped. The change from y to h is monic triangular, with determinant one.

G is positive definite. J is invertible, and

    gamma_k=d!/(1/2)_d=4^d/binom(2d,d),
    det J=(-1)^binom(k,2) gamma_k^k,
    beta_k=(-1)^k det G det J.

Schur elimination gives

    beta(T)/beta_k=det(TI-H),
    H=J^(-1)(D G^(-1)C-B).                                (6)

Thus the eigenvalues of H are precisely all complex roots of the actual canonical polynomial, with multiplicity. Passing to its primitive integer scalar multiple changes neither these roots nor H.

Define the two ACTUAL compact Grams

    L_ij=sigma_k(h^(i+j)), K_ij=sigma_k(h^(k+i+j)),
    dsigma_k(y)=[exp(sqrt(y))+c_k(1+y)^(-k)]dy/(2sqrt(y)),
    c_k=4^k/binom(2k-2,k-1).

The full moment identities, with S=e+pi, say exactly

    L=eC+B+SJ, K=eG+D.

Consequently

    H-SI=J^(-1)(K G^(-1)C-L).                             (7)

The cancellation of eC in (7) follows from these full identities. It is not an omission of the low rational block or an assertion that R_k can be replaced by an isolated pi contribution.

Under y=x^2 the measure becomes

    dsigma_k=[e^x+c_k(1+x^2)^(-k)]dx, 0<=x<=1.

Write s_k=sigma_k(1). The elementary full half-line beta integral gives

    c_k integral_0^infinity(1+x^2)^(-k)dx=2pi,
    0<s_k<=e-1+2pi<9.                                    (8)

Also L>0 and L<=K<=2^k L, since 1<=h<=2 on the compact interval.

## 3. Positive Gram similarity, exact determinant, and missing norm

Set

    A0=L^(1/2)J^(-1)L^(1/2),
    Z=L^(-1/2)K L^(-1/2),
    X=L^(1/2)G^(-1)C L^(-1/2), Y=ZX,
    N=A0Y.

Then Z is symmetric positive definite, I<=Z<=2^k I. The matrix A0 is symmetric, but J and hence A0 are indefinite for k>=2. Equation (7) becomes

    L^(1/2)(H-SI)L^(-1/2)=-A0(I-Y)=-A0+N.                 (9)

Neither X nor Y nor N is claimed self-adjoint. Symmetry of the two positive Grams does not imply symmetry of their ordered product with C and J^(-1).

The inherited complete signed comparison gives

    beta(S)=(-1)^k theta_k det G det L,
    exp(-192e^4)<=theta_k<=1

for k>=131072. On the other hand, swapping block columns in the compact pencil [C,G;L,K] gives

    beta(S)=(-1)^k det G det(L-KG^(-1)C).

Hence

    det(I-Y)=theta_k.                                     (10)

This retains the full error, not only the top coefficient. The inequality |det Q|<=||Q||^k for any k-by-k matrix proves (4). It does not prove that I-Y is well conditioned or close to identity.

For any desired r_k, the explicit positive-metric operator inequality

    [A0(I-Y)]^*[A0(I-Y)] <= r_k^2 I                       (11)

would imply rho(H-SI)<=r_k. It is an UNPROVED sufficient inequality, not a necessary condition on the spectral radius. Using ||A0|| ||I-Y|| instead loses the cancellations needed in (11); equations (3)-(4) show that this particular scalar product of norms is necessarily exponentially large for growing k.

## 4. Exact inverse of the complete jet matrix

This step is finite algebra using every jet coefficient. Let u_l=binom(2l,l)/4^l, u_0=1, so gamma_k=1/u_d. For 0<=r<=d,

    nu_k(h^r)=r!a_r,
    nu_k(h^(d-l))/gamma_k=u_l, 0<=l<=d.

Let R be the reversal matrix, and let Q be the nilpotent lower shift with Q_(i+1,i)=1. Then

    RJ=gamma_k (I-Q)^(-1/2),
    J^(-1)=gamma_k^(-1)(I-Q)^(1/2)R.                      (12)

All power series in Q terminate at degree d. If

    (1-z)^(1/2)=sum_(l>=0) b_l z^l,

then b_0=1 and b_l=-u_l/(2l-1)<0 for l>=1. It follows that

    (J^(-1))_ij=gamma_k^(-1)b_(i+j-d) if i+j>=d,
    (J^(-1))_ij=0 if i+j<d.

The finite absolute coefficient sum is 2-u_d<=2: indeed sum_(l=0)^d b_l=u_d, by the binomial coefficient recurrence. As ||Q^l||<=1 and R is orthogonal,

    ||J^(-1)||<=2/gamma_k.                                (13)

This bound shows that the obstruction below is not an accidentally omitted jet factorial. The exact factorial normalization is already absorbed in gamma_k.

## 5. Exponentially large symmetric reference: a Rayleigh proof

Let v(h)=(1,h,...,h^d)^T and define W:R^k->L2(sigma_k) by Wq=v(h)^T q. Then W^*W=L. The finite-rank self-adjoint operator W J^(-1)W^* has the same nonzero eigenvalues as A0; using W L^(-1/2) identifies it isometrically with A0 on ran(W).

Take the test function 1, which belongs to ran(W). Put

    ell_i=integral h^i dsigma_k, so W^*1=(ell_0,...,ell_d)^T.

By (12),

    <1,W J^(-1)W^*1>
      =gamma_k^(-1) sum_(l=0)^d b_l
                     sum_(i+j=d+l) ell_i ell_j.

Every ell_i is positive, and all b_l for l>=1 are negative. Retaining just the last negative term bounds this ABOVE by

    u_d sum_(i+j=d)ell_i ell_j
      -[u_d^2/(2d-1)] ell_d^2
    <= (d+1)s_k^2 2^d - [u_d^2/(2d-1)]ell_d^2.           (14)

Here ell_i<=s_k 2^i and u_d<=1. On the other hand, the exponential part of sigma alone gives

    ell_d>=integral_0^1(1+x^2)^d dx
          >=2^d integral_0^1 x^d dx=2^d/(d+1).

The elementary central-binomial bound u_d>=1/(2sqrt(d)), d>=1, follows by induction from u_1=1/2 and

    u_(d+1)/u_d=(2d+1)/(2d+2)>=sqrt(d/(d+1)).

Divide (14) by ||1||^2=s_k and use s_k<9. The Rayleigh quotient q_k satisfies

    q_k <=9(d+1)2^d -4^d/[72d^2(d+1)^2].                 (15)

For d>=64,

    2^d>=1296d^2(d+1)^3.

At d=64 the right side is below 2^44, whereas the left side is 2^64. The ratio of the right side at successive d is at most (1+1/d)^5<2 for d>=64, which proves the inequality thereafter. Therefore (15) is at most

    -4^d/[144d^2(d+1)^2].

The variational principle for the symmetric A0 proves (3). In particular A0 has a negative eigenvalue of this size; -A0 has a positive eigenvalue at least this large. This is a spectral property of the reference itself, invariant under any further similarity of that reference.

## 6. One quantitative upper reduction for the complete operator

We now give a deliberately conservative explicit full upper bound, and stop rather than obscure the conditioning loss.

First, (8), (13), and positivity imply

    ||A0||<=||J^(-1)|| tr L
       <=2s_k(4^k-1)/(3gamma_k)
       <= a_k:=6(4^k-1)/gamma_k.                          (16)

For comparison of G with L, use exactly the elementary polynomial evaluation bound in the complete signed-error source, its equation (7). For p of degree below k, A=a k^2, a=1/(48e^4), and I=[k^2,4k^2], it gives

    integral_I |p(y+1)|^2dy
       >=3*16^(-(k-1)) sup_[-1,A]|p(y+1)|^2.

For k>=131072, A>=1. The mu tail density on I is at least exp(-2k-1)/(4k), and h^k>=k^(2k) there. Meanwhile the compact L norm is at most 9 times the displayed supremum. Hence the inherited estimate directly yields

    G>=g_k L,
    g_k=k^(2k)exp(-2k-1)/[12k16^(k-1)].                   (17)

This is a congruence comparison, not a statement that unscaled monomial entries are well conditioned.

Here is a fully explicit bound for the other relative matrix. In the h coefficient basis,

    ||L^(-1)||<=4k^2 144^(k-1).

To justify it, L>=G_gamma/2, where G_gamma is the gamma Gram in the same h basis. Its inverse is the sum of outer products of the coefficient vectors of the gamma-orthonormal polynomials p_i(y)=sqrt((4i+1)/2)P_(2i)(sqrt(y)). The ordinary Legendre recurrence bounds the sum of absolute coefficients of P_n by (1+sqrt(2))^n. For n=2i this is below 6^i. Replacing y by h-1 increases the coefficient sum by at most 2^i. Thus each squared coefficient norm is at most 2k 144^i, and their sum is at most 2k^2 144^(k-1). Loewner inversion gives the claimed bound for L^(-1).

For C=M-J, its entries satisfy

    |C_ij|<=4^(k-1)(4k-4)!+2k-1.

Indeed mu(h^r)=sum_(s=0)^r binom(r,s)D_(2s)<=2^r(2r)! for r<=2k-2, including r=0. The jet entries have modulus at most gamma_k<=2k-1, or are zero. Therefore

    ||L^(-1/2)C L^(-1/2)|| <= eta_k,
    eta_k=4k^3 144^(k-1)[4^(k-1)(4k-4)!+2k-1].

Using (17),

    ||X||<=eta_k/g_k, ||Y||<=2^k eta_k/g_k,
    ||N||<=a_k 2^k eta_k/g_k.

Finally the entire actual operator obeys

    rho(H-SI)<=||L^(1/2)(H-SI)L^(-1/2)||
       <= R_k:=a_k[1+2^k eta_k/g_k].                     (18)

Every term of (18) is explicit in k; no unknown arithmetic content or numerical root computation occurs. Factorial bounds or Stirling immediately give

    log R_k=2k log k+O(k).

The estimate grows and is not an approximation theorem. It also does not show that N is small. Its factor eta_k/g_k reflects the mismatch between compact-normalized low moments and tail-normalized moments. Refining separate entry bounds further is outside this bounded step; the obstruction in Section 5 already shows that a small reference-plus-small-remainder proof is not available in this splitting.

## 7. What a small remainder would actually imply

For the exact decomposition (9), the usual resolvent argument for the symmetric -A0 gives

    spec(H-SI) subset union_(lambda in spec(-A0))
                            {z:|z-lambda|<=||N||}.        (19)

This is an upper spectral inclusion, not a pairing of ordered eigenvalues for a nonselfadjoint perturbation. In particular one may not apply the Hermitian Weyl eigenvalue inequality directly to N.

A simple cluster-count refinement supplies a useful conditional converse. For any Hermitian A and arbitrary E of size k,

    rho(A+E)>=||A||-(2k-1)||E||                           (20)

whenever the right side is positive. To see this, enlarge the disk radii slightly above ||E||. The connected component of the union of disks containing an extreme eigenvalue of absolute value ||A|| has at most k centers; overlapping disks connect consecutive centers at distance at most twice the radius. Every point of that component therefore has absolute value at least ||A||-(2k-1)radius. Along A+tE, 0<=t<=1, all eigenvalues stay inside the same union by the resolvent estimate. Each separated component preserves its algebraic eigenvalue count, so the extreme component contains at least one eigenvalue of A+E. Let the enlargement tend to zero. The argument also covers coincident centers; no diagonalizability of A+E is used.

For A=-A0 and E=N, let

    b_k=4^(k-1)/[144(k-1)^2 k^2].

Equations (3),(20) imply the CONDITIONAL statement

    if ||N||<=b_k/[2(2k-1)],
    then rho(H-SI)>=b_k/2.                               (21)

No premise of (21) is asserted for the actual N. Its meaning is that a very small additive correction to this natural symmetric reference would force an exponentially far root, rather than put all roots near S. To obtain a small actual disk in the compact metric, large directional cancellation would be required; merely invoking positivity and perturbative smallness has the wrong reference scale.

The norm lower bound ||-A0+N||>=||A0||-||N|| is another immediate obstruction to (11) for a small N, but alone would not be a spectral-radius bound. Equation (21) explicitly distinguishes these two issues.

## 8. Coprimality and precise stopping point

The upper disk (18) is centered at the same actual S for every k and grows with k. It gives no disjoint root sets and hence no coprimality theorem at separated dimensions. Even shrinking disks centered at a common S would be nested rather than disjoint; an additional all-root lower error bound or another algebraic separation argument would be needed to deduce actual coprimality. The inherited product estimate and nonzero beta(S) are not such an individual lower bound.

This report establishes an explicit full norm upper bound, an exponentially large eigenvalue of the natural symmetric reference, and the exact determinant obstruction (10). It does NOT establish a far root or a shrinking disk for the actual matrix without further hypotheses. It does not refute the possibility of a different symmetrizer or a direct structured estimate on the full H-SI.

The useful next interface, if pursued, is a proof of (11) for the FULL operator with a useful r_k, or a resolvent/localization estimate that uses correlations between A0 and Y rather than bounding them separately. One must retain B via L=eC+B+SJ and retain both endpoints in K=eG+D. A determinant comparison, a positive G or L, or the small-m root-annulus theorem cannot supply this interface in the diagonal regime.

No old small-m theorem, fixed/slow-growing exclusion, scalar endpoint asymptotic, determinant leading constant, primitive-content threshold, actual gcd growth, or irrationality claim is reproved here. This bounded operator-reduction task ends at this explicit obstruction and upper bound.
