# Smith pivots, local root groups, and the actual centered invariant

Author: Main. Status: conditional author proofs from the exact Stage 10 reduction; not independently reviewed. This document consolidates the recent local Newton-polygon deductions and proves a further simple-root consequence of the deepest-pivot condition. No occurrence theorem for the required actual residue conditions, global content estimate, or irrationality conclusion is asserted.

## 1. Actual polynomial and exact normalization

The starting source is work/parallel_batch_01/main/DIAGONAL_LOCAL_CONTENT_REDUCTION_STAGE10.md. Use its canonical diagonal family m=k, with k>=2 and h=y+1:

    beta(T)=det[[C,G],[B+TJ,D]],
    C=M-J,
    M_ij=mu(h^(i+j)),
    G_ij=mu(h^(k+i+j)),
    J_ij=nu_k(h^(i+j)),
    B_ij=R_k(h^(i+j)),
    D_ij=R_k(h^(k+i+j)).

Every block is k by k. The rational remainder R_k includes both endpoint contributions. All changes from monomials to the h basis have determinant one. The exact leading coefficient is

    beta_k=(-1)^k det J det G,
    det G=F*_k>0.

Let delta be the complete physical integer clearer, I_j=delta beta_j, and g=gcd(I_0,...,I_k)>0. Orient the primitive polynomial P to have positive leading coefficient

    A=|I_k|/g.

Fix a prime p>6k with a=v_p(F*_k)>0. The complete clearer delta and det J are p-adic units, and all displayed matrix entries are p-integral. Put d=v_p(g). Then

    v_p(A)=a-d,  0<=d<=a.

The analytic nonvanishing P(S)!=0, where S=e+pi, is needed only for subsequent approximation applications and is inherited for k>=131072. The local algebra here needs only the exact construction and k>=2.

Stage 10 gives an invertible p-adic congruence splitting G into a unit block G0 and an r by r nonunit block E, where r is the nullity of G modulo p. It gives exactly

    chi(z)=z^k beta(1/z)=u(z)f(z),
    f(z)=det[E-zQ(z)],                                  (1)

where u is a unit in Z_p[[z]] and Q has p-integral formal coefficients. Every nonunit pivot remains inside E. Specifically, if W^T G W=diag(G0,E) and

    K(z)=W^T C(J+zB)^(-1)D W,

partitioned by these blocks, then

    Q(z)=K11(z)+z K10(z)(G0-zK00(z))^(-1)K01(z).

All inverses have unit constant determinants. Empty-block conventions apply when r=k. In particular, if Z is the last r columns of W,

    Q(0)=Z^T C J^(-1)D Z.

The factor u includes the exact congruence determinant, so no nonunit basis factor is omitted.

## 2. The first coefficient of minimal valuation

Write f_j=[z^j]f. By the triangular unit-series transformation in (1),

    d=min_(0<=j<=k)v_p(f_j).

Define

    t=min{j:0<=j<=k and v_p(f_j)=d}.

Multiplication by u preserves the first index of minimal valuation as well as the minimum itself. Indeed, after division by p^d, every earlier coefficient is zero modulo p, while the coefficient at t is multiplied by u(0), a unit. Thus t is also the order at zero of the reduction of chi/p^d.

The Newton polygon of chi/p^d has exactly t roots of positive valuation, with multiplicity, and their valuations sum to a-d. Its constant coefficient has valuation a-d, its first coefficient of valuation zero is at t, and all other coefficients have nonnegative valuation. Its negative-slope portion therefore runs from (0,a-d) to (t,0).

Reciprocation proves the corresponding facts for beta:

    exactly t roots have negative p-adic valuation,
    sum over these roots of -v_p(root)=a-d=v_p(A).     (2)

If beta has a zero root, it belongs to the other group; chi can then have degree below k without affecting (2). Roots and multiplicities are understood in an algebraic closure of Q_p.

Every repeated root belongs entirely to one of the two valuation groups. Hence every algebraic multiplicity is at most max(t,k-t). In particular:

- 0<t<k excludes a pure power of one linear polynomial.
- If k is even and t=k/2, no root has multiplicity greater than k/2.

These statements do not assume real-rootedness.

When d=0, reduction of (1) gives f(z)=(-z)^r det Q(z) modulo p. If ell is the first index at which det Q(z) has a unit coefficient, then

    t=r+ell,

with ell<=k-r. Thus a singular Q(0) does not end the local test: later coefficients can still supply a nontrivial root split.

## 3. Bounds using all nonunit Smith pivots

Let the Smith valuations of E be

    1<=alpha_1<=...<=alpha_r,
    H_j=alpha_1+...+alpha_j, H_0=0,
    H_r=a.

Every minor of E of size s is divisible by p^(H_s). Expanding det[E-zQ(z)] by the columns selected from zQ shows, for 0<=j<=r,

    v_p(f_j)>=H_(r-j).

A contribution to degree j uses at most j columns from zQ, and therefore contains a minor of E of size at least r-j. This proves the bound even though Q depends on z. Multiplication by the integral unit series u preserves it, because earlier coefficients have stronger divisibility. Since delta is a p-unit,

    v_p(I_(k-j))>=H_(r-j),  0<=j<=r.                 (3)

This is a statement about the complete coefficients. It requires no invertibility hypothesis on Q(0).

Suppose now det Q(0) is a unit. Stage 10 implies d=0, t=r, and the endpoints of the relevant Newton polygon are (0,a) and (r,0). Its coefficient points lie above the convex polygon through (j,H_(r-j)). If the r positive valuations of the reciprocal roots are ordered as

    0<lambda_1<=...<=lambda_r,

the Newton-polygon comparison gives

    sum_(i=1)^j lambda_i>=H_j, 1<=j<=r,
    sum_(i=1)^r lambda_i=a.                           (4)

Equivalently, the sum of the largest j denominator valuations is at most the sum of the largest j Smith valuations. One way to see the direction is to evaluate the Newton polygons at the integer abscissa r-j: the ordinate of the actual polygon there is the sum of the smallest j positive root valuations.

If all alpha_i equal alpha, (4) forces all lambda_i=alpha. For r=1 the unique negative-valuation root is simple, belongs to Q_p, and has valuation -a. Its rationality over Q_p follows either from Hensel lifting of the degree-one residual polynomial or from the invariant singleton root group.

If det Q(0) is a unit and 0<r<k, a pure rational power is excluded. If r=k, such a power would require k dividing a, since all its roots would have the same integer negative valuation. These are conditional tests, not established properties of the actual family at a supplied prime.

## 4. Residue conditions attaining the pivot polygon

Because p is odd and E is symmetric, an invertible congruence over Z_p diagonalizes it as

    E=diag(p^alpha_1 epsilon_1,...,p^alpha_r epsilon_r),

with epsilon_i units. This follows by choosing an entry of smallest valuation; if it is off-diagonal, an elementary basis replacement produces a diagonal entry of that valuation because 2 is a unit. Orthogonal elimination and induction complete the diagonalization. Apply the same congruence to Q and include its unit determinant factor in u.

Write Q0=Q(0) in these coordinates. For each distinct pivot depth b let

    T_b={i:alpha_i>=b}.

Suppose all trailing principal blocks Q0[T_b,T_b] are invertible modulo p. This includes the full Q0 at the smallest depth.

At a strict gap alpha_i<alpha_(i+1), put j=r-i. Division of the degree-j coefficient by p^(H_i) gives the residue

    (-1)^j(epsilon_1...epsilon_i)
        det Q0[{i+1,...,r},{i+1,...,r}].              (5)

Indeed, terms with fewer than j selected Q columns have greater valuation. Among terms with exactly j such columns, the strict gap makes the first i diagonal E entries the unique subset of minimum valuation. Higher coefficients of Q do not enter a term using j columns at degree exactly j. Earlier coefficients multiplied by positive-degree terms of u have greater valuation as well.

The assumed unit residues attain every vertex of the pivot polygon. Therefore the negative root valuations of beta are exactly

    -alpha_1,...,-alpha_r,

including multiplicities. Invertibility of the full Q0 alone is insufficient. For the abstract matrices E=diag(p,p^3) and Q0=[[0,1],[1,0]], det(E-zQ0)=p^4-z^2 and both reciprocal root valuations equal 2. This is only an algebraic illustration of the missing minor condition, not an asserted realization by the actual moments.

## 5. Residual polynomials for each depth

Under the trailing-block conditions, fix a distinct depth b and partition the indices as

    L={alpha_i<b}, I={alpha_i=b}, H={alpha_i>b}.

Define over the residue field

    R_b=Q0[I,I]-Q0[I,H]Q0[H,H]^(-1)Q0[H,I],
    F_b(w)=det[diag(epsilon_i:i in I)-w R_b].

The correction is absent if H is empty. The constant and leading coefficients of F_b are nonzero. The leading assertion follows from the Schur determinant identity for two consecutive trailing blocks.

Put N_b=sum_i min(alpha_i,b). The polynomial

    p^(-N_b) chi(p^b w)

has p-integral coefficients. To check this directly in (1), divide row i of E-p^b wQ(p^b w) by p^min(alpha_i,b); every resulting coefficient is integral. The unit series u(p^b w) remains integral, and equality with chi justifies the polynomial assertion despite the intermediate formal series.

Modulo p, rows indexed by L contain only their unit diagonal entries. Eliminating them leaves the I,H block

    [[diag(epsilon_I)-wQ0[I,I], -wQ0[I,H]],
     [-wQ0[H,I],                 -wQ0[H,H]]].

Its determinant is a nonzero scalar times w^|H| F_b(w). Thus the nonzero roots of F_b are precisely the residual roots for the depth-b group.

If F_b is squarefree, its simple nonzero roots lift by Hensel's lemma and all roots in that valuation group are simple. If F_b is irreducible of degree greater than one, the group forms an irreducible local factor of that degree and has no rational root. Finite residue fields are perfect, so irreducibility here also gives separability.

If every F_b is squarefree, all r negative-valuation roots are simple. Any repeated root then belongs to the remaining group of size k-r. Every root multiplicity is at most max(1,k-r). For k>=2 and r>=ceil(k/2), this excludes every multiplicity greater than k/2.

No theorem in this document supplies these residue conditions on an actual infinite family.

## 6. A single deepest pairing suffices for partial survival

There is a useful weaker hypothesis that does not require Q0 to be invertible. Assume the largest Smith depth

    b=alpha_r

occurs only once and Q0_rr is a unit. For r=1 the uniqueness condition is automatic. The linear coefficient of f is

    -sum_i Q0_ii product_(j!=i) p^alpha_j epsilon_j.

Its unique minimum-valuation term is i=r. Therefore

    v_p(I_(k-1))=a-b,
    d<=a-b,
    v_p(A)>=b.                                      (6)

The contribution from u_1 f_0 has valuation at least a and cannot cancel that minimum. This accounts for both the unit-series multiplier and the complete physical clearer.

Now define the actual integer centered invariant

    D(I)=(k-1)I_(k-1)^2-2k I_k I_(k-2).

If r>=2, (3) implies

    v_p(I_k I_(k-2))>=2a-b-alpha_(r-1)>2(a-b).

If r=1, a=b and integrality gives v_p(I_k I_(k-2))>=a>0. In both cases the first term in D(I) has strictly smaller valuation. Since p>6k, k-1 is a unit. Hence exactly

    v_p(D(I))=2(a-b),
    v_p(D(P))=2(a-b-d),                              (7)

where D(P)=D(I)/g^2; the global orientation sign cancels after squaring.

In particular D(P) is nonzero. A pure rational linear power has zero centered invariant, so this deepest-pairing condition excludes it. It does not by itself exclude all dominant rational factors.

The condition has an intrinsic description before choosing the diagonal coordinates. Let

    L_b={x in Z_p^k:Gx belongs to p^b Z_p^k}.

Because b is the unique largest Smith valuation of G, the image of L_b modulo p is one-dimensional. If z reduces to a generator of this image, the condition is

    z^T C J^(-1)D z !=0 modulo p.                    (8)

Different generator lifts multiply the residue by a nonzero square. Congruence by the unit coordinate changes identifies (8) with Q0_rr. Thus it is a condition on the deepest filtered subspace of the actual moment matrix.

## 7. Further consequence: one simple root at the deepest valuation

Under the hypotheses of Section 6, the largest denominator valuation b occurs for exactly one root, and that root is simple and belongs to Q_p. This assertion does not require d=0 or invertibility of the whole Q0.

To prove it, use the complete coefficients of chi. Its constant coefficient has valuation a, and its linear coefficient has valuation a-b. For 2<=j<=r, uniqueness of the largest depth gives

    H_(r-j)+jb>a.

Indeed the sum of the largest j depths is strictly smaller than jb. For j>r, integrality gives v_p([z^j]chi)+jb>a, since a<=rb and j>r. It follows that

    F(w)=p^(-a)chi(p^b w)

is a polynomial over Z_p whose reduction has degree exactly one and nonzero constant term. The two surviving coefficients are those of degrees zero and one. Its unique nonzero residue root is simple and lifts uniquely to a unit root in Z_p.

There is exactly one unit root over the algebraic closure as well, counted with multiplicity: this is the horizontal segment of length one in the Newton polygon of F. There are no roots of chi of valuation greater than b, because at such a valuation the constant term strictly dominates every other term. Thus beta has exactly one root with valuation -b, it is simple, and every other negative root valuation has smaller absolute value.

For a reduced rational root p0/q with v_p(q)=b, this result forces that root to be simple. It does not say that the unique local root is globally rational.

## 8. Scope, relationship to previous work, and next interface

They give conditional denominator survival, root groups, residue-factor criteria, and centered-invariant valuations. They do not control how often the required primes or residue conditions occur.
