# Complete proportional-pole threshold: an explicit variational formula

Author: Main. Status: author proof conditional on the explicitly inherited complete insertion and nonvanishing identities. This document supplies a fuller justification of the variational calculation in the preceding author note. It has not received independent review. No bound establishing favorable actual primitive content is claimed.

## 1. Actual family and statement

Fix 0<rho<1. Let m=ceil(rho k), r=k-m, and take k sufficiently large that 1<=m<k and the inherited analytic threshold k>=131072 holds. S always denotes the actual e+pi.

The historical construction and complete analytic inputs are:

- work/session_20261002_codex_continuation/main/GENERAL_POLE_RANK_M_PRIMITIVE_INTERFACE.md;
- work/session_20261002_codex_continuation/agent3_analysis/GROWING_POLE_ORDER_SHORT_COMPLETE_PRODUCT.md.

Use the actual rational polynomial beta_(k,m), its full physical clearer delta_(k,m), the integers I_j=delta_(k,m) beta_j, and g=gcd(I_0,...,I_m)>0. Orient its primitive scalar multiple P to have positive leading coefficient A=|I_m|/g. Both beta_m and P(S) are nonzero by the inherited complete theorem. Every endpoint jet and the final coefficient gcd remain included.

Define the positive ratio and exact rational-target threshold

    R_(k,m)=|beta_(k,m)(S)/beta_(k,m),m|,
    T_(k,m)(v)=v^(-m)/R_(k,m), v>=1.

Then exactly |P(S)|<v^(-m) if and only if A<T_(k,m)(v).

The result proved below is

    log T_(k,m)(v)=Phi(rho) k^2-m log v+o_rho(k^2),

where the remainder is independent of v, and

    Phi(rho)=4rho log(1+sqrt(2))-3rho^2 log2,
        0<rho<=1/sqrt(2),

    Phi(rho)=(1+rho)^2 log(1+rho)
             +(1-rho)^2 log(1-rho)
             -2rho^2 log rho+(1-3rho^2)log2,
        1/sqrt(2)<=rho<1.

No uniformity as rho tends to zero or one is asserted. The earlier entropy restriction is unnecessary for this leading-order result. Its removal follows from a different comparison and a proved logarithmic-integral limit, rather than an unsupported extension of the previous kernel bound.

## 2. Reduction retaining the actual compact measure

Let gamma(dy)=y^(-1/2)dy on [0,1],

    c_m=4^m/binom(2m-2,m-1),
    sigma_m(dy)=[exp(sqrt(y))+c_m(1+y)^(-m)]gamma(dy)/2,
    kappa_m=[4^(m-1)/binom(2m-2,m-1)]^m.

For a positive measure w, D_n(w) denotes its degree-below-n monomial Gram determinant. Put

    J_(r,m)=D_r(sigma_m(1+y)^(2m)).

The inherited full-value and leading-coefficient insertion identities give

    R_(k,m)=xi_(k,m) D_k(sigma_m)/(kappa_m J_(r,m)),
    |log xi_(k,m)|<=C, C=192e^4.

Indeed both complete integrals are compared with the same positive tail determinant F*, with multiplicative factors in [exp(-C),1]. Dividing their comparisons gives the displayed bound. This comparison concerns the complete polynomial, not a selected period coordinate.

The exact Cauchy determinant for gamma gives

    log D_k(gamma)=-(log4)k^2+O(k).

The pointwise bounds gamma/2<=sigma_m<=(e+c_m)gamma/2 and c_m<=8sqrt(m) show

    log D_k(sigma_m)=-(log4)k^2+O(k log k).

Also log kappa_m=O(k log k). Thus only the k^2 coefficient of log J_(r,m) remains to be determined.

## 3. Removing the fixed endpoint singularity with a quantified error

Let

    H_(r,m)=D_r((1+y)^(2m)dy on [0,1]).

For every polynomial p of degree below r, q(y)=(1+y)^m p(y) has degree below k. The Legendre evaluation kernel on [0,1] gives

    sup_[0,1]|q|^2<=k^2 integral_0^1 |q|^2dy.

Split the integral with gamma at y=k^(-2). On the lower interval its upper bound is (2/k)sup|q|^2; on the upper interval y^(-1/2)<=k. Therefore

    integral |q|^2dy<=integral |q|^2dgamma
                     <=3k integral |q|^2dy.

These inequalities are Loewner inequalities for the associated Grams. Combining them with the full actual multiplier in sigma_m yields

    2^(-r)H_(r,m)<=J_(r,m)
      <=[3k(e+c_m)/2]^r H_(r,m).

Consequently

    log J_(r,m)=log H_(r,m)+O(k log k).

In particular the actual rational contribution and singular endpoint cannot change the k^2 coefficient. This statement is proved with an explicit determinant comparison; neither contribution is simply omitted.

Under x=(1+y)/2, the measure supplies a scalar 2^(2m+1), and the monomial basis change supplies the squared determinant 2^(r(r-1)). Hence exactly

    H_(r,m)=2^(2mr+r^2) Z_(r,m),
    Z_(r,m)=D_r(x^(2m)dx on [1/2,1]).

## 4. A direct logarithmic-integral limit

Put s=1-rho and let K=[1/2,1]. For positive measures mu of mass s supported on K define

    E_rho(mu)=integral integral log|x-z| dmu(x)dmu(z)
                +2rho integral log x dmu(x).

The logarithmic energy is allowed to be minus infinity. We prove

    lim k^(-2)log Z_(r,m)=max_(mu(K)=s) E_rho(mu).

The positive determinant integral is exactly

    Z_(r,m)=1/r! integral_(K^r)
               product_(i<j)(x_i-x_j)^2 product_i x_i^(2m) dx.

Upper bound: for a fixed positive M replace log|x-z| by the continuous kernel

    L_M(x,z)=max(log|x-z|,-M).

For an empirical measure mu_k=k^(-1)sum_i delta_(x_i), of mass r/k, the exponent in the integral is at most

    k^2 integral integral L_M dmu_k dmu_k
       +2mk integral log x dmu_k+rM.

The last term restores the artificially included diagonal. The integration volume is at most one and 1/r!<=1. Maximize the displayed continuous functional over measures of mass r/k, divide by k^2, and pass to the limit using compactness and m/k->rho, r/k->s.

Finally let M increase to infinity. The maxima of the truncated energies decrease to the maximum of the true energy. To justify this last interchange, choose maximizing measures for an increasing sequence of cutoffs and extract a weakly convergent subsequence. For every fixed cutoff the corresponding limiting continuous energy bounds the limiting maxima from above. Then take the infimum over the fixed cutoffs. The reverse inequality follows by testing any fixed measure. This also gives existence of a maximizer whenever the maximum is finite, as it is for the explicit measure below.

Lower bound: it suffices to test the candidate maximizing measure constructed in Section 5. Write that measure as s times a probability measure nu with density f on its support. It has finite logarithmic energy and finite entropy integral f log f. These facts follow from its explicit square-root endpoint behavior, including the transition case.

Restrict the determinant integral to this support and express Lebesgue integration as expectation under independent samples from nu. Jensen's inequality gives

    log Z_(r,m)>=-log(r!)
      +r(r-1) integral integral log|x-z| dnu(x)dnu(z)
      +2mr integral log x dnu(x)
      -r integral f log f dx.

All terms are well-defined and finite. Division by k^2 gives E_rho(s nu) in the limit. Thus no unproved quantile-spacing estimate is needed. Once maximality of the candidate is established, the upper and lower bounds prove the limit.

Only an o(k^2) remainder follows from this argument. It does not claim an O(k log k) upper remainder for the logarithmic integral.

## 5. Explicit maximizing measure

Set

    a=max(1/2,rho^2),
    domega_a(x)=dx/[pi sqrt((x-a)(1-x))], a<x<1,
    deta_a(x)=(sqrt(a)/x)domega_a(x).

Both omega_a and eta_a have mass one. Define

    mu_rho=omega_a-rho eta_a.

Its density is nonnegative because rho<=sqrt(a), and its mass is s. If a=rho^2, the density simplifies to

    dmu_rho(x)=sqrt(x-rho^2)dx/[pi x sqrt(1-x)].

We check maximality rather than assuming an equilibrium formula. Write U_mu(x)=integral log|x-z|dmu(z), and put

    C_a=log((1-a)/4),
    L_a=2log((1+sqrt(a))/2).

Elementary arcsine integration gives

    U_(omega_a)(x)=C_a on [a,1],
    integral log x domega_a=L_a,
    integral log x deta_a=log a-L_a.

For example these scalar integrals follow by the substitution x=(1+a)/2+(1-a)cos(theta)/2 and then the tangent half-angle substitution. The Stieltjes transforms give a convenient check of the potential identities. With the square root chosen to behave as z at infinity,

    G_omega(z)=1/sqrt((z-a)(z-1)),
    G_eta(z)=[1+sqrt(a)/sqrt((z-a)(z-1))]/z.

Thus U_eta(x)-log x is constant on [a,1]. Integrating it against omega and using symmetry of mutual energy identifies that constant as C_a-L_a.

It follows that on the support,

    U_(mu_rho)(x)+rho log x=s C_a+rho L_a.

If a=1/2 there is no unused part of K. If a=rho^2>1/2, the derivative of this weighted potential on [1/2,a) is

    (1-a/x)/[-sqrt((a-x)(1-x))]>0.

It is therefore strictly below the support constant there. All maximizing potential inequalities are satisfied.

For completeness, these inequalities imply global maximality. If nu is another measure of mass s with finite energy and delta=nu-mu_rho, expansion of the energy difference gives

    E_rho(nu)-E_rho(mu_rho)
      =2 integral [U_(mu_rho)+rho log x] ddelta
           +integral integral log|x-z| ddelta(x)ddelta(z).

The first term is nonpositive by the potential inequality and equal masses. The second is nonpositive for a zero-mass signed measure on the real line. One elementary justification uses

    -log u=integral_0^infinity (exp(-tu)-exp(-t))dt/t, u>0.

The constant term vanishes against a zero-mass measure, while exp(-t|x-z|) is positive definite; its Fourier transform is 2t/(t^2+xi^2). Truncation and smoothing justify passage to finite-energy measures. Competitors of energy minus infinity cannot improve the maximum. This proves maximality. Strict negative definiteness also gives uniqueness, although uniqueness is not required for the threshold calculation.

## 6. Energy evaluation and the two branches

The constant-potential identity and the scalar integrals above give

    E_rho(mu_rho)=s^2 C_a+2rho L_a-rho^2 log a.

For instance, integrate the weighted potential against mu_rho and add rho integral log x dmu_rho. Since

    integral log x dmu_rho=(1+rho)L_a-rho log a,

the displayed expression follows exactly.

Sections 3-5 now give

    lim k^(-2)log J_(r,m)
      =(1-rho^2)log2+s^2 C_a+2rho L_a-rho^2 log a.

The complete insertion formula from Section 2 implies

    Phi(rho)=log4+(1-rho^2)log2
                 +(1-rho)^2 C_a+2rho L_a-rho^2 log a.

Substituting a=1/2 and a=rho^2 proves the two explicit formulas in Section 1. Their agreement at rho=1/sqrt(2) also follows because the common formula has the same value of a there.

The support transition has a direct meaning: for rho<=1/sqrt(2), the maximizing measure fills [1/2,1]; above this value its left endpoint moves to rho^2. This is a property of the auxiliary positive determinant, not of arithmetic saturation or the roots of the actual polynomial beta.

## 7. Optimization and arithmetic limitation

On the first branch Phi'=4log(1+sqrt(2))-6rho log2, which is positive through rho=1/sqrt(2). On the second branch,

    Phi'=2[(1+rho)log(1+rho)-(1-rho)log(1-rho)
                 -2rho log rho-3rho log2],
    Phi''=2log((1-rho^2)/(8rho^2))<0.

The derivative is positive at the transition and tends to -2log2 as rho tends to one. Therefore there is a unique maximizing proportion in (1/sqrt(2),1), determined by setting the bracket in Phi' to zero. No numerical value is asserted; none was computed for this document.

Optimization concerns the analytic threshold only. It does not optimize the unknown actual primitive leading coefficients or their gcds.

For any fixed rho in (0,1), an unbounded sequence satisfying

    limsup log A_(k,ceil(rho k))/k^2<Phi(rho)

would eventually beat every fixed rational-denominator target. The reason is that the strict k^2 margin dominates both the o(k^2) remainder and m log v for each fixed v. The inherited nonzero evaluation would then imply irrationality of S.

This sufficient arithmetic premise has not been proved. The raw coefficients, full clearer, final gcd g, and actual primitive leading coefficient remain the open interface. In particular the variational calculation does not transfer a determinant asymptotic into a content estimate.
