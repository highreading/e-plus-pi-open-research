## 1. Objects and attribution

Let gamma(dy)=y^(-1/2)dy on [0,1], and define

    w0(dy)=exp(sqrt(y)) gamma(dy)/2,
    c_m=4^m/binom(2m-2,m-1),
    sigma_m(dy)=[exp(sqrt(y))+c_m(1+y)^(-m)]gamma(dy)/2.

D_k(w) denotes the monomial Gram determinant for degrees below k. Write

    E_(k,m)=log[D_k(sigma_m)/D_k(w0)].

## 2. A uniform bounded error for the fixed reference

We prove

    log D_k(w0)=-log(4)k^2+[log(2pi)+2/pi]k
                 -(1/8)log k+O(1).                         (1)

Let J be multiplication by y in the gamma-orthonormal polynomial basis on the nonnegative indices, and let Pi_k project onto its first k coordinates. J is self-adjoint, tridiagonal, and has spectrum in [0,1]. Put

    h(y)=exp(sqrt(y))/2,
    A=h(J), B_k=Pi_k A Pi_k,
    C_k=(I-Pi_k)A Pi_k.

The determinant of B_k is D_k(w0)/D_k(gamma). We first prove a uniform bound for the Hilbert--Schmidt norm of C_k.

Expand h in Chebyshev polynomials:

    h(y)=a_0+sum_(n>=1) a_n T_n(2y-1).

The function whose cosine coefficients occur here is phi(theta)=exp(cos(theta/2))/2, for 0<=theta<=pi. It satisfies phi'(0)=0, phi'(pi)=-1/4, and |phi''|<=e/4. Twice integrating the cosine coefficient by parts proves

    |a_n|<=K_h/n^2,
    K_h=1/(2pi)+e/2.                                    (2)

The series converges uniformly. Since J is tridiagonal, the off-diagonal compression of T_n(2J-I) has rank at most n. Its operator norm is at most one by the spectral theorem. Its Hilbert--Schmidt norm is therefore at most sqrt(n). The triangle inequality, (2), and sum n^(-3/2)<=3 give

    ||C_k||_HS<=3K_h.                                  (3)

This includes the entire infinite tail.

For a positive bounded operator A>=aI, its compression B, and off-diagonal block C, the logarithmic compression defect obeys

    0<=log det B-tr(Pi log A Pi)<=||C||_HS^2/(2a^2).     (4)

Here the trace and determinant are on the finite-dimensional compressed space. To prove (4), use the integral representation of log. At s>=0, the Schur complement gives

    Pi(A+sI)^(-1)Pi-(B+sI)^(-1)>=0.

Writing the complementary block as D, the difference equals

    H C*(D+sI)^(-1) C (B+sI)^(-1),
    H=Pi(A+sI)^(-1)Pi.

All three inverse factors have operator norm at most (a+s)^(-1). Cyclicity of the trace and the Hilbert--Schmidt inequality bound its trace by ||C||_HS^2/(a+s)^3. Integration proves (4). The inverse difference is positive even though this particular product representation is not manifestly self-adjoint. The estimates also justify the improper integral.

For h above, a=1/2. Thus (3)-(4) imply

    0<=log[D_k(w0)/D_k(gamma)]
          -tr(Pi_k log h(J)Pi_k)<=C_h,
    C_h=18K_h^2.                                      (5)

## 3. Exact evaluation of the trace

The gamma-orthonormal polynomial of degree i is

    p_i(y)=sqrt((4i+1)/2) P_(2i)(sqrt(y)),

where P_n is the ordinary Legendre polynomial. For even n,

    integral_0^1 x P_n(x)^2 dx=P_n(0)^2/2.             (6)

Here is an elementary verification. For n>=2 even, integrate the Legendre Sturm--Liouville Wronskian identity for degrees n and n+1, and then n and n-1. The boundary at 1 vanishes. At zero use P_n'(0)=0,

    P_(n+1)'(0)=(n+1)P_n(0),
    P_(n-1)'(0)=-nP_n(0).

Both adjacent cross integrals equal P_n(0)^2/2. Inserting them in

    xP_n=((n+1)P_(n+1)+nP_(n-1))/(2n+1)

proves (6). The n=0 case follows directly.

Put u_i=binom(2i,i)/4^i. Since log h(y)=sqrt(y)-log2, equation (6) gives

    tr(Pi_k log h(J)Pi_k)
      =-k log2+sum_(i=0)^(k-1)(4i+1)u_i^2/2
      =-k log2+2k^2u_k^2.                             (7)

The sum telescopes because u_(i+1)/u_i=(2i+1)/(2i+2), and hence

    2(i+1)^2u_(i+1)^2-2i^2u_i^2=(4i+1)u_i^2/2.

We have obtained the finite bracket

    -k log2+2k^2u_k^2
      <=log[D_k(w0)/D_k(gamma)]
      <=-k log2+2k^2u_k^2+C_h.                        (8)

Stirling's formula gives 2k^2u_k^2=2k/pi-1/(2pi)+O(1/k). The exact Cauchy determinant is

    D_k(gamma)=product_(i=0)^(k-1)
       2^(4i+1)/[(4i+1)binom(4i,2i)^2].

For i>=1 the logarithm of its ith factor is

    -4i log2+log pi-1/(8i)+O(i^(-2)).

Summation yields

    log D_k(gamma)=-log(4)k^2+k log(4pi)
                    -(1/8)log k+O(1).

Combining this with (8) proves (1). No limiting value for the bounded defect in (5) is asserted.

## 4. Uniform endpoint correction for different k and m

The already-read rational-compression argument extends to k>=m>=4. Let

    ell=ceil(log c_m), t=ell/m, a_m=c_m^(1/ell).

Then 1<=ell<=m, a_m<=e, and

    1+c_m(1+y)^(-m)
      <=[(y+t(1+a_m))/(y+t)]^ell.                     (9)

Indeed (1+y)^m>=(1+my/ell)^ell, and the remaining inequality is the binomial expansion with positive terms.

Base-Gram comparison bounds exp(E_(k,m)) by the determinant of the gamma compression of the right side of (9). The difference between compressing this rational function of J and evaluating it at J_k has rank at most ell. To recall the justification, the compressed resolvent difference at a positive parameter s has the form eta(s)v(s)v(s)^T, with v(s)=(J_k+sI)^(-1)e_last. Differentiating q-1 times puts the inverse-power difference in the span of v,...,v^(q-1). Expanding the rational function into inverse powers at the single parameter t therefore gives rank at most ell. The tail resolvent is analytic since its spectrum is nonnegative.

Both positive matrices have spectra in [1,(1+a_m)^ell]. Rank interlacing bounds the logarithmic determinant difference by ell^2 log(1+a_m). The Legendre characteristic-polynomial bound from Stage 7 then gives

    0<=E_(k,m)<=U_(k,m),
    U_(k,m)=2sqrt(1+e) k ell^(3/2)/sqrt(m)
               +ell log(2k+1)+ell^2 log(1+e).          (10)

This follows by bounding asinh z by z in the characteristic ratio. The degree of the Legendre polynomial remains 2k, while t=ell/m; thus dimension and pole order have not been conflated.

There is a matching lower scale. Logarithmic compression and comparison of evaluation kernels give

    E_(k,m)>=(2/e) integral_0^1 K_(gamma,k)(x^2,x^2)
         log[1+c_m exp(-x)(1+x^2)^(-m)]dx.             (11)

For m>=ceil(exp(16)), restrict this integral to

    m^(-1/2)<=x<=sqrt(log m/(4m)).

The Chebyshev comparison gives K_(gamma,k)(x^2,x^2)>=k/(2pi) on this interval when k>=m. More explicitly its lower bound is k/pi-1/(pi|sin(2 arccos x)|), and the subtracted term is at most sqrt(m)/(pi sqrt(3)). The interval length is at least sqrt(log m/m)/4. The bound c_m>=4sqrt(m), together with log(1+x^2)<=x^2, makes the logarithmic factor at least (log m)/4. Thus

    E_(k,m)>=k(log m)^(3/2)/(16pi e sqrt(m)).           (12)

Since ell=O(log m), the final two terms of (10) are absorbed by its first scale uniformly for k>=m sufficiently large. For example log(2k+1)/k decreases for large k, so the worst comparison occurs at k=m. Equations (10)-(12) prove

    E_(k,m)=Theta(k(log m)^(3/2)/sqrt(m)),              (13)

with absolute positive bounding constants, uniformly for k>=m>=ceil(exp(16)). This is a two-sided order estimate, not an asymptotic coefficient.

Along sequences m->infinity, m<=k, equation (13) shows

    E_(k,m)=o(m)
      iff m/[k^(2/3)log k]->infinity.                 (14)

Indeed E/m tends to zero iff m/[k^(2/3)log m] tends to infinity. That condition implies m>=k^(2/3) eventually, hence log m>=(2/3)log k, proving necessity in (14). Sufficiency follows from log m<=log k.

## 5. Exact rational content and the common benchmark

For the fixed canonical determinant polynomial beta_(k,m), let delta be any positive integer clearing all coefficients, I_j=delta beta_j, and g=gcd(I_0,...,I_m)>0. Define its positive rational coefficient content

    eta_(k,m)=g/delta.

This is independent of the choice of complete integer clearer. It is not invariant under arbitrary rescaling of the canonical determinant itself; that determinant normalization remains fixed here.

Let P be its primitive integer scalar multiple. The inherited complete value identity gives, uniformly for k>=131072 and 1<=m<=k,

    |P(S)|=theta_(k,m) F*_k D_k(sigma_m)/eta_(k,m),
    exp(-C)<=theta_(k,m)<=1,
    C=192e^4.

The same positive tail determinant F*_k applies to every m in this range. No actual coefficient gcd is replaced by a guaranteed divisor.

Define the explicit positive benchmark

    Bcal_k=F*_k 4^(-k^2)(2pi exp(2/pi))^k k^(-1/8),
    Hsharp_(k,m)=eta_(k,m)/Bcal_k.

Equation (1) implies, with an absolute error independent of m,

    log|P_(k,m)(S)|
      =E_(k,m)-log Hsharp_(k,m)+O(1).                 (15)

The benchmark is positive real analytic data, not an integer or a divisibility assertion. The unexpanded reference determinant also has the exact expression

    D_k(w0)=det[e D_(2(i+j))-(2(i+j))!]_(0<=i,j<k),

where D_n is the derangement number. Its positivity follows from the integral Gram representation.

For any integer v>=1 the exact threshold on rational content is

    eta*_(k,m)(v)=v^m theta_(k,m)F*_kD_k(sigma_m),
    |P(S)|<v^(-m) iff eta_(k,m)>eta*_(k,m)(v).

Consequently

    log eta*_(k,m)(v)
      =log Bcal_k+m log v+E_(k,m)+O(1),               (16)

where the error is independent of v and m in the inherited range.

For every sequence m->infinity, equation (15) gives the equivalence

    |P_(k,m)(S)|<v^(-m) eventually for every fixed v
      iff [log Hsharp_(k,m)-E_(k,m)]/m->infinity.      (17)

This is because the target for -log|P(S)|/m is log v, and log v is unbounded over positive integers v. In the range (14), (17) is equivalent to log Hsharp/m->infinity. Outside that range the endpoint term must remain. An unbounded limsup of the right side of (17) suffices after selecting a subsequence. None of these content-growth premises has been proved for the actual family.

## 6. Complete diagonal correction

For m=k the inherited exact highest coefficient has factor

    kappa_k=[4^(k-1)/binom(2k-2,k-1)]^k,
    log kappa_k=(k/2)log k+(k/2)log pi+O(1).

Put b=log(2sqrt(pi))+2/pi. The full ratio and threshold therefore satisfy

    log|beta_(k,k)(S)/beta_(k,k),k|
      =-log(4)k^2-(k/2)log k+bk
          -(1/8)log k+E_(k,k)+O(1),                  (18)

    log T_(k,k)(v)
      =log(4)k^2+(k/2)log k-(b+log v)k
          +(1/8)log k-E_(k,k)+O(1).                  (19)

The error is independent of v. By (13), the endpoint correction in (18)-(19) has order sqrt(k)(log k)^(3/2). Its exact coefficient is not established. The bounded fixed-reference error now makes this correction visible in the complete ratio.

## 7. Consequence for comparing proportions and the remaining gap

For fixed proportional m, the term E_(k,m) is o(k). Thus the critical rational content has no proportion-dependent term at order k^2: in (16), proportion enters the rational-target cost m log v and the smaller endpoint correction. The function Phi(rho) from Stage 8 describes the leading-coefficient threshold; its dependence cancels against the same insertion determinant in the raw leading coefficient when one asks for the necessary canonical coefficient content.

This does not show that one proportion has more favorable actual content. A uniform bound or construction for eta=g/delta relative to Bcal_k remains absent. Equations (15)-(17) specify that missing arithmetic estimate more precisely, but do not prove it.

No new computation or independent review is claimed. The fixed-reference proof and the uniform endpoint transfer are new author deductions. The endpoint coefficient and the actual primitive-content problem remain open.
