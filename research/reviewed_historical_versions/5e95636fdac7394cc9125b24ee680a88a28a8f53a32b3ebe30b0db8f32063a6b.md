For a positive measure w let D_k(w) be its monomial Gram determinant for degrees below k, and write D_k=D_k(gamma). The inherited complete insertion identities give

    beta_k=(-1)^[k+binom(k,2)] kappa_k F*_k,

Define I_j=delta beta_j, g=gcd(I_0,...,I_k)>0, and orient the primitive polynomial P to have positive leading coefficient A_k=|I_k|/g. Then exactly

## 2. Endpoint bump: an explicit sublinear determinant cost

We prove

    0<=log[D_k(sigma_k)/D_k(w0)]
      <=log(1+c_k)+log(2k+1)+2k asinh(sqrt((1+c_k)/k))
      =O(k^(3/4)).                                    (2)

First, c_k<=8sqrt(k). For n=k-1>=1, the elementary bound binom(2n,n)>=4^n/(2sqrt(n)) proves this. One proof of that bound starts at n=1 and uses

    [binom(2n+2,n+1)/4^(n+1)]/[binom(2n,n)/4^n]
       =(2n+1)/(2n+2)>=sqrt(n/(n+1)).

The k=1 bound follows directly.

Let G, G0 and H_b be the degree-below-k Grams of gamma, w0 and (1+y)^(-k)gamma. Then G0>=G/2. For positive definite A and positive semidefinite B, det(A+B)/det A decreases when A increases in Loewner order. Indeed it equals det(I+B^(1/2)A^(-1)B^(1/2)). Consequently

    D_k(sigma_k)/D_k(w0)
       <=det(I+c_k G^(-1/2)H_b G^(-1/2)).              (3)

Bernoulli's inequality gives (1+y)^k>=1+ky for y>=0. Put

    t=1/k, u=(1+c_k)/k, d=u-t=c_k/k.

In a gamma-orthonormal polynomial basis, let H_t be the compression of multiplication by (y+t)^(-1) to degrees below k. Applying the preceding pointwise inequality to (3) yields

    D_k(sigma_k)/D_k(w0)<=det(I+d H_t).                (4)

Let J be multiplication by y on L2(gamma), represented in its orthonormal polynomial basis, and J_k its first k by k block. The operator J is bounded, self-adjoint, and has spectrum in [0,1]. Its coupling between this block and the remaining coordinates has rank one, by the three-term recurrence.

The block Schur complement of J+tI therefore gives

    H_t=(J_k+tI)^(-1)+E_t,
    E_t>=0, rank E_t<=1,
    H_t<=t^(-1)I.                                    (5)

For clarity, write J+tI in block form [A,B;B*,D]. Its inverse's first block is (A-BD^(-1)B*)^(-1). The matrix BD^(-1)B* is positive semidefinite of rank at most one. The inverse difference from A^(-1) is positive semidefinite of rank at most one. This proves (5), including the infinite-dimensional tail since D>=tI.

Write E_t=zz* when nonzero. Since E_t<=H_t<=t^(-1)I, ||z||^2<=1/t. The determinant lemma gives

    det(I+d H_t)
      <=(1+d/t) det[I+d(J_k+tI)^(-1)]
      =(u/t) det(J_k+uI)/det(J_k+tI).                 (6)

The rank-zero case satisfies the same inequality.

For gamma, the degree-k orthogonal polynomial in y is proportional to P_(2k)(sqrt(y)), where P_n is the ordinary Legendre polynomial. The characteristic polynomial of J_k is its monic normalization. Hence

    det(J_k+uI)/det(J_k+tI)
       =|P_(2k)(i sqrt(u))|/|P_(2k)(i sqrt(t))|.       (7)

All roots of the degree-k orthogonal polynomial lie in (0,1). Thus the denominator in (7) is at least |P_(2k)(0)|. Explicitly

    |P_(2k)(0)|=binom(2k,k)/4^k>=1/(2k+1),

using that the central binomial coefficient is the largest term in the binomial sum.

The classical Laplace integral for Legendre polynomials, extended as a polynomial identity to complex arguments, gives

    |P_(2k)(i sqrt(u))|
       <=(sqrt(u)+sqrt(1+u))^(2k)
       =exp[2k asinh(sqrt(u))].                       (8)

Indeed substitute i sqrt(u) and choose the square root i sqrt(1+u) in the integral; the modulus of its integrand base is at most sqrt(u)+sqrt(1+u). Equations (4)-(8) prove the upper bound in (2). The lower bound follows from sigma_k>=w0.

Finally u<=9/sqrt(k), so 2k asinh(sqrt(u))<=6k^(3/4). The remaining logarithms are O(log(k+1)). This proves the sublinear cost without treating the bump as uniformly bounded pointwise.

## 3. A fixed positive multiplier: first-order determinant limit

For every continuous positive function h on [0,1], we prove

    lim_(k->infinity) (1/k) log[D_k(h gamma)/D_k(gamma)]
       =integral_0^1 log h(y) dy/[pi sqrt(y(1-y))].    (9)

Here is a proof using only the recurrence and finite-dimensional approximation.

In the normalized Legendre basis on [-1,1], multiplication by x has adjacent coefficient

    b_n=n/sqrt((2n-1)(2n+1)), n>=1, b_0=0.

Passing to the even subspace and multiplying twice shows that J, multiplication by y, has diagonal

    a_i=b_(2i)^2+b_(2i+1)^2 ->1/2

and off-diagonal coefficient between i and i+1

    d_i=b_(2i+1)b_(2i+2) ->1/4.

For any fixed nonnegative integer r, expand tr(J_k^r) as a sum over paths of length r. Paths starting within r positions of either boundary contribute O(1) to the trace, and all terms are uniformly bounded. At interior starting positions tending to infinity, the weights tend to those of the constant tridiagonal matrix with diagonal 1/2 and adjacent coefficients 1/4. Cesaro averaging therefore proves

    (1/k)tr(J_k^r)
      ->(1/(2pi))integral_0^(2pi)(1/2+(1/2)cos theta)^r dtheta.

The eigenvalues of J_k lie in [0,1]. Polynomial approximation extends this limit to every continuous test function, giving the arcsine measure on [0,1]. In particular,

    (1/k)tr log h(J_k)-> integral log h d(arcsine).    (10)

Let P_k now denote orthogonal projection onto the first k coordinates, only within this section. The Gram ratio in (9) equals det H_k, where

    H_k=P_k h(J) P_k restricted to that subspace.

Choose a real polynomial p of fixed degree d approximating h uniformly within epsilon<min(h)/2. Both p and h are bounded away from zero. The matrices H_k and P_k p(J)P_k differ in operator norm by at most epsilon. Likewise h(J_k) and p(J_k) differ by at most epsilon. Weyl's eigenvalue inequality and the Lipschitz bound for log on a common positive interval imply an O(epsilon k) bound for each corresponding logarithmic determinant difference, with constant independent of k and epsilon in this range.

The two matrices P_k p(J)P_k and p(J_k) differ by rank bounded in terms of d alone: their entries agree away from the last d coordinates, since a path contributing to a polynomial of degree d cannot reach the omitted boundary from farther away. Both matrices have spectra in a common fixed positive interval. The rank bound and eigenvalue interlacing imply that their logarithmic determinants differ by O_p(1).

Divide these bounds by k, take k to infinity, then epsilon to zero. Equation (10) proves (9). This argument establishes the first-order limit only; it asserts no strong Szego remainder or unproved uniform varying-weight theorem.

## 4. Actual compact determinant and full diagonal ratio

Apply (9) to h(y)=exp(sqrt(y))/2. The arcsine integral is

    integral log h d(arcsine)=2/pi-log2,

because integral_0^1 sqrt(y) dy/[pi sqrt(y(1-y))]=2/pi. Combining (2) and (9) gives

    log[D_k(sigma_k)/D_k]=k(2/pi-log2)+o(k).           (11)

The exact Cauchy determinant is

    D_k=product_(i=0)^(k-1)
           2^(4i+1)/[(4i+1)binom(4i,2i)^2].

Elementary Stirling estimates give

    log D_k=-(log4)k^2+k log(4pi)+O(log(k+1)),
    log kappa_k=(k/2)log k+(k/2)log pi+O(1).

These weaker remainders suffice here; Child 3's draft contains more precise reference expansions. Since log theta_k lies in the fixed interval [-C,0], equations (1) and (11) prove

    log|beta(S)/beta_k|
       =-(log4)k^2-(1/2)k log k
          +[log(2sqrt(pi))+2/pi]k+o(k).               (12)

This is the actual complete ratio, not merely a reference determinant expansion. No assertion about individual diagonal roots is needed.

## 5. Exact arithmetic threshold and its scope

For a fixed positive integer v define

    T_k(v)=kappa_k/[v^k theta_k D_k(sigma_k)].

By (1), exactly

    |P(S)|<v^(-k) iff A_k<T_k(v).

Equation (12) gives

    log T_k(v)
       =(log4)k^2+(1/2)k log k
          -[log(2sqrt(pi))+2/pi+log v]k+o(k).         (13)

If S were rational with reduced denominator v, the inherited P(S)!=0 and integer coefficients would force |P(S)|>=v^(-k). Therefore verifying the strict actual arithmetic inequality for an eligible k would exclude that denominator. No such inequality is established here.

For example, conditional on an unbounded subsequence with

    log A_k=(log4)k^2+(1/2)k log k+b k+o(k),

a fixed v is eventually excluded by the primitive-value criterion when

    b<-[log(2sqrt(pi))+2/pi+log v].

A fixed finite b does not by itself exclude every denominator v. One sufficient condition for all fixed v is that the residual linear coefficient tends to minus infinity. This distinction prevents a critical-scale asymptotic from being overstated as an irrationality proof.

Every threshold uses A_k=|I_k|/g with the actual complete coefficient gcd. No guaranteed divisor, basis index, or response-only coefficient is substituted.
