On [0,1] let gamma(dy)=y^(-1/2)dy, w0=exp(sqrt(y))gamma/2, sigma_k=w0(1+u_k), where

 u_k(y)=c_k exp(-sqrt(y))(1+y)^(-k),
 c_k=4^k/binom(2k-2,k-1), f_k=log(1+u_k), M=log(1+c_k).

Let P_gamma and P0 be the degree-below-k orthogonal polynomial projections in L2(gamma) and L2(w0), respectively. Put

 T_gamma=tr(P_gamma f_k P_gamma),
 V_gamma=||(I-P_gamma)f_k P_gamma||_HS^2,
 V0=||(I-P0)f_k P0||_HS^2,
 J_k=integral_0^1 f_k(y)dy/[pi sqrt(y(1-y))].

All O constants below are absolute, independent of k and of the moving level sets used in the proof. We prove, for k>=2,

 T_gamma=k J_k+O(M log(k+1)+k epsilon_k),                  (1)
 V0<=e V_gamma=O(M^2 log(k+1)+k epsilon_k^2),             (2)
 epsilon_k=f_k(1/4)<=c_k(4/5)^k.

Since M=(1/2)log k+O(1) and c_k=O(sqrt(k)), these imply T_gamma=kJ_k+O(log^2 k) and V0=O(log^3 k). The displayed equality with O in (2) means an upper bound, not equality of V0 with e V_gamma.

With Q_k=sqrt(k)(log k)^(3/2), alpha=2/(3pi sqrt(2)), the adopted Stage 2 estimates and inherited scalar asymptotic consequently give the ACTUAL determinant theorem

 E_(k,k):=log[D_k(sigma_k)/D_k(w0)]
         =alpha Q_k+O(sqrt(k)log k).                     (3)

Thus the remainder is o(Q_k). D_k denotes the monomial Gram determinant for degrees below k. The proof below retains the boundary layer at y=0, both endpoints of the full Legendre interval, and the exponentially small noncompact tail of the bump.

## 2. Explicit local even-Legendre trace

Set F(x)=f_k(x^2) for 0<=x<=1. It is nonnegative and decreasing, with F(0)=M and F(1/2)=epsilon_k. The gamma-orthonormal polynomial of degree i is

 p_i(y)=sqrt((4i+1)/2) P_(2i)(sqrt(y)),

so K_gamma(x^2,x^2)=sum_(i<k)(4i+1)P_(2i)(x)^2/2 and T_gamma=2 integral_0^1 F(x)K_gamma(x^2,x^2)dx.

We establish a uniform approximation on 0<=x<=1/2 directly from the Legendre differential equation, without invoking an asymptotic valid only away from a shrinking boundary. Write t=arcsin x, 0<=t<=pi/6, n=2i>=2, lambda=n+1/2, and

 v_n(t)=sqrt(cos t) P_n(sin t).

Substitution into ((1-x^2)P_n')'+n(n+1)P_n=0 gives exactly

 v_n''+[lambda^2+q(t)]v_n=0, q(t)=1/(4cos^2 t).

Here 0<q<=1/3, v_n(0)=P_n(0), v_n'(0)=0 for even n. Variation of constants gives

 v_n(t)=P_n(0)cos(lambda t)
       -(1/lambda) integral_0^t sin(lambda(t-s))q(s)v_n(s)ds.

Gronwall yields |v_n(t)|<=|P_n(0)|exp(t/(3lambda)), and therefore

 v_n(t)=P_n(0)[cos(lambda t)+O(1/n)]                       (4)

uniformly on this entire interval, including t=0. Central-binomial Stirling bounds give

 (4i+1)P_(2i)(0)^2/2=2/pi+O(1/i).

Squaring (4) and dividing by cos t, which is bounded below, now proves

 p_i(x^2)^2=[1+cos((4i+1)t)]/[pi cos t]+O(1/i).

The i=0 contribution can be inserted with an O(1) error. Summing gives

 K_gamma(x^2,x^2)=k/[pi sqrt(1-x^2)]
  +[sum_(i=0)^(k-1)cos((4i+1)t)]/[pi cos t]+O(log(k+1)). (5)

The finite geometric sum is bounded by min(k,1/|sin(2t)|). On 0<=t<=pi/6, sin(2t) is bounded below by a positive absolute multiple of t, and dx=cos t dt. Thus the absolute integral of the oscillatory term in (5), multiplied by F<=M, is at most

 C M integral_0^(pi/6) min(k,1/t)dt <= C M log(k+1).

In particular the layer t<=1/k contributes O(M), rather than being discarded. The O(log(k+1)) term in (5) contributes at most C M log(k+1) as well.

For x>=1/2, F(x)<=epsilon_k. The reference kernel has total mass 2 integral_0^1 K_gamma(x^2,x^2)dx=k, while the arcsine measure also has mass one. Hence both omitted trace integrals together are bounded by 2k epsilon_k, without any endpoint approximation near x=1. This proves (1). The identity kJ_k=(2k/pi)integral_0^1 F(x)dx/sqrt(1-x^2) fixes every normalization factor.

## 3. Full Legendre central-interval number variance

We first prove an auxiliary estimate uniform in every 0<=a<=1/2 and every N>=2. In L2([-1,1],dx) let Pi_N project onto all polynomials of degree <N, let

 l_n(x)=sqrt((2n+1)/2)P_n(x),
 L_N(x,z)=sum_(n<N)l_n(x)l_n(z), I_a=[-a,a].

Then

 ||(I-Pi_N)1_(I_a)Pi_N||_HS^2
   =integral_(x in I_a) integral_(z outside I_a) |L_N(x,z)|^2 dz dx
   <=C log(N+1).                                         (6)

Here the complement is within [-1,1]. The formula follows from the projection identity, so there is no missing factor 1/2: the two cross regions combine to this one integral.

We supply bounds which also control z near +/-1. The elementary Laplace representation of P_n gives, for n>=1,

 |P_n(x)|<=sqrt(pi)/sqrt(2n(1-x^2)), |x|<1.

Indeed take absolute values in the integral of (x+i sqrt(1-x^2)cos phi)^n, use 1-u<=exp(-u), reflect to [0,pi/2], and use sin phi>=2phi/pi; the resulting Gaussian integral gives this bound. Consequently |l_n(x)|<=C for every n>=0 and |x|<=3/4. Thus on this central square,

 |L_N(x,z)|<=C N.

The Christoffel--Darboux identity, with a_N=N/sqrt((2N-1)(2N+1))<=1, is

 L_N(x,z)=a_N [l_N(x)l_(N-1)(z)-l_(N-1)(x)l_N(z)]/(x-z).

It also gives |L_N(x,z)|<=C/|x-z| when both points are central. The central part of the cross integral is therefore bounded by C times the integral of min(N^2,|x-z|^(-2)). A cross pair must cross at least one of the two boundaries +/-a. For one boundary b, set r=b-x>=0, s=z-b>=0. Enlarging to 0<=r,s<=2 gives the bound

 integral_0^2 integral_0^2 min(N^2,(r+s)^(-2))dr ds
 <=integral_0^4 d min(N^2,d^(-2))dd
 <=C(1+log N).

There are two boundaries. This upper bound remains uniform when a shrinks to zero or the two boundary layers overlap.

For the remaining region |z|>3/4, x in I_a has |x-z|>=1/4. Christoffel--Darboux and boundedness of l_N(x), l_(N-1)(x) give

 |L_N(x,z)|^2<=C[l_N(z)^2+l_(N-1)(z)^2].

Integrating uses their exact L2 norms, each one, and |I_a|<=1. This part is O(1). Thus the endpoint regions near +/-1 are retained and controlled, proving (6).

## 4. Layer representation bounds the logarithmic variance

Extend F evenly to [-1,1] as F(x)=f_k(x^2), and put

 G(x)=(F(x)-epsilon_k)_+,
 H(x)=F(x)-G(x).

Then G is supported in [-1/2,1/2], even and decreasing with |x|, and 0<=H<=epsilon_k on the whole interval. Layer cake represents G as

 G(x)=integral_0^(M-epsilon_k) 1_(I_(a(s)))(x) ds,

where 0<=a(s)<=1/2. Endpoint choices in these level sets have zero measure. Linearity of the cross-block map and the Hilbert--Schmidt triangle inequality, followed by (6), yield

 ||(I-Pi_N)G Pi_N||_HS
 <=C M sqrt(log(N+1)).

The integral is justified as a Bochner integral in Hilbert--Schmidt space: the integrand is measurable and bounded in that norm by (6), on a finite parameter interval. Alternatively one can first use monotone step approximations and pass in L2 on the finite-dimensional input space.

The remainder obeys ||(I-Pi_N)H Pi_N||_HS<=sqrt(N)epsilon_k. Hence

 ||(I-Pi_N)F Pi_N||_HS^2
 <=C M^2 log(N+1)+2N epsilon_k^2.                          (7)

There is no derivative bound growing like sqrt(k) in (7). Only the total logarithmic amplitude and a uniform interval-variance bound enter. This is why the shrinking support does not cause a loss.

To return to gamma, use the isometry U:L2(gamma)->L2([-1,1],dx)_even given by (Uf)(x)=f(x^2). It is onto the even subspace since integral_-1^1 |f(x^2)|^2dx=integral_0^1 |f(y)|^2dgamma(y). The image of ran(P_gamma) is span(l_0,l_2,...,l_(2k-2)). For N=2k this is exactly the even part of Pi_N. Multiplication by F is even, and Pi_N and its complement preserve parity. Therefore the full variance in (7) is the sum of its nonnegative even and odd parts; the even part equals V_gamma. Taking N=2k proves

 V_gamma<=C M^2 log(k+1)+C k epsilon_k^2.                  (8)

Finally scaling a measure by a positive constant does not change this variance, so replace w0 by wtilde=exp(sqrt(y))gamma. It lies between gamma and e gamma. In a gamma-orthonormal polynomial basis let B be the wtilde Gram, so B>=I. Choose as trial polynomial approximations the gamma projections of f_k p_i. Their residual Gram in wtilde is at most e times their residual Gram in gamma. The best residual Gram C in wtilde is no larger in Loewner order. Thus

 V0=tr(B^(-1)C)<=tr C<=e V_gamma.

This elementary bounded-measure variance comparison proves (2); it is not a repeat of the different trace-transfer estimate in Stage 2.
