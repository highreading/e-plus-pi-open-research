# Exact odd saturation of the rectangular even Gamma minors

Author theorem, 2026-10-02. This continues the fresh-gated short-stack content target. Classical Gamma moments, integer basis changes, Lucas congruence and rational-ODE pole analysis are background tools. The specific rectangular even-moment determinantal divisor below is the author result. It does not determine the larger content of the ACTUAL fixed lower-block output pair.

## 1. The exact input divisor

For k>=1 define the k by 2k integer Gamma matrix

    A_ij=integral_0^infinity exp(-t) [t(t-2)]^(i+j) dt,
    0<=i<k, 0<=j<2k.

Let delta_k be the positive gcd of all its k by k minors, and F_k=product_(i=0)^(k-1)i!. Then

    v_p(delta_k)=2 v_p(F_k)  for EVERY odd prime p.

In particular its odd part is exactly the odd part of F_k^2. The divisibility delta_k by F_k^2 was already proved in the complete-pair factorial theorem. The present result proves optimality at every odd prime for this rectangular Gamma INPUT. Additional powers of 2 are not classified here.

The same conclusion holds for the gcd of ALL (k-1) by (k-1) minors of A:

    v_p(delta_(k-1;A))=2 v_p(F_(k-1))  for odd p.

The lower bound applies to every row/column subset; for the upper bound use the first k-1 rows and first 2k-2 columns and apply the preceding theorem with dimension k-1. Empty minors have gcd 1.

## 2. Integer recurrence and normalized blocks

Write a_m=A_0m and T_m=a_m/m!. The explicit factorial sum shows T_m is an integer. Integration by parts, with the complete endpoint at t=0 retained, gives

    a_0=1, a_1=0,
    a_(m+1)=2(m+1)(2m+1)a_m+4m(m+1)a_(m-1),  m>=1,
    T_0=1, T_1=0,
    T_(m+1)=(4m+2)T_m+4T_(m-1),  m>=1.

For the integration-by-parts proof put w=t(t-2) and b_m=integral exp(-t)(t-1)w^m dt. For m>=1, the derivative of (t-1)w^m has zero boundary contribution and yields b_m=(2m+1)a_m+2m a_(m-1). The derivative of w^(m+1) gives a_(m+1)=2(m+1)b_m. The exceptional m=0 boundary gives b_0=0 and a_1=0; it is not replaced by the homogeneous recurrence at m=0.

Define the integer normalized matrix

    B_ij=A_ij/(i!j!)=binom(i+j,i) T_(i+j).

For an odd prime p the finite factorial sum for T_m gives

    T_(p-2)=0 mod p,  T_(p-1)=1 mod p.

Indeed for m=p-2 only s=0,1 survive modulo p in the sum

    T_m=sum_(s=0)^m binom(m,s)(-2)^(m-s)(m+s)!/m!,

and their residues cancel. For m=p-1 only s=0 survives and equals 1. The recurrence then gives T_p=-2 and T_(p+1)=0 modulo p. Its coefficients are periodic modulo p, so comparison of the two initial values in successive blocks proves

    T_(ap+b)=(-2)^a T_b mod p,
    a>=0, 0<=b<p.

For i=ap+b,j=cp+d, Lucas congruence now yields

    B_(ap+b,cp+d)=(-2)^(a+c) binom(a+c,a) B_(b,d) mod p.

When b+d>=p both sides vanish: the low binomial digit has a carry. When b+d<p the displayed identity follows directly. Thus B is, blockwise, a diagonally scaled ordinary Pascal matrix tensored with its p by p low block.

The low block B_p=(B_bd)_(0<=b,d<p) is anti-triangular modulo p. All entries with b+d>=p vanish, while on b+d=p-1 one has B_bd=(-1)^b. Its determinant is 1 modulo p. The high Pascal matrix P_ac=binom(a+c,a) has every leading principal determinant 1, by the integer factorization P=L L^t with L_ac=binom(a,c) for c<=a.

## 3. Factorial-optimal columns for p<=2k-1

Write k=up+s, 0<=s<p. If s=0, select the first k columns. The normalized k-square block is the scaled P_u tensor B_p and is a p-unit.

If s>0, select all columns in high blocks 0,...,u-1, and s columns in high block u. Take the latter low column indices D so the s by s submatrix of B_p in its first s rows is a p-unit. Such D exists because B_p is invertible. Eliminate the u complete high blocks: the high Pascal Schur complement is 1, and the remaining low submatrix is precisely that chosen p-unit block, up to unit diagonal scalings. Therefore this normalized k by k minor is a p-unit.

Every selected column is below (u+1)p. This is <=2k whenever u>=1; when u=0 it follows from p<=2k-1. Thus the columns lie in the ACTUAL width 2k. No extra columns are borrowed from a different kernel family.

For 0<=b<p one has

    v_p((ap+b)!)=a+v_p(a!),

independent of b. The selected columns have exactly the same number in each p-block as the row indices 0,...,k-1. Their factorial product therefore has valuation v_p(F_k). Since

    det A_J=F_k (product_(j in J)j!) det B_J,

the selected Gamma minor has valuation exactly 2v_p(F_k). This proves saturation for all odd p<=2k-1, including all higher factorial depths; it is not merely a rank-mod-p observation about an uncleared coefficient.

## 4. Large primes: the rational-ODE obstruction

For p>2k-1, all row and column factorials are units. It suffices to prove A has full row rank over F_p.

Let f(z)=sum_(m>=0)a_m z^m as a FORMAL series, without any analytic convergence assertion. Its exact recurrence gives

    4z^3(1+z)f''+2z^2(5+8z)f'+(8z^2+2z-1)f=2z-1.

Suppose a nonzero row relation existed. Choose its highest nonzero index d<=k-1 and write its coefficients q_0,...,q_d. The equations are

    sum_(i=0)^d q_i a_(i+j)=0, 0<=j<2k.

Since a_0=1, d=0 is impossible. Put Q(z)=sum_(i=0)^d q_i z^(d-i), so Q(0)=q_d!=0. There is P of degree<=d-1 such that

    f-P/Q=O(z^(d+2k)).

Apply the differential operator above. Its derivative coefficients vanish to orders 3 and 2, so the error remains of order at least d+2k. Multiply the rational residual by Q^3. Its numerator has degree at most 3d+1: each of the second-derivative, first-derivative, zeroth-order and forcing terms obeys this bound. But d+2k>=3d+2, so this residual numerator must vanish identically. Thus P/Q would be a proper rational solution of the same inhomogeneous ODE over F_p.

Over the algebraic closure, any pole away from z=0,-1, of order h<=d, has an uncancelled highest term proportional to h(h+1), because the second-derivative coefficient is nonzero there. Both factors are nonzero modulo p. There is no pole at 0 because Q(0)!=0. At z=-1, the coefficient of the highest pole term is

    -2h(2h-1),

which is again nonzero since h<=d<=k-1 and p>2k-1. Therefore the proper rational function has no finite poles, so it is identically zero. This contradicts the nonzero forcing 2z-1. Full row rank follows. Consequently at least one actual Gamma maximal minor is a p-unit, and v_p(delta_k)=0=2v_p(F_k).

## 5. Scope for the complete M24 output

This theorem proves that the generic Gamma minor factors used in SHORT_STACK_FACTORIAL_ACTUAL_CONTENT.md have no further odd determinantal divisor. The constant-coefficient proof can use k-1 Gamma rows, while the S coefficient uses k. Their common F_(k-1)^2 remains a valid ACTUAL full-pair factor.

It does not assert that the full fixed coefficients have exactly that common odd factor. The integer lower block L_k R, its arctangent residues, and cancellations among Gamma minors can and do supply more content; W_k is already a separate example. Those actual coefficients must still use

    q_k=abs(I_1)/gcd(I_0,I_1).

Thus the result closes an extra-generic-Gamma route, rather than proving a global denominator obstruction. A further reduction of the leading arithmetic cost must use the specified lower block and its full output carries, or change the construction while retaining the complete target/error.
