# Smith root-denominator caps and higher centered invariants

Author: Main. Status: author proofs from the canonical complete diagonal construction. The root cap requires no effective-pairing nondegeneracy; the higher-invariant survival theorem has an explicit additional hypothesis. Independent review is pending. S=e+pi remains unresolved.

## 1. Exact actual objects

Use the canonical family m=k>=2 and h=y+1. For 0<=i,j<k retain

    M_ij=mu(h^(i+j)),       G_ij=mu(h^(k+i+j)),
    J_ij=nu_k(h^(i+j)),     B_ij=R_k(h^(i+j)),
    D_ij=R_k(h^(k+i+j)),    C=M-J,
    beta(T)=det[[C,G],[B+TJ,D]].

The rational functional R_k includes both endpoints. In particular B is the complete low block, not the high-degree integral replacement. These are the exact objects of DIAGONAL_LOCAL_CONTENT_REDUCTION_STAGE10.md. The h-basis changes are unimodular.

Let delta be the specified complete physical clearer, I_j=delta beta_j, g=gcd(I_0,...,I_k)>0. Orient the primitive polynomial as

    P(T)=A T^k+a_1 T^(k-1)+...+a_k,
    A=|I_k|/g>0.

Thus a_j/A=I_(k-j)/I_k=beta_(k-j)/beta_k. The leading coefficient is

    beta_k=(-1)^k det J det G,
    det G=F*_k>0.

Fix p>6k. The specified delta and det J are p-units, and all blocks are p-integral. Write a=v_p(det G), d=v_p(g), and n=v_p(A)=a-d. Let b be the largest Smith valuation of G, taking b=0 when a=0. The integer matrix G is nonsingular, so its largest positive integer Smith invariant s_max(G) exists and b=v_p(s_max(G)).

The large-prime assumptions are essential. An arbitrary enlarged clearer containing p would not have the stated unit property. The primitive quantities themselves are independent of such an enlargement.

## 2. A direct characteristic-matrix identity

Define the rational k by k matrix

    H=J^(-1)(D G^(-1) C-B).

Then exactly

    beta(T)/beta_k=det(T I-H).                         (1)

Indeed, swapping the two k-column blocks gives

    beta(T)=(-1)^k det G det(B+TJ-D G^(-1) C),

and factoring J gives (1). This uses invertibility over Q, not p-adic invertibility of G. The full low block B remains present.

Smith normal form gives p^b G^(-1) integral over Z_p. All remaining factors in H are p-integral, including J^(-1). Hence

    p^b H belongs to M_k(Z_p).                         (2)

Every root alpha of beta is an eigenvalue of H. Equation (2) implies that p^b alpha is integral over Z_p, so

    v_p(alpha)>=-b                                    (3)

for every nonzero root. A zero root satisfies the corresponding denominator bound by convention. This proves the individual-root cap without any hypothesis on C J^(-1) D restricted to a radical or filtered subspace.

Equivalently,

    p^(kb) P(p^(-b)X)/A
       =X^k+t_1 X^(k-1)+...+t_k belongs to Z_p[X],
    t_j=p^(jb)a_j/A.                                  (4)

Stage 11 gives another proof: v_p(I_(k-j))>=a-jb for all j. For j within the positive-pivot range, subtract the sum of the j largest depths from a. Beyond that range, integrality suffices. Both arguments retain the complete coefficient vector and final gcd.

## 3. Rational roots and the largest integer Smith invariant

If u/q is a reduced rational root, q>0, then Gauss's lemma gives q|A. At p>6k, (3) additionally gives v_p(q)<=b. If the root has exact multiplicity e, the primitive factorization

    P(T)=(qT-u)^e F(T),   F primitive in Z[T]

implies q^e|A, and consequently

    v_p(q)<=min(b,floor((a-d)/e)).                     (5)

For an integer N>0 write N_large for its part supported on primes greater than 6k. Since a=0 forces n=0, no other large primes divide A. Thus

    q_large divides gcd(A,s_max(G))_large.            (6)

This is an actual-family denominator restriction. It supplies no favorable estimate for the size of s_max(G), and makes no assertion at the excluded small primes.

## 4. Integral centered coefficients

For 2<=j<=k define

    Z_j(P)=binom(k,j)(-a_1)^j
       +sum_(l=1)^j binom(k-l,j-l)
           k^l A^(l-1) a_l (-a_1)^(j-l).              (7)

These are integers, homogeneous of degree j in the coefficients of P. More precisely, Z_j(P) is (kA)^j times the coefficient of X^(k-j) in

    P(X-a_1/(kA))/A.

This translation centers the roots at their mean. In degree two,

    Z_2(P)=-(k/2)[(k-1)a_1^2-2kAa_2].                (8)

The expression in (7) is integral even when k is odd; (8) is an identity over Q whose value is that integer.

Because p>6k, k is a p-unit. Equation (4) shows that the p^b-scaled roots and their mean are p-integral. Their centered elementary symmetric functions are therefore p-integral. It follows that

    v_p(Z_j(P))>=j max(0,n-b),  2<=j<=k.              (9)

For completeness, set Z_0 scaling conventions aside and substitute t_l from (4) directly into (7):

    Psi_j=p^(jb)Z_j(P)/A^j
      =binom(k,j)(-t_1)^j
       +sum_(l=1)^j binom(k-l,j-l)
            k^l t_l (-t_1)^(j-l) belongs to Z_p.

This proves v_p(Z_j)>=j(n-b); ordinary integrality supplies zero when that bound is negative. No pairing hypothesis is used in (9).

Define the positive integer

    C_k=product_(p>6k) p^max(0,v_p(A)-v_p(s_max(G)))
       =A_large/gcd(A_large,s_max(G)_large).

Then

    C_k^j divides Z_j(P),  2<=j<=k.                   (10)

The product is finite. This is an exact divisibility assertion, not a replacement of the actual gcd g by guaranteed content.

## 5. A deepest block forces one higher invariant to survive

Assume now a>0. Let the positive Smith depths be alpha_1<=...<=alpha_r. Suppose the largest depth b occurs exactly s times, where 1<=s<k. In a p-unit congruence diagonalizing the nonunit block, write

    E=diag(p^alpha_i epsilon_i),
    chi(z)=z^k beta(1/z)=u(z)det[E-zQ(z)].

Here the epsilon_i are units, u is an integral unit series, and Q is an integral matrix series, as in Stages 10-12. Assume the trailing s by s block Q_deep of Q(0) is nonsingular modulo p.

Intrinsically, this says that the actual bilinear form

    (x,y) -> x^T C J^(-1) D y

restricts nonsingularly to V_b, the reduction modulo p of {x:Gx belongs to p^b Z_p^k}. The dimension of V_b is s. No symmetry or residual squarefreeness is assumed.

Then some j with 2<=j<=s+1 satisfies

    v_p(Z_j(P))=j(n-b).                               (11)

In particular that invariant is nonzero, and P cannot be a pure linear power.

Proof of the reduction. Substitute z=p^b w. Divide each shallow row of E-p^b wQ(p^b w) by its p^alpha_i, and each deepest row by p^b. The total extracted factor is p^a. Modulo p the shallow rows have only their unit diagonal entries. The remaining determinant is det(diag(epsilon_deep)-wQ_deep). Since b>=1, the reduction of u(p^b w) is u(0). Dividing by the nonzero constant term yields

    sum_(l=0)^k t_l w^l
       =chi(p^b w)/beta_k
       =det(I_s-wK) modulo p,                        (12)

where t_0=1 and K=diag(epsilon_deep)^(-1)Q_deep. Equation (4) justifies integrality of the quotient. Thus t_s is a unit and t_l vanishes modulo p for l>s.

Proof of survival. The reductions of Psi_j defined after (9) are k^j times the centered coefficients of

    X^k+bar(t_1)X^(k-1)+...+bar(t_s)X^(k-s).

Suppose Psi_2,...,Psi_(s+1) all vanish modulo p. The first centered coefficient is zero by construction. Undoing the translation, the coefficients through order s+1 must therefore obey

    bar(t_l)=binom(k,l)(bar(t_1)/k)^l,
       0<=l<=s+1.

But bar(t_(s+1))=0, and binom(k,s+1) is a p-unit because p>k. Thus bar(t_1)=0, forcing bar(t_s)=0, a contradiction. Some Psi_j is a unit, which is exactly (11).

Stage 12's partial-survival result gives n>=sb, so the right side of (11) is nonnegative. When s=1 the result reduces to the quadratic centered-invariant conclusion of Stage 11. When s>1 it does not assert that the quadratic invariant survives. The restriction s<k is essential to the argument using t_(s+1)=0.

## 6. An optional Archimedean interface

Suppose separately that all complex roots satisfy |alpha-x|<=R for some x in C and R>0. Their mean lies in the same closed disk, so their centered moduli are at most 2R. Consequently

    |Z_j(P)| <= (kA)^j binom(k,j)(2R)^j.

If Z_j(P) is nonzero, (10) gives |Z_j(P)|>=C_k^j. Therefore

    A/C_k >= 1/[2kR binom(k,j)^(1/j)].                (13)

The deepest-block hypothesis in Section 5 supplies at least one such nonzero invariant with j<=s+1. This interface is conditional on a separately proved radius bound. The shrinking annuli known for m log k=o(k) cannot be transferred to the diagonal family m=k on the basis of this document.

## 7. Scope and remaining arithmetic

The unconditional-within-construction results are the large-prime individual-root cap, rational-root restriction (6), and centered-invariant divisibility (9)-(10). The direct characteristic-matrix proof additionally records the exact rational matrix behind the root cap.

The survival theorem (11) requires an actual deepest-block pairing hypothesis that has not been established on an infinite family. It does not prove a favorable upper bound on A, a sufficient normalized-content estimate, or enough total prime weight to exclude the diagonal construction. Large prime Smith data also leave small-prime contributions unresolved.
