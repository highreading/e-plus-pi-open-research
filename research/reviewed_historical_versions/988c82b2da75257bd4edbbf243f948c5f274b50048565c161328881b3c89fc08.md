# All-prime normalization bound with one copy of the actual det G

Author: Child 1. New bounded small-prime normalization research. Status: author proof, not independent verification. No numerical scan or new computation is used.

## 1. Result and exact surviving arithmetic term

Use the canonical diagonal m=k>=2 and the FIXED physical normalization of Main Stage10 and the preceding actual-return note. Put d=k-1 and

 Bc=binom(2d,d), b=oddpart(Bc),
 gamma=4^d/Bc,
 O_r=lcm{positive odd integers <=r},
 N_k=2^[k v_2(d!)] b^k O_(4k-3)^k.               (1)

Here O_r is a named auxiliary integer, not a replacement physical clearer. Let

 beta(T)=det[[C,G],[B+TJ,D]],
 beta_k=(-1)^k det J det G,
 I_j=delta beta_j, g=gcd_j I_j, A_lead=|I_k|/g,

with the unchanged delta in Section 2. Then the ALL-PRIME divisibility bound is

 A_lead divides |det G| N_k.                     (2)

Equivalently, for every prime p, a_p=v_p(det G),

 v_p(A_lead)<=a_p+loss(k,p),                     (3)

where the explicit loss is

 loss(k,2)=k v_2((k-1)!),
 loss(k,p)=k[v_p(b)+floor(log_p(4k-3))] for odd p<=4k-3,
 loss(k,p)=0 for p>4k-3.                         (4)

No Smith-rank hypothesis is required, and the coefficient of a_p is ONE, not k. In particular every prime 4k-3<p<=6k has zero normalization loss.

If A_small is the p<=6k part of A_lead, the exact uncontrolled term surviving in this bound is

 A_small divides (det G)_(p<=6k) N_k,
 log A_small <= sum_(p<=6k) v_p(det G) log p + log N_k.   (5)

The logarithmic normalization loss satisfies

 log N_k=k[v_2((k-1)!)log 2+log b+log O_(4k-3)]=O(k^2). (6)

No estimate for the det G term in (5) is asserted. In particular (6) is not an O(k^2) bound for log A_small itself.

 E_clear=b 2^(3k+2) ell_k ell_(6k),

The proof of (2) never replaces I_j by another chosen physical pair. Instead it bounds denominators of the same monic polynomial q(T)=beta(T)/beta_k. ## 3. Square-root inverse and the complete endpoint decomposition

For reference, the preceding author result gives

 nu_k(P)=gamma [z^d](1-z)^(-1/2)P,
 R_k(P)=-f(P)+4 gamma I(quo_(z^k)P)+nu_k(tau P),   (9)

where

 f(z^n)=sum_(r=0)^n binom(n,r)(2r)!,
 I(z^n)=sum_(r=0)^n binom(n,r)/(2r+1),
 tau(0)=0, tau'(z)=2/((2-z)sqrt(1-z)).

Let N be multiplication by z modulo z^k and Rrev the coefficient-reversal matrix. The exact inverse and complete B split are

 J^(-1)=gamma^(-1) sqrt(1-N) Rrev,
 B=-F+4 gamma Kplus+J tau(N),                    (10)

F_ij=f(z^(i+j)), and Kplus_ij=I(z^(i+j-k)) for i+j>=k and zero otherwise. In particular the endpoint multiplier is not deleted at small primes.

Write c_n=binom(2n,n)/4^n. The coefficient s_n=[z^n]sqrt(1-z) is

 s_0=1, s_n=-Catalan_(n-1)/2^(2n-1) for n>=1.

Thus all s_n have ONLY dyadic denominators. Since gamma^(-1)=Bc/4^d, at every odd p each entry of J^(-1) has valuation at least v_p(b). This divisibility is important: it cancels an odd b denominator in products with J^(-1), rather than creating another one.

At p=2 put s_2(n) for the binary digit sum and g2=v_2(gamma)=2d-s_2(d). For n>=0, v_2(s_n)=s_2(n)-2n (including n=0). The exact largest denominator depth in column j of J^(-1) is

 g2+2j-s_2(j), 0<=j<k.                           (11)

The maximum is 2g2. These depths are NOT charged entry-by-entry in the final bound: the determinant argument below retains the single exact index det J=(-1)^binom(k,2) gamma^k, which is sharper. In particular the square-root inverse has not been treated as integral at 2.

For tau_n=(1/n)sum_(b=0)^(n-1)2^(-b)c_(n-1-b), its odd denominator divides the odd part of n. A safe dyadic denominator depth is 2(n-1)+v_2(n). Thus tau(N) itself need not be integral at small primes. The following recombination establishes the needed integrality of the COMPLETE R_k instead of ignoring this denominator.

## 4. New denominator control of the complete rational endpoint

Let eta_m be the rational part of integral_0^1 (1+x^2)^(-m)dx. For m>=1 write l=m-1. The substitution x=tan(theta), followed by the finite cosine expansion, gives the exact rational identity

 eta_(l+1)=4^(-l) sum_(1<=j<=l, j odd)
              (-1)^((j-1)/2) binom(2l,l-j)/j.    (12)

For l=0 the sum is empty. Indeed cos^(2l)(theta) has constant coefficient 4^(-l)binom(2l,l) and its cos(2j theta) coefficient is 2*4^(-l)binom(2l,l-j). Integrating from 0 to pi/4 gives sin(j pi/2)/j for the nonconstant terms. The pi part is c_l pi/4 and is retained in nu_k, not mixed into eta.

For 0<=n<k, formula (12) yields

 4 gamma eta_(k-n)
 =4^(n+1)/Bc * sum_(1<=j<=k-n-1, j odd)
       (-1)^((j-1)/2) binom(2(k-n-1),k-n-1-j)/j. (13)

For n>=k the compact rational part is 4 gamma I(z^(n-k)). Therefore throughout the ACTUAL range 0<=n<=3k-2, and for each odd prime p,

 v_p(R_k(z^n))>=-v_p(b)-v_p(O_(4k-3)).           (14)

The factorial part is integer. The low endpoint denominators in (13) are at most k-1; the high compact denominators are at most 4k-3. Thus (14) accounts for both sides of the low/high boundary and every entry of B and D.

### Dyadic cancellation in the complete normalized endpoint

The preceding odd-denominator estimate alone is insufficient at 2. Use the elementary recurrence

 eta_1=0,
 eta_(m+1)=1/(m 2^(m+1))+(2m-1)/(2m)eta_m.

Divide by the corresponding pi coefficient c_m/4. It gives

 4 eta_(l+1)/c_l = S_l,
 S_l=sum_(j=1)^l 2^(j+1)/(j binom(2j,j)).         (15)

Each summand has exact valuation

 v_2(2^(j+1)/(j binom(2j,j)))
 =j+1-v_2(j)-s_2(j)
 =1+v_2((j-1)!)>=1.                             (16)

Thus S_l belongs to 2 Z_2. For n<k the normalized rational compact term equals

 4 gamma eta_(k-n)=nu_k(z^n) S_(k-n-1).

The inherited least period clearer b is odd, so nu_k(z^n) belongs to Z_2. Hence every such compact term lies in 2 Z_2. For n>=k, 4 gamma has positive dyadic valuation and I(z^(n-k)) has odd denominator, giving the same conclusion. Consequently

 R_k(z^n) belongs to Z_2 for every n>=0,          (17)

and in fact it is a dyadic unit: f(z^n) is odd and the compact term is even. In particular B and D are integral over Z_2, as are C,G,J. This proves that the large powers of 2 in (7) do not constitute an unavoidable entry-denominator cost here. It does not change the fixed physical clearer.

Combining (14) and (17), the integer

 E_aux=b O_(4k-3)

clears every entry of B,D in the actual range, and b clears C,J. This is an auxiliary lattice certificate only; the final g still refers to (7).

## 5. Odd-prime bordered determinant bound: det G occurs once

Over Q the Schur complement identity gives

 det G * q(T)
  =det [[G, C J^(-1)], [D, T Id+B J^(-1)]].      (18)

For example, the Schur complement is T Id+B J^(-1)-D G^(-1)C J^(-1), whose characteristic determinant is that of T Id-H because its matrix is J H J^(-1). This is an identity over Q[T]. It is not an instruction to clear each entry of G^(-1), and the bordered matrix in (18) contains NO inverse of G.

Fix an odd p, let beta_p=v_p(b), alpha_p=v_p(O_(4k-3)). From (10), M is integer and

 C J^(-1)=M J^(-1)-Id belongs to M_k(Z_p).

From (14) and the divisibility of J^(-1) by p^beta_p,

 D belongs to p^(-beta_p-alpha_p) M_k(Z_p),
 B J^(-1) belongs to p^(-alpha_p) M_k(Z_p).

Multiply just the k lower rows of the bordered matrix (18) by p^(beta_p+alpha_p). All entries become p-integral polynomials. Therefore

 p^[k(beta_p+alpha_p)] det G * q(T) belongs to Z_p[T].

Its least local denominator exponent is n_p=v_p(A_lead), so

 n_p<=v_p(det G)+k(beta_p+alpha_p).               (19)

This is the required lattice-faithful bound with one copy of det G, for arbitrary actual Smith depths. For odd p>4k-3 both beta_p and alpha_p vanish, giving zero loss. No generic Stage16 module argument is needed.

## 6. Dyadic bound retaining the full determinant index

By (17), the complete pencil is already integral over Z_2. A sharper content factor comes from the inherited binomial top-row divisibility, now applied before any large physical multiplier.

Use w=y-1=z-2 as a monic basis for the top row polynomials and all 2k column polynomials. These are determinant-one integer transformations. The inherited Gamma identity is

 mu(w^n)=2^n n! U_n, U_n in Z,

and the inherited complete period identity says b nu_k(w^n)/2^n is integer. Since b is odd, BOTH terms in (mu-nu_k)(w^n) are divisible by 2^n over Z_2. Thus the transformed top entry at row i and column j is divisible by 2^(i+j). The lower entries remain Z_2[T]-integral because the transformations are integer and the original complete B,D,J are integral.

Expand the 2k-square determinant along its k top rows. Each selected top minor has the row factor 2^sum_(i=0)^(k-1)i. Its k distinct nonnegative column indices have sum at least the same value. Therefore the entire polynomial, coefficient by coefficient, satisfies

 beta(T) belongs to 2^[k(k-1)] Z_2[T].           (20)

This uses the previously established input divisibility, not a repetition of its odd-saturation proof or old finite audit. The new point is that the complete rational endpoint is integral at 2, so (20) is a divisibility of beta itself rather than just of a heavily cleared I vector.

The leading coefficient has exact valuation

 v_2(beta_k)=a_2+k v_2(gamma)=a_2+k(2d-s_2(d)).

Combine this with (8) and (20):

 n_2<=a_2+k(2d-s_2(d))-kd
      =a_2+k(d-s_2(d))
      =a_2+k v_2(d!).                            (21)

No denominator of J^(-1), gamma, or tau has disappeared without an accounting: (11) displayed the inverse-entry depths, (15)-(17) recombined the full endpoint, and (21) uses the exact determinant index det J with the coefficient content (20). Counting the inverse-entry denominators again would double count and weaken this result.

## 7. Total loss, fixed-gcd consequences, and stop condition

Equations (19) and (21) prove (2)-(4) at every prime. The support of N_k is below 4k-3, and

 sum_(p<=6k) loss(k,p)log p=log N_k.

The elementary estimates v_2(d!)<=d, b<=4^d, and log lcm(1,...,r)=O(r) imply (6). For example the standard bound lcm(1,...,r)<=4^r gives the explicit safe estimate

 log N_k <= k(11k-9)log 2.

No prime-distribution asymptotic or numerical experiment is needed. The exact expression (6) is substantially sharper than this convenient coarse constant.

The fixed physical gcd also has the corresponding lower bound obtained from (8), not by redefining the clearer. At 2,

 v_2(g)>=v_2(delta)+k(k-1).

At odd p, (19) is equivalently

 v_p(g)>=v_p(delta)-k v_p(b)-loss(k,p),

with the additional always-valid lower bound v_p(g)>=0. These are lower bounds for the same final g, and they do not purport to determine it.

This bounded stage stops at one complete all-prime normalization theorem. The uncontrolled term is exactly the small-prime part of det G in (5), retained with multiplicity one. No bound for its growth, no useful primitive-error estimate, and no irrationality conclusion for e+pi is obtained. No small-prime scan, changed physical clearer, generic reproof of Stage16, or new first-return identity was used.
