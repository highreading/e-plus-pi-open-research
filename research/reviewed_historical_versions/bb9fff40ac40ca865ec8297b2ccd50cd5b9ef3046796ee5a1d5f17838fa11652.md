## Original weighted identity

The inherited exact differential identity is

 mu(4yU''+2U'-U)=2U'(1)-U(1).

Apply it to U=h^(k+2)P. Define

 A_k(y)=4yh^2,
 B_k(y)=8(k+2)yh+2h^2,
 C_k(y)=4(k+2)(k+1)y+2(k+2)h-h^2,
 T_k P=A_k P''+B_k P'+C_k P.

The product rule gives exactly

 lambda(T_k P)=2^(k+2)[2P'(1)+(k+1)P(1)].                 (1)

Indeed 4yU''+2U'-U=h^k T_kP. At y=1, h=2, and differentiation of h^(k+2) contributes (k+2)2^(k+1)P(1). Subtracting U(1) produces the coefficient k+1 in (1). Both endpoint terms and the factor 2^(k+2) are retained.

Applying the original identity directly to h^kP instead gives the equally exact rational-functional expression

 lambda(4yP''+2P'-P)+mu(h^(k-1)[8kyP'+2kP])
   +4k(k-1)mu(yh^(k-2)P)
 =2^k[2P'(1)+(k-1)P(1)].                              (2)

Formula (1) absorbs the lower-weight functionals in (2) by using h^(k+2). It does not divide by h, which has a zero in F_p. These two equivalent ways of tracking the weight explain why its extra factors cannot be omitted.

## Radical and allowed tests

Let R be the space of polynomials f of degree below k over F_p for which

 lambda(fq)=0 for every q of degree below k.             (3)

Its dimension is nullity(G modulo p). Derivatives and lambda are reduced coefficientwise. For any polynomial q, expand (1) on fq:

 lambda(A_k f''q+(2A_k q'+B_k q)f')+lambda(f T_kq)
 =2^(k+2)[2f'(1)q(1)+2f(1)q'(1)+(k+1)f(1)q(1)].        (4)

Here deg A_k=3, deg B_k=2, deg C_k=2. Thus deg T_kq<=deg q+2. For deg q<=k-3, the second term on the left is zero by (3), giving the exact reduced system

 lambda(A_k f''q+(2A_kq'+B_kq)f')
 =2^(k+2)[2f'(1)q(1)+2f(1)q'(1)+(k+1)f(1)q(1)].        (5)

For k=2 this test space is empty. The displayed formulas remain valid, but there is no equation (5) with a nonzero permitted test. The degree calculation is essential: using all q of degree below k in (5) would silently discard additional moments.

## Explicit missing moments at the top two tests

For f in R set eta_0(f)=lambda(y^k f) and eta_1(f)=lambda(y^(k+1)f). These are genuine additional moments, not part of the radical conditions.

In y coordinates the coefficient of y^2 in C_k is -1, its coefficient of y is 4(k+2)(k+1)+2(k+2)-2, and the leading coefficients of A_k and B_k are 4 and 8(k+2)+2. Consequently

 T_k(y^n)=-y^(n+2)+d_n y^(n+1)+(terms of degree at most n),
 d_n=4(n+k+2)^2-2(n+k+2)-2.

It follows exactly that

 lambda(f T_k(y^(k-2)))=-eta_0(f),
 lambda(f T_k(y^(k-1)))=-eta_1(f)+d_(k-1)eta_0(f).         (6)

Thus (4), together with (5) and (6), is an explicit boundary system on the entire test space of degree below k. It contains the two endpoint jets and two additional moment functionals. No inverse of d_n is required. Its residue may vanish, and p>6k does not guarantee that this quadratic expression is a unit.

The largest lambda argument in (4) for these tests has degree at most 2k; after multiplication by h^k, the largest mu argument has degree at most 3k. Derangement moments of that index are defined integrally. They need not be nonzero modulo p and no division by their factorial representation is used.

## Multiplication truncation and the corrected partial closure

Use h coefficients and the truncated shift S representing hf minus its terminal coefficient times h^k. The previously proved identity is

 GS-S^T G=e v^T-v e^T,
 e=e_(k-1), v_i=lambda(h^(k+i)).

For vectors x,y in the radical, it implies

 (e^Tx)(v^Ty)-(v^Tx)(e^Ty)=0 modulo p.

If e is nonzero on the radical, choose x0 there with e^Tx0=1. Then v^Tx=(v^Tx0)e^Tx on the radical, and

 G Sx=((v^Tx0)e-v)(e^Tx) modulo p.

In particular S(R intersect ker e^T) is contained in R under this hypothesis. This is the corrected conditional partial closure, not invariance or noninvariance of the full radical. If e vanishes on the radical, the displayed alternating relation supplies no corresponding proportionality to e, and this argument supplies no partial closure conclusion.

In polynomial terms, lambda(h^k f)=v^Tx is the first additional h-moment. For f in R it equals eta_0(f), since h^k-y^k has degree below k. Similarly lambda(h^(k+1)f)=eta_1(f)+(k+1)eta_0(f). Thus the multiplication identity connects to the extra moments in (6) with explicit triangular factors. It does not determine eta_1 or the derivative pairings in (5).
