## Actual horizontal problem

Let O=Z_p, p>6k, k>=2. Put h=y+1, m_n=mu(h^n), and

 w_n=nu_k(h^n)=n! 2^n binom(k-1,n)(2k-2n-3)!!/(2k-3)!! for 0<=n<k,
 w_n=0 for n>=k.

Thus w_(k-1)=gamma_k is a unit. All the weights, not only the top weight, remain. Let a_n=m_n-w_n. For z(h)=sum_(j=0)^(k-1) z_j h^j, define

 t_n(z)=sum_j z_j m_(n+j), u_n(z)=sum_j z_j w_(n+j).

The common congruences Gz=Cz=0 modulo p^q are EXACTLY

 t_n(z)=u_n(z) mod p^q, 0<=n<2k.                    (1)

For n>=k the right side is zero and these are precisely the G equations; for n<k they are C equations. Hence this is the actual rectangular matching condition, not the older G/D system. Its first k prescribed moments form Jz. Since J is a unit, a primitive z satisfying (1) must have at least one unit t_n for n<k. In particular the same vector cannot annihilate all these mu moments integrally modulo p. This observation does NOT rule out (1), which prescribes Jz rather than zero.

Over Q there is no nonzero common vector because G is positive definite: z^T Gz=mu(h^k z(h)^2)>0 for real nonzero z. The pushforward defining mu has infinite positive support and h>0 on that support. This argument, not invertibility of J, rules out a characteristic-zero common annihilator. It says nothing about modular injectivity.

## New explicit recurrence restriction

The actual moment sequence satisfies, for n>=2,

 m_n=(4n^2-2n)m_(n-1)-4n(n-1)m_(n-2)-(n-1)2^n.       (2)

Proof: apply mu(4yP''+2P'-P)=2P'(1)-P(1) to P=h^n and expand y=h-1. The right side is (n-1)2^n. Thus every endpoint and the inhomogeneous term are retained. No division by a moment is involved.

Consequently, for k>=3 and p>6k, no monomial h^j, 0<=j<k, belongs even to ker G modulo p. Otherwise m_(k+j), m_(k+j+1), m_(k+j+2) would vanish. Equation (2) at n=k+j+2 forces (n-1)2^n=0. But n-1<=2k<p, a contradiction. For k=2, the needed values are m_2=12, m_3=296, m_4=15952, derived directly from (2) and m_0=1,m_1=2. The first monomial is excluded by 12; the second by gcd(296,15952)=8. Thus the exclusion holds for k=2 as well at p>12.

This proves a modest actual-family restriction on the horizontal common kernel: no nonzero vector supported on one h coordinate can occur. A primitive solution modulo p^q cannot reduce to such a vector. This is not a dimension bound and does not exclude combinations of two or more coordinates.

## Complete multiplication boundary system at all depths

Let S e_j=e_(j+1) for j<k-1, S e_(k-1)=0; set e=e_(k-1), e0=e_0,

 v_i=m_(2k+i), c0_i=m_(k+i), so c0=G e0.

The exact identities are

 GS-S^T G=e v^T-v e^T,
 CS-S^T C=e c0^T-c0 e^T,
 JS=S^T J.                                           (3)

To verify, compare each entry of a Hankel matrix times the truncated shift. Interior entries cancel, the terminal row supplies the boundary row, and the terminal column supplies its negative. The terminal corner cancels. The jet boundary is zero because every w_n with n>=k vanishes. The largest boundary index 3k-1 is retained, although its corner contribution cancels.

Let N_q={z in O^k:Gz,Cz in p^q O^k}. For z in N_q, symmetry gives c0^Tz=e0^TGz=0 modulo p^q. Therefore

 CSz=-c0(e^Tz) mod p^q,
 GSz=e(v^Tz)-v(e^Tz) mod p^q.                        (4)

In particular z with e^Tz=0 and v^Tz=0 modulo p^q has Sz in N_q. For x,z in N_q the G identity additionally gives

 (e^Tx)(v^Tz)-(v^Tx)(e^Tz)=0 mod p^q.                 (5)

If N_q has x0 with e^Tx0 a unit, set lambda=(v^Tx0)/(e^Tx0). Then v^Tz=lambda e^Tz modulo p^q for every z in N_q. Under this extra hypothesis, terminal-zero vectors in N_q shift back into N_q. Reduction gives the corresponding conditional partial closure. No blanket invariance or noninvariance statement is made. The C equation in (4), with its actual c0=G e0 boundary, is an additional constraint absent from the old G/D system.

## Higher-depth length and an exact remaining moment premise

Let d_1,...,d_k>=0 be the local Smith depths of the horizontal matrix [G C]. Its transpose has the same depths because C and G are symmetric. Therefore the number of solutions of (1) in (O/p^q)^k is p^(sum_i min(q,d_i)). In particular

 c=sum_i d_i=lim_(q->infinity) log_p |N_q/p^q O^k|.     (6)

This use of Smith form only identifies which higher-depth problem is being studied; it is not asserted as a new arithmetic bound. The new information in (2)–(5) restricts these solutions, but has not bounded their number. The mod-p dimension counts the positive depths, not their sum c.

A single precise additional actual-moment premise sufficient to settle the horizontal question is the following finite recurrence-interpolation assertion:

For the fixed sequence m_n determined by m_0=1,m_1=2 and (2), and the full weights w_n above, every degree-below-k polynomial z satisfying the 2k equations (1) modulo p is zero.           (P)

This premise specifies the actual inhomogeneous recurrence and the entire prescribed jet, rather than generic observability or a Schur formula. Neither (2) nor invertibility of J proves it: summing (2) with z_j introduces j-dependent coefficients, hence moments of derivatives of z, which (1) does not eliminate. Applying a scalar order-two recurrence directly to t_n(z) would incorrectly treat those j-dependent coefficients as constant. Equations (3)–(5) likewise leave the terminal coefficient and extra moment v^Tz. This is the exact obstruction to the tempting shift argument.

If (P) were proved, all d_i would be zero, hence c=0 at that prime and the cross-linking image s would be zero. In Stage16 this would give h_p=b, not h_p=0 in general. If it fails, higher-depth solutions still need control to determine c. No assertion that (P) holds on an infinite range, nor any actual counterexample to it, is made.
