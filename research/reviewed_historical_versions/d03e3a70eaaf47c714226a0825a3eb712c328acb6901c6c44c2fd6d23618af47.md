## Limited review

Hilbert--Schmidt boundary estimate: VALID. With phi(theta)=exp(cos(theta/2))/2, the cosine coefficient convention is a_n=(2/pi) integral phi(theta)cos(n theta)dtheta for n>=1. Two integrations by parts bound its absolute value by (2/pi)n^(-2)(1/4+pi e/4), exactly the stated K_h/n^2. The endpoint derivative at pi must be included and is included. A degree-n polynomial of a tridiagonal operator couples across the cutoff only within n coordinates; its off-diagonal compression therefore has rank at most n and norm at most one for T_n(2J-I). The absolutely summable Hilbert--Schmidt bounds justify summing the full infinite tail and give 3K_h.

Resolvent trace bound: VALID. In blocks [B,C*;C,D], the complementary D+s is boundedly invertible, including in infinite dimension. The finite compressed inverse H differs from (B+s)^(-1) by H C*(D+s)^(-1) C(B+s)^(-1). Although this product is not manifestly positive, the Schur complement establishes positivity independently. The absolute trace is bounded by ||C||_HS^2/(a+s)^3 using the three inverse norms. Integration gives ||C||_HS^2/(2a^2), with the stated direction. For a=1/2 and ||C||_HS<=3K_h the constant is 18K_h^2.

Even-Legendre trace: VALID. The adjacent Wronskian integrals, with their boundary values at zero, each give P_n(0)^2/2 for even n; substitution in the three-term recurrence proves integral_0^1 xP_n(x)^2 dx=P_n(0)^2/2. The n=0 case is separate and agrees. Multiplying by 4i+1 gives the trace contribution (4i+1)u_i^2/2. The recurrence for u_i verifies the displayed telescoping identity exactly, including its lower endpoint. Thus the trace is -k log2+2k^2u_k^2.

Fixed-reference expansion: VALID. Stirling gives 2k^2u_k^2=2k/pi-1/(2pi)+O(1/k). The reference Gram factors have summable remainders after their -1/(8i) terms. Combining these facts with the bounded compression defect gives the stated O(1) expansion. It does not prove convergence of that bounded remainder, and Stage 9 does not claim convergence.

Transfer to k>=m: VALID as a consequence of the inherited endpoint argument. The rational majorant uses pole order m in t=ell/m and matrix dimension k in the characteristic polynomial P_(2k). The common inverse-power span still has dimension at most ell, so the determinant correction remains ell^2 log(1+a_m). For the lower bound, k>=m makes the Chebyshev kernel correction O(sqrt(m)) at most a fixed fraction of k on the indicated interval. The interval length and bump lower bound depend on m. The residual upper terms are uniformly absorbed because log(2k+1)/k decreases in the range used. No dimension has been exchanged with pole order.

## Original research: definitions and exact gap

Restrict henceforth to m tending to infinity and lambda k<=m<=k for a fixed lambda>0. Define

 b_m(y)=c_m exp(-sqrt(y))(1+y)^(-m),
 f_m(y)=log(1+b_m(y)),
 Q_(k,m)=k(log m)^(3/2)/sqrt(m).

Let P be the orthogonal projection onto polynomials of degree below k in L2(w0), and A be multiplication by 1+b_m. Its determinant compression is exactly exp(E_(k,m)). Set

 T_(k,m)=tr(P log A P)=integral K_(w0,k)(y,y) f_m(y) dw0(y),
 Delta_(k,m)=E_(k,m)-T_(k,m)>=0.

These are the actual weight, actual kernel, and actual compression. No fixed-reference replacement occurs in these definitions.

Write B=PAP, C=(I-P)AP, D=(I-P)A(I-P), and H_s=P(A+s)^(-1)P. The resolvent proof gives the exact nonnegative integral

 Delta=integral_0^infinity tr[H_s C*(D+s)^(-1) C(B+s)^(-1)] ds.

Since A>=I, it also gives

 0<=Delta<=||C||_HS^2/2.

For this multiplication operator the latter quantity has the exact kernel representation

 ||C||_HS^2=(1/2) double integral (b_m(y)-b_m(z))^2 |K_(w0,k)(y,z)|^2 dw0(y)dw0(z).

Indeed expand tr(Pb_m^2P)-tr((Pb_mP)^2) and use the projection identity for K. This retains the full bump and is not an estimate using derivatives of a substituted fixed multiplier. The bound by this squared-bump integral may be wasteful: its amplitude is of order m, whereas f_m has amplitude of order log m. No little-o conclusion for Delta follows merely from writing this identity.

## A rigorous scalar integral with the proposed coefficient

Define the scalar arcsine integral

 J_m=integral_0^1 f_m(y)dy/[pi sqrt(y(1-y))]
     =(2/pi) integral_0^1 log[1+c_m exp(-x)(1+x^2)^(-m)] dx/sqrt(1-x^2).

Then

 J_m=[2/(3pi sqrt(2))](log m)^(3/2)/sqrt(m)
       +O(sqrt(log m)/sqrt(m)).                                      (1)

This is an original scalar-integral statement, not a theorem about T or E.

Proof. Put L=log c_m. The central-binomial estimates give L=(1/2)log m+O(1). Let X=sqrt(4L/m), which is below 1/2 for all sufficiently large m, and put

 g(x)=L-x-m log(1+x^2).

On [0,X],

 |g(x)-(L-mx^2)|<=x+(m/2)x^4.

The function log(1+exp(t)) is 1-Lipschitz, and its difference from max(t,0) lies between zero and log2. Consequently the integral over [0,X] differs from the integral of (L-mx^2)_+ by at most

 O(X+X^2+mX^5).

Replacing 1/sqrt(1-x^2) by 1 contributes O(LX^3), because the logarithmic integrand is at most L+log2 there. These errors are O(sqrt(L/m)) as m tends to infinity and L is comparable to log m.

On [X,1/2], log(1+x^2)>=x^2/2 and log(1+exp(g))<=exp(g), so the tail is bounded by a constant times

 exp(L) integral_X^infinity exp(-mx^2/2)dx
 <=exp(-L)/(mX).

On [1/2,1], the logarithmic integrand is at most c_m(5/4)^(-m), whose product with the integrable arcsine factor has exponentially small integral. Thus the entire integral equals

 (2/pi) integral_0^sqrt(L/m) (L-mx^2)dx+O(sqrt(L/m))
 =4L^(3/2)/(3pi sqrt(m))+O(sqrt(L/m)).

Substitution L=(1/2)log m+O(1) proves (1). The terms exp(-x), the exact (1+x^2)^(-m), and the arcsine endpoint factor have all been estimated explicitly rather than dropped formally.

## What is still needed for the actual kernel trace

Let nu_k be the probability measure whose density is K_(w0,k)(y,y)dw0(y)/k. It has mass one. Let omega be the arcsine probability measure and put

 d_k=sup_(0<=t<=1)|nu_k([0,t])-omega([0,t])|.

Since f_m is decreasing, integration by parts for signed measures of total mass zero gives the sufficient estimate

 |T_(k,m)-kJ_m|<=k d_k [f_m(0)-f_m(1)]<=k d_k log(1+c_m).

It would therefore suffice to prove, uniformly in the stated proportional window,

 d_k=o(sqrt(log m/m)).                                      (2)

A stronger local discrepancy estimate could also suffice. No such quantitative estimate for the actual w0 kernel has been proved in this stage. Weak convergence for fixed test functions does not imply (2) or control this shrinking endpoint window. The elementary comparisons between gamma and w0 only give fixed multiplicative bounds and cannot identify the leading coefficient.

Thus (1) justifies the scalar coefficient but does not establish a trace asymptotic. This distinction is essential even if the expected kernel asymptotic is plausible.

## Compression defect: precise unresolved inequality

Independently of the trace question, the determinant coefficient requires

 Delta_(k,m)=o(Q_(k,m)).                                    (3)

One sufficient but possibly unnecessarily strong version, from the exact kernel identity above, is

 double integral (b_m(y)-b_m(z))^2 |K_(w0,k)(y,z)|^2 dw0(y)dw0(z)
       =o(Q_(k,m)).                                        (4)

Failure to obtain (4) would not refute (3), because the unweighted Hilbert--Schmidt estimate loses the inverse factors in the exact resolvent integral. Those factors may be crucial where the bump is large. A more useful target is a direct bound for that displayed resolvent integral using the actual kernel or a proved logarithmic-multiplier estimate with its full dependence on m.

The fixed-weight O(1) estimate cannot be used with an m-independent constant: here the function changes with m and its amplitude and spatial variation grow. The fixed h Chebyshev estimate in the reviewed proof establishes nothing uniform for A=1+b_m. No unavailable varying-weight theorem is invoked to bridge this gap.
