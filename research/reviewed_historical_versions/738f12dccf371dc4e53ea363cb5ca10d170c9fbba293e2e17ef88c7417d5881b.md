## 1. Fixed branch, normalization, and source scope

The inputs already read are Main's CYCLIC_DENOMINATOR_RETURN_CONTENT_STAGE17.md (immutable version ev-777a0fe3f7429a077c370bd3e9d1950a89b4e24d8837e905cbf97c9bebb1f376), DIAGONAL_LOCAL_CONTENT_REDUCTION_STAGE10.md, and the historical main/GENERAL_POLE_RANK_M_PRIMITIVE_INTERFACE.md. Their exact canonical family is retained.

Let k=m>=2, z=y+1, and 0<=i,j<k. Set

 M_ij=mu(z^(i+j)), G_ij=mu(z^(k+i+j)),
 J_ij=nu_k(z^(i+j)), C=M-J,
 B_ij=R_k(z^(i+j)), D_ij=R_k(z^(k+i+j)).

All bases here are monic z-power bases; their change from y-powers has determinant one. The physical polynomial and complete primitive normalization remain

 beta(T)=det[[C,G],[B+TJ,D]],
 beta_k=(-1)^k det(J)det(G),
 I_j=delta beta_j, g=gcd_j I_j, A_lead=|I_k|/g.

Explicitly, b_k=oddpart binom(2k-2,k-1), ell_r=lcm(1,...,r), E_clear=b_k 2^(3k+2) ell_k ell_(6k), and delta=b_k^k E_clear^k. No clearer or basis index is changed by this note.

Fix p>6k, O=Z_p, and suppose actual G has exactly one positive Smith depth a=v_p(det G)>0. Use precisely a Stage17 split

 G^(-1)=G0+p^(-a)x y^T,
 H0=J^(-1)(D G0 C-B),
 u*=J^(-1)Dx, v*^T=y^T C.

G0,x,y are p-integral. The notation y in this row vector is unrelated to the old polynomial variable; below its polynomial is Y(z). Set e_u=minimum entry valuation of u*, e_v=minimum entry valuation of v*. Zero-vector depth is infinity. If e_u+e_v>=a, H is integral, and Stage17 already gives n=v_p(A_lead)=0 and d=v_p(g)=a. No active return analysis is needed in that case.

Otherwise let e=e_u+e_v<a, t=a-e>0 and u=p^(-e_u)u*, v=p^(-e_v)v*. Stage17 gives

 r_j=v^T H0^j u, w=min_(0<=j<k) v_p(r_j),
 d=e+min(t,w), n=t-min(t,w).                       (1)

This note supplies actual moment formulas for r_j and a specific sufficient moment identity that would determine its capped ideal. It does not reprove the determinant-lemma argument for (1).

## 2. Explicit jet inverse: one universal square-root series

Write

 gamma=4^(k-1)/binom(2k-2,k-1), c_k=4 gamma,
 c_n=binom(2n,n)/4^n, c(z)=(1-z)^(-1/2).

The complete jet formula implies

 nu_k(z^n)=gamma c_(k-1-n) for 0<=n<k,
 nu_k(z^n)=0 for n>=k.

Indeed nu_k(z^n)=n! b_(k-1,n); simplifying the double-factorial formula in the source gives exactly this expression. Therefore, for every polynomial P,

 nu_k(P)=gamma [z^(k-1)] c(z)P(z).                (2)

All series in this note may be truncated modulo z^k. If a vector b has entries b_i and q has degree <k, the equations nu_k(z^i q)=b_i have the explicit unique solution

 q(z)=gamma^(-1) sqrt(1-z)
          sum_(i=0)^(k-1) b_i z^(k-1-i) mod z^k. (3)

To prove (3), the equations specify coefficient k-1-i of gamma c(z)q as b_i. Multiplication by the inverse unit series gives the result. This describes J^(-1) without an unevaluated matrix inverse. J remains globally rational, not integer-unimodular; its determinant is the retained (-1)^binom(k,2) gamma^k. It is a p-unit for p>6k.

## 3. COMPLETE rational remainder as a quotient plus an endpoint multiplier

For polynomial z-powers define the actual functionals

 f(z^n)=sum_(r=0)^n binom(n,r)(2r)!,
 I(z^n)=integral_0^1 (1+x^2)^n dx
       =sum_(r=0)^n binom(n,r)/(2r+1),
 mu(z^n)=sum_(r=0)^n binom(n,r)D_(2r).

For any P write P=P_<+z^k P_>, with deg(P_<)<k. These are ordinary monic polynomial remainder and quotient, not reduction modulo an unknown determinant.

Define the universal rational series tau by

 tau(0)=0, tau'(z)=2/((2-z)sqrt(1-z)),
 tau(z)=sum_(n>=1) tau_n z^n,
 tau_n=(1/n) sum_(b=0)^(n-1) 2^(-b)c_(n-1-b).

Thus tau=z+z^2/2+7z^3/24+... . Its truncation modulo z^k lies in O[z], with linear coefficient 1. The complete identity is

 R_k(P)=-f(P)+4 gamma I(P_>)+nu_k(tau P).         (4)

In particular (4) includes the low rational endpoints that cannot be obtained by extending the high-block formula to B.

Proof of the low part. Write eta_m for the rational part of integral_0^1 (1+x^2)^(-m)dx. Elementary integration by parts gives

 eta_1=0,
 eta_m=1/((m-1)2^m)+(2m-3)/(2m-2) eta_(m-1), m>=2.

The pi coefficient of that integral is c_(m-1)/4. With eta(z)=sum_(n>=0) eta_(n+1)z^n, the recurrence gives

 (1-z)eta'(z)-eta(z)/2=1/(2(2-z)).

It follows that 4 sqrt(1-z)eta(z) has constant zero and derivative 2/((2-z)sqrt(1-z)); hence c(z)tau(z)=4 eta(z). For n<k, (2) now gives

 nu_k(tau z^n)=4 gamma eta_(k-n),

which is the entire rational compact endpoint for z^n. For n>=k the jet term vanishes and the compact integrand is the polynomial (1+x^2)^(n-k), giving 4 gamma I(z^(n-k)). The exponential rational endpoint is -f(z^n) at every n. This proves (4) on monomials and hence on all polynomials.

Identity (4) is exact over Q in every degree. Its O-integrality here is asserted only in the actual required range deg P<=3k-2; the displayed compact denominators are then below 6k. No assertion about denominators in unbounded degrees at fixed p is needed.

### Exact complete-B matrix split

Let N be multiplication by z modulo z^k: N e_j=e_(j+1) for j<k-1 and N e_(k-1)=0. Put

 F_ij=f(z^(i+j)),
 Kplus_ij=I(z^(i+j-k)) if i+j>=k, and 0 otherwise.

Then (4) gives

 B=-F+4 gamma Kplus+J tau(N).                    (5)

Every term in the COMPLETE B is now displayed. In particular the low endpoint term J tau(N) is generally nonzero and must not be deleted. The high block still has its inherited exact formula

 D_ij=-f(z^(k+i+j))+4 gamma I(z^(i+j)).

Define the integral operator

 Aop=J^(-1)(D G0 C+F-4 gamma Kplus).

The actual integral propagation operator has the new decomposition

 H0=Aop-tau(N).                                  (6)

The tau term is explicit, nilpotent, and has unit linear coefficient. Equation (6) is not a similarity assertion and does not claim that Aop vanishes or commutes with N.

## 4. Actual first-return identity with the low endpoint retained

Represent vectors by polynomials of degree <k. Put X(z)=sum x_i z^i and Y(z)=sum y_i z^i. Define

 U*=polynomial of J^(-1)Dx,
 Z*=polynomial of J^(-1)Cy,
 U0=p^(-e_u)U*, Z=p^(-e_v)Z*.

C and J are symmetric. Since J is p-unimodular, Z* has depth exactly e_v. Thus U0 and Z are integral primitive polynomials. They can both be computed explicitly by (3): use b_i=R_k(z^(k+i)X) for U*, and b_i=mu(z^iY)-nu_k(z^iY) for Z*.

For j>=0 set U_j=polynomial of H0^j u and let

 Q_j=polynomial of G0 C U_j,
 P_j=rem_(z^k)(Z U_j),
 T_j=Z Q_j-quo_(z^k)(Z U_j).

Here G0 C U_j means its action on the coefficient vector. In particular all mu, factorial, compact, and endpoint data are actual; no arbitrary moment replacement occurs. Then

 r_j=nu_k(P_j),                                  (7)
 r_(j+1)=f(P_j)-f(z^k T_j)+4 gamma I(T_j)
                              -nu_k(tau P_j).    (8)

Proof. Since v^T=Z^T J, (7) is precisely r_j=v^T H0^j u written in the jet pairing. For (8), Hankel symmetry and the definition of H0 give

 r_(j+1)=R_k(Z(z^k Q_j-U_j))
        =R_k(z^k T_j-P_j).

Apply the complete remainder identity (4), noting that the remainder is -P_j and the quotient is T_j. This proves (8). There is no use of the matrix determinant lemma. The largest degree on the right is 3k-2, within the retained physical range.

The initial pairing has the separate high-moment formula

 p^e r0=-f(z^k X Z*)+4 gamma I(X Z*).              (9)

This follows from nu_k(Z*U*)=(Z*)^T D x. Formula (9) explicitly preserves BOTH contraction depths e_u,e_v; it does not divide away unrecorded powers of p.

### Concrete consequence for the all-return ideal

Let P0,T0 be the actual polynomials just defined, and set

 E0=f(P0)-f(z^k T0)+4 gamma I(T0),
 theta0=nu_k(P0), theta1=E0-nu_k(tau P0).

For every active branch and k>=2,

 min(t,w)<=min(t,v_p(theta0),v_p(theta1)),
 d<=e+min(t,v_p(theta0),v_p(theta1)).              (10)

For k=2 equality holds because these are all required returns. For general k, if either theta0 or theta1 is a unit, then w=0, d=e, and n=t. More generally, for 1<=s<=t, failure of either explicit congruence theta0=theta1=0 mod p^s proves min(t,w)<s. Satisfaction of both congruences is only a necessary condition when k>2.

Thus (8) isolates an actual first-return cancellation between factorial moments, a polynomial compact integral, and the known nonconstant endpoint multiplier. It does not assume that this cancellation is rare or bounded. In particular r0=0 alone says nothing about the last term in (8).

## 5. A specific remaining moment-annihilation identity

The explicit endpoint operator admits a stronger CONDITIONAL reduction of all returns. This section states a useful sufficient premise, not a proved property of the actual Smith branch.

Let M_U=O[N]U0 inside O[z]/(z^k). Keep this actual generated module, not its saturation. Because tau=z+O(z^2), the substitution matrix with columns 1,tau,...,tau^(k-1), in the z-power basis, is triangular with diagonal one. Formal compositional inversion uses only integral operations here. Therefore

 O[tau(N)]U0=O[N]U0=M_U.                          (11)

For any P of degree <k, the observation vector (nu_k(tau^j P))_(j=0)^(k-1) has matrix S^T J, where S has columns tau^j. Its determinant is exactly det J, a p-unit. Hence

 ideal_(0<=j<k){nu_k(tau^j P)}
       = ideal{coefficients of P} in O.           (12)

This is an exact coefficient-ideal statement about the endpoint operator, not about H0 by itself.

Suppose, for a specified 1<=s<=t, the ACTUAL operator Aop in (6) satisfies

 Aop M_U subset p^s O^k.                          (13)

Then induction using integral H0 proves

 H0^j U0=(-tau(N))^j U0 mod p^s O^k for all j>=0.

Indeed tau(N)^j U0 lies in M_U, its Aop image is zero at this precision, and H0 preserves p^s O^k. From (7), (11), and (12), if c(P0) is the minimum valuation of the coefficients of P0=rem_(z^k)(Z U0), then

 min(s,w)=min(s,c(P0)).                           (14)

In particular if (13) holds for s=t, Stage17 becomes the explicit formula

 d=e+min(t,c(P0)), n=t-min(t,c(P0)).               (15)

All coefficient depths, including cancellations in the truncated product ZU0, remain in (15).

At precision one, c(P0)=0 exactly when

 ord_z(U0 mod p)+ord_z(Z mod p)<k.                 (16)

Both reduced polynomials are nonzero by primitivity. Their product has a nonzero coefficient before degree k precisely under (16). Thus, CONDITIONAL on (13) at s=1, this simple actual jet-support condition forces a unit return. The complementary support case is not a global theorem about actual returns.

### What (13) asks in actual moment data

There is no hidden matrix condition in the proposed premise. For b=0,...,k-1 put

 P_b=rem_(z^k)(z^b U0), Q_b=polynomial of G0 C P_b.

Since J is p-unimodular, condition (13) is equivalent to the following explicitly specified k^2 congruences, for 0<=i,b<k:

 f(z^i P_b)-f(z^(k+i)Q_b)
  +4 gamma I(z^i Q_b-quo_(z^k)(z^i P_b))
       =0 mod p^s.                               (17)

To check equivalence, multiply Aop P_b by J and use its definition together with (5); its i-th entry is exactly the left side of (17). The P_b generate M_U. The unknown G0 action remains the actual Smith-complement action, and C still includes the complete jet. No endpoint term has been discarded: it is the explicit tau(N) term separated in (6).

Neither (13) nor (17) has been proved for the actual growing family. They are sufficient, not asserted necessary. The direct first-return identity (8) remains valid whether or not they hold. This is the precise remaining identity at which this bounded reduction stops.
