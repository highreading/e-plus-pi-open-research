# Primitive leading coefficients and a dominant rational-factor exception

Author: Main, current continuation. Status: author proof of an elementary polynomial theorem, followed by a specialization using explicitly inherited analytic estimates. No independent review is claimed. No irrationality conclusion or leading-scale content estimate for e+pi is established.

This document consolidates and strengthens the preceding author notes on squarefree layers. The squarefree-layer argument remains valid, but the theorem below needs neither squarefreeness nor the absence of rational roots.

## 1. General polynomial theorem

Let x be a real number, let 0<2R<1, and let P be a primitive integer polynomial of degree m>=1 with positive leading coefficient A. Suppose every complex root of P, counted with multiplicity, belongs to |z-x|<=R. Define

    L=log(1/(2R))>0.

Then either

    log A >= mL/2,                                             (1)

or P has a rational root p/q, in reduced form with q>0, whose exact multiplicity e satisfies

    e>m/2,  log q<L/2.                                        (2)

In the second alternative this is the unique rational root with q<exp(L/2), and

    log A >= (m-e)L+(2e-m)log q.                              (3)

Uniqueness here concerns the indicated small-denominator rational root. Other rational roots with larger denominators are not excluded.

All roots may be nonreal except the rational root singled out by the second alternative. No simplicity hypothesis is imposed.

## 2. Proof: nonlinear irreducible factors

Factor P over Z into distinct primitive irreducible polynomials with positive leading coefficients:

    P=product_i f_i^(e_i),
    deg f_i=d_i, leading coefficient a_i>0.

Gauss's lemma and primitivity give this equality without any nonunit constant multiplier. Thus

    m=sum_i e_i d_i,  log A=sum_i e_i log a_i.

For an irreducible factor f of degree d>=2, characteristic zero implies that f is separable. Its discriminant is therefore a nonzero integer. If its leading coefficient is a and its roots are alpha_1,...,alpha_d, then

    1 <= |Disc(f)|
       =a^(2d-2) product_(i<j)|alpha_i-alpha_j|^2
       <=a^(2d-2)(2R)^[d(d-1)].

Consequently

    log a >= dL/2.                                           (4)

This applies to each distinct nonlinear irreducible factor even when that factor occurs repeatedly in P.

Suppose now log A<mL/2. At least one irreducible factor must violate log a_i>=d_i L/2. By (4), that factor is linear and has primitive form qT-p with gcd(p,q)=1, q>0, and log q<L/2.

If two distinct rational roots p/q and p'/q' both had q,q'<exp(L/2), their distance would be at least

    1/(q q')>exp(-L)=2R,

contradicting their membership in the same disk. The small-denominator rational root is unique.

## 3. Proof: the multiplicity threshold

Let e be the exact multiplicity of qT-p, and write

    P(T)=(qT-p)^e Q(T),  n=deg Q=m-e.

The polynomial Q is primitive with positive leading coefficient B, and Q(p/q)!=0. Thus A=q^e B. When n=0, primitivity and the positive orientation give Q=1 and B=1.

The resultant of qT-p and Q is a nonzero integer. Its exact root formula, also valid for n=0 with an empty product, gives

    1 <= |Res(qT-p,Q)|
       =q^n B product_(Q(beta)=0)|p/q-beta|
       <=q^n B (2R)^n.

Taking logarithms and using A=q^e B proves

    log A >= nL+(e-n)log q
           =(m-e)L+(2e-m)log q,

which is (3). If e<=m/2, subtract mL/2 from the right side:

    (m-e)L+(2e-m)log q-mL/2
       =(2e-m)(log q-L/2)>=0.

This contradicts log A<mL/2. Hence e>m/2, completing the theorem.

The strict majority condition is essential to this argument. For a power of one primitive rational linear polynomial, the complementary factor is constant and the resultant bound is consistent with A=q^m.

## 4. Unconditional formulation retaining factor multiplicity

Let e_* be the largest multiplicity among rational linear factors of P, with e_*=0 when P has no rational root. Then

    log A >= min(m/2,m-e_*) L.                               (5)

If (1) holds, this is immediate. Otherwise the exceptional multiplicity e exceeds m/2, so it is necessarily the largest rational-factor multiplicity: e=e_*. Since log q>=0 and 2e-m>0, equation (3) gives log A>=(m-e_*)L.

In particular, if no rational factor has multiplicity greater than m/2, then

    log A>=mL/2.

Squarefree polynomials of degree at least two and polynomials without rational roots satisfy this condition, but it also allows repeated nonlinear factors and many repeated rational factors.

For m=1 the unconditional bound (5) is zero, as it should be: every primitive integer linear polynomial has one rational root with multiplicity one.

## 6. Exact exceptional interface and limitations

If the leading coefficient is smaller than the right side of (7), the theorem identifies a rational root p/q with

    |S-p/q|<=R,
    q<(2R)^(-1/2),
    multiplicity e>m/2,

and the complete primitive leading coefficient still satisfies (3). Because the inherited theorem gives P(S)!=0, this rational root differs from S. Its existence would supply a particular rational approximation, not a contradiction by itself. No theorem here rules out that factor or constructs it.

Even when (7) applies, the content saving is of order km. The inherited raw-leading logarithm is 4k^2 log k+O(k^2); this argument does not produce a fixed positive deficit from that leading content scale. It therefore does not resolve the actual odd-content problem, establish a small primitive value, or decide the rationality of S.

The outstanding new algebraic question is whether the actual determinant family can have a rational factor of multiplicity greater than m/2 in a useful growing regime. Neither nonvanishing at S, root localization, nor arbitrary-direction realization in a different widened family answers that question.

## 7. Evidence and relation to earlier notes

The proof uses only Gauss's lemma, discriminant integrality, resultant integrality and the displayed root disk. These are classical ingredients, explicitly derived in the required normalization above. No new computation or numerical factorization has been performed for this theorem.

The earlier squarefree-layer argument yielded log A>=(m-r1)L/2, where r1 counted degree-one layers. That bound remains valid but is unnecessary for (5). The present theorem narrows the possible loss to a dominant rational linear factor. It does not assert squarefreeness or establish an unconditional positive lower bound when P is a pure power of a rational linear polynomial.
