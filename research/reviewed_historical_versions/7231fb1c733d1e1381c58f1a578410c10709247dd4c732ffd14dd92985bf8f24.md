# Trace denominator and an exact pure-power alternative

Author: Main. Status: elementary author proof, not independently reviewed. The application to the actual short stack uses the explicitly inherited complete root-localization and nonvanishing inputs. No actual uniform content estimate or irrationality conclusion is established.

## 1. General polynomial statement

Let

    P(T)=a_0+...+a_m T^m

be a primitive integer polynomial of degree m>=2 with positive leading coefficient A=a_m. Let x be real, and suppose every complex root alpha_i, counted with multiplicity, satisfies |alpha_i-x|<=R, where R>0. Write the root mean in reduced form:

    r=(alpha_1+...+alpha_m)/m=-a_(m-1)/(mA)=p/q,
    gcd(p,q)=1, q>0,
    q=mA/gcd(a_(m-1),mA).

Then

    2 A m^2 q^2 R^2<1

implies exactly

    P(T)=(qT-p)^m,    A=q^m.                         (1)

No real-rootedness, separability or factorization hypothesis is required. The trace denominator q is the fully reduced denominator of the mean; the factor m in its formula is essential.

## 2. Proof by integral translation and dilation

Define

    F(X)=q^m P((p+X)/q)
        =sum_(j=0)^m a_j q^(m-j)(p+X)^j.

This belongs to Z[X] and has leading coefficient A. Its coefficient of X^(m-1) is mAp+a_(m-1)q=0.

The triangle inequality gives |r-x|<=R. Every root of F is q(alpha_i-r), and hence has modulus at most 2qR. For h>=2, its coefficient C_(m-h) therefore satisfies

    |C_(m-h)|<=A binom(m,h)(2qR)^h
               <=A (2mqR)^h/h!.

Put u=2mqR. The stated hypothesis is A u^2/2<1. Because A>=1, it implies u<sqrt(2). For h>=2 the sequence u^h/h! decreases, since the ratio of successive terms is u/(h+1)<1. Consequently every coefficient C_(m-h), 2<=h<=m, has modulus strictly less than one. It is an integer and must vanish. Thus F(X)=A X^m and

    P(T)=(A/q^m)(qT-p)^m.

The polynomial (qT-p)^m is primitive by Gauss's lemma. If a rational scalar times a primitive integer polynomial has integer coefficients, that scalar is an integer, by a Bezout combination of its coefficients. Since P is also primitive and both leading coefficients are positive, the scalar here equals one. This proves (1).

The m=1 case is already a primitive rational linear polynomial and needs no small-radius criterion.

## 3. Contrapositive and exact physical normalization

If P is not a pure power of a primitive rational linear polynomial, then

    2 A m^2 q^2 R^2>=1,
    q>=1/(sqrt(2A) mR).                              (2)

For the actual short-stack polynomial beta_(k,m), take k>=131072 and 2<=m<=k. The construction and complete clearer are in

    work/session_20261002_codex_continuation/main/GENERAL_POLE_RANK_M_PRIMITIVE_INTERFACE.md.

Write

    I_j=delta_(k,m) beta_j,
    g=gcd(I_0,...,I_m)>0,
    P=the primitive integer scalar multiple with positive leading coefficient.

Then, exactly,

    A=|I_m|/g,
    r=-I_(m-1)/(m I_m),
    q=m|I_m|/gcd(I_(m-1),m I_m).                     (3)

The cancellation of g from the formula for q is exact: it occurs in both numerator and denominator before reduction. It does not remove g from A or from the hypothesis of (1).

Choose any justified complete every-root radius R for this same polynomial. Main's Stage 2 and Child 4's original refinement give one such radius under their stated inherited analytic hypotheses. Applying (1)-(2) yields either the exact pure-power identity or the trace-denominator lower bound. The coefficient vector still contains every physical endpoint, moment clearer and final gcd. No basis change is made.

If the pure-power alternative holds, the inherited complete nonvanishing at S=e+pi gives

    P(S)=(qS-p)^m!=0,
    |P(S)|=|qS-p|^m.                                (4)

This is an exact compression to a primitive linear form, not an estimate establishing smallness. The coefficient-size hypothesis needed to trigger (1) has not been proved for the actual family.

## 4. A centered coefficient invariant and its limitations

For any polynomial as above, define the integer

    D(P)=(m-1)a_(m-1)^2-2m A a_(m-2).

The elementary symmetric identities give

    D(P)/A^2
      =m sum_i(alpha_i-r)^2
      =sum_(i<j)(alpha_i-alpha_j)^2.                 (5)

Indeed sum alpha_i=-a_(m-1)/A and sum_(i<j)alpha_i alpha_j=a_(m-2)/A. Expanding the last sum in (5) proves the formula. Translating the variable leaves D unchanged, and multiplying all coefficients by a scalar multiplies D by its square.

A pure rational power has D=0. The converse is false for general complex roots: the squares in (5) are complex squares and can cancel. No positivity inference is permitted without a separate hypothesis establishing the relevant real-root structure.

The disk hypothesis nevertheless gives the valid modulus bound

    |D(P)|<=2m(m-1)A^2 R^2.                          (6)

There are m(m-1)/2 unordered pairs and each squared difference has modulus at most 4R^2. If D(P)!=0, integer nonvanishing implies

    A>=1/(sqrt(2m(m-1)) R).                          (7)

For the fully cleared physical vector, let

    D(I)=(m-1)I_(m-1)^2-2m I_m I_(m-2).

Then D(P)=D(I)/g^2, independent of the common orientation sign. Nonvanishing can therefore be studied before or after removal of the complete content, while the size estimate (7) concerns the actual primitive A.

The complete coefficients centered at S may also be used to compute (5), since translation invariance holds algebraically. They must come from the full actual polynomial; replacing them by coefficients of a positive reference determinant is not justified by a comparison of values alone.

## 5. New actual-family question and evidence scope

A useful narrower target than excluding every majority rational multiplicity is to establish D(beta)!=0 on an explicit infinite growing-pole range with m>=3. This would exclude pure rational powers and make (2) applicable, although it would not by itself exclude other dominant rational factors. The old quadratic distinct-root result is an inherited special case, not a new task.

An actual proof must retain the first three complete leading coefficients, including every endpoint derivative and terminal moment. The natural effective-matrix symmetry is already known to fail in the documented example; matrix nullity does not determine algebraic multiplicity. Neither issue is bypassed by (5).
