# Multiple depth cuts and rational-root multiplicity

Author: Main. Status: conditional author proof, not independently reviewed. This stage combines several passing restrictions from Stage 12 at the SAME prime. It does not establish their occurrence for actual matrices or a global primitive-content estimate.

## 1. Actual objects and hypotheses

Use the canonical diagonal family m=k>=2. With h=y+1 and 0<=i,j<k, retain

    M_ij=mu(h^(i+j)), G_ij=mu(h^(k+i+j)),
    J_ij=nu_k(h^(i+j)), B_ij=R_k(h^(i+j)),
    D_ij=R_k(h^(k+i+j)), C=M-J,
    beta(T)=det[[C,G],[B+TJ,D]].

R_k is the complete rational functional with both endpoints. The low block B is not replaced by its high-degree formula. Set I_j=delta beta_j with the specified complete physical clearer, g=gcd(I_0,...,I_k)>0, and orient the primitive polynomial P to have positive leading coefficient A=|I_k|/g.

Fix p>6k dividing det G. The complete clearer delta and det J are p-units, and all blocks are p-integral. Put

    a=v_p(det G), d=v_p(g), v_p(A)=a-d.

Let the positive Smith depths of G be

    alpha_1<=...<=alpha_r,
    H_i=sum_(l=1)^i alpha_l, H_0=0, H_r=a.

The exact Stage 10 reduction, including its unit congruence factors, is

    chi(z)=z^k beta(1/z)=u(z)det[E-zQ(z)],
    E=diag(p^alpha_l epsilon_l),

where every epsilon_l is a unit, u is an integral unit series, and Q is an integral matrix series. Its constant pairing comes from C J^(-1) D after the same congruences. Higher terms still retain B.

A passing cut of size s means 1<=s<=r, with i=r-s either zero or a strict depth break alpha_i<alpha_(i+1), and the trailing s by s block of Q(0) nonsingular modulo p. Equivalently, the actual bilinear pairing x^T C J^(-1) D y is nonsingular on the image modulo p of {x:Gx belongs to p^b Z_p^k}, where b=alpha_(i+1).

Stage 12 proves, under precisely this condition:

    b_s=alpha_(r-s+1),
    c_s=alpha_(r-s) if s<r, otherwise c_s=0,
    W_s=a-H_(r-s).

Exactly s roots of beta have denominator valuation at least b_s; their denominator valuations sum to W_s. Every remaining root has denominator valuation at most c_s, and b_s>c_s. Also

    v_p(I_(k-s))=H_(r-s),
    v_p(A)>=W_s.

For a nonzero root alpha, denominator valuation is max(0,-v_p(alpha)); for a zero root it is zero. Roots are counted with multiplicity in an algebraic closure of Q_p. Stage 12 remains an author proof pending the assigned review. The deductions below are conditional on that result and its exact hypotheses.

## 2. Several passing cuts partition the roots

Suppose the passing sizes selected at this prime are

    0<s_1<...<s_t<=r.

Set s_0=0 and W_0=0. Write b_j=b_(s_j), c_j=c_(s_j), and W_j=W_(s_j). The selected strict breaks imply b_1>...>b_t.

Let N_j be the multiset of roots with denominator valuation at least b_j. Stage 12 gives

    |N_j|=s_j, sum_(alpha in N_j) denominator_valuation(alpha)=W_j.

The threshold definition gives N_1 contained in ... contained in N_t. Hence the full multiset of k roots partitions as

    E_1=N_1,
    E_j=N_j minus N_(j-1), 2<=j<=t,
    E_(t+1)=all roots minus N_t.

These groups have sizes

    n_j=s_j-s_(j-1), 1<=j<=t,
    n_(t+1)=k-s_t.

For the nonempty high groups their exact denominator weights are

    w_j=W_j-W_(j-1), 1<=j<=t.

In particular, w_j is a positive integer. This follows also from the difference of the corresponding sums of positive Smith depths.

Every root in E_1 has denominator valuation at least b_1. For 2<=j<=t, every root in E_j satisfies

    b_j <= denominator_valuation(alpha) <= c_(j-1).

Every root in E_(t+1) has denominator valuation at most c_t. These intervals are separated at each selected cut by b_j>c_j.

The tail weight is exactly

    (a-d)-W_t=H_(r-s_t)-d,

using the Stage 11 identity that the total denominator weight of all roots is v_p(A)=a-d. It is nonnegative but need not be known without the actual final gcd. A zero root, if present, is in this tail.

This construction needs only the selected trailing blocks to be nonsingular. The full Q(0) and unselected trailing blocks may be singular.

## 3. Multiplicity bound from the joint partition

Every occurrence of a fixed root has the same valuation. It therefore lies entirely in one E_j. Consequently every root multiplicity is at most

    M_p=max(s_1,s_2-s_1,...,s_t-s_(t-1),k-s_t).

Zero-size groups can be omitted. This conclusion concerns all roots, not only rational roots, and does not require squarefree residual face polynomials.

In particular, if every displayed group size is at most floor(k/2), no root has multiplicity greater than k/2. This eliminates the dominant rational-factor exception wherever the previous global factor arguments require its exclusion, without asserting any new root-radius bound.

With several eligible primes, the multiplicity of a fixed algebraic root is preserved under embeddings into algebraic closures of Q_p. Therefore every root multiplicity is bounded by the minimum of the obtained M_p. No independence assumption about primes is involved.

There is no claim that the needed cuts exist in the actual family. The statement only combines verified cut hypotheses more efficiently than treating each cut separately.

## 4. Additional arithmetic for a rational root

Suppose a0/q is a reduced rational root of P of exact multiplicity e, where q>0. Gauss's lemma gives an exact factorization

    P(T)=(qT-a0)^e F(T),

with F primitive in Z[T], up to the harmless common orientation sign. Thus

    A=q^e A_F,

where A_F is the positive absolute leading coefficient of F. This retains the final primitive normalization.

At the selected prime put nu=v_p(q), a nonnegative INTEGER. The e copies of this root lie in one E_j, so e<=n_j.

If j<=t, then nu>=b_j, and also nu<=c_(j-1) when j>=2. If j=t+1, then nu<=c_t.

If the root fills an entire high group, meaning e=n_j with j<=t, the exact group weight gives

    w_j=e nu.

Therefore e divides w_j, and nu=w_j/e must satisfy the relevant valuation interval. Failure of this integer divisibility excludes a rational root of multiplicity n_j filling that group. It does not exclude a nonrational root with fractional p-adic valuation.

For a proposed majority multiplicity e>k/2, there is at most one group with size at least e. Hence:

- If every group is smaller than e, that multiplicity is impossible.
- If the only possible group is a high group E_j, the reduced rational-root denominator must contain p^b_j.
- If the only possible group is the tail, v_p(q)<=c_t.
- If the only possible group has size exactly e and is high, e must divide its exact weight w_j.

These tests preserve the possibility that a rational root occupies only part of a larger group.

For several distinct eligible primes where a proposed majority root is forced into a high group, choose the corresponding lower exponent b(p). Then

    product_p p^b(p) divides q,
    log A >= e log q >= e sum_p b(p) log p.

This is a conditional lower bound on the actual primitive leading coefficient. It obstructs sufficiently small primitive values; it does not supply the favorable upper bound on A needed to construct them. Different cuts at the same prime must not be counted as independent prime factors.

If the majority root is forced into the tail at p, all high-group denominator weight W_t belongs to the complementary primitive factor F. In that case v_p(A_F)>=W_t. This follows by summing the denominator valuations of its roots, or from the primitive Newton-polygon identity applied to F. When the rational root lies in a high group, only W_t-e nu is guaranteed to remain among those high roots of F.
