## Original lemma: approximation rates sufficient for trace transfer
Let mu be a finite positive measure on [0,1] with all polynomial Gram matrices positive definite. Let P_k project onto degrees below k. Let s be continuous with 0<a<=s<=b, and R_k project in L2(mu) onto s ran P_k. Put e_d=inf_{deg q<=d}||s-q||_infinity. For J=floor(log_2 k), define

 H_k=2b+sum_{j=1}^J 2^(j/2)(e_(2^j)+e_(2^(j-1)))+sqrt(k)e_(2^J).

Then for every bounded real f,

 |tr((R_k-P_k)f)| <= (2/a)sqrt(k)||f||_infinity H_k.       (S1)

Proof. Choose best uniform approximants q_j of degree <=2^j. Their existence follows from finite dimensionality of polynomial spaces. Decompose s=q_0+sum_{j=1}^J(q_j-q_(j-1))+(s-q_J). A polynomial multiplier of degree d sends degrees below k-d into ran P_k; its cross block consequently has rank <=min(d,k), and operator norm at most its uniform norm. The cross block of q_0 has HS norm <=||q_0||<=2b (since e_1<=b). Each difference contributes at most 2^(j/2)(e_(2^j)+e_(2^(j-1))), and the tail at most sqrt(k)e_(2^J). Hence ||(I-P_k)sP_k||_HS<=H_k. The projection formula R=sP(Ps^2P)^(-1)Ps and Ps^2P>=a^2 P give ||R-P||_HS<=sqrt(2)H_k/a. Rank(R-P)<=2k and trace duality prove (S1).

For tests ||f_k||=O(log k), H_k=o(sqrt(log k)) suffices for trace error o(Q_k), Q_k=sqrt(k)(log k)^(3/2). In particular e_d=O(d^-r) with any r>1/2 gives H_k=O(1) and error O(sqrt(k)log k). A weaker sufficient borderline rate is e_d=O(d^-1/2 (log(d+2))^-beta), beta>1/2: the dyadic sum is O(1+J^(1-beta)) for beta<1, O(log(J+2)) for beta=1, and bounded for beta>1, all o(sqrt(J)). These are sufficient conditions, not necessary thresholds. Bare O(d^-1/2) gives only H_k=O(log k) by this argument and does not certify the desired remainder.

## Defect requires no approximation rate for s
For w=s^2 mu, residual Gram minimization gives V_w(f)<= (b/a)^2 V_mu(f), where V_mu(f)=||(I-P_k)fP_k||_HS^2. Indeed in a mu-orthonormal polynomial basis the w Gram is >=a^2 I; the best residual Gram is <=b^2 times the mu residual Gram. Take the trace against the inverse Gram. For 0<=f<=M the tilted Gram identity gives

 0<=log(D_k(e^f w)/D_k(w))-tr(P_w f) <=(e^M-1-M)V_w(f)/M^2.

Thus for M=(1/2)log k+O(1), V_mu=o((log k)^(7/2)) suffices for defect o(Q_k). Fixed positive upper and lower bounds on s suffice for this transfer; no smoothness or polynomial approximation is needed. Combined with the reviewed reference estimates, (S1) supplies the remaining trace requirement.

## Actual nonsmooth endpoint
Here mu=gamma=y^-1/2 dy and s=exp(sqrt(y)/2); the constant 1/2 in w0 is immaterial. In the Chebyshev coordinate y=(1+cos t)/2 the function is phi(t)=exp(cos(t/2)/2), 0<=t<=pi. It has bounded second derivative, phi'(0)=0 and phi'(pi)=-1/4. Twice integrating cosine coefficients gives |a_n|<=K/n^2, including the nonzero endpoint derivative boundary term. The degree-d partial sum therefore has uniform error <=K sum_{n>d}n^-2=O(1/d). This proves the r=1 premise of (S1), even though s is not differentiable at y=0. No uniform actual-weight kernel theorem is required.
