# Controlled paired extraction: stage 1

Status: author-level algebraic proofs, with exact finite computation receipts for k=1,2,3,4. No independent review is asserted. S is the actual e+pi; its rationality remains open.

## Actual matrices and normalization

Use width n=2k+1 and the complete endpoint sequences
C_r=D_(2r)-(-1)^r,
R_r=-(2r)!+4 sum_(a=1)^r (-1)^(r-a)/(2a-1),
V_(i,j)=(-1)^i(-1)^j.
Let delta be a positive integer clearing the FULL R matrix, e_j=(-1)^j, and let Delta_C be the gcd of all k-minors of C. Assume rank C=k. Form the integer (n-2)-by-n matrix
N=[C; delta(R_i-(-1)^i R_0), 1<=i<k],
r=delta R_0,
B=[N;r;e], d=det B.
Assume d != 0 throughout. This is an explicit algebraic hypothesis: the historical border-nonzero theorem could supply it for k>=24, but that analytic proof was not reread or independently reviewed in this stage. It is directly verified for the four finite receipts.

For an (n-1)-by-n matrix M write cof(M)_j=(-1)^j det M_[omit j], with indices starting at zero. Put c0=cof([N;r]), c1=delta cof([N;e]), and G=[c0^t;c1^t]. The complete signed maximal minors of [N;r+T delta e] are c0+T c1. Row subtraction and moving row R_0 past the other k-1 lower rows show that G is the coefficient matrix of the original physical stack [C;delta(R+TV)] up to the SINGLE common sign (-1)^(k-1). This sign changes neither its image lattice nor any content.

Let Q be a saturated integer basis of ker C, and A the two-row coefficient map of the signed maximal minors of delta(R+TV)Q. The exterior coordinate vector w lies in Z^(k+1). In this codimension-one setting every integer w is realizable by an integer (k+1)-by-k matrix Z, and
 delta^k det((R+SV)QZ)=(Aw)_0+S(Aw)_1.
The whole-stack identity is G Z^n=Delta_C A Z^(k+1), up to the irrelevant common sign. This follows by a unimodular Smith column reduction of C: each surviving whole-stack minor contains every top Smith pivot, whose product is Delta_C. The exterior column transformation is unimodular. No separately primitive nullspace columns may replace a saturated Q in this statement.

## 1. An actual determinantal relation and second Smith invariant

Let h_N be the gcd of the (n-2)-minors of N and g_G the gcd of all entries of G. Then
 gcd(all 2-minors of G)=delta |d| h_N.                 (1)
Consequently, writing d1(A)|d2(A) for the two Smith invariants,
 d1(A)=g_G/Delta_C,
 d2(A)=delta |d| h_N/(Delta_C g_G).                  (2)
In particular rank A=2.

Proof. The cofactor identity, equivalently the complementary-minor identity for the invertible matrix B, says that for each i<j,
 c0_i c1_j-c0_j c1_i = +/- delta d det N_[omit i,j].
The signs depend on the omitted indices and disappear on taking gcd. One direct proof is to use cof([N;r])=d B^(-1)e_n and cof([N;e])=-d B^(-1)e_(n-1), with one-based unit-vector subscripts. The identity for the corresponding 2-minors of B^(-1) follows by the adjugate identity, or by expanding a determinant along the last two rows. This proves (1). The Smith invariants of a rank-two integer matrix are its entry gcd and its 2-minor gcd divided by its entry gcd. Finally, scaling a rank-two lattice by Delta_C multiplies both Smith invariants by Delta_C, proving (2).

This is a relation between the actual complete coefficient vectors, not a rational change of basis of the fixed width-2k matching kernel. It reduces the unknown exponent to a border determinant, a rectangular content, and an entry content. It does NOT evaluate the latter contents asymptotically.

## 2. Exact cost of preserving the linear coefficient

Write a0,a1 for the rows of A, h1=gcd(entries of a1)>0, and I=[Z^2:A Z^(k+1)]. Then the possible constant corrections with zero response are EXACTLY
 {a0 z: z integer, a1 z=0}=tau Z,
 tau=I/h1=delta |d| h_N/(Delta_C gcd(entries of c1)). (3)

Proof. Projection of the coefficient lattice onto its second coordinate is h1 Z. Its intersection with the horizontal axis is tau Z times {0}. Choose a lattice vector with second coordinate h1 and the positive horizontal generator. These give a lattice basis, whose determinant is tau h1. Thus I=tau h1, and (1) gives the displayed formula. In particular tau is an integer; there is an integer lift of its generator.

There is also a quantitative lower bound on every lift. Define
 K_A=inf_(rho real) ||a0-rho a1||_1.
Rank two implies K_A>0. If a1 z=0 and a0 z=t tau, then
 ||z||_infinity >= |t| tau/K_A.                      (4)
Indeed |a0 z|=|(a0-rho a1)z| <= ||a0-rho a1||_1 ||z||_infinity for every rho. The infimum gives (4). Therefore an extraction class allowing corrections of exterior-coordinate height at most H has NO nonzero response-preserving correction when H<tau/K_A. This is a specified bounded-class obstruction, not a global no-go theorem. Its numerical strength depends on the selected saturated basis Q, which must be retained in any application.

Suppose an initial lift w0 gives (b0,a), a!=0. Every response-preserving correction gives exactly (b0+t tau,a), and every integer t occurs without a height restriction. Its physical determinant, final gcd, actual denominator, and primitive absolute error are respectively
 delta^(-k)(b0+t tau+a S),
 g_t=gcd(b0+t tau,a),
 q_t=|a|/g_t,
 |b0+t tau+a S|/g_t.
If ||w0||_infinity and ||w0+z||_infinity are both at most H, then ||z||_infinity<=2H; hence (4) applies with 2H. A constant correction does not preserve the final gcd or reduced denominator. Formula (3) therefore cannot by itself be interpreted as an approximation gain.

## 3. Exact prescribed-pair cost in a two-coordinate extraction class

Fix two columns of A forming an invertible integer 2-by-2 matrix W, and restrict w to those coordinates. Put D=det W. For a primitive integer target t=(b,a)^t, let u=adj(W)t and
 m=gcd(|D|,u_1,u_2), lambda=|D|/m.
Then the least positive multiplier for which lambda t is attainable in this restricted class is EXACTLY lambda, and its unique supported lift is
 w_supported=lambda u/D,
 ||w||_infinity=||u||_infinity/m.                    (5)
Proof. Integrality is equivalent to D dividing lambda u_i for both i. Their joint divisibility gives the stated least lambda. Invertibility gives uniqueness and the height formula.

The complete physical determinant is lambda delta^(-k)(b+aS). Since t is primitive, its integer coefficient gcd is exactly lambda. For a>0 the final denominator is a and the primitive value is b+aS. Thus the physical multiplier and exterior-coordinate cost in (5) must BOTH be counted; neither supplies new smallness of b+aS. The restricted multiplier can exceed the unrestricted lattice multiplier, which is only known to divide d2(A).

The height in (4)-(5) is exterior-coordinate height. It is not silently identified with the height of Z or QZ. If Z has entry height H_Z, its k-minors satisfy ||w||_infinity<=k! H_Z^k, so every realization obeys
 H_Z >= (||w||_infinity/k!)^(1/k).
The physical polynomial coefficient height also depends on Q. No uniform small-height integer completion theorem is claimed here.

## Unresolved selection problem and next interface

The useful next interface is a joint bound for tau/K_A in a specified saturated basis, together with an actual rule selecting a bounded lift and controlling gcd(b0+t tau,a). Alternatively one can combine (2) with genuine bounds for the actual h_N and g_G. Formula (2) alone shifts rather than solves their arithmetic content problem.

This stage neither constructs a small nonzero primitive value at S nor proves it impossible in the widened family. Arbitrary target realization is already known and is not counted as a new approximation result. No rank>1 extension is claimed: general exterior vectors in that setting may violate Plucker relations.
